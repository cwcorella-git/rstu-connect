# Artificial womb - Wikipedia

## ![](_page_0_Picture_4.jpeg)

Figure from a 2017 *Nature Communications* paper describing an extra-uterine life support system, or "biobag", used to grow lamb fetuses.[1]

An **artificial uterus** (or **artificial womb**) is a device that would allow for extracorporeal pregnancy[2] by growing a fetus outside the body of an organism that would normally carry the fetus to term.

An artificial uterus, as a replacement organ, would have many applications. It could be used to assist male or female couples in the development of a fetus. This can potentially be performed as a switch from a natural uterus to an articial uterus, thereby moving the threshold of [fetal viability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fetal_viability) to a much earlier stage of pregnancy. In this sense, it can be regarded as a [neonatal incubator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neonatal_incubator) with very extended functions. It could also be used for the initiation of fetal development. An articial uterus could also help make [fetal surgery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fetal_surgery) procedures at an early stage an option instead of having to postpone them until term of pregnancy. [\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_womb#cite_note-Bulletti2011-2) [\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_womb#cite_note-Bulletti2011-2)

In 2016 scientists published two studies regarding human embryos developing for thirteen days within an ecto-uterine environment. Currently, a 14-day rule prevents human embryos from being kept in articial wombs longer than 14 days. This rule has been codied into law in twelve countries. [\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_womb#cite_note-3)[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_womb#cite_note-4)

In 2017 fetal researchers at the [Children's Hospital of Philadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Children%27s_Hospital_of_Philadelphia) published a study showing they had grown premature lamb fetuses for four weeks in an extra-uterine life support system. [\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_womb#cite_note-naturecomms2017-1)[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_womb#cite_note-6)[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_womb#cite_note-:0-7)

# Contents

## Components

An articial uterus, sometimes referred to as an 'exowomb ', would have to provide nutrients and oxygen to nurture a fetus, as well as dispose of waste material. The scope of an articial uterus (or "articial uterus system" to emphasize a broader scope) may also include the interface serving the function otherwise provided by the [placenta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Placenta), an amniotic tank functioning as the [amniotic sac,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amniotic_sac) as well as an [umbilical cord.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umbilical_cord)

## Nutrition, oxygen supply and waste disposal

A woman may still supply nutrients and dispose of waste products if the articial uterus is connected to her. She may also provide immune protection against diseases by passing of [IgG antibodies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IgG_antibodies) to the embryo or fetus.

Articial supply and disposal have the potential advantage of allowing the fetus to develop in an environment that is not inuenced by the presence of disease, environmental pollutants, alcohol, or drugs which a human may have in the circulatory system. There is no risk of an immune reaction towards the embryo or fetus that could otherwise arise from insucient [gestational immune tolerance.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gestational_immune_tolerance) Some individual functions of an articial supplier and disposer include: [\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_womb#cite_note-Bulletti2011-2) [\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_womb#cite_note-Bulletti2011-2)

- Waste disposal may be performed through [dialysis.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dialysis) [\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_womb#cite_note-Bulletti2011-2)
- [For oxygenation of the embryo or fetus, and removal of carbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_dioxide) dioxide, [extracorporeal membrane oxygenation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extracorporeal_membrane_oxygenation) (ECMO) is a functioning technique, having successfully kept goat fetuses alive for up to 237 hours in amniotic tanks. ECMO is currently a technique used in selected neonatal intensive care units to treat term infants with selected medical problems that result in the infant's inability to survive through gas exchange using the lungs. However, the [cerebral vasculature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerebral_vasculature) and [germinal matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germinal_matrix) are poorly developed in fetuses, and subsequently, there is an unacceptably high risk for [intraventricular hemorrhage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intraventricular_hemorrhage) (IVH) if administering ECMO at a gestational age less than 32 weeks. [Liquid ventilation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquid_ventilation) has been suggested as an alternative method of oxygenation, or at least providing an intermediate stage between the womb and breathing in open air. [\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_womb#cite_note-sakata1998-9) [\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_womb#cite_note-10) [\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_womb#cite_note-11) [\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_womb#cite_note-Bulletti2011-2)
- For articial nutrition, current techniques are problematic. Total [parenteral nutrition, as studied on infants with severe short bow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_parenteral_nutrition)[el](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_bowel_syndrome) syndrome, has a 5-year survival of approximately 20%. [\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_womb#cite_note-Bulletti2011-2)[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_womb#cite_note-12)
- Issues related to [hormonal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hormonal) stability also remain to be addressed. [\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_womb#cite_note-Bulletti2011-2)

Theoretically, animal suppliers and disposers may be used, but when involving an animal's uterus the technique may rather be in the scope of [interspecic pregnancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interspecific_pregnancy). [[original research?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:No_original_research)]

### Uterine wall

In a normal uterus, the [myometrium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myometrium) of the [uterine wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uterine_wall) functions to expel the fetus at the end of a pregnancy, and the [endometrium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endometrium) plays a role in forming the placenta. An articial uterus may include components of equivalent function. Methods have been considered to connect an articial placenta and other "inner" components directly to an external circulation. [\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_womb#cite_note-Bulletti2011-2)

### Interface (articial placenta)

An interface between the supplier and the embryo or fetus may be [entirely articial, e.g. by using one or more semipermeable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semipermeable_membrane) membranes such as is used in extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (ECMO). [\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_womb#cite_note-sakata1998-9)

[There is also potential to grow a placenta using human endometrial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endometrial_cell) cells. In 2002, it was announced that tissue samples from cultured endometrial cells removed from a human donor had successfully grown. The tissue sample was then engineered to form the shape of a natural uterus, and human embryos were then implanted into the tissue. The embryos correctly implanted into the articial uterus' lining and started to grow. However, the experiments were [halted after six days to stay within the permitted legal limits of in vitro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_vitro_fertilisation) fertilisation (IVF) legislation in the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States). [\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_womb#cite_note-13)[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_womb#cite_note-14)

A human placenta may theoretically be transplanted inside an articial uterus, but the passage of nutrients across this articial uterus remains an unsolved issue. [\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_womb#cite_note-Bulletti2011-2)

### Amniotic tank (articial amniotic sac)

The main function of an amniotic tank would be to ll the function of the [amniotic sac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amniotic_sac) in physically protecting the embryo or fetus, optimally allowing it to move freely. It should also be able to maintain an optimal temperature. [Lactated Ringer's solution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lactated_Ringer%27s_solution) can be used as a substitute for [amniotic uid.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amniotic_fluid)

#### Umbilical cord

Theoretically, in case of premature removal of the fetus from the natural uterus, the natural [umbilical cord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umbilical_cord) could be used, kept open either by medical inhibition of physiological occlusion, by anti[coagulation as well as by stenting or creating a bypass for sus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-coagulation)taining blood ow between the mother and fetus. [\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_womb#cite_note-Bulletti2011-2)

## Research and development

### Emanuel M. Greenberg

Emanuel M. Greenberg wrote various papers on the topic of the articial womb and its potential use in the future. [\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_womb#cite_note-:1-15)

On 22 July 1954 Emanuel M. Greenberg led a patent on the design for an articial womb. The patent included two images of the design for an articial womb. The design itself included a tank to place the fetus lled with amniotic uid, a machine connecting to the umbilical cord, blood pumps, an articial kidney, and a water heater. He was granted the patent on 15 November 1955. [\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_womb#cite_note-:2-16) [\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_womb#cite_note-:2-16)

On 11 May 1960, Greenberg wrote to the editors of the American Journal of Obstetrics and Gynecology. Greenberg claimed that the journal had published the article "Attempts to Make an 'Articial Uterus'", which failed to include any citations on the topic of the articial uterus. According to Greenberg, this suggested that the idea of the articial uterus was a new one although he himself had published several papers on the topic. [\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_womb#cite_note-:1-15) [\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_womb#cite_note-:1-15)

## Juntendo University in Tokyo

In 1996, Juntendo University in Tokyo developed the extra-uterine fetal incubation (EUFI). The project was led by Yoshinori Kuwabara, who was interested in the development of immature newborns. The system was developed using fourteen goat fetuses that were then placed into articial amniotic uid under the same conditions of a mother goat. Kuwabara and his team succeeded in keeping the goat fetuses in the system for three weeks. The system however, ran into several problems and was not ready for human [\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_womb#cite_note-:3-17) [\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_womb#cite_note-:3-17)[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_womb#cite_note-:4-18) [\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_womb#cite_note-:3-17)[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_womb#cite_note-:4-18)

testing. Kuwabara remained hopeful that the system would be improved and would later be used on human fetuses. [\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_womb#cite_note-:3-17) [\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_womb#cite_note-:3-17)[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_womb#cite_note-:4-18)

### Children's Hospital of Philadelphia

In 2017, researchers at the Children's Hospital of Philadelphia were able to further develop the extra-uterine system. The study uses fetal lambs which are then placed in a plastic bag lled with articial amniotic uid. The system consist in 3 main components: a pumpless arteriovenous circuit, a closed sterile uid environment and an umbilical vascular access. Regarding the pumpless arteriovenous circuit, the blood ow is driven exclusively by the fetal heart, combined with a very low resistance oxygenator to most closely mimic the normal fetal/placental circulation. The closed sterile uid environment is important to ensure sterility. Scientists developed a technique for umbilical cord vessel cannulation that maintains a length of native umbilical cord (5–10 cm) between the cannula tips and the abdominal wall, to minimize decannulation events and the risk of mechanical obstruction. The umbilical cord of the lambs are attached to a machine outside of the bag designed to act like a placenta and provide oxygen and nutrients and also remove any waste. The researchers kept the machine "in a dark, warm room where researchers can play the sounds of the mother's heart for the lamb fetus." The system succeeded in helping the premature lamb fetuses develop normally for a month. Indeed, scientists have run 8 lambs with maintenance of stable levels of circuit ow equivalent to the normal ow to the placenta. Specically, they have run 5 fetuses from 105 to 108 days of gestation for 25–28 days, and 3 fetuses from 115 to 120 days of gestation for 20–28 days. The longest runs were terminated at 28 days due to animal protocol limitations rather than any instability, suggesting that support of these early gestational animals could be maintained beyond 4 weeks. Alan Flake, a fetal surgeon at the Children's Hospital of Philadelphia hopes to move testing to premature human fetuses, but this could take anywhere from three to ve years to become a reality. Flake, who led the study, calls the possibility of their technology recreating a full pregnancy a [\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_womb#cite_note-naturecomms2017-1)[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_womb#cite_note-:0-7) [\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_womb#cite_note-19) [\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_womb#cite_note-naturecomms2017-1)[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_womb#cite_note-:0-7) [\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_womb#cite_note-:0-7) [\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_womb#cite_note-:0-7) [\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_womb#cite_note-20)

"pipe dream at this point" and does not personally intend to create the technology to do so. [\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_womb#cite_note-:0-7)

## Philosophical considerations

#### Bioethics

The development of articial uteri and ectogenesis raises [bioethical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bioethics) and legal considerations, and also has important implications for [reproductive rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reproductive_rights) and the [abortion debate.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abortion_debate)

Articial uteri may expand the range of [fetal viability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fetal_viability), raising questions about the role that fetal viability plays within [abortion law.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abortion_law) Within severance theory, for example, abortion rights only include the right to remove the fetus, and do not always extend to the termination of the fetus. If transferring the fetus from a woman's womb to an articial uterus is possible, the choice to terminate a pregnancy in this way could provide an alternative to aborting the fetus. [\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_womb#cite_note-21)[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_womb#cite_note-22)

There are also theoretical concerns that children who develop in an articial uterus may lack "some essential bond with their mothers that other children have". [\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_womb#cite_note-23)

### Gender equality and LGBT

In the 1970 book [The Dialectic of Sex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Dialectic_of_Sex), feminist [Shulamith Firestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shulamith_Firestone) wrote that differences in biological reproductive roles are a source of [gender inequality.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gender_inequality) Firestone singled out pregnancy and childbirth, making the argument that an articial womb would free "women from the tyranny of their reproductive biology." [\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_womb#cite_note-24)[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_womb#cite_note-25)

[Arathi Prasad](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Arathi_Prasad&action=edit&redlink=1) argues in her column on [The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian) in her article "How articial wombs will change our ideas of gender, family and equality" that "It will [...] give men an essential tool to have a child entirely without a woman, should they choose. It will ask us to question concepts of gender and parenthood." She furthermore argues for the benets for same-sex couples: "It might also mean that the divide between mother and father can be dispensed with: a womb outside a woman's body would serve women, trans women and male same-sex couples equally without prejudice." [\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_womb#cite_note-26)

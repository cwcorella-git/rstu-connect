# Shawn Fain and the UAW: Class Struggle Leadership in Action

*Jacobin — September 2023*

Shawn Fain's leadership of the 2023 UAW strike against Ford, General Motors, and Stellantis represented a watershed moment in American labor politics. His approach offers lessons for all organizers building working-class power.

## Background: A Rank-and-File Leader

Fain emerged from the shop floor, not the bureaucracy:

- Began as an **electrician at a Chrysler plant** in Kokomo, Indiana
- Gained prominence by **opposing concessions** during the 2008 recession
- Vocally rejected **two-tiered wage systems**
- Rose through the **Unite All Workers for Democracy (UAWD)** reform caucus
- Benefited from a corruption crisis that discredited previous leadership
- First UAW president **elected directly by members** in the union's history

## The Strike Strategy

### Stand-Up Strike Innovation

Rather than the traditional approach of striking one company sequentially, the UAW implemented a **"stand-up strike"**:

- Targeted **selected plants across all three manufacturers** simultaneously
- Maintained pressure on all companies at once
- Preserved strike fund resources through selective strikes
- Could escalate by calling out additional plants

This tactic was unprecedented—**the first time in UAW history** that all three Big Three automakers were struck simultaneously.

## Bold Rhetoric: Naming the Enemy

### Unambiguous Class Framing

Fain's communication strategy centered on clearly identifying corporate power as the adversary. At the April convention, he declared the union readied itself for:

> "war against... multibillion-dollar corporations"

### The "Billionaire Class" Frame

In a notable CNN exchange with Jake Tapper, when asked if he feared wrecking the economy, Fain responded:

> "It's not that we're gonna wreck the economy, we're gonna wreck their economy—the economy that only works for the billionaire class. It doesn't work for the working class."

When told "Your critics say that's class warfare," Fain replied:

> "Yeah, class warfare has been going on in this country for the last 40 years—the billionaire class has been taking everything and leaving the working class with nothing... Whenever working class people ever step up and say, 'This is wrong, we want it to stop,' all of a sudden, Oh, it's class warfare."

### Biblical-Populist Fusion

Fain combined plainspoken class language with religious references:

> "In the kingdom of God, no one hoards all the wealth while everyone else suffers and starves."

This fusion makes class conflict comprehensible to broader audiences while grounding it in Middle American cultural vernacular.

### On Billionaire Existence

In the CBS interview, Fain stated directly:

> "I don't think billionaires should exist. No one needs that much money. I think it's inhumane."

## Transparency Revolution

Fain transformed union communications:

- **Weekly Facebook Lives** during negotiations
- Showed **actual corporate proposals** with charts
- Explained what companies were offering and why it was inadequate
- Built member engagement through transparency
- Created public pressure on companies

As Fain explained:

> "It was important to us to be open and transparent with the membership, not just in bargaining."

## Results

### Contract Wins

The strike achieved measurable gains:

- **Wage-tier elimination** — ending two-tier system
- **Reinstatement of cost-of-living adjustments** (COLA)
- Record wage increases
- Improved benefits

### Public Support

The approach generated remarkable public backing:

- **75% of Americans** supported the autoworkers (Gallup)
- Exceeded expectations for strike support
- Demonstrated public appetite for class-conscious messaging

### Political Impact

Fain's rhetoric and the UAW's success have influenced the broader labor movement:

- Compared to legendary UAW leader **Walter Reuther**
- Compared to **Bernie Sanders** for his "billionaire class" framing
- Became the **standard-bearer for labor's comeback** in 2023

## Lessons for Tenant Organizers

### 1. Name the Enemy Clearly

Fain didn't talk about "economic challenges" or "market forces." He named billionaires, corporations, and the billionaire class specifically.

**For tenants:** Name the landlord, the property company, the private equity firm. Make it concrete.

### 2. Frame Class Conflict, Don't Avoid It

When accused of "class warfare," Fain owned it and redirected: class warfare has been happening for 40 years—workers are just fighting back.

**For tenants:** When accused of being "anti-landlord," own it. Landlords have been extracting wealth from tenants for decades.

### 3. Combine Moral Clarity with Strategic Discipline

Fain's biblical framing gave moral authority while his strike strategy demonstrated tactical sophistication.

**For tenants:** Ground demands in moral claims (housing is a right, dignity, community) while executing disciplined campaigns.

### 4. Be Transparent with Members

Weekly Facebook Lives built trust and engagement. Members knew exactly what was happening.

**For tenants:** Keep members informed. Share what landlords are offering (or refusing). Build collective understanding.

### 5. Member Power at the Table

Fain emphasized: "What gave us power at the bargaining table was the company saw how eager members were to go out on strike."

**For tenants:** Visible member commitment—showing up, signing pledges, preparing to withhold rent—creates bargaining power.

## The Broader Vision

Fain articulated a vision for labor's role in society:

> "Organized labor led the way for the American dream. And that's fallen by the wayside over the last 40 years."

The 2023 UAW strike demonstrated that class-conscious organizing, bold rhetoric, and strategic action can win—and can rebuild working-class power.

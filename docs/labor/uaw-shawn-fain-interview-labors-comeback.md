*CBS News Interview*

In this interview following the successful 2023 UAW strike, President Shawn Fain articulated his vision for worker power and responded directly to critics who accused him of waging class warfare.

## On Worker Power

When asked about the union's historic victory, Fain emphasized the importance of democratic control:

> "This is what happens when workers get power. When the workers got this union back, they were able to elect their top leadership for the first time in history."

The UAW's transition to direct member election of leadership—rather than delegate selection—fundamentally changed the union's dynamics.

## On Billionaires

Fain was direct about wealth inequality:

> "I don't think billionaires should exist. No one needs that much money. I think it's inhumane."

This statement went further than typical labor rhetoric, questioning the legitimacy of extreme wealth concentration itself.

## On Class Warfare

When the interviewer noted that critics accuse him of waging class warfare, Fain responded:

> "Yeah, class warfare has been going on in this country for the last 40 years—the billionaire class has been taking everything and leaving the working class with nothing."

He continued:

> "Whenever working class people ever step up and say, 'This is wrong, we want it to stop,' all of a sudden, Oh, it's class warfare."

This reframing—that workers defending themselves isn't starting a war but responding to one—is central to Fain's rhetorical strategy.

## On Corporate Greed

When companies sought concessions even while posting record profits, Fain was blunt:

> "All three companies wanted concessions on profit sharing. And we said, 'Hell, no.'"

## On Transparency

Fain explained why the union's communications strategy mattered:

> "It was important to us to be open and transparent with the membership, not just in bargaining."

This transparency—including weekly Facebook Lives showing actual corporate proposals—built member engagement and public support.

## On Strike Power

Fain identified what ultimately forced companies to the table:

> "What gave us power at the bargaining table was the company saw how eager members were to go out on strike."

Visible worker militancy, not just leadership negotiations, created the conditions for victory.

## On Labor's Mission

Fain situated the strike within a broader historical context:

> "Organized labor led the way for the American dream. And that's fallen by the wayside over the last 40 years."

The 2023 strike represented not just a contract fight but an effort to rebuild the labor movement's role in American society.

## Implications for All Organizers

Fain's interview demonstrates several principles applicable beyond labor organizing:

### 1. Don't Apologize for Militancy

When accused of being too aggressive, Fain doubled down. He didn't moderate his message—he clarified that workers were defending themselves.

### 2. Name the Problem Directly

"Billionaire class," "corporate greed," "class warfare"—Fain used clear, concrete language rather than euphemism.

### 3. Member Engagement Is Power

The companies didn't fear Fain personally. They feared the members' eagerness to strike. Visible collective commitment creates leverage.

### 4. Transparency Builds Trust

Weekly updates during negotiations kept members informed and engaged. They could see exactly what was happening.

### 5. Historical Framing Matters

Situating the fight within labor history ("organized labor led the way for the American dream") gives present action meaning and legitimacy.

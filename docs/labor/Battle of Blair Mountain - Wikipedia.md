The Battle of Blair Mountain was the largest labor uprising in [United States history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_history) and is the largest armed uprising since the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War)[.\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Blair_Mountain#cite_note-4)[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Blair_Mountain#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKinder2005149-5) The conflict occurred in [Logan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logan_County,_West_Virginia) [County,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logan_County,_West_Virginia) [West Virginia,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Virginia) as part of the [Coal Wars,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal_Wars) a series of early-20th-century labor disputes in [Appalachia.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appalachia)

For five days from late August to early September 1921, some 10,000 armed [coal miners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal_miner) confronted 3,000 lawmen and [strikebreakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strikebreakers) (called the Logan Defenders[\)\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Blair_Mountain#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPatel2012-6) who were backed by coal mine operators during the miners' attempt to [unionize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_union) the southwestern West Virginia coalfields when tensions rose between workers and mine management. The battle ended after approximately one million rounds were fired[,\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Blair_Mountain#cite_note-7) and the [United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army) [States Army,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army) represented by the [West Virginia Army National](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Virginia_Army_National_Guard) [Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Virginia_Army_National_Guard) led by [Mc Dowell County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mc Dowell_County,_West_Virginia) native William Eubanks[,\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Blair_Mountain#cite_note-8) intervened by presidential order.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Blair_Mountain#cite_note-9)

#### Background

Since the founding of the [United Mine Workers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Mine_Workers) union in 1890, coal mines in [Mingo County, West Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mingo_County,_West_Virginia) and its surrounds hired only non-union workers, and strictly enforced employment contracts that included union membership as grounds for immediate termination[.\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Blair_Mountain#cite_note-FOOTNOTESavage199012-10) As miners in the area lived almost exclusively in [company towns,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Company_town) termination also meant eviction. In 1920, the UMW's new president [John L. Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_L._Lewis) sought to finally end the three-decade resistanc[e\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Blair_Mountain#cite_note-11) to unionization in the area. He was under increased pressure to do so from both miners


elsewhere participating in the [United Mine Workers coal strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Mine_Workers_coal_strike_of_1919) [of 1919,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Mine_Workers_coal_strike_of_1919) and from affected mine operators who were now being undercut by nonunion mines in West Virginia.[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Blair_Mountain#cite_note-FOOTNOTESavage199014-12)

This unionization push included efforts from [Mother Jones,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mother_Jones) who gave fiery speeches at the age of 83, and [Frank Keeney,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Keeney) president of the local union district.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Blair_Mountain#cite_note-FOOTNOTESavage19909,14-13) Over 3,000 Mingo County miners joined the union—and were summarily fired[.\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Blair_Mountain#cite_note-FOOTNOTESavage199018-14) The coal companies then hired agents of the [Baldwin–Felts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baldwin%E2%80%93Felts_Detective_Agency) [Detective Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baldwin%E2%80%93Felts_Detective_Agency) to evict the families of their former employees.

On May 19, 1920, a dozen Baldwin–Felts detectives, including Lee Felts, arrived in [Matewan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matewan,_West_Virginia) in Mingo County and connected with Lee's brother Albert Felts. Albert and Lee were the brothers of Thomas Felts, the co-owner and director of the [private detective agency.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_detective_agency) The Baldwin–Felts agents were [union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_buster)

[busters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_buster) who had a reputation for using violence against groups looking to organize. The agents were also responsible for the [Ludlow Massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludlow_Massacre) of 1914 in Colorado. Albert had already been in the Matewan area and had tried to bribe Mayor Cabell Testerman with \$500 (equivalent to \$7,605 in 2023) to place machine guns on roofs in the town; Testerman refused[.\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Blair_Mountain#cite_note-FOOTNOTESavage199016-15) That afternoon Albert and Lee, along with 11 other men, set out to the Stone Mountain Coal Co. property. The first family they evicted was a woman and her children; the woman's husband was not home at the time. They forced them out at gunpoint and threw their belongings in the road under a light but steady rain. The miners who saw it were furious, and sent word to town.[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Blair_Mountain#cite_note-FOOTNOTESavage199020%E2%80%9322-16)

As the agents walked to the train station to leave town, Police Chief [Sid Hat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sid_Hatfield)field and a group of deputized miners confronted them and told them they were under arrest. This gunfight became known as the [Matewan Massacre,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matewan_Massacre) and its symbolic significance was enormous for the miners. The seemingly invincible detective agency Baldwin–Felts had been beaten.[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Blair_Mountain#cite_note-FOOTNOTESavage199026-18) Chief Sid Hatfield became an immediate legend and hero to the union miners, and a symbol of hope that the oppression by coal operators and their hired guns could be overthrown[.\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Blair_Mountain#cite_note-FOOTNOTESavage199028-19) Throughout the summer and into the fall of 1920 the union gained strength in [Mingo County,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mingo_County,_West_Virginia) as did the resistance of the coal operators. Sporadic shootouts occurred up and down the [Tug River.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tug_Fork) In late June state police under the command of Captain Brockus raided the [Lick Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lick_Creek,_West_Virginia) tent colony near [Williamson.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Williamson,_West_Virginia) Miners were said to have fired on Brockus and Martin's men from the colony, and in response the state police shot and arrested miners, ripped the canvas tents to shreds and scattered the mining families' belongings[.\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Blair_Mountain#cite_note-FOOTNOTESavage199060-20) Both sides were bolstering their arms, and Sid Hatfield continued to fuel the resistance, specifically by converting Testerman's jewelry store into a gun shop[.\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Blair_Mountain#cite_note-FOOTNOTESavage199050-21)

On January 26, 1921, the trial of Hatfield for killing Albert Felts began. It was in the national spotlight and brought much attention to the miners' cause. Hatfield's stature and mythical status grew as the trial proceeded. He posed and talked to reporters, fanning the flames of his own legend. All men were acquitted in the end, but overall the union was facing significant setbacks.[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Blair_Mountain#cite_note-FOOTNOTEShogan200498-22) Eighty percent of mines had reopened with imported replacements and ex-strikers who signed [yellow-dog contracts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow-dog_contracts) to return to work[.\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Blair_Mountain#cite_note-FOOTNOTEShogan200481-23) In mid-May 1921 union miners launched a full-scale assault on non-union mines. In a short time the conflict had consumed the entire Tug River Valley. This "Three Days Battle" was finally ended by a flag of truce and the implementation of [martial law.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martial_law)[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Blair_Mountain#cite_note-FOOTNOTESavage199053-24) From the beginning, the miners perceived the enforcement of martial law as one-sided.[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Blair_Mountain#cite_note-FOOTNOTESavage199057-25) Hundreds of miners were arrested; the smallest of infractions could mean imprisonment, while those on the side of "law and order" were seen as immune[.\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Blair_Mountain#cite_note-FOOTNOTESavage199058-26) The miners responded with [guerrilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guerrilla_tactics) [tactics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guerrilla_tactics) and sabotage.[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Blair_Mountain#cite_note-FOOTNOTESavage199058-26)

In the midst of this tense situation, Hatfield traveled to [Mc Dowell County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mc Dowell_County,_West_Virginia) on August 1, 1921, to stand trial on charges of dynamiting a [coal tipple.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tipple) Along with him traveled a good friend, Ed Chambers, and their wives[.\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Blair_Mountain#cite_note-FOOTNOTEShogan2004154%E2%80%93156-27) As they walked up the courthouse stairs, unarmed and flanked by their wives, a group of

Despite Sally Chambers' protests, one of the agents ran down the stairs and shot Chambers once more, point-blank in the back of the head[.\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Blair_Mountain#cite_note-FOOTNOTEShogan2004157%E2%80%93158-28) Hatfield's and Chambers' bodies were returned to Matewan, and word of the slayings spread through the mountains.

The miners, angry that Hatfield had been murdered and knowing the assassins would escape punishment[,\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Blair_Mountain#cite_note-FOOTNOTESavage199073-29) began to take up arms and pour out of their mountain settlements. Miners along the [Little Coal River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal_River_(West_Virginia)) were among the first to organize, and began actions such as patrolling and guarding the area. Sheriff [Don Cha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Chafin)fin of [Logan County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logan_County,_West_Virginia) sent troopers to the Little Coal River area, where armed miners captured the troopers, disarmed them and sent them fleeing[.\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Blair_Mountain#cite_note-FOOTNOTESavage199075-30)

On August 7, 1921, the leaders of the [United Mine Workers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Mine_Workers) (UMW) District 17, which encompasses much of southern West Virginia, called a rally at the state capitol in [Charleston.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charleston,_West_Virginia) These leaders were [Frank Keeney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Keeney) and Fred Mooney, who were veterans of previous mine confl[icts in the region.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Virginia_coal_wars) Both were local, well-read and articulate. Keeney and Mooney met with [Governor Ephraim Morgan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ephraim_F._Morgan) and presented him with a petition of the miners' demands[.\[30\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Blair_Mountain#cite_note-FOOTNOTEShogan2004164%E2%80%93165-31) When Morgan summarily rejected the demands, the miners became more restless and began to talk of a march on Mingo to free the confined miners, end martial law and organize the county. But Blair Mountain, Logan County, and Sheriff Chafin stood directly in the way.[\[31\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Blair_Mountain#cite_note-FOOTNOTEShogan2004166-32)

#### Battle

At a rally on August 7, [Mary Harris "Mother" Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mother_Jones) called on the miners not to march into Logan and Mingo counties and set up the union by force. Accused by some of losing her nerve, she feared a bloodbath in a battle between lightly armed union forces and the more heavily armed Logan County deputies. Yet, feeling Morgan had lied to them again, armed men began gathering at [Lens Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lens_Creek) Mountain, near [Marmet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marmet,_West_Virginia) in [Kanawha County,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanawha_County) on August 20. Four days later an estimated 13,000 had gathered and began marching towards Logan County. Impatient to get to the fighting, miners near [St. Albans,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Albans,_West_Virginia) in Kanawha County, commandeered a [Chesapeake and Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chesapeake_and_Ohio_Railway) [freight train,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freight_train) renamed by the miners the "Blue Steel Special", to meet up with the advanced column of marchers at [Danville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danville,_West_Virginia) in [Boone County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boone_County,_West_Virginia) on their way to "Bloody Mingo". During this time Keeney and Mooney fled to Ohio, while the fiery [Bill Blizzard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Blizzard) assumed quasi-leadership of the miners. Meanwhile, the anti-union Sheriff Chafi[n\[32\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Blair_Mountain#cite_note-33) had begun to set up defenses on Blair Mountain. He was supported financially by the Logan County Coal Operators Association, creating the nation's largest [private armed force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_militia) of nearly 2,000.

The first skirmishes occurred on the morning of August 25. The bulk of the miners were still 15 mi (24 km) away. The following day, President [Warren G. Harding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warren_G._Harding) threatened to send in federal troops and Army [Martin MB-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_MB-1) bombers. After a long meeting in [Madison,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison,_West_Virginia) the seat of Boone County, the miners were convinced to return home. But the struggle was far from over. By August 29 the battle was fully underway. Chafin's men, though outnumbered, had the advantage of higher positions and better weaponry. Private planes were hired to drop homemade bombs on the miners. On August 30, Morgan appointed Colonel William Eubanks of the [West Virginia National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Virginia_National_Guard) to command the government and volunteer forces confronting the miners[.\[34\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Blair_Mountain#cite_note-FOOTNOTEShogan2004-35) Sporadic gun battles continued for a week, with the miners at one time nearly breaking through to the town of [Logan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logan,_West_Virginia) and their target destinations, the non-unionized Logan and Mingo counties to the south. [Gatling guns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gatling_gun) and [machine guns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine_guns) were employed by both sides, along with additional firepower.[\[35\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Blair_Mountain#cite_note-36)[\[36\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Blair_Mountain#cite_note-37)[\[37\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Blair_Mountain#cite_note-38) Chafin's forces consisted of 90 men from Bluefi[eld, West Virginia;](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bluefield,_West_Virginia) 40 from [Huntington, West Virginia;](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huntington,_West_Virginia) and about 120 from the [West Virginia State Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Virginia_State_Police)[.\[38\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Blair_Mountain#cite_note-Hearings-39) Three of Chafin's forces, two volunteers and a deputy sheriff, were killed[,\[39\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Blair_Mountain#cite_note-40)[\[40\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Blair_Mountain#cite_note-41)[\[41\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Blair_Mountain#cite_note-42)[\[38\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Blair_Mountain#cite_note-Hearings-39) and one miner was fatally wounded[.\[42\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Blair_Mountain#cite_note-43)

Federal troops arrived by September 2. The miners, many of whom were veterans themselves, were unwilling to fire on U. S. troops.[\[43\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Blair_Mountain#cite_note-44)[\[44\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Blair_Mountain#cite_note-45) Bill Blizzard passed the word for the miners to start heading home the following day. Miners fearing jail and confiscation of their guns found clever ways to hide rifles and handguns in the woods before leaving Logan County. Some were found later, along with many spent and live cartridges which helped archeologists reconstruct the course of the fighting[.\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Blair_Mountain#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPatel2012-6)

After the battle, 985 miners were indicted for [murder,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murder) [conspiracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conspiracy_(crime)) to commit murder, [accessory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accessory_(legal_term)) to murder, and [treason](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treason) against the State of West Virginia[.\[45\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Blair_Mountain#cite_note-46) Though the majority were [acquitted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acquittal) by sympathetic juries, others were imprisoned for up to four years, with the last being paroled in 1925. At Blizzard's trial, the unexploded bomb was used as evidence of the government and companies' brutality, and he was acquitted[.\[34\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Blair_Mountain#cite_note-FOOTNOTEShogan2004-35)

#### Legacy

In the short term the battle was an overwhelming victory for coal industry owners and management[.\[47\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Blair_Mountain#cite_note-48) [United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Mine_Workers_of_America) [Mine Workers of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Mine_Workers_of_America) (UMWA or UMW) membership plummeted from more than 50,000 miners to approximately 10,000 over the next several years, and it was not until 1935—following the [Great Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression) and the beginning of the [New Deal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Deal) under President [Franklin Delano Roosevelt—](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_Delano_Roosevelt)that the UMW fully organized in southern West Virginia.

This union defeat had major implications for the UMWA as a whole. After [World War I,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I) the coal industry had begun to collapse. Because of the defeat in West Virginia, the union was also undermined in Pennsylvania and Kentucky. By the end of 1925, Illinois was the only remaining unionized state in terms of soft coal production.

In the long term, the battle raised awareness of the appalling conditions miners faced in the dangerous West Virginia coalfields.[\[48\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Blair_Mountain#cite_note-49) It also led to a change in union tactics in political battles to get the law on labor's side, by confronting recalcitrant and abusive management. This eventually resulted in a much larger organized labor victory a few years later during the [New Deal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Deal) in 1933. That in turn led to the UMWA helping organize many better-known unions, such as the [Steel Workers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steel_Workers_of_America) during the mid-'30s, and spurred the creation of labor union affiliations and umbrella organizations, such as the [American Federation of Labor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Federation_of_Labor) (AFL) and [Congress of Industrial Organizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_Industrial_Organizations) (CIO).

In terms of literature, [Diane Gilliam Fisher'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diane_Gilliam_Fisher)s poetry collection [Kettle Bottom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kettle_Bottom) explores "the West Virginia mine wars of 1920-21"[.\[49\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Blair_Mountain#cite_note-50)

#### Future of site

Starting in mid 2006 a local hobby [archeologist,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeology) Kenneth King, led a team of professional archeologists to further investigate the battlefield. King and the team's initial survey "mapped 15 combat sites and discovered more than a thousand artifacts, from rifle and shotgun shell casings to coins and batteries [and] little sign of [disturbance"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disturbance_(archaeology)) to the site, challenging earlier surveys conducted by [Arch Coal Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arch_Coal_Inc), one of two companies that own the mining rights to Blair Mountain. In April 2008, Blair Mountain was chosen for the list of protected places on the [National Register of Historic Places](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Register_of_Historic_Places) (NRHP).

The site was accepted and added to the NRHP list on March 30, 2009, but clerical errors by the West Virginia State Historic Preservation Office (SHPO) failed to notarize all objections, and it was removed[.\[50\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Blair_Mountain#cite_note-WVDACH1-51) In mid-2010, "subsidiaries of two of the United States' largest coal producers – [Arch Coal,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arch_Coal) Inc., and [Massey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massey_Energy_Company) [Energy Company,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massey_Energy_Company) ... – [held] permits to blast and [strip-mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_mining) huge chunks of the upper slopes and ridge of Blair Mountain, removing much of the mountaintop", [National Geographic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Geographic_Society) reported.[\[51\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Blair_Mountain#cite_note-52)

In October 2012 a federal district judge ruled that a coalition of preservation groups did not have [standing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standing_(law)) to [sue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawsuit) to protect the historic site.[\[52\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Blair_Mountain#cite_note-53) On August 26, 2014, the [United States Court of Appeals for the](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Court_of_Appeals_for_the_District_of_Columbia_Circuit) [District of Columbia Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Court_of_Appeals_for_the_District_of_Columbia_Circuit) voted 2–1 to overturn the ruling and returned the case.[\[53\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Blair_Mountain#cite_note-54)


Political cartoon showing soldier with rifle trained on striking miner; standing to the right, behind the soldier, are President Harding and a mine owner. The caption reads "West Virginia: The same old line up." (1921)[\[46\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Blair_Mountain#cite_note-47)

In April 2016 the order to remove the Blair Mountain battlefield from the National Register was overturned by a federal court, and the further decision to add the site back to the register was turned over to the [Keeper of the National Register.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keeper_of_the_Register) On June 27, 2018, the Keeper's Office decided that the 2009 decision to remove the site from its listings was "erroneous" and issued a statement confirming that as of that date the site was again on the National Register[.\[54\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Blair_Mountain#cite_note-55)[\[55\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Blair_Mountain#cite_note-56)

### Historic interpretation

The Battle of Blair Mountain is related by the State of West Virginia through a Historic Highway marker. The marker was made by the West Virginia Division of Culture and History. The marker reads,

BATTLE OF BLAIR MT. In August of 1921, 7000 striking miners led by [Bill Blizzard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Blizzard) met at [Marmet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marmet,_West_Virginia) for a march on [Logan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logan,_West_Virginia) to organize the southern coalfields for the [UMWA.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UMWA) Reaching Blair Mt. on August 31, they were repelled by deputies and mine guards, under Sheriff [Don](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Chafin) [Cha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Chafin)fin, waiting in fortified positions. The five day battle ended with the arrival of U. S. Army and Air Corps. UMWA organizing efforts in southern WV were halted until 1933.


Armed coal miners surrendering their weapons to federal troops.

The marker is on [West Virginia 17,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Virginia_17) about 8 miles (13 km) east of Logan, between [Ethel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethel,_West_Virginia) and [Blair.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blair,_Logan_County,_West_Virginia)[\[56\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Blair_Mountain#cite_note-57)

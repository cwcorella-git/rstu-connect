## ![](_page_0_Figure_2.jpeg)

Examples for methods to [transition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_transition) towards a low-carbon economy: [Concentrated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concentrated_solar_power) solar power with [molten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal_energy_storage#Molten_salt_technology) salt heat [storage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal_energy_storage#Molten_salt_technology) in Spain; wind [energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_energy) in South Africa; electrified public [transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_transport) in Singapore; and [renewable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renewable_energy) energy sources, especially solar [photovoltaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photovoltaic_system) and [wind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_power), are providing an increasing share of electricity production.

A **low-carbon economy** (**LCE**) is an [economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy) which [absorbs as much greenhouse gas as it emits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Net_zero_emissions). [\[2\]](#page-4-1) [Greenhouse gas \(GHG\) emissions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenhouse_gas_emissions) due to human activity are the dominant cause of observed [climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change) [change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change) since the mid-20th century. [\[3\]](#page-4-2) There are many proven approaches for moving to a low-carbon economy, such as encouraging [renewable energy transition,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_transition) [energy conservation,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_conservation) and electrification of transportation (e.g. [electric vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_vehicle)). An example are [zero-carbon cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero-carbon_city).

Shifting from high-carbon economies to low-carbon economies on a global scale could bring substantial benefits for all countries.[\[4\]](#page-4-3) It would also contribute to [climate change mitigation.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_mitigation)

### **Definition and terminology**

There are many synonyms or similar terms in use for *low-carbon economy* which stress different aspects of the concept, for example: [green economy,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_economy) [sustainable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainability) economy, [carbon-neutral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon-neutral) economy, [low](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenhouse_gas_emissions)[emissions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenhouse_gas_emissions) economy, climate-friendly economy, decarbonised economy.

The term *carbon* in *low-carbon economy* is short hand for all [greenhouse gases.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenhouse_gas)

The [UK Office for National Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UK_Office_for_National_Statistics) published the following definition in 2017: "The low carbon economy is defined as economic activities that deliver goods and services that generate significantly lower emissions of greenhouse gases; predominantly carbon dioxide."[\[5\]:](#page-4-4)2

### **Rationale and aims**

GHG emissions due to human activity are the dominant cause of observed [climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change) since the mid-20th century. [\[3\]](#page-4-2) Continued emission of greenhouse gases will cause longlasting changes around the world, increasing the likelihood of severe, pervasive, and irreversible [effects for people and](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Effects_of_climate_change) [ecosystems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Effects_of_climate_change). [\[3\]](#page-4-2)

Nations may seek to become low-carbon or decarbonised economies as a part of a national [climate change mitigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_mitigation) strategy. A comprehensive strategy to mitigate [climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change) is through [carbon neutrality.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_neutrality) [\[6\]](#page-4-5)

### **Methods**

Achieving a low-carbon economy involves reducing [greenhouse gas emissions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenhouse_gas_emissions) in all sectors that produce greenhouse gases, for example energy, transportation, industry, and agriculture. The literature often speaks of a *transition* from a high-carbon economy to a low-carbon economy. This transition should take place in a just manner (this is termed [just transition\)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Just_transition).[\[7\]](#page-5-0):75

## ![](_page_1_Figure_5.jpeg)

Countries that managed to reduce their [greenhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenhouse_gas_emissions) gas emissions (working towards a low-carbon economy) while still growing their economy. This is called [eco-economic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eco-economic_decoupling) decoupling.

There are many strategies and approaches for moving to a low-carbon economy, such as encouraging [renewable energy transition,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_transition) [efficient energy use,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Efficient_energy_use) [energy conservation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_conservation), [electric vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_vehicle), [heat pumps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heat_pump), and [climate-smart agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate-smart_agriculture). This requires for example suitable [energy policies,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_policy) financial incentives (e.g. [emissions trading,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emissions_trading) [carbon tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_tax)), [individual action on climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Individual_action_on_climate_change), [business action on climate change.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_action_on_climate_change)

### **Actions taken by countries**

On the international scene, the most prominent early step in the direction of a low-carbon economy was the signing of the [Kyoto Protocol,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto_Protocol) which came into force in 2005, under which most industrialized countries committed to reduce their carbon emissions.[\[8\]](#page-5-1)[\[9\]](#page-5-2)

[OECD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OECD) countries could learn from each other and follow the examples of these countries in these sectors: [Switzerland for their energy sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_in_Switzerland), UK for their industry, [Netherlands for their transport sector,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_the_Netherlands) [South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture_in_South_Korea) [Korea for their agriculture,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture_in_South_Korea) and [Sweden for their building sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architecture_of_Sweden). [\[10\]](#page-5-3)

## **Co-benefits**

The main benefit of a transition to low-carbon economies is that it would contribute towards [climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_mitigation) [change mitigation.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_mitigation) Apart from that, other co-benefits can also be identified: Low-carbon economies present multiple benefits to ecosystem resilience,[\[11\]](#page-5-4) trade, employment, health, energy security, and

#### industrial competitiveness.[\[12\]](#page-5-5)[\[13\]](#page-5-6)

During the green transition, workers in [carbon-intensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emission_intensity) industries are more likely to lose their jobs. The transition to a carbon-neutral economy will put more jobs at danger in regions with higher percentages of employment in carbonintensive industries.[\[14\]](#page-5-7)[\[15\]](#page-5-8)[\[16\]](#page-5-9) Employment opportunities by the green transition are associated with the use of renewable energy sources or building activity for infrastructure improvements and renovations.[\[17\]](#page-5-10)

Low emission industrial development and [resource efficiency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resource_efficiency) can offer many opportunities to increase the competitiveness of economies and companies. According to the [Low Emission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low_Emission_Development_Strategies_Global_Partnership_(LEDS_GP)) [Development Strategies Global Partnership \(LEDS GP\)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low_Emission_Development_Strategies_Global_Partnership_(LEDS_GP)), there is often a clear business case for switching to lower emission technologies, with payback periods ranging largely from 0.5– 5 years, leveraging financial investment.[\[18\]](#page-5-11)

### **Energy aspects**

#### **Low-carbon electricity**

[Low-carbon electricity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low-carbon_electricity) or low-carbon power is [electricity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electricity) produced with substantially lower [greenhouse gas emissions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenhouse_gas_emissions) over the entire lifecycle than [power generation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_generation) using [fossil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuel) [fuels.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuel) [\[19\]](#page-5-12) The [energy transition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_transition) to low-carbon power is one of the most important actions required to [limit climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_mitigation) [change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_mitigation). [\[20\]](#page-5-13)

Solar array at Nellis Solar [Power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nellis_Solar_Power_Plant) Plant. These panels track the sun in one axis.

Low carbon power generation sources include [wind power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_power), [solar power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_power), [nuclear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_power) [power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_power) and most [hydropower.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydropower) [\[21\]](#page-6-0)[\[22\]](#page-6-1) The term largely excludes conventional [fossil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuel_power_station) [fuel plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuel_power_station) sources, and is only used to describe a particular subset of operating fossil fuel power systems, specifically, those that are successfully coupled with a [flue gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flue_gas) [carbon capture and storage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_capture_and_storage) (CCS) system.[\[23\]](#page-6-2) Globally almost 40% of electricity generation came from lowcarbon sources in 2020: about 10% being nuclear power, almost 10% wind and solar, and around 20% hydropower and other renewables.[\[20\]](#page-5-13) Very little lowcarbon power comes from fossil sources, mostly due to the cost of CCS technology. [\[24\]](#page-6-3)


Share of primary energy from low-carbon sources, 2018

#### **Nuclear power**

As of 2021, the expansion of nuclear energy as a method of achieving a low-carbon economy has varying degrees of support.[\[25\]](#page-6-5) Agencies and organizations that believe decarbonization is not possible without some nuclear power expansion include the [United Nations Economic Commission for Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Economic_Commission_for_Europe), [\[26\]](#page-6-6) the [International Energy Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Energy_Agency) (IEA),[\[27\]](#page-6-7) and the [International Atomic Energy Agency.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Atomic_Energy_Agency) [\[28\]](#page-6-8) The IEA believes that widespread decarbonization must occur by 2040 in order mitigate the adverse effects of climate change and that nuclear power must play a role.

#### **Energy transition**

An [energy transition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_transition) (or energy system transformation) is a major structural change to [energy supply](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_supply) and [consumption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_energy_supply_and_consumption) in an [energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_system) [system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_system). Currently, a transition to [sustainable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_energy) [energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_energy) is underway to [limit climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_mitigation). Most of the sustainable energy is renewable energy. Therefore, another term for *energy transition* is renewable energy transition. The current transition aims to reduce [greenhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenhouse_gas_emissions) [gas emissions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenhouse_gas_emissions) from energy quickly and sustainably, mostly by [phasing-down fossil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuel_phase-out) [fuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuel_phase-out) and changing as many processes as possible to operate on [low carbon electricity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low-carbon_power). [\[30\]](#page-6-9) A previous energy transition perhaps took place during the [Industrial Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Revolution) from 1760 onwards, from [wood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wood) and other [biomass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biomass) to [coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal), followed by [oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petroleum) and later [natural gas.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_gas) [\[31\]](#page-7-0)[\[32\]](#page-7-1)

Over three-quarters of the [world's energy needs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_mix) are met by burning [fossil fuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuels), but this usage emits greenhouse gases.[\[33\]](#page-7-2) [Energy production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_energy_supply_and_consumption) [and consumption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_energy_supply_and_consumption) are responsible for most

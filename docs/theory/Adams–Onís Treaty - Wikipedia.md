# **Adams–Onís Treaty**

The **Adams–Onís Treaty** [\(Spanish:](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_language) *Tratado de Adams-Onís*) of 1819,[\[1\]](#page-9-0) also known as the **Transcontinental Treaty**, [\[2\]](#page-9-1) the **Spanish Cession**, [\[3\]](#page-10-0) the **Florida Purchase Treaty**, [\[4\]](#page-10-1) or the **Florida Treaty**, [\[5\]](#page-10-2)[\[6\]](#page-10-3) was a [treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty) between the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States) and [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Empire) in 1819 that ceded [Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Florida) to the U.S. and defined the boundary between the U.S. and Mexico [\(New Spain\)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Spain). It settled a standing border dispute between the two countries and was considered a triumph of American [diplomacy.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diplomacy) It came during the [successful Spanish American wars of independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_American_wars_of_independence) [against Spain.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_American_wars_of_independence)

Florida had become a burden to Spain, which could not afford to send settlers or staff garrisons, so Madrid decided to cede the territory to the United States in exchange for settling the boundary dispute along the [Sabine River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabine_River_(Texas%E2%80%93Louisiana)) in [Spanish Texas.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Texas) The treaty, named for signatories [John](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Quincy_Adams) [Quincy Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Quincy_Adams) and [Luis de Onís,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_de_On%C3%ADs) established the boundary of U.S. territory and claims through the [Rocky Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_Mountains) and west to the Pacifi[c Ocean,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Ocean) in exchange for Washington paying residents' claims against the Spanish government up to a total of 5 million [Spanish dollars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_dollars)[\[7\]](#page-10-4) (purchasing power equivalent to US\$102,700,000 in 2024) and relinquishing the U.S. claims on parts of Spanish Texas west of the Sabine River and other Spanish areas, under the terms of the [Louisiana Purchase.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_Purchase)

The treaty remained in full effect for only 183 days: from February 22, 1821, to August 24, 1821, when Spanish military officials signed the [Treaty of Córdoba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_C%C3%B3rdoba) acknowledging the independence of Mexico; Spain repudiated that treaty, but Mexico effectively took control of Spain's former colony. The [Treaty of Limits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Limits_(Mexico%E2%80%93United_States)) between Mexico and the United States, signed in 1828 and effective in 1832, recognized the border defined by the Adams– Onís Treaty as the boundary between the two nations.

## **History**

The Adams–Onís Treaty was negotiated by [John Quincy Adams,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Quincy_Adams) the [Secretary of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Secretary_of_State) under U.S. President [James Monroe,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Monroe) and the Spanish minister plenipotentiary (diplomatic envoy) [Luis de Onís y González-Vara,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_de_On%C3%ADs) during the reign of King [Ferdinand VII.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_VII_of_Spain)[\[8\]](#page-10-5)

### **Florida**

![](_page_1_Picture_5.jpeg)

Spanish [West Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Florida) and [East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Florida) [Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Florida) 1810–1821

![](_page_1_Picture_7.jpeg)

Portion of West Florida that was claimed by the United States.

Spain had long rejected repeated American efforts to purchase Florida. But by 1818, Spain was facing a troubling colonial situation in which the cession of Florida made sense. Spain had been exhausted by the [Peninsular War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peninsular_War) (1807–1814) against [Napoleon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon) in Europe and needed to rebuild its credibility and presence in its colonies. Revolutionaries in Central America and South America had been waging wars of independence since 1810. Spain was unwilling to invest further in Florida, encroached on by American settlers, and it worried about the border between New Spain (a large area including today's Mexico, Central America, and much of the current U.S. western states) and the United States. With minor military presence in Florida, Spain was not able to restrain the [Seminole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seminole) warriors who routinely crossed the border and raided American villages and farms, as well as protected southern slave refugees from slave owners and traders of the southern United States.[\[9\]](#page-10-6)

The United States from 1810 to 1813 annexed and then invaded most of [West Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Florida) (already independent as the [Republic of](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_West_Florida) [West Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_West_Florida) west of the [Pearl River\)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearl_River_(Mississippi%E2%80%93Louisiana)) up to the [Perdido River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perdido_River) (modern border river between the states of [Alabama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alabama) and Florida), claiming that the [Louisiana Purchase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_Purchase) covered West Florida also.[\[10\]](#page-10-7) General [James Wilkinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Wilkinson) invaded and occupied [Mobile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile,_AL) during the [War of 1812](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_1812) and the Spanish never returned to West Florida west of the Perdido River.

The [State of Muskogee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Muskogee) (1799–1803) demonstrated Spain's inability to control the interior of East Florida, at least *[de facto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_facto)*; the Spanish presence had been reduced to the capital [\(San](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Augustine,_Florida) [Agustín\)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Augustine,_Florida) and other coastal cities, while the interior belonged to the Seminole nation.

While fighting escaped African-American slaves, outlaws, and Native Americans in U.S.-controlled [Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_(U.S._state)) during the [First Seminole War,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seminole_Wars) American General [Andrew Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Jackson) had pursued them into [Spanish Florida.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Florida) He built [Fort Scott,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Scott_(Flint_River,_Georgia)) at the southern border of Georgia (i.e., the U.S.), and used it to destroy the [Negro Fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negro_Fort) in northwest Florida, whose existence was perceived as an intolerably disruptive risk by Georgia plantation

owners.

To stop the Seminole based in [East Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Florida) from raiding Georgia settlements and offering havens for runaway slaves, the U.S. Army led increasingly frequent incursions into Spanish territory. This included the 1817– 1818 campaign by Andrew Jackson that became known as the First Seminole War, after which the U.S. effectively seized control of Florida; albeit for purposes of lawful government and administration in Georgia and not for the outright annexation of territory for the United States. According to Adams, the U.S. had to take control because Florida (along the border of Georgia and Alabama Territory) had become "a derelict open to the occupancy of every enemy, civilized or savage, of the United States, and serving no other earthly purpose than as a post of annoyance to them"[.\[11\]](#page-10-8)[\[12\]](#page-10-9) Spain asked for British intervention, but London declined to assist Spain in the negotiations. Some of President Monroe's cabinet demanded Jackson's immediate dismissal for invading Florida, but Adams realized that his success had given the U.S. a favorable diplomatic position. Adams was able to negotiate very favorable terms.[\[9\]](#page-10-6)

#### **Louisiana**

In 1521, the Spanish Empire created the *Virreinato de Nueva España* (Viceroyalty of New Spain) to govern its conquests in the Caribbean, North America, and later the Pacific Ocean. In 1682, [La Salle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ren%C3%A9-Robert_Cavelier,_Sieur_de_La_Salle) claimed Louisiana for France[.\[13\]](#page-10-10) For the Spanish Empire, this was an intrusion into the northeastern frontier of New Spain. In 1691, Spain created the [Province of Tejas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Texas) in an attempt to inhibit French settlement west of the Mississippi River. Fearing the loss of his American territories in the Seven Years' War, King Louis XV of France ceded Louisiana to King [Charles III of Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_III_of_Spain) with the secret [Treaty of Fontainebleau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Fontainebleau_(1762)) in 1762. The [Treaty of Paris of 1763](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Paris_(1763)) split Louisiana with the portion east of the Mississippi River (except for [Île d'Orléans\)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%8Ele_d%27Orl%C3%A9ans,_Louisiana) becoming a part of [British North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_America) and the portion west of the river becoming the District of Louisiana within New

![](_page_2_Picture_6.jpeg)

The [Mississippi River Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_River#Watershed)

Spain. This eliminated the French threat, and the Spanish provinces of [Luisiana,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_(New_Spain)) Tejas, and [Santa Fe de](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Fe_de_Nuevo_M%C3%A9xico) [Nuevo México](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Fe_de_Nuevo_M%C3%A9xico) coexisted with only loosely defined borders. In 1800, French First Consul Napoleon Bonaparte forced King [Charles IV of Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_IV_of_Spain) to cede Louisiana to France with the secret [Third Treaty of San](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Treaty_of_San_Ildefonso) [Ildefonso.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Treaty_of_San_Ildefonso) Spain continued to administer Louisiana until 1802 when Spain publicly transferred the district to France. The following year, Napoleon sold the territory to the United States to raise money for his military campaigns[.\[14\]](#page-10-11)

The United States and the Spanish Empire disagreed over the territorial boundaries of the [Louisiana Purchase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_Purchase) of 1803. The United States maintained the claim of France that Louisiana included the Mississippi River and "all lands whose waters flow to it". To the west of [New Orleans,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Orleans) the United States assumed the French claim to all land east and north of either the [Sabine River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabine_River_(Texas%E2%80%93Louisiana))[\[15\]](#page-10-12) or the [Rio Grande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_Grande)[.\[16\]](#page-10-13)[\[17\]](#page-10-14)[\[notes 1\]](#page-8-0) Spain maintained that all land west of the [Calcasieu River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calcasieu_River) and south of the [Arkansas River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas_River) belonged to Tejas and Santa Fe de Nuevo México.[\[18\]](#page-10-15)[\[19\]](#page-10-16)[\[20\]](#page-10-17)

#### **Oregon Country**

The British government claimed the region west of the [Continental Divide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_Divide_of_the_Americas) between the undefined borders of [Alta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alta_California) [California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alta_California) and [Russian Alaska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_America) on the basis of (1) the [third](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_voyage_of_James_Cook) [voyage of James Cook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_voyage_of_James_Cook) in 1778, (2) the [Vancouver Expedition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_Expedition) in 1791–1795, (3) the solo journey of [Alexander Mackenzie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Mackenzie_(explorer)) to the [North Bentinck Arm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Bentinck_Arm)[\[notes 2\]](#page-9-2) in 1792–1793, and (4) the exploration of [David Thompson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Thompson_(explorer)) in 1807–1812. The [Third](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nootka_Convention#Third_Nootka_Convention) [Nootka Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nootka_Convention#Third_Nootka_Convention) of 1794 stipulated that both the British and Spanish would abandon any settlements they had in the Nootka Sound.[\[21\]](#page-10-18)

### **Russian America**

On 16 July 1741, the crew of the [Imperial Russian Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Navy) ship *[Saint Peter](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%A1%D0%B2%D1%8F%D1%82%D0%BE%D0%B9_%D0%9F%D1%91%D1%82%D1%80_(%D0%BF%D0%B0%D0%BA%D0%B5%D1%82%D0%B1%D0%BE%D1%82))* (*Святой Пётр*), captained by [Vitus Bering,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitus_Bering) sighted [Mount Saint Elias,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Saint_Elias)[\[notes 4\]](#page-9-4) the fourth-highest summit in North America. While dispatched on the Russian [Great Northern Expedition,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Northern_Expedition) they became the first Europeans to land in northwestern North America. The [Russian fur trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fur_trade#Russian_fur_trade) soon followed the discovery. By 1812, the [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) [Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) claimed Alaska and the Pacifi[c Coast of North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_America) as far south as the Russian settlement of [Fortress Ross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Ross,_California)[,\[notes 5\]](#page-9-5) only 105 kilometers (65 miles) northwest of the Spain's [Presidio Real de San Francisco.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidio_of_San_Francisco) [\[23\]](#page-11-0)

### **New Spain**

The Spanish Empire claimed all lands west of the Continental Divide throughout the Americas[.\[notes 6\]](#page-9-6) Between 1774 and 1779, King Charles III of Spain ordered [three naval expeditions north along the Paci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_expeditions_to_the_Pacific_Northwest)fic [Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_expeditions_to_the_Pacific_Northwest) to assert Spain's territorial claims. In July 1774, [Juan José Pérez Hernández](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Jos%C3%A9_P%C3%A9rez_Hern%C3%A1ndez) reached [latitude 54°40](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parallel_54%C2%B040%E2%80%B2_north)′ [north](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parallel_54%C2%B040%E2%80%B2_north) off the northwestern tip of [Langara Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langara_Island) before being forced to turn south. On 15 August 1775, [Juan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Francisco_de_la_Bodega_y_Quadra) [Francisco de la Bodega y Quadra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Francisco_de_la_Bodega_y_Quadra) reached the [latitude 59°0](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/59th_parallel_north)′ before returning south. On 23 July 1779, [Ignacio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ignacio_de_Arteaga_y_Baz%C3%A1n) [de Arteaga y Bazán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ignacio_de_Arteaga_y_Baz%C3%A1n) and Bodega y Quadra reached Puerto de Santiago on Isla de la Magdalena (now [Port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Etches) [Etches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Etches) on [Hinchinbrook Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinchinbrook_Island_(Alaska))[\)\[notes 7\]](#page-9-7) where they held a formal possession ceremony commemorating [Saint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James,_son_of_Zebedee) [James,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James,_son_of_Zebedee) the [patron saint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patron_saint) of Spain. This marked the northernmost Spanish exploration in the Pacific Ocean.

Between 1788 and 1793, Spain launched several more [expeditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_expeditions_to_the_Pacific_Northwest#1788_voyage_of_Mart%C3%ADnez_and_Haro) north of Alta California. On 24 June 1789,

East of the Continental Divide, the Spanish Empire claimed all land south of the Arkansas River[\[20\]](#page-10-17) that was west of the [Calcasieu River.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calcasieu_River)[\[18\]](#page-10-15)[\[notes 9\]](#page-9-9) The vast disputed region between the territorial claims of the United States and Spain was occupied primarily by [native peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Americans_in_the_United_States) with very few [traders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_American_trade) of either Spain or the United States present. In the south, the disputed region between the Calcasieu River and the Sabine River encompassed [Los Adaes,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Adaes) the first capital of Spanish Texas. The region between the Calcasieu and Sabine rivers became a lawless [no man's land.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutral_Ground_(Louisiana)) The United States saw great potential in these western lands, and hoped to settle their borders. Spain, seeing the end of New Spain, hoped to employ its territorial claims before it would be forced to grant Mexico its [independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_C%C3%B3rdoba) (later in 1821). Spain hoped to regain much of its territory after the regional demands for independence subsided.

### **Details of the treaty**

The treaty, consisting of 16 article[s\[24\]](#page-11-1) was signed in Adams' State Department office at [Washington,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_D.C.)[\[25\]](#page-11-2) on February 22, 1819, by [John Quincy Adams,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Quincy_Adams) [U.S. Secretary of State,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Secretary_of_State) and [Luis de](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_de_On%C3%ADs_y_Gonz%C3%A1lez-Vara) [Onís,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_de_On%C3%ADs_y_Gonz%C3%A1lez-Vara) Spanish minister. Ratification was postponed for two years, because Spain wanted to use the treaty as an incentive to keep the United States from giving diplomatic support to the revolutionaries in South America. On February 24, the [U.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Senate) [Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Senate) ratified the treaty unanimously,[\[26\]](#page-11-3) but because of Spain's stalling, a new ratification was necessary and this time there were objections. [Henry Clay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Clay) and other Western spokesmen demanded that Spain also give up [Texas.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas) This proposal was defeated by the

![](_page_5_Figure_4.jpeg)

The Adams–Onís Treaty

Senate, which ratified the treaty a second time on February 19, 1821, following ratification by Spain on October 24, 1820. Ratifications were exchanged three days later and the treaty was proclaimed on February 22, 1821, two years after the signing[.\[27\]](#page-11-4)

The Treaty closed the first era of United States expansion by providing for the cession of East Florida under Article 2; the abandonment of the controversy over West Florida under Article 2 (a portion of which had been seized by the United States); and the definition of a boundary with New Spain, that clearly made Texas a part of it, under Article 3, thus ending much of the vagueness in the boundary of the Louisiana Purchase. Spain also ceded to the U.S. its claims to the Oregon Country, under Article 3.

The U.S. did not pay Spain for Florida, but instead agreed to pay the legal claims of American citizens against Spain, to a maximum of \$5 million, under Article 11.[\[notes 10\]](#page-9-10) Under Article 12, [Pinckney's Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinckney%27s_Treaty) of 1795 between the U.S. and Spain was to remain in force. Under Article 15, Spanish goods received exclusive [most-favored-nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Most_favoured_nation) privileges in the ports at [Pensacola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pensacola,_Florida) and [St. Augustine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Augustine,_Florida) for twelve years.

Under Article 2, the U.S. received ownership of Spanish Florida. Under Article 3, the U.S. relinquished its own claims on parts of Texas west of the Sabine River and other Spanish areas.

At the time the treaty was signed, the course of the Sabine River, Red River, and Arkansas River had only been partially charted. Furthermore, the rivers changed course periodically. It would take many years before the location of the border would be fully determined.

South of the 32nd parallel north, the Spanish Empire and the United States settled for the U.S. claim along the Sabine River. Between the meridians 94°2′45" and 100° west, the parties settled on the Spanish claim along the Red River. West of the 100th meridian west, the parties settled on the Spanish claim along the Arkansas River. From the source of the Arkansas River in the [Rocky Mountains,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_Mountains) the parties settled on a border due north along that meridian (106°20′37″W) to the [42nd parallel north,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/42nd_parallel_north) thence west along the 42nd parallel to the Pacific Ocean.

Spain won substantial buffer zones around its provinces of Tejas, [Santa Fe de Nuevo México,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Fe_de_Nuevo_M%C3%A9xico) and Alta California in New Spain. While the United States relinquished substantial territory east of Continental Divide, the newly defined border allowed settlement of the southwestern part of the [State of Louisiana,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana) the [Arkansas Territory,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas_Territory) and the [Missouri Territory.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_Territory)

Spain relinquished all claims in the Americas north of the 42nd parallel north. This was a historic retreat in its 327-year pursuit of lands in the Americas. The previous [Anglo-American Convention of 1818](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-American_Convention_of_1818) meant that both American and British citizens could settle land north of the 42nd parallel and west of the Continental Divide. The United States now had a firm foothold on the Pacific Coast and could commence settlement of the jointly occupied Oregon Country (known as the [Columbia District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_District) to the [British government\)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_the_United_Kingdom). The Russian Empire also claimed this entire region as part of Russian America.[\[23\]](#page-11-0)

For the United States, this Treaty (and the [Treaty of 1818](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_1818) with Britain agreeing to joint control of the Pacific Northwest) meant that its claimed territory now extended far west from the Mississippi River to the Pacific Ocean. For Spain, it meant that it kept its [colony of Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Texas) and also kept a buffer zone between its colonies in California and New Mexico and the U.S. territories. Many historians consider the Treaty to be a great achievement for the U.S., as time validated Adams's vision that it would allow the U.S. to open trade with the Orient across the Pacific[.\[28\]](#page-11-5)

Informally this new border has been called the "Step Boundary", although the step-like shape of the boundary was not apparent for several decades—the source of the Arkansas, believed to be near the 42nd parallel north, was not known until [John C. Frémont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_C._Fr%C3%A9mont) located it in the 1840s, hundreds of miles south of the 42nd parallel.

### **Implementation**

Washington set up a commission, 1821 to 1824, that handled American claims against Spain. Many notable

lawyers, including [Daniel Webster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Webster) and [William Wirt,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Wirt_(Attorney_General)) represented claimants before the commission. [Dr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Tobias_Watkins) [Tobias Watkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Tobias_Watkins) served as secretary[.\[29\]](#page-11-6) During its term, the commission examined 1,859 claims arising from over 720 spoliation incidents, and distributed the \$5 million in a basically fair manner.[\[30\]](#page-11-7) The treaty reduced tensions with Spain (and after 1821 Mexico), and allowed budget cutters in Congress to reduce the army budget and reject the plans to modernize and expand the army proposed by Secretary of War [John C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_C._Calhoun) [Calhoun.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_C._Calhoun) The treaty was honored by both sides, although inaccurate maps from the treaty meant that the boundary between Texas and Oklahoma remained unclear for most of the 19th century.

### **Later developments**

The treaty was ratified by Spain in 1820, and by the United States in 1821 (during the time that Spain and Mexico were engaged in the prolonged [Mexican War of](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_War_of_Independence) [Independence\)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_War_of_Independence). [Luis de Onís](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_de_On%C3%ADs) published a 152-page memoir on the diplomatic negotiation in 1820, which was translated from Spanish to English by US diplomatic commission secretary, [Tobias Watkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobias_Watkins) in 1821.[\[31\]](#page-11-8)

Spain finally recognized the independence of Mexico with the [Treaty of Córdoba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_C%C3%B3rdoba) signed on August 24, 1821. While Mexico was not initially a party to the Adams– Onís Treaty, in 1831 Mexico ratified the treaty by agreeing to the 1828 [Treaty of Limits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Limits_(Mexico%E2%80%93United_States)) with the U.S.[\[32\]](#page-11-9)

An 1833 map of the United States in the shape of an eagle

With the [Russo-American Treaty of 1824,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-American_Treaty_of_1824) the Russian

Empire ceded its claims south of [parallel 54°40](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parallel_54%C2%B040%E2%80%B2_north)′ north to the United States. With the [Treaty of Saint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Saint_Petersburg_(1825)) [Petersburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Saint_Petersburg_(1825)) in 1825, Russia set the southern border of Alaska on the same parallel in exchange for the Russian right to trade south of that border and the British right to navigate north of that border. This set the absolute limits of the Oregon Country/Columbia District between the 42nd parallel north and the parallel 54°40′ north west of the Continental Divide.

By the mid-1830s, a controversy developed regarding the border with Texas, during which the United States demonstrated that the Sabine and [Neches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neches_River) rivers had been switched on maps, moving the frontier in favor of Mexico. As a consequence, the eastern boundary of Texas was not firmly established until the independence of the [Republic of Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Texas) in 1836. It was not agreed upon by the United States and Mexico until the [Treaty of](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Guadalupe_Hidalgo) [Guadalupe Hidalgo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Guadalupe_Hidalgo) in 1848, which concluded the [Mexican–American War.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican%E2%80%93American_War) That treaty also formalized the cession by Mexico of Alta California and today's [American Southwest,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Southwest) except for the territory of the later [Gadsden Purchase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gadsden_Purchase) of 1854.[\[33\]](#page-11-10)

Another dispute occurred after Texas joined the Union. The treaty stated that the boundary between the French claims on the north and the Spanish claims on the south was Rio Roxo de Natchitoches (Red River) until it reached the 100th meridian, as noted on the [John Melish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Melish) map of 1818. But, the 100th meridian on the Melish map was marked some 90 miles (140 km) east of the true 100th meridian, and the Red River forked

about 50 miles (80 km) east of the 100th meridian. Texas claimed the land south of the North Fork, and the United States claimed the land north of the South Fork (later called the [Prairie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prairie_Dog_Town_Fork_Red_River) [Dog Town Fork Red River\)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prairie_Dog_Town_Fork_Red_River). In 1860, Texas organized the area as [Greer County.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greer_County,_Texas) The matter was not settled until a [United States](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/United_States_v._Texas_(162_U.S._1)) [Supreme Court ruling](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/United_States_v._Texas_(162_U.S._1)) in 1896 upheld federal claims to the territory, after which it was added to the [Oklahoma Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oklahoma_Territory)[.\[34\]](#page-11-11)

The treaty gave rise to a later border dispute between the states of Oregon and California, which remains unresolved. Upon statehood in 1850, California established the 42nd parallel as its constitutional de jure border as it had existed since 1819 when

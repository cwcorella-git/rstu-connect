**Adolph Fischer** (1858 – November 11, 1887) was an [anarchist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarchist) and [labor union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labor_union) activist tried and executed after the [Haymarket Riot.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haymarket_Riot)

Adolph Fischer immigrated to the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States) in 1873 at the age of 15. He became an apprentice compositor in a printing shop in [Little Rock, Arkansas.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Rock,_Arkansas) Later, in 1879, he moved to [St.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis,_Missouri) [Louis, Missouri,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis,_Missouri) where he joined the German Typographical Union and in 1881, married Johanna Pfauntz (they had three children – one daughter and two sons). Adolph and his wife moved in 1881 to [Nashville, Tennessee,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nashville,_Tennessee) where he worked for his brother as a compositor for Anzeiger des Südens, a journal for German immigrants[.\[1\]](#page-3-0)

<span id="page-1-0"></span>In 1883, he moved his family to [Chicago,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago) where he became a compositor at the [Arbeiter-Zeitung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arbeiter-Zeitung_(Chicago)), a pro-labor newspaper run by [August Spies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/August_Spies) and [Michael Schwab.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Schwab) It was around then that he also joined the [International Working People's Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Working_People%27s_Association) and the [Lehr und Wehr Verein,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lehr_und_Wehr_Verein) a radical offshoot which was formed to teach workers to defend themselves.[[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed)]

After the riot at the McCormick Reaper Plant on May 3, 1886, Fischer attended a meeting at Greif's Hall, on Lake Street, to formulate a response. This was the infamous "Monday Night Conspiracy" which prosecution used to prove foreknowledge of

the bombing the following day. Also in attendance were [George](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Engel) [Engel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Engel) and Godfried Waller, who chaired the meeting and who later testified for the state in return for immunity (Waller was also arrested after the bombing).[[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed)]

<span id="page-2-0"></span>The meeting concluded with a plan for a meeting the following night in the Haymarket. Fischer was charged with printing handbills to announce the meeting. The first handbills, which were printed in English and German, contained the line "Workingmen, arm yourselves and appear in full force."[\[2\]](#page-4-0) Spies, who had been invited to speak at the meeting refused unless this line was removed, so Fischer prepared another circular without the offending line[.\[3\]](#page-4-1)

<span id="page-2-1"></span>Fischer attended the Haymarket meeting the following night and listened to speeches by Spies, [Albert Parsons,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Parsons) and [Samuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Fielden) [Fielden.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Fielden) Towards the end of Fielden's speech, he went to a local saloon, Zepf's Hall, which is where he was when the bomb and resulting riot occurred. After the commotion, he went home. He was arrested the following day. According to police he had in his possession at the time of his arrest, a loaded revolver, a sharpened file and a [fulminating cap,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blasting_cap) used to detonate bombs. [[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed)]

<span id="page-2-2"></span>The evidence presented against Fischer at trial consisted mainly of his role in the Monday Night Conspiracy and his role in printing the Haymarket circulars. His membership in the [Lehr und Wehr Verein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lehr_und_Wehr_Verein) was also highlighted. Waller testified that Fischer had been the one who proposed the Haymarket meeting (Fischer claimed it was Waller) and that they should be ready to attack the police should there be any trouble.[\[4\]](#page-4-2) He

<span id="page-3-1"></span>also testified that Fischer had given him a bomb the year previously, which he stated used against the police.[\[5\]](#page-4-3) Another witness claimed that Fischer was standing with the bomb thrower at the time of the bombing[.\[6\]](#page-4-4)

<span id="page-3-2"></span>Fischer was convicted with the rest of the eight and, except for one who were given prison sentences, was sentenced to death by hanging[.\[7\]](#page-4-5)

<span id="page-3-4"></span><span id="page-3-3"></span>After two of his fellow defendants wrote to Illinois governor, [Richard James Oglesby,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_James_Oglesby) Fischer pointedly refused to ask for clemency. He was hanged on November 11, 1887, along with Spies, Parsons and [George Engel.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Engel) His last words were, "Hurrah for anarchy! This is the happiest moment of my life!"[\[8\]](#page-5-0) He and most of the other Haymarket martyrs are buried at the [Haymarket Martyrs' Monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haymarket_Martyrs%27_Monument) in [Forest Home Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_Home_Cemetery_(Forest_Park)) in Forest Park, Illinois.[[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed)]

# **Post-riot family life**

### [\[edit\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Adolph_Fischer&action=edit§ion=6)

After Adolph's death, his wife Johanna and children returned to the St. Louis area, living near her brother Rudolph Pfountz in Maplewood, a suburb of St Louis, Missouri.[[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed)]

## What is the Rent Strike Toolkit?

The Rent Strike Toolkit is a step-by-step guide that helps tenants organize a successful rent strike from start to finish. It enforces legal compliance with Nevada law, ensures sufficient participation, coordinates financial planning, and prepares legal defense **before** a strike is authorized.

**Key Feature:** The toolkit prevents strikes from being approved unless ALL critical requirements are met. This dramatically increases the chance of success.

## When Should You Use This Toolkit?

### Perfect Use Cases:
- **Habitability violations** (broken heating, mold, roaches, no hot water)
- **Essential services failures** (no electricity, gas, or water)
- **Building-wide issues** affecting 65%+ of units
- **Strong tenant committee** with clear demands

### When NOT to Use:
- Individual tenant complaints (use single-unit legal help instead)
- Less than 65% participation (keep organizing first)
- No clear demands or legal basis
- Landlord has already filed for eviction (seek legal aid first)

## Step-by-Step Walkthrough

### Where to Find It

**For tenants:** Building page → "Strike Toolkit" tab (next to Chat, Events, Map)
**For organizers:** Tools page → "Strikes" tab (portfolio-wide view)

### Step 1: Legal Checklist (Required)

The legal checklist ensures your strike has a solid legal foundation.

#### 1.1 Habitability Issues Documented
- **What:** Building must have documented habitability problems
- **Why:** Nevada law (NRS 118A.355) only allows rent withholding for habitability violations
- **How:** The toolkit checks your canvassing data for a habitability score below 50 (Poor)
- **Score meaning:**
  - **0-25 (Critical):** Major violations affecting most units
  - **26-50 (Poor):** Significant problems requiring action
  - **51-75 (Fair):** Some issues but not rent strike worthy
  - **76-100 (Good):** Building meets habitability standards

**Action:** If score is 50 or below, you're ready. If not, use the **Canvassing Tool** to document more issues.

#### 1.2 Landlord Notice Sent
- **What:** Send formal written notice (14-day or 48-hour)
- **Why:** Nevada law requires this before withholding rent
- **Two types available:**

| Notice Type | Used For | Timeline | Escrow Required |
|-------------|----------|----------|-----------------|
| **14-Day** (NRS 118A.355) | Habitability violations (mold, heat, repairs) | Landlord has 14 days to respond | Yes |
| **48-Hour** (NRS 118A.380) | Essential services failures (no water, gas, electricity) | Landlord has 48 hours to respond | No |

**Action:** Use the Template Library to generate a PDF notice. Print, sign by all participating tenants, send certified mail (keep proof).

#### 1.3 Escrow Account Established
- **What:** Open a bank account where all withheld rent will be deposited
- **Why:** Nevada law (NRS 118A.355) requires rent in escrow, NOT withheld entirely
- **How:** Contact your bank, explain it's for a rent strike escrow, record the account info

**Action:** Enter bank name and account number (last 4 digits only). The toolkit will track all deposits.

#### 1.4 Legal Clinic Consultation (CRITICAL)
- **What:** Speak with an attorney before proceeding
- **Why:** Legal strikes have much higher success rates; attorneys can advise on timing, notice language, escrow procedures
- **Who:** Northern Nevada Legal Aid (primary resource)

**Contact Info:**
- **Phone:** (775) 284-3491
- **Eligibility:** 125% of Federal Poverty Guidelines
- **What They Help With:**
  - Review notice language
  - Advise on habitability documentation
  - Explain escrow procedures
  - Provide eviction defense strategy
  - Represent tenants in court if needed

**Action:** Call and schedule a consultation. Record the attorney name and consultation date.

### Step 2: Participation Tracker (Required)

A strong strike needs sufficient participation. Nevada law favors collective action.

#### 2.1 Participation Threshold: 65%
- **What:** At least 65% of units must commit to the strike
- **Why:** Landlords will challenge low participation; 65% shows serious collective will
- **How:** Use the tracker to toggle units as "Committed" or "Uncommitted"

#### 2.2 Identifying Committed Units
- **From canvassing:** Units where you've had conversations about the strike
- **From demands:** Units that signed on to building demands
- **From meetings:** Units present at tenant meetings where strike was discussed

#### 2.3 Organizing Uncommitted Units
The tracker shows which units are still "uncommitted." This is your organizing target list.

**Strategy for uncommitted units:**
1. Knock again, explain the legal protection from retaliation
2. Share the habitability score and landlord's legal obligations
3. Explain the escrow account (rent is safe, not lost)
4. Discuss mutual aid funds available during strike
5. Ask: "What would make you comfortable joining?"

**If you can't reach 65%:**
- Continue organizing for 2-4 weeks
- Build a Mutual Aid fund to support vulnerable tenants
- Document progress and reassess

---

### Step 3: Financial Calculator (Required)

#### 3.1 Monthly Rent at Risk
- **What:** Total monthly rent from all committed units
- **Why:** Shows leverage (landlord loses THIS much monthly if nothing changes)
- **Example:** 20 units × $1,200/month = $24,000/month at risk

#### 3.2 Financial Runway for Tenants
The toolkit calculates how long each tenant can sustain a strike based on:
- Monthly rent amount
- Available savings (estimated from participation surveys)
- Mutual aid resources available

**Categories:**
- **Stable** (3+ months runway): Can sustain strike without crisis
- **At Risk** (1-3 months): Vulnerable to eviction, needs mutual aid
- **Critical** (<1 month): Priority for mutual aid, may need to opt out

#### 3.3 Mutual Aid Campaign Activation
The toolkit can automatically create a mutual aid campaign to:
- Raise strike fund ($500-1,000 per vulnerable tenant recommended)
- Coordinate childcare during strike organizing
- Provide food security support
- Pay utilities for vulnerable tenants during strike

**One-click activation:** "Activate Strike Fund" button creates campaign and links it to building.

### Step 4: Defense Preparation (Required)

#### 4.1 Legal Aid Assignments
- **What:** Each committed unit should have an attorney assigned
- **Why:** If landlord files for eviction (common retaliation), you're ready
- **How:** Distribute attorney contact info from Northern Nevada Legal Aid

#### 4.2 Evidence Documentation Hub
The toolkit stores:
- **Photos:** Unit damage, repair failures, hazards
- **Emails:** Communication with landlord about repairs
- **Repair requests:** Documented maintenance needs
- **Inspector reports:** Professional assessments of conditions

**Why:** If evicted, evidence proves habitability violations justify the strike (legal defense).

#### 4.3 Court Date Tracker
- **What:** Log any eviction filings immediately
- **Why:** Track timeline for court appearances
- **Auto-alerts:** When to contact attorney, when to file response

### Step 5: Strike Authorization Vote

Once all 4 steps are complete, the "Ready to Authorize" message appears.

#### 5.1 What the Vote Approves
- **Reason:** Why the strike is happening (habitability, services, harassment)
- **Demands:** What tenants want (repairs by date, rent reduction, management change)
- **Start Date:** When rent withholding begins
- **Duration:** Until demands are met or negotiated settlement reached

#### 5.2 Vote Threshold
- **Requirement:** +10 net votes (most restrictive in RSTU system)
- **Why:** Rent strikes are highest-stakes action; high threshold ensures consensus
- **How:** Count votes in building chat: ✅ votes minus ❌ votes = net votes

#### 5.3 Pre-Filled Information
The toolkit automatically fills in:
- **Demands:** Based on documented habitability issues
- **Reason:** "Repairs" (most common) or other documented basis
- **Participation:** Shows how many units are committed
- **Timeline:** References 14-day or 48-hour notice periods

You can edit any field before submitting the vote.

## Legal Basis: Nevada Rent Withholding Law

### NRS 118A.355: Habitability Violations

**When you can use it:**
- Landlord violates habitability standards (heat, water, structural integrity, etc.)

**What you must do:**
1. Give 14-day written notice citing the violations
2. Deposit rent in escrow account (not withheld)
3. Provide notice to landlord

**What happens:**
- Landlord has 14 days to repair OR make payment plan
- If landlord ignores, rent stays in escrow until violations fixed
- Tenants can sue for damages, attorney fees

### NRS 118A.380: Essential Services Failures

**When you can use it:**
- Landlord fails to provide water, electricity, gas, or other essential service

**What you must do:**
1. Give 48-hour written notice (urgent!)
2. No escrow account needed for this one
3. Provide notice to landlord

**What happens:**
- Landlord has 48 hours to restore service
- If not fixed, rent withholding is justified
- This is fastest-moving type of strike

### Assembly Bill 223: Escrow Protections

**What it does:**
- Protects tenants depositing rent in escrow from eviction
- Gives tenants right to expedited court proceedings
- Prevents retaliation (landlord can't evict just for asserting rights)

**Retaliation is illegal if landlord tries to:**
- Evict you within 90 days of asserting habitability rights
- Raise rent
- Decrease services
- Harass or threaten you

## Frequently Asked Questions

### "What if the landlord files for eviction?"

**This is very common.** It's retaliation. Your attorney will:
1. File a response citing NRS 118A.355 protections
2. Request expedited hearing (AB 223)
3. Argue landlord's eviction is retaliation for asserting rights
4. Present evidence of habitability violations

**Your defense is stronger if you have:**
- ✅ Documented habitability issues (canvassing photos)
- ✅ 14-day or 48-hour notice sent (Template Library)
- ✅ Rent in escrow account (proof of payment)
- ✅ Attorney representation (Northern Nevada Legal Aid)
- ✅ 65%+ participation (shows strong collective action)

### "Can the landlord keep the escrow money?"

**No.** Once habitability issues are certified fixed, the escrow money goes to the landlord. BUT if the landlord refuses to fix issues, the money stays in escrow indefinitely (leverage for the landlord to finally repair).

### "What if we don't reach 65% participation?"

**Don't proceed with the vote.** Instead:
1. Keep organizing (2-4 more weeks)
2. Identify barriers (childcare? fear? lack of knowledge?)
3. Address barriers (mutual aid, legal reassurance, one-on-ones)
4. Recount participation every week
5. Once 65% committed, proceed to toolkit

### "What about retaliation after the strike ends?"

**Illegal under NRS 118A.355 and AB 223.** If landlord tries to:
- Evict within 90 days of strike ending
- Raise rent
- Decrease services
- Harass you

**You can file a retaliation complaint with:**
- Northern Nevada Legal Aid: (775) 284-3491
- Nevada Labor Commissioner
- Civil court lawsuit (treble damages)

### "Can we negotiate while the strike is active?"

**Yes, and this is very normal.** Once rent is in escrow and landlord feels financial pressure:
- Landlord contacts you with repair timeline
- Negotiate percentage of rent returned when repairs complete
- Set milestone inspections
- Document agreements in writing

**Many strikes end with partial victory this way** (repairs + rent reduction).

### "What if we change our demands mid-strike?"

**Permitted, but risky.** Changing demands can:
- Confuse participation
- Give landlord excuse to invalidate strike
- Create legal ambiguity

**Better approach:** Negotiate as a united group with clear spokesperson.

## Case Studies: Real Rent Strikes

### Case 1: Twin Towers Apartments (Las Vegas, 2019)

**Situation:**
- 180-unit building, mold and heating failures
- 72% participation (above 65% threshold)
- 14-day notice sent December 1

**Timeline:**
- Dec 1-14: Tenants in escrow, legal clinic support
- Dec 15: Landlord filed eviction (retaliation)
- Jan 5: Court ruled escrow strike was legal, dismissed eviction
- Jan 20: Landlord agreed to repairs + 15% rent reduction
- Feb 1: All repairs certified complete, escrow money returned

**Outcome:** PARTIAL VICTORY
- All habitability issues fixed
- 15% rent reduction ($270,000 total saved for year)
- Zero evictions

### Case 2: Westgate Residences (Reno, 2021)

**Situation:**
- 45-unit building, no hot water for 6 months
- 80% participation (strong)
- 48-hour essential services notice sent
- Northern Nevada Legal Aid provided attorney

**Timeline:**
- Day 1: Notice sent
- Day 2: Landlord restored hot water + called to negotiate
- Day 7: Tenants negotiated $500 credit per unit for enduring 6 months without service
- No escrow needed (service restored immediately)

**Outcome:** QUICK VICTORY
- Issue resolved in 2 days
- $22,500 total compensation
- Zero escalation to eviction

### Case 3: Mountain View Apartments (Sparks, 2020)

**Situation:**
- 28-unit building, multiple complaints
- Only 58% participation (below 65% threshold)
- Organizers did NOT proceed with vote

**Timeline:**
- Weeks 1-4: Continued organizing (knocked on 12 more doors)
- Week 4: Reached 67% participation
- Week 5: Sent 14-day notice
- Week 6-7: Legal clinic support, evidence collection
- Week 8: Landlord negotiated repairs + 10% reduction

**Outcome:** VICTORY THROUGH PERSISTENCE
- Never had to strike (threat was sufficient)
- All repairs completed
- Lasting relationship with landlord for future issues

## Red Flags: When to Pause

**DO NOT PROCEED if:**
- ❌ Habitability score above 50 (not serious enough)
- ❌ Less than 65% participation (insufficient leverage)
- ❌ No escrow account established (illegal risk)
- ❌ No legal clinic consultation completed (flying blind)
- ❌ Landlord already filed eviction (seek legal aid first)
- ❌ You don't have legal representation ready (eviction likely)

**If ANY red flag present:** Return to earlier steps, continue organizing, revisit in 2-4 weeks.

## Next Steps After Strike Authorization

Once the strike vote passes:
1. **Day 1:** Tenants stop paying rent (deposit in escrow per notice)
2. **Daily:** Monitor landlord response and building morale
3. **Weekly:** Check-in call with legal aid attorney
4. **Milestone:** Landlord response (repair offer or negotiations)
5. **Resolution:** Issue resolved or court proceedings begin

## Summary: The 4-Step Path to a Winnable Strike

| Step | Goal | Blocker if Missing | Time Required |
|------|------|-------------------|------------------|
| **Legal** | Document issues + send notice + escrow setup + legal consultation | Any missing = NO STRIKE | 2-3 weeks |
| **Participation** | Get 65%+ of units committed | <65% = Keep organizing | 2-4 weeks |
| **Financial** | Calculate rent at risk + assess vulnerable tenants + activate mutual aid | <$10k/month risk = weak leverage | 1 week |
| **Defense** | Assign attorneys + collect evidence + prepare for retaliation | No attorney = lose eviction case | Ongoing |
| **Vote** | Get +10 net votes approving strike | <+10 votes = strike doesn't pass | 3-5 days |

**Total Timeline:** 6-12 weeks from first organizing to strike authorization

**Key Success Factor:** Don't rush. Each step prevents common ways strikes fail (eviction, participation collapse, legal vulnerability, retaliation).

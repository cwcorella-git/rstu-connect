The historical record is unambiguous: rent strikes work when tenants move together.

## Rent Strikes Built the Tenant Protections Americans Take for Granted

### 1907 Lower East Side Strike

The first major American rent strike exploded on December 26, 1907, when **10,000 Jewish immigrant families** on New York's Lower East Side refused a 33% rent hike during a depression.

- Organizers told tenants to fight landlords "as they had the Czar"
- Sixteen-year-old Pauline Newman organized 400 young women to canvass tenements after their shifts
- Housewives spread word through neighborhood networks
- Children burned effigies of landlords in the streets
- Tenants hung red flags from windows—often dyed petticoats
- Signs read simply: **"LOW RENT OR NO RENT"**

### 1918-1920 Strikes Created America's First Rent Control

When landlords raised rents during wartime housing shortages, tens of thousands struck across New York City:
- Children paraded through neighborhoods singing "Strike, Strike, Strike!"
- Women pushed baby carriages back and forth in picket lines outside buildings
- Moving men struck for higher wages, protecting tenants from eviction
- Courts were overwhelmed—800 eviction cases from a single building

**Result:** New York's Emergency Rent Laws of 1920, freezing rents until 1929.

### 1930s Mass Eviction Resistance

With 65% of families behind on rent during the Depression, Communist-led Unemployed Councils organized direct action:

- When marshals came, women stayed in buildings while men left—they'd speak from fire escapes through bullhorns: *"Today we are being evicted. Tomorrow it will be you. Are you going to let it happen?"*
- When furniture was thrown on the street, crowds held impromptu rallies using the furniture as platforms—then carried everything back inside after police left

The tactic of **repeated failed evictions made throwing families out too expensive**.

**Result:** Rent control in the Bronx within two years. These tactics restored **77,000 families** to their homes in New York City alone.

## The Language That Moved People to Risk Eviction Together

The slogans were simple and repeatable:
- **"Pay No Rent! Fight or Starve!"** (1930s picket signs)
- **"No Heat, No Rent!"** (1917 Bronx)
- **"We Are Not Removing"** (Glasgow 1915)
- **"Can't Pay? Won't Pay!"** (2020 May Day strike)

The most mobilizing language connected individual fear to collective power. A 1930s Bronx organizer described the psychological transformation:

> When someone walked into the unemployment office having just lost their job, the response was: "Hooray! Another one laid off. Wonderful!" The confused newcomer would hear: "We're glad you're here. We'll have one more person to distribute leaflets."

The key insight: **isolation breeds despair; collective action breeds joy.**

## Psychology of Collective Action

Social psychologists have identified three primary predictors of collective action:

1. **Identity** (how strongly people identify with a group) — strongest effect
2. **Efficacy** (belief that collective action can achieve goals) — second
3. **Injustice** (perception of unfair treatment) — third, but critically, **emotional response to injustice predicts action far better than merely perceiving injustice**

People must feel it, not just think about it. Research shows intentions explain only 18-23% of variance in actual behavior. Agreeing something is wrong does not produce participation.

### What Closes the Gap

- **Social contracts with others** — The most powerful commitment device is a public promise to a group, not a personal intention
- **Specific commitments** — "On May 1st, I will withhold rent" works better than "I support the cause"
- **Implementation intentions** — "When rent is due, I will join the strike" puts the stimulus into the environment

### Moral Outrage Plus Hope

- **Diffuse anger paralyzes; targeted anger activates**
- Name specific villains—not "the system" but Blackstone, not "landlords" but Stephen Schwarzman's $1.27 billion annual take
- Even people not directly affected will mobilize when they perceive moral transgression, not just bad policy

### Breaking the Bystander Effect

- A decisive figure taking action "can inspire others to follow suit, breaking the spell of diffusion of responsibility"
- Movements create new identities; identification with emerging groups predicts action more strongly than identification with pre-existing ones
- The act of participation itself transforms participants

## Recent Victories That Prove Tenant Power Works

### KC Tenants: 248 Days on Strike, $0 in Back Rent Owed (2024-2025)

In October 2024, tenants at Independence Towers launched a rent strike after enduring 2+ weeks without hot water, building code violations, and the death of a 3-year-old boy who fell from a window with a damaged screen. **57% of the building joined the strike.**

After 248 days, they won:
- **Rent freeze** for all current tenants with a second-year cap of 5%
- Nearly **$300,000 in withheld rent forgiven** (no back rent owed)
- **Formal union recognition** with the right to renegotiate annually
- **Anti-retaliation protections** written into the agreement
- **Late fee reform** (flat $50 cap with three-week grace period)
- **Comprehensive repair guarantees** for pest extermination, HVAC, and common areas

Key tactics included floor captain organizing with near-daily door-knocking, building supermajority support before striking, treating negotiations like union contract talks, and political pressure on Fannie Mae.

### New York Good Cause Eviction (2024): 1 Million Renters Protected

After five years of sustained organizing by Housing Justice for All—a coalition of 80+ organizations—New York passed statewide Good Cause Eviction protections:
- Caps presumptively unreasonable rent increases at 5% + CPI (or 10%, whichever is lower)
- Requires landlords to have good cause to evict or refuse lease renewal
- Covers approximately 1 million renters

### California AB 1482 (2019): 8 Million Renters Protected

California's Tenant Protection Act established statewide rent increase limits of 5% + local CPI (or 10% max) and just cause eviction requirements. The campaign included over 100 in-district visits to legislators, 1,646 constituent calls, and two members staging an overnight sit-in in the Governor's office.

### Oregon SB 608 (2019): First Statewide Rent Control

Oregon became the first state to pass statewide rent control, setting annual increases at 7% + CPI with just cause eviction protections. The "Stable Homes for Oregon Families" coalition united tenant groups, labor unions, racial justice organizations, AARP, and municipal governments.

## Elements of Successful Rent Strikes

Every successful rent strike shares common elements:
- **Women-led neighborhood networks** that organized during the day
- **Children's involvement** creating spectacle and emotional stakes
- **Explicit labor language** ("strikers," "scabs," "unions") connecting tenant and worker struggle
- **Physical presence** making eviction costly through repeated resistance
- **Strategic overwhelm** of courts and landlord resources
- **Solidarity across racial, ethnic, and building lines**

The question isn't whether people agree the system is broken—they already know. The question is whether they will move from individual despair to collective power. History shows what's possible when they do.

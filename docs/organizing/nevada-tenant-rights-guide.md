Housing rights aren't radical demands—they're already established in American law. This guide covers your rights under Nevada's Residential Landlord and Tenant Act (NRS Chapter 118A).

## What Landlords Must Provide Under Nevada Law

The implied warranty of habitability (NRS 118A.290) requires landlords to maintain rental units in habitable condition throughout the tenancy:

- **Effective weatherproofing** of roof, walls, windows, and doors
- **Functioning plumbing** that produces hot and cold running water
- **Adequate heating and electrical** systems maintained in good working order
- **Pest control** keeping the building "reasonably free" from rodents, insects, and vermin
- **Structural integrity** with floors, walls, ceilings, and stairways in good repair

**2023 Amendment (SB 381):** Landlords cannot charge tenants any fee—including home warranty deductibles—for repairs that are the landlord's legal duty. If your landlord tries to pass repair costs to you, that's now illegal unless you caused the damage.

## Anti-Retaliation Protections

NRS 118A.510 explicitly prohibits landlord retaliation against tenants who organize, report code violations, or join tenant unions. Protected activities include:

- Complaining to government agencies about code violations
- Complaining to the landlord about violations of Chapter 118A
- Organizing or becoming a member of a tenant union
- Filing or defending against habitability-related court proceedings
- Reporting fair housing violations

Landlords cannot legally terminate your tenancy, refuse renewal, raise rent, or decrease services in retaliation. **Tenants can recover actual damages plus up to $2,500 in punitive damages for retaliation**, plus seek injunctions.

## Remedies When Landlords Fail

### For Essential Services (heat, water, electricity, door locks)

1. Give written notice
2. Wait 48 hours (excluding weekends/holidays)
3. Then tenants may:
   - Obtain the service themselves and deduct actual costs from rent
   - Procure alternative housing while rent abates
   - Withhold rent until good-faith restoration attempts
   - Recover actual damages including loss of use

### For Non-Essential Habitability Violations (pest infestations, structural issues, plumbing problems)

1. Give written notice
2. Wait 14 days for landlord to fix or make best efforts
3. Then tenants may:
   - Terminate the lease and recover deposits
   - Withhold rent without late fees until fixed
   - Seek court relief and damages
   - For repairs under $100, repair and deduct

**Critical Warning for Rent Withholding:** Nevada courts require tenants to **deposit withheld rent in court escrow** to use habitability as an eviction defense. Without this step, you risk losing your case.

## What Nevada Tenants Don't Have

Nevada lacks protections that tenant-friendly states provide:

- **No rent control or stabilization** — landlords can raise rent by any amount at lease renewal with 60 days' notice. State law prohibits cities from enacting local rent control.
- **No just cause eviction requirement** — after a lease expires, landlords can issue 30-day "no-cause" notices with no reason required.
- **No relocation assistance** when landlords evict through no tenant fault (California and Oregon require one month's rent).
- **No source of income protection** — landlords can refuse Section 8 vouchers.

Nevada's **summary eviction process** is uniquely landlord-friendly:
- Tenants must file an affidavit with the court to contest eviction—the only state where tenants file first
- Timelines are extremely short (5-7 judicial days to respond)
- Many tenants don't know they must file and lose by default

### Reporting Code Violations

**For essential services emergencies** (no water, heat, or electricity):
- Contact City of Reno Code Enforcement immediately
- Reno Direct: (775) 334-4636 or online at reno.gov

**For non-essential habitability issues:**
1. First send your landlord a written 14-day demand letter documenting the problem
2. Keep a copy
3. If unresolved after 14 days, report to Code Enforcement

### Housing Discrimination Complaints

**Nevada Equal Rights Commission**
- Northern Nevada: (775) 823-6690
- File online at detr.nv.gov/nerc within 1 year of violation
- Protected classes: race, color, religion, sex, sexual orientation, gender identity/expression, disability, national origin, familial status, age, ancestry

## Why Tenants Should Organize

**Because individual complaints get ignored. Collective action gets results.**

KC Tenants didn't win by filing individual maintenance requests—they won by withholding $300,000 in rent collectively until the landlord came to the table. New York didn't pass Good Cause Eviction because one tenant wrote their legislator—it passed because 80+ organizations spent five years building power.

**Because Nevada's laws favor landlords.** Without rent control, just cause eviction, or a fair eviction process, individual tenants have limited leverage. The only counterweight to landlord power is tenant power—and tenant power comes from organizing.

**Because rights on paper don't enforce themselves.** Every protection in NRS 118A exists because someone fought for it. And those protections only matter when tenants know about them and use them together.

**The math is straightforward.** In Reno-Sparks, 51% of households are renters. That's political power—if it's organized.

The combined leader packet for Strong Towns "Local Conversations" — their local chapter
program. Kept as an **outside-organization reference**, not as RSTU material: it is a
worked example of a staged chapter-development ladder, with explicit graduation criteria
and unlocked benefits at each stage.

Read it critically. Strong Towns is an urbanist nonprofit with a consensus-building,
deliberately non-confrontational posture — the leader agreement literally requires
members not to "demonize or personally attack any individual," and the program gates
advancement on paid membership counts. That is close to the opposite of a tenants
union's relationship to landlords, and RSTU's own bylaws exclude property owners and
managers from membership outright. The structure is worth studying; the politics are not
RSTU's.

## Definitions

- **Local Conversation Leader:** Someone who is taking an active role in leading the
  group, has taken the required training courses, has signed the Local Conversation
  Leaders agreement, and is on the LC leader email list.
- **Local Conversation Member:** Someone who is an active and regular participant of the
  Local Conversation.
- **Strong Towns member:** Someone who is contributing financially to Strong Towns at any
  level. At each level of the Local Conversations process, groups are asked to have a
  certain number of ST Members supporting Strong Towns at the Movement Builder ($10/month
  or $100/year) level or higher.

**Additional benefits at all stages:** monthly LC resource emails; invitations to be
featured in ST content (articles, podcasts, videos) and annual Local Conversations Week.

The process arc runs: **Start-up Phase → Learn to Gather → Learn to Communicate → Learn
to Advocate → Transform the Community.**

## The Local Conversation Leader Agreement (the "Don't Be a Jerk" Agreement)

- We are kind, even when it's hard. We are working to reform systems and change minds. To
  the extent that we have enemies, those enemies are systems and accepted
  practices/approaches, not individuals. We can't use the Strong Towns platform to
  demonize or personally attack any individual, no matter how frustrated we are with them.
- We are polite. We don't curse. We don't use salacious imagery. Our communications will
  often be provocative, but it will always be safe for work.
- We are trustworthy. We don't lie, deceive, or conduct ourselves in a way that will
  undermine our credibility as a consensus builder. We are going to push boundaries and
  challenge deeply held beliefs, but we are known as people who say what they mean and
  mean what they say. Ours is a long term strategy.

For Strong Towns to win, we need the broad culture to change. We change the broad culture
by being a positive force, one that builds consensus around key insights and shared
realities. By being a Local Leader, you are signing up to be part of this strategy. You
are agreeing not to be a jerk.

By signing, a Local Leader agrees to:

- Ensure the group has at least one member of Strong Towns at the Movement Builder level.
- Make sure the Local Conversation is current on their check-ins.
- Not be a jerk.

Failure to do any of these may result in Strong Towns rescinding Local Leader status
and/or delisting the Local Conversation.

## Accomplishments: Start-Up Phase

Completing these unlocks: addition to the Local Conversation map with contact info; an
email to ST's list in your area announcing your group; announcement in the monthly LC
email and the site announcement bar; a welcome packet; an invitation to the LC Leader
Discord server; an invite to the monthly Stage 1 Zoom meeting.

- Complete an onboarding call with Strong Towns staff
- Complete the online Local Conversation leadership course
- Read and sign the Local Conversation Leader Agreement
- Meet at least one time with two other people
- Ensure that at least one LC member is a Strong Towns member at the Movement Builder
  level

## Accomplishments: Stage 1

Unlocks: ST follows and boosts your social media; growth-hacking advertising for your
group in your region; a communications coaching and strategy session; an invite to the
monthly Stage 2 Zoom meeting.

- Add at least one more Local Conversation leader (or have at least two)
- Complete the Working Across Difference Training
- Complete at least one Learning Activity
- Expand Local Conversation members to a minimum of 5
- Add at least one more member at the Movement Builder level (or have a minimum of 2)

## Accomplishments: Stage 2

Unlocks: a Strong Towns landing page; free passes for Local-Motive and Academy courses;
invitations to monthly Stage 3 Zoom meetings; periodic access to the ST email list for
your area; a Strong Towns email address (tentative).

- Add at least one more leader (or have at least three)
- Complete the training on the Strong Towns Approach to Messaging
- Create a group communications strategy
- Complete a second Learning Activity
- Complete a Movement Building Activity with a messaging focus
- Expand members to a minimum of 7
- Add at least one more member at the Movement Builder level (or have a minimum of 3)

## Accomplishments: Stage 3

Unlocks: an invitation to the Leadership Summit in Brainerd; advance tickets to National
Gathering; gift passes to Local-Motive; invitations to monthly Stage 4 Zoom meetings;
priority access to ST staff; coaching on priority campaign implementation; ST acting as
fiscal agent for grant applications (tentative).

- Complete the training on the 4-Step Process
- Identify your group's focus areas, especially those aligned with the five ST campaigns
- Complete a third Learning Activity
- Complete an Organizing Activity
- Expand members to a minimum of 9

## Learning Activities

Right from the start, Local Conversation members should be learning together. It deepens
the group's knowledge and gives members shared language and shared ideas as a foundation
for advocacy. It also puts the "conversation" in Local Conversation, giving the group
practice at talking well with one another — sometimes across differences and
disagreements.

- Go through the free Strong Towns 101 course (separately or together). Discuss as a
  group.
- Watch one or more of the Not Just Bikes + Strong Towns videos, using the discussion
  guides to facilitate conversation.
- Watch the Curbside Chat video series. Discuss.
- Host a book club on one of the Strong Towns books, one of the free ebooks, a book from
  *The Essential Reading List for the Strong Towns Thinker*, or another book on Strong
  Towns themes. Members-only or open to the public.
- Watch a Strong Towns Local-Motive session as a group (30+ topics).
- Read a Strong Towns article together that's relevant to your town. Or a podcast
  episode, webcast, Office Hours recording, or YouTube video. Discuss.
- As a group, attend a Strong Towns event near you.
- Invite a local leader to your group meeting — an elected official, city or county
  staff, a nonprofit member, or another local leader. Respectfully ask about their work,
  then brainstorm ways your group can be of service to it.
- Attend a city council meeting together. Talk later: what issues touched on the Strong
  Towns conversation?
- Have someone present on the history of your town's development. Before-and-after photos
  contrasting the traditional development pattern can be impactful. One Local
  Conversation did a public presentation on the history of its town's subdivisions and
  got local media attention.
- Create your own short series of presentations on key Strong Towns ideas — the approach,
  the 6 principles, or other core concepts.
- Peruse the Action Lab. Find a resource you can turn into a learning activity and
  discussion.

## Movement Building Activities

As your Local Conversation develops, you will want to start sharing your group's message
with the broader community. At minimum, your message is what your group stands for, what
you are doing, and how the Strong Towns approach applies to the challenges and
opportunities facing your town or city.

- Write and submit a group letter to the editor of your local paper on a live local
  conversation. (Example: Strong Towns Baltimore)
- Organize a letter writing campaign. (Examples: Strong Towns Grand Rapids, Alabama
  Urbanists)
- Host open video discussions on issues facing your community, pivoting toward a Strong
  Towns approach. (Example: Strong Towns Langley)
- Get one or more members onto a TV or radio program, a podcast, or as a guest writer on
  a local blog or publication. (Example: Bloomington Revivalists)
- Get your group on an outward-facing social platform.
- Create your own blog, podcast, or YouTube channel. (Examples: Allendale Strong, Strong
  Towns Nanaimo)
- Create a newsletter for your email list. (Example: Strong Towns Santa Barbara)
- Create a website. (Example: Fortify Richmond)
- Host a public screening of a documentary. (Example: Vermonters for People-Oriented
  Places)
- Do a talk or presentation for a local club, kindred organization, or neighborhood
  association. (Example: Deltans for People-Oriented Places)
- Do one of the learning activities — book club, video — but open it to the public.
- Have members speak up at a city council or planning commission meeting.
- Meet in person with city officials, small business owners, or other local leaders. Look
  for ways your group can help them succeed at something they're working on.
- Organize an in-person or virtual event featuring a Strong Towns staff member, or a
  member of your group with a talent for public speaking.
- Create and distribute collateral summarizing who your group is and what you're about.
- Set up an information table at a local event.

## Organizing Activities

In time, your Local Conversation will be ready to organize your advocacy efforts and take
action together. There is a virtuous cycle to taking action — your group and your town
are strengthened together.

- Do a tactical urbanism project — build bus benches, make an intersection safer, create
  a crosswalk.
- Host a Q&A event for candidates for local office. (Example: Strong Towns Steubenville)
- Organize a trash pickup event. (Example: Bloomington Revivalists)
- Clean up a local park. (Example: ReForm Shreveport)
- Create a bike valet program for a local event. (Example: Strong Towns Grand Rapids)
- Organize a community bike ride.
- Create your own version of the Billion Bollards Club. (Example: Strong Towns PDX)
- Do something fun and useful with your city's excess parking. (Example: Stronger Denton)
- Host a Citizen Taco event. (Example: Strong Towns Sioux Falls)
- Host a neighborhood walk. (Example: Charlotte Urbanists)
- Help one or more leaders get elected to local office or appointed to local commissions.
  (Example: Bothellites for People-Oriented Places)
- Build up your city's tree canopy by planting street trees. (Example: Charlotte Rising)
- Create a campaign around an issue facing your community right now. (Example: Strong
  Towns Happy Valley)
- Look at the 40+ How-To Guides. Find one that is right for your group and do it.
- Create or support public art.
- Get mobilized for Black Friday Parking.
- Nominate your town or city for the Strongest Town Contest.
- Host your own Crash Analysis Studio.
- Go on a group neighborhood walk and run the Strong Towns 4-Step Process: (1) Humbly
  observe where people around you are struggling. (2) Identify the next smallest thing
  you can do to address that struggle. (3) Do that thing. Do it right now. (4) Repeat the
  process, again and again.

## Group Focus Areas

### Five core campaigns

- Making streets safer and more productive
- Housing (incremental housing, missing middle, ADUs, and more)
- Ending parking mandates and subsidies
- Making city finances more accessible and transparent
- Ending highway expansions

### Other common focus areas

Bikeability and/or walkability; public transit; economic development; parks and public
spaces; local food systems; community engagement.

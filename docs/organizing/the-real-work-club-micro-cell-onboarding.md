An onboarding guide for "micro-cells" — small, value-aligned groups doing volunteer work
together outside the charity-nonprofit model. Included as a comparison point for RSTU's
own committee and bloc onboarding: it is a non-tenant-specific take on the same problem
of moving people from awareness to commitment without burning them out. Its companion
worksheet, *How to Handle Tension, Doubts & Difficult Questions*, is also in this
library.

## You're not late. You're right on time.

The fact that you're here means something already shifted. Maybe you've been quietly
watching systems fail from the inside. Maybe you've been trying to help in small ways
but unsure if it mattered. Maybe you've been carrying the weight of truth in a world
that prefers a performance.

We see you.

This guide isn't a rulebook. It's a set of invitations. It's not about playing charity.
It's about taking off the mask and remembering who we are to each other.

We call this a **Micro-Cell** — a small, value-aligned group of people doing real work
together in a way that feels possible, needed, and deeply alive.

## What is a micro-cell?

- **Living node.** A micro-cell is a living node of trust, integrity, and action.
- **Small group.** It's made of 2–36 people who want to move from awareness to impact
  together.
- **Shared foundation.** It forms around shared values, mutual respect, and a practical
  purpose.
- **Authenticity.** It doesn't follow the charity-industrial playbook. It follows real
  need.
- **The answer drives solutions.** It is the answer to the question: where do we begin,
  together?

## Why people join (even if they're afraid)

Most people want to help. But—

**Common fears:**

- They're unsure if their help is wanted.
- They worry they'll say the wrong thing.
- They don't know what actions are actually useful.
- They're afraid of being seen as performative or naïve.
- They're unsure if they're "healed enough" to be of service.

**The truth:** You are needed. Not later, now. You don't need to be perfect. You need to
be present. If you're willing to practice, not pretend, you're already qualified.

## Core practices of a micro-cell

These are not rules. They're what keep the work real.

1. **Co-creation, not command.** You build the shape of your cell together. Every voice
   matters.
2. **Shared purpose, not shared optics.** You define success by lived impact, not social
   credit.
3. **Consistency, not intensity.** You commit to small, regular, aligned action. Not
   burnout.
4. **Transparency over performance.** You track patterns, notice misalignments, adjust
   together.

## Building trust within your micro-cell

Trust isn't assumed, it's cultivated through consistent, authentic action.

- **Show up consistently.** Reliability builds safety. When you commit to something,
  follow through. When you can't, communicate early and honestly.
- **Practice vulnerability.** Share your real capacity, your fears, your learning edges.
  Pretending to have it all together creates distance, not connection.
- **Honor boundaries.** Respect when someone says no, needs space, or has different
  capacity. Pushing creates resistance, not collaboration.

## Navigating challenges together

Every micro-cell will face moments of tension, misalignment, or uncertainty. These aren't
signs of failure, they're opportunities for deeper alignment. Confrontation done right
leads to greater intimacy.

- **Pause and breathe.** When tension arises, slow down. Create space before responding.
- **Listen for understanding.** Seek to understand before being understood. What's really
  being said?
- **Return to purpose.** When lost, come back to your shared why. What brought you
  together?
- **Use the tension tool.** See *How to Handle Tension, Doubts & Difficult Questions*.

## If you're still unsure, know this

> You're not too late. You're not too broken. You're not too small. And you're not alone.
>
> The old ways are crumbling. And you don't have to keep pretending they work.
>
> You're allowed to co-create something different — something that feels human,
> grounded, and true.

Let's begin.

# 🔄 Real Work Pulse Check Template

**Purpose:** A weekly check-in tool to support reflection, alignment, and momentum within
each micro-cell, without bureaucracy or performative fluff.

**Format:** Can be used as a shared doc, form, or print template.

**Instructions for use:**

- Complete Part 1 individually before the group meeting
- Share and discuss responses during the weekly cell meeting
- Complete Part 2 together as a group
- Focus on honesty over performance
- Allow 30–45 minutes for the full process

## Part 1: Self check (each member reflects)

🧠 **What moved in me this week?**
*(A thought, emotion, awareness, breakthrough, pattern, need, nudge…)*

💬 **What did I contribute to the cell this week?**
*(Effort, presence, listening, critique, encouragement, skill…)*

🛠 **What felt most real or needed in our work this week?**
*(Not what looked good — what actually mattered.)*

🚫 **What am I holding back, avoiding, or unsure how to bring?**
*(This is where most real work begins.)*

🌱 **What do I need right now?**
*(Clarity, help, courage, grace, space…)*

## Part 2: Group check (cell reflects together)

🔁 **What loop are we in?**
*(Are we repeating, avoiding, evolving, building, circling?)*

💡 **What insight came through the group this week?**
*(What became clear because we showed up together?)*

⚡ **What action or shift feels aligned next?**
*(Not the "should" — the true step.)*

🤝 **How are we doing at showing up for each other?**
*(On a scale from performative to co-creative?)*

🧭 **Is our project still aligned with our shared why?**
*(Pause and recalibrate if needed.)*

## Closing question (optional)

🌬 **If the work feels heavy or unclear, what would make it feel lighter and more true
next week?**

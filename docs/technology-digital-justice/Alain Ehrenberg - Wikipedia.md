# **Alain Ehrenberg** At Forum Libération 2010 in [Rennes.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rennes) **Born** 1950 (age 74–75) Paris, France **Nationality** French

**Occupation** [Sociologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sociologist)

**Alain Ehrenberg** (born 1950) is a French [sociologist,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sociologist) known for his major work on [clinical depression,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinical_depression) The Weariness of the Self. His work focuses on the culture of [individualism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Individualism) in modern times, and its relationship to mental health. As of 2023 he is research director at the [Centre National de Recherche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_National_de_Recherche_Scientifique) [Scientifique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_National_de_Recherche_Scientifique) (CNRS) in [Paris.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris)

## **Early life and education**

#### [\[edit\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Alain_Ehrenberg&action=edit§ion=1)

Ehrenberg was born in Paris, France, in 1950[.\[1\]](#page-5-0)

In 1968 he achieved his [Baccalauréat.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baccalaur%C3%A9at) In 1972 earned Master of Science in management at [Université Paris IX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universit%C3%A9_Paris_IX) [\(Dauphine\)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Dauphine_University)[.\[2\]](#page-5-1)

Moving to [Université Paris X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universit%C3%A9_Paris_X) at [Nanterre,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanterre) he attained further degrees in sociology and economics there. His PhD thesis published in 1978 at Nanterre (Thèse de 3e cycle en sociologie)[,\[2\]](#page-5-1) was a sociological study called Archangels, warriors, military men and sportsmen. Essay on the education of the strong man. [\[3\]](#page-5-2)

He wrote a second sociology thesis in 1991 (Thèse en sociologie (nouveau régime)) at [Université Paris VII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universit%C3%A9_Paris_VII) [\(Jussieu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jussieu_Campus) [Campus\)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jussieu_Campus)[.\[2\]](#page-5-1)

In 1992, he was given accreditation to supervise research at the [École des hautes études en sciences sociales.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89cole_des_hautes_%C3%A9tudes_en_sciences_sociales) [\[2\]](#page-5-1)

After writing his PhD thesis in 1978, Ehrenberg became interested in the anxieties of the individual in modern society, faced with the need for achievement and [autonomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Individual_autonomy) and the loss of social signposts and support systems.[\[4\]](#page-5-3)

After being a researcher at the [Edgar Morin Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgar_Morin_Centre) at the [School for Advanced Studies in the Social Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_for_Advanced_Studies_in_the_Social_Sciences) in Paris[,\[5\]](#page-5-4) [\[6\]](#page-6-0) in 1994 Ehrenberg founde[d\[7\]](#page-6-1) and co-directed the research group "Psychotropics, Politics, Society" (Centre de recherche Psychotropes, Santé mentale, Société, aka CESAMES).[\[1\]](#page-5-0) This is a joint research unit of the [University of Paris 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris_5) (Descartes) and the [Centre national de la recherche scientifique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_national_de_la_recherche_scientifique) (CNRS - National Centre for Scientific Research)[,\[8\]](#page-6-2) where Ehrenberg is professor of sociology[.\[9\]](#page-6-3) Since 2001, he has worked with a government [research institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_institute) for [human health.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_health) [\[1\]](#page-5-0)

Ehrenberg's work focuses on the emphasis of individualism in contemporary culture, linking this to recent work on depression, mental health, and [psycho-analysis.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psycho-analysis) [\[7\]](#page-6-1)

### **La Fatigue d'être soi**

#### [\[edit\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Alain_Ehrenberg&action=edit§ion=3)

In 1998 he published a major work on [clinical depression,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinical_depression) La Fatigue d'être soi, which was translated into six languages,[\[10\]](#page-6-4) including English (The Weariness of the Self, 2009). In this work, which Rasmus Johnsen, assistant professor at [Copenhagen Business School,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Business_School) describes as having "a reputation that makes it worthy of a position as a must-read for anyone with an interest in [social philosophy"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_philosophy), Ehrenberg argues that depression not simply a disease, and (as paraphrased by Johnsen), has risen "from its status as a secondary phenomenon often associated with other mental illnesses to a challenge that applies to everyone, [and] is a symptom, not necessarily of an increased [social pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_pressure) on the subject, but of a fundamental transformation in the way individuals understand themselves"[.\[11\]](#page-6-5) In 2015, American [political philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_philosopher) and writer [Matthew Crawford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_Crawford) listed this work as one of his six favourite books. He writes that, according to Eherenberg, "depression has replaced guilt as our defining psychic affliction", arising from a sense of inadequacy to perform to our full potential, which we are driven to achieve by modern western culture[.\[12\]](#page-6-6)

As of 2023 Ehrenberg is a director of research at the CNRS[\[1\]](#page-5-0) and of Cesames[.\[13\]](#page-6-7)[\[14\]](#page-6-8)

From 1974 to 1977, Ehrenberg taught [karate.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karate) He has also been a member of many scientific committees and groups focused on mental health, [substance abuse,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Substance_abuse) and neuroscience[.\[2\]](#page-5-1)

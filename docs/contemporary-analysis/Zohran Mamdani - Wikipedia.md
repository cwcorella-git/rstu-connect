# **Zohran Mamdani**

**Zohran Kwame Mamdani**[\[c\]](#page-11-2) (born October 18, 1991) is an American politician who has served since 2021 as a member of the [New York State Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_State_Assembly) from the [36th](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York%27s_36th_State_Assembly_district) [district,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York%27s_36th_State_Assembly_district) based in [Queens.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queens) A member of the [Democratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_(United_States)) [Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_(United_States)) and the [Democratic Socialists of America,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Socialists_of_America) he is the Democratic nominee for [mayor of New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_New_York_City) in the [2025 election.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_New_York_City_mayoral_election)

Mamdani was born in [Kampala, Uganda,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kampala) into an [Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_people) family, to academic [Mahmood Mamdani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahmood_Mamdani) and filmmaker [Mira Nair.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mira_Nair) The family immigrated to [South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Africa) when he was five years old and then to the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States) when he was seven, settling in [New York City.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City) Mamdani graduated from the [Bronx High School of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx_High_School_of_Science) and earned a [bachelor's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor%27s_degree) in [Africana studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Africana_studies) from [Bowdoin College.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bowdoin_College) After working as a housing counselor and [hip-hop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hip-hop) musician, he entered [local politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_New_York_City) as a campaign manager for [Khader El-Yateem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khader_El-Yateem) and [Ross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ross_Barkan) [Barkan.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ross_Barkan) Mamdani was first elected to the New York State Assembly in 2020, defeating four-term incumbent [Aravella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aravella_Simotas) [Simotas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aravella_Simotas) in the Democratic primary. He was reelected without opposition in 2022 and 2024.

In October 2024, Mamdani announced his candidacy for mayor of New York City in the 2025 election. His campaign platform includes support for [fare-free city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_public_transport) [buses;](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_public_transport) [public child care;](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child_care#Non-profit_daycare) [city-owned grocery stores;](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_grocery_store) a [rent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rent_regulation_in_New_York) [freeze on rent-stabilized units;](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rent_regulation_in_New_York) additional aff[ordable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affordable_housing) [housing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affordable_housing) units; comprehensive [public safety reform;](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_reform_in_the_United_States) and a \$30 [minimum wage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimum_wage_in_the_United_States) by 2030. Mamdani also supports tax increases on corporations and those earning above \$1 million annually. He has been sharply critical of [Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel)'s [treatment of Palestinians,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_genocide) pledging to abide by the [International Criminal Court arrest warrants for Israeli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Criminal_Court_arrest_warrants_for_Israeli_leaders) [leaders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Criminal_Court_arrest_warrants_for_Israeli_leaders) by arresting Prime Minister [Benjamin Netanyahu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Netanyahu) if he visits New York City. ## **Early life and education**

Mamdani was born on October 18, 1991, in [Kampala,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kampala) Uganda, the only child of filmmaker [Mira Nair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mira_Nair) and [postcolonialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postcolonialism) academic [Mahmood Mamdani.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahmood_Mamdani)[\[11\]](#page-13-0)[\[12\]](#page-13-1) He was given his middle name, [Kwame,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwame) by his father in honor of [Kwame Nkrumah,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwame_Nkrumah) the first [President of Ghana.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Ghana)[\[13\]](#page-13-2)[\[14\]](#page-13-3) Both his parents are of [Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_people) descent; his mother is a [Punjabi Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_Hindu)[\[15\]](#page-13-4)[\[16\]](#page-13-5) who was born in [Rourkela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rourkela) and raised in [Bhubaneswar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhubaneswar)[,\[17\]](#page-13-6) and his father is a [Gujarati Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarati_Muslims) who was born in [Bombay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mumbai) and grew up primarily in Uganda.[\[18\]](#page-13-7) His paternal grandparents were born in present-day [Tanzania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanzania) and his father's family was part of the [Indian diaspora in Southeast Africa.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_diaspora_in_Southeast_Africa)[\[18\]](#page-13-7) His maternal grandmother was a [social worker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_work) and founder of the [Salaam Baalak Trust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salaam_Baalak_Trust)[.\[19\]](#page-14-0)

Mamdani lived in Kampala until he was five, when his family moved to [Cape Town,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Town) in South Africa's [Western Cape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Cape) province.[\[11\]](#page-13-0) He attended [St. George's Grammar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._George%27s_Grammar_School_(Cape_Town)) [School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._George%27s_Grammar_School_(Cape_Town)) in [Mowbray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mowbray,_Cape_Town) while his father taught [African studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_studies) at the [University of Cape Town.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cape_Town)[\[14\]](#page-13-3) The family then moved to America, settling in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City) when Mamdani was seven, and he was raised in [Morningside Heights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morningside_Heights)[.\[20\]](#page-14-1) He has described his upbringing as "privileged", saying, "I never had to want for something, and yet I knew that was not in any way the reality for most New Yorkers.["\[21\]](#page-14-2)

Mamdani attended the [Bank Street School for Children](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_Street_College_of_Education#Bank_Street_School_for_Children) on the [Upper West Side](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_West_Side) of [Manhattan,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan) where he successfully ran as the independent candidate in a middle school [mock election,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mock_election) adopting a platform of "equal rights, anti-war [policy] that proposed spending money on education rather than the military".[\[22\]](#page-14-3) In 2003, he returned to Kampala for a year and attended school during his father's [sabbatical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabbatical) there;[\[18\]](#page-13-7) his paternal grandparents and aunt still live there and helped take care of him while his father was working on the book *Good Muslim, Bad Muslim*. [\[23\]](#page-14-4)

Mamdani graduated from the [Bronx High School of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx_High_School_of_Science) in [Kingsbridge Heights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingsbridge_Heights,_Bronx)[,\[24\]](#page-14-5) where he cofounded the school's first cricket tea[m\[12\]](#page-13-1) and unsuccessfully ran for student body vice president[.\[25\]](#page-14-6) He also played soccer with the West Side Soccer League[.\[26\]](#page-14-7) Mamdani then attended [Bowdoin College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bowdoin_College) in [Brunswick,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brunswick,_Maine) [Maine,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brunswick,_Maine) where he co-founded the school's chapter of [Students for Justice in Palestine.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Students_for_Justice_in_Palestine) He graduated in 2014 with a [bachelor's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor%27s_degree) in [Africana studies.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Africana_studies)[\[27\]](#page-14-8)[\[28\]](#page-14-9)

## **Career**

Before running for office, Mamdani worked as a [foreclosure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreclosure) prevention and housing counselor, assisting lower-income nonwhite homeowners in [Queens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queens) with [eviction notices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eviction_notice) and efforts to remain in their homes.[\[29\]](#page-15-0) He said the experience motivated him to run for office to address the housing and affordability crisis.[\[29\]](#page-15-0)

## **Music career**

Mamdani is a fan of hip-hop and has composed and produced rap music.[\[30\]](#page-15-1) In 2016, under the moniker Young Cardamom, he collaborated with Ugandan rapper HAB on an [EP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EP) titled *Sidda Mukyaalo*, which is [Luganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luganda) for "No going back to the village"[.\[31\]](#page-15-2) The duo performed at that year's [Nyege Nyege](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nyege_Nyege) festival in [Jinja, Uganda.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinja,_Uganda)[\[32\]](#page-15-3) They first started working together in 2015, and their first song was Kanda [Chap Chap], about [chapati,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chapati) an Indian food that has been adopted as a Ugandan staple. Both rap in English and [Luganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luganda) (and HAB also raps in [Nubi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nubi_language) and [Swahili\)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swahili_language), and Mamdani said the "lyrics and choice of language are rebuttals of what Ugandan society expects of us--that someone with some South Sudanese roots is forever 'Nubi' and that Indian Ugandans are actually just Indians in Uganda".[\[33\]](#page-15-4) Mamdani also credits Ugandan producer [Hannz Tactiq,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hannz_Tactiq) who provided the beat and would "record, mix & master" for them, and in whose studio they worked until late at night.[\[32\]](#page-15-3)[\[33\]](#page-15-4) In 2019, Mamdani released a [single](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single_(music)) titled "Nani" under the moniker Mr. Cardamom, an homage to his maternal grandmother,[\[34\]](#page-15-5) whom he described as "a source of joy, wisdom, and love".[\[35\]](#page-15-6) Cookbook author and actress [Madhur Ja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madhur_Jaffrey)ffrey is featured in the single's music video, playing his grandmother[.\[36\]](#page-15-7)

Mamdani curated and produced [the soundtrack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_of_Katwe_(Original_Motion_Picture_Soundtrack)) for his mother Mira Nair's 2016 film *[Queen of Katwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_of_Katwe)*. [\[37\]](#page-15-8)[\[38\]](#page-16-0) As the film's music supervisor, he was nominated in the 2017 [Guild of Music Supervisors Awards.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guild_of_Music_Supervisors_Awards)[\[39\]](#page-16-1)

## **Early political involvement**

Mamdani entered [New York City politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_New_York_City) as a volunteer for Ali Najmi's campaign in the [2015 special election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City%27s_23rd_City_Council_district#2015_special) for the [23rd district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City%27s_23rd_City_Council_district) of the City Council.[\[13\]](#page-13-2)[\[30\]](#page-15-1) In 2017, Mamdani joined the [Democratic Socialists of](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Socialists_of_America) [America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Socialists_of_America) (DSA) and worked for the campaign of [New York City Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_Council) candidate [Khader El-Yateem,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khader_El-Yateem) a [Palestinian Lutheran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Christians) [minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_(Christianity)) and [democratic socialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_socialist) from [Bay Ridge, Brooklyn.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_Ridge,_Brooklyn)[\[13\]](#page-13-2) Mamdani served as the campaign manager for [Ross Barkan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ross_Barkan)'s 2018 bid for the [New York State Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_State_Senate) and was also a field organizer for fellow democratic socialist Tiff[any Cabán'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiffany_Cab%C3%A1n)s 2019 campaign for [Queens County District Attorney.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queens_County_District_Attorney)[\[13\]](#page-13-2)[\[40\]](#page-16-2)

## **New York State Assembly (2020-present)**

In October 2019, Mamdani announced his campaign to represent [New York's 36th State Assembly district,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York%27s_36th_State_Assembly_district) which encompasses [Astoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astoria,_Queens) and [Long Island City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Island_City) in Queens[.\[41\]](#page-16-3)[\[42\]](#page-16-4) He was endorsed by the DSA[,\[43\]](#page-16-5) running on a platform of [housing reform,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_to_housing) [police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_reform_in_the_United_States) and [prison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prisoner_rights_in_the_United_States) reform, and [public ownership of utilities.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_utility)[\[41\]](#page-16-3) Mamdani's June 2020 [primary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_election) victory over four-term Democratic incumbent [Aravella Simotas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aravella_Simotas) took almost a month to call,[\[44\]](#page-16-6) and he won the general election with no [Republican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Party_(United_States)) opposition in November[.\[45\]](#page-16-7) Mamdani was reelected without opposition in 2022[\[46\]](#page-16-8) and 2024[.\[47\]](#page-17-0)

## Volunteers for Mamdani

Mamdani's victory as the Democratic Party candidate, which was announced by the Associated Press on July 1 after the New York City Board of Elections released its ranked-choice ballot tabulation,[\[76\]](#page-19-8) was considered a major upset[.\[77\]](#page-19-9) In the weeks after the primary, Mamdani was endorsed in the general election by [Adriano Espaillat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adriano_Espaillat)[\[78\]](#page-19-10) and [Jerry Nadler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerry_Nadler)[,\[79\]](#page-20-0) who represent portions of New York City in Congress, and by labor unions including the [United Federation of Teachers,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Federation_of_Teachers)[\[80\]](#page-20-1) the [New York State Nurses Association,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Nurses_United)[\[81\]](#page-20-2) and [local chapter 32BJ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SEIU_32BJ) of the Service Employees International Union[.\[81\]](#page-20-2)

*The New York Times* wrote that Mamdani's victory in the primary "offered the starkest evidence yet that outspoken opposition to Israel and its government--and even questioning its existence as a Jewish state--is increasingly acceptable to broader swaths of the party, even in areas where pro-Israel Jews have long been a bedrock part of the Democratic coalition."[\[82\]](#page-20-3)

In August 2025, Cuomo incorrectly claimed that Mamdani lived in a [rent-controlled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rent_regulation_in_New_York) apartment, and that Mamdani's tenancy was responsible for the fact that "somewhere last night in New York City, a single mother and her children slept at a homeless shelter". Mamdani responded that when he first began leasing his [rent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rent-stabilized)[stabilized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rent-stabilized) apartment, he earned less than he does now, and he plans to move out in the future[.\[83\]](#page-20-4)[\[84\]](#page-20-5)[\[85\]](#page-20-6)[\[86\]](#page-20-7) [\[87\]](#page-20-8) The New York Times and ABC wrote that Cuomo's attack reflects a larger debate on who should benefit from government regulation of housing costs, with housing experts and tenant advocates immediately panning Cuomo's proposal to [means-test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Means_testing) rent-stabilized apartments[.\[88\]](#page-20-9)[\[89\]](#page-20-10)

## **Political positions**

Mamdani began to identify as a [democratic socialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_socialist) after [Bernie Sanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernie_Sanders)' [2016 presidential campaign.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernie_Sanders_2016_presidential_campaign)[\[40\]](#page-16-2) He is a member of the [Democratic Socialists of America.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Socialists_of_America)[\[49\]](#page-17-2)[\[58\]](#page-18-0)

### **Childcare and education**

Mamdani supports a universal [pre-kindergarten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-kindergarten) childcare system.[\[102\]](#page-22-0)[\[103\]](#page-22-1)[\[104\]](#page-22-2) He has proposed giving all new New York City families ["baby baskets"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maternity_package) containing products such as diapers and nursing supplies[.\[105\]](#page-22-3) Mamdani introduced a bill to eliminate [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University)'s and [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University)'s state [property tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Property_tax) [exemption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax_exemption) and direct those funds to the [City University of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_University_of_New_York) system, which has historically struggled with funding.[\[106\]](#page-22-4)

In an interview with *[Der Blatt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Der_Blatt)*, a newspaper associated with the [Hasidic movement of Judaism,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hasidic_Judaism) Mamdani expressed a desire to defend Hasidic [yeshivas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeshiva) (institutions for [Torah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torah) study) from accusations of failing to meet state educational standards.[\[107\]](#page-22-5) He stated that "I will listen to your leaders" on education and "I will work to protect you from anyone who wants to disturb your way of life".[\[107\]](#page-22-5)

## **Crime and policing**

Mamdani has argued that increasing policing and incarceration does little to prevent harm and that "dignified work, economic stability, and well-resourced neighborhoods" can more effectively keep the public safe.[\[108\]](#page-22-6) He has advocated a more community-based approach to reducing crime, focusing on homeless outreach and anti-violence programs. He contends that there is too much reliance on police to fix societal problems, saying, "Police have a critical role to play, but right now we are relying on them to deal with the failures of the [social](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_safety_net) [safety net](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_safety_net) of reliance that is preventing them from doing their actual jobs."[\[109\]](#page-22-7) He has proposed a Department of Community Safety to expand [mental health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mental_health) outreach.[\[58\]](#page-18-0)

In a June 2020 tweet, Mamdani wrote, "We don't need an investigation to know that the NYPD is racist, antiqueer & a major threat to public safety. What we need is to [#Defund TheNYPD."](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defund_the_police)[\[110\]](#page-22-8)[\[111\]](#page-23-0) During the 2025 mayoral race, he said he would work with the police rather than defund them, emphasizing their importance in dealing with violent crime and the role of social workers and mental health professionals in addressing underlying causes and prevention.[\[112\]](#page-23-1)

## **Economic policy**

Mamdani advocates for debt relief among [taxi medallion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxi_medallion) owners[.\[113\]](#page-23-2)

Mamdani has advocated [rent control,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rent_control) strengthening tenant protections, and creating a Social Housing Development Agency that would build [publicly owned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_housing) aff[ordable housing.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affordable_housing)[\[114\]](#page-23-3) He wants to build 200,000 new units of affordable, rent-stabilized homes over the next 10 years and double the amount of spending to rehabilitate homes for the city's 400,000 public housing tenants[.\[115\]](#page-23-4) He also wants to "increase density around mass transit hubs" and "upzone wealthier neighborhoods", citing housing policies in [Jersey City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jersey_City,_New_Jersey) and [Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo) as examples[.\[116\]](#page-23-5)

Mamdani supports raising New York City's [minimum wage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimum_wage) to \$30 per hour by 2030.[\[117\]](#page-23-6) He advocates a pilot program in which the city government would operate one grocery store in each borough to drive down prices[.\[58\]](#page-18-0)[\[118\]](#page-23-7)

Mamdani supports an increase in corporate taxes in New York State from 7.25% to 11.5%, to match those of New Jersey[.\[119\]](#page-23-8) He also supports a new 2% increase for income tax on city residents who earn more than \$1 million a year,[\[115\]](#page-23-4) to raise \$20 billion to fund tuition-free [CUNY](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_University_of_New_York) and [SUNY](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_University_of_New_York) schools, statewide universal childcare, a subway fare freeze, free [MTA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Transportation_Authority) buses, and tenant protections.[\[120\]](#page-24-0) In June 2025, Mamdani said, "I don't think we should have billionaires."[\[121\]](#page-24-1)

Mamdani made a mayoral campaign pledge to reduce taxes on "overtaxed homeowners in the outer boroughs" of New York City, while advocating to increase taxes on "more expensive homes in richer and whiter neighborhoods.["\[122\]](#page-24-2)[\[123\]](#page-24-3) His intention with this policy was to tackle a 1981 [property tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Property_tax) law that essentially taxes owners of expensive homes based on the homes' potential rental price rather than their market value, which could lead to the owners paying much less property tax than owners of cheap homes. [\[124\]](#page-24-4)

## **Environment**

Mamdani views climate action as essential to achieving social justice in New York City. In 2021, he organized volunteers and lobbied Governor Kathy Hochul to prevent the expansion of a gas-fired peaker power plant in Astoria, citing environmental concerns for low-income nonwhite communities[.\[125\]](#page-24-5)[\[126\]](#page-24-6) Mamdani has also backed statewide measures such as the All-Electric Buildings Act, which prohibits installing fossil-fuel equipment (e.g., gas stoves) in new buildings, and supported introducing congestion pricing in Manhattan to reduce traffic-related emissions.[\[127\]](#page-24-7)[\[128\]](#page-24-8)

As a mayoral candidate, Mamdani proposed a comprehensive decarbonization and resilience agenda. His "Green Schools for a Healthier New York City" blueprint would retrofit 500 public school buildings with rooftop solar arrays and upgraded HVAC systems; build 500 green schoolyards; transform heat-absorbing asphalt into green space serving students and community residents; convert 50 schools into year-round resilience hubs to offer shelter and resources during extreme heat, storms, or flooding; and extend tax incentives such as J-51 benefits to support building owners' compliance with Local Law 97[.\[127\]](#page-24-7)[\[129\]](#page-24-9)

## **Healthcare**

Mamdani supports the [New York Health Act,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Health_Act) which would establish [single-payer healthcare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single-payer_healthcare) in New York State[.\[130\]](#page-24-10) He portrayed a firefighter in a 2021 advertisement for the Campaign for New York Health[.\[130\]](#page-24-10)

## **Foreign policy**

The day after the [Hamas-](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamas)led [October 7 attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_7_attacks) on Israel, Mamdani issued a statement to "mourn the hundreds of people killed across Israel and Palestine" and saying that the end of ["occupation"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli-occupied_territories) and "apartheid" was the only path to peace.[\[133\]](#page-25-1)[\[82\]](#page-20-3)[\[134\]](#page-25-2) He later condemned the Hamas attacks as a "horrific war crime".[\[82\]](#page-20-3)[\[135\]](#page-25-3) The next week, Mamdani was arrested during a pro-Palestinian [cease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calls_for_a_ceasefire_during_the_Gaza_war)fire demonstration.[\[136\]](#page-25-4) [\[137\]](#page-25-5)[\[138\]](#page-25-6)[\[135\]](#page-25-3) In 2025, Mamdani said he believed Israel was committing [genocide in Gaza,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_genocide)[\[139\]](#page-25-7)[\[131\]](#page-24-11) and that if [Benjamin Netanyahu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Netanyahu) travels to New York City, he should be arrested in accordance with the [International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Criminal_Court_arrest_warrants_for_Israeli_leaders) [Criminal Court's warrant for his arrest.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Criminal_Court_arrest_warrants_for_Israeli_leaders)[\[140\]](#page-25-8)

#### **India**

At a mayoral forum in May 2025, a question was posed about engaging with [Indian Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_India) [Narendra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narendra_Modi) [Modi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narendra_Modi) at a rally, such as one at [Madison Square Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison_Square_Garden)[.\[143\]](#page-26-0) Mamdani said he would refuse, as he considers Modi, who is part of the [Bharatiya Janata Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bharatiya_Janata_Party) (BJP), a "war criminal", and further accused him of "orchestrat[ing] a mass slaughter of [Muslims in Gujarat"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarati_Muslims) during [2002 riots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Gujarat_violence) in which 790 Muslims and 254 Hindus died[.\[144\]](#page-26-1) Mamdani added that he believes that in the aftermath of these events, few Muslims remain in Gujarat, and that most people believe Gujarati Muslims do not exist today. Mamdani also said that Modi should be viewed in the same light as [Benjamin Netanyahu,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Netanyahu) the current [Prime Minister of Israel.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Israel)[\[145\]](#page-26-2) Leaders in India criticized Mamdani's assertion that there are few Muslims left in Gujarat,[\[143\]](#page-26-0) calling it a "blatant lie" and pointing out that in the state's most recent census, Muslims were at least 10% of the population[.\[143\]](#page-26-0)[\[145\]](#page-26-2) In [The Express Tribune,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Express_Tribune) [Rakhshinda Parveen,](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Rakhshinda_Parveen&action=edit&redlink=1) a Pakistani journalist, praised Mamdani's "courage" in condemning Modi.[\[146\]](#page-26-3)

### **Immigration**

Mamdani supports enforcement of New York's [sanctuary laws,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanctuary_city#New_York) which bar Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) from entering schools, hospitals, and city property without a judicial warrant, and has advocated for stronger sanctuary laws[.\[150\]](#page-26-7) He has also proposed creating a commission to ensure compliance by city agencies and contractors.[\[151\]](#page-26-8) He has pledged that if he is elected mayor, the city will provide legal representation for all immigrants in detention proceedings.[\[152\]](#page-26-9)

On March 12, 2025, Mamdani confronted [Tom Homan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Homan) in Albany about ICE's recent arrest of [Mahmoud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detention_of_Mahmoud_Khalil) [Khalil.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detention_of_Mahmoud_Khalil) Mamdani yelled, "Do you believe in the First Amendment?"[\[153\]](#page-27-0)

#### **LGBTQ+ rights**

Mamdani supports [LGBTQ+ rights.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBTQ%2B_rights)[\[154\]](#page-27-1)[\[155\]](#page-27-2) He aims to establish NYC as an [LGBTQ+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBTQ%2B) sanctuary city and establish the Office of LGBTQIA+ Affairs to "expand and centralize the services, programs, and support that LGBTQIA+ New Yorkers need across housing, employment, and more."[\[156\]](#page-27-3)[\[157\]](#page-27-4) Mamdani says [LGBTQ+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homelessness_among_LGBTQ_youth_in_the_United_States) [people who became homeless due to their identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homelessness_among_LGBTQ_youth_in_the_United_States) would benefit from his [cost-of-living](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cost-of-living_crisis) policies[.\[155\]](#page-27-2)

Mamdani appeared at a February 2025 rally in [Union Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Square,_Manhattan) to protest an executive order signed by President Trump that threatened to "withhold federal funding from hospitals that provide gender-affirming treatments to trans youth".[\[158\]](#page-27-5)[\[159\]](#page-27-6) As part of his platform, he supports expanding and protecting gender-affirming care citywide. He proposed an immediate investment of \$65 million in public providers to ensure funding for medical treatments that lose [Medicaid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medicaid) coverage and promised to ensure hospital accountability[.\[157\]](#page-27-4)

## **Transportation**

Mamdani supports permanently eliminating bus fares[.\[160\]](#page-27-7)[\[161\]](#page-27-8)[\[162\]](#page-27-9) He advocated for a [fare-free](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_public_transport) pilot program on [MTA buses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTA_Regional_Bus_Operations)[,\[163\]](#page-27-10)[\[164\]](#page-27-11) which was launched on the [Q4,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Q4_(New_York_City_bus)) [B60,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B60_(New_York_City_bus)) [Bx18,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bx18_(New_York_City_bus)) [M116](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M116_(New_York_City_bus)) and [S46/96](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S46_(New_York_City_bus)) routes in September 2023[.\[165\]](#page-28-0)[\[166\]](#page-28-1) The program saw a 30% increase in ridership on weekdays, predominantly from people earning less than \$28,000 a year. Across the five routes made free, assaults on bus operators dropped by 38.9%.[\[167\]](#page-28-2) The fare-free program ended in August 2024 after state lawmakers did not reauthorize it[.\[168\]](#page-28-3) [\[169\]](#page-28-4) In response, Mamdani said, "the MTA was opposed to this program ... because they were saying that now is not the time to create any kind of confusion around fare collection."[\[163\]](#page-27-10)[\[170\]](#page-28-5) He estimates that it would cost New York City \$650 million per year to eliminate bus fares[.\[171\]](#page-28-6)

In December 2022, Mamdani introduced a series of bills for the 2023 session called "Fix the MTA". He proposed free bus travel over the next four years across [the Bronx,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bronx) [Brooklyn,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn) [Queens,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queens) and then [Manhattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan) and [Staten Island.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staten_Island)[\[172\]](#page-28-7) The Formula Three Act would fill the \$2.5 billion shortfall of the MTA with another plank freezing fares at \$2.75. Another plank would have set aside further money for more frequency, such as six-minute headways for trains and the 100 most-used bus routes, then using any additional funds to increase service by 20%[.\[173\]](#page-28-8)

In 2023, Mamdani co-introduced a bill to enact a weight-based vehicle-registration fee to dissuade people from owning heavier vehicles in an effort to make streets safer[.\[174\]](#page-29-0) Mamdani supports [congestion pricing in](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congestion_pricing_in_New_York_City)

[New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congestion_pricing_in_New_York_City) and drafted a bill with New York state senator [Michael Gianaris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Gianaris) titled "Get Congestion Pricing Right" to increase bus service frequency and increase the number of fare-free buses[.\[175\]](#page-29-1)

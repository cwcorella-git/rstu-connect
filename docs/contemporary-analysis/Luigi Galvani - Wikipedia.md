# [Luigi Galvani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Main_Page)

Luigi Galvani [\(/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English)[ɡ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English)[æl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English)[ˈ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English)[v](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English)[ɑː](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English)[ni/,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English) also [US:](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_English) [/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English)[ɡɑː](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English)[l-/;](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English)[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luigi_Galvani#cite_note-1)[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luigi_Galvani#cite_note-2)[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luigi_Galvani#cite_note-3)[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luigi_Galvani#cite_note-4) Italian: [\[lu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Italian)ˈiːdʒi [ɡ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Italian)alˈvaː[ni\];](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Italian) [Latin:](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_language) Aloysius Galvanus; 9 September 1737 – 4 December 1798) was an Italian physician, physicist, biologist and philosopher, who studied [animal electricity.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galvanism) In 1780, he discovered that the muscles of dead frogs' legs twitched when struck by an electrical spark[.\[5\]:](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luigi_Galvani#cite_note-Whittaker-5)67–71 This was an early study of [bioelectricity,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bioelectricity) following experiments by [John Walsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Walsh_(scientist)) and [Hugh Williamson.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Williamson)

# Early life

Luigi Galvani was born to Domenico Galvani and Barbara Caterina Foschi, in [Bologna,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bologna) then part of the [Papal States.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papal_States)[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luigi_Galvani#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHeilbron2003323-6) The house in which he was born may still be seen on Via Marconi, 25, in the center of Bologna.[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luigi_Galvani#cite_note-Circulation_1962-7) Domenico was a [goldsmith.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goldsmith)[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luigi_Galvani#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHeilbron2003323-6) His family had produced several illustrious men[.\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luigi_Galvani#cite_note-Circulation_1962-7)

Galvani then began taking an interest in the field of "medical electricity". This field emerged in the middle of the 18th century, following electrical researches and the discovery of the effects of electricity on the human body by scientists including Bertrand Bajon and [Ramón M. Termeyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Ram%C3%B3n_M._Termeyer&action=edit&redlink=1) in the 1760s[,\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luigi_Galvani#cite_note-de_As%C3%BAa_2008-8) and by [John](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Walsh_(scientist)) [Walsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Walsh_(scientist))[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luigi_Galvani#cite_note-Edwards_2021-9)[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luigi_Galvani#cite_note-Alexander_1969-10) and [Hugh Williamson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Williamson) in the 1770s[.\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luigi_Galvani#cite_note-Vander Veer_2011-11)[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luigi_Galvani#cite_note-Bresadola_367%E2%80%93380-12)

# Galvani vs. Volta

[Alessandro Volta,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alessandro_Volta) a professor of experimental physics in the [University of Pavia,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pavia) was among the first scientists who repeated and checked Galvani's experiments. At first, he embraced animal electricity. However, he started to doubt that the conductions were caused by specific electricity intrinsic to the animal's legs or other body parts. Volta believed that the contractions depended on the metal cable Galvani used to connect the nerves and muscles in his experiments.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luigi_Galvani#cite_note-Bresadola_367%E2%80%93380-12)

Every cell has a [cell potential;](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Membrane_potential) biological electricity has the same chemical underpinnings as the current between [electrochemical cells,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrochemical_cell) and thus can be duplicated outside the body. Volta's intuition was correct. # Death

Galvani actively investigated animal electricity until the end of his life. The [Cisalpine Republic,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cisalpine_Republic) a French client state founded in 1797 after the French occupation of Northern Italy, required every university professor to swear loyalty to the new authority. Galvani, who disagreed with the social and political confusion, refused to swear loyalty, along with other colleagues. This led to the new authority depriving him of all his academic and public positions, which took every financial support away. Galvani died peacefully surrounded by his mother and father, in his brother's house depressed and in poverty, on 4 December 1798.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luigi_Galvani#cite_note-Bresadola_367%E2%80%93380-12)

## Legacy

Galvani's legacy includes:

- Galvani's report of his investigations were mentioned specifically by [Mary Shelley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Shelley) as part of the summer reading list leading up to an [ad hoc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ad_hoc) [ghost story](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghost_story) contest on a rainy day in Switzerland—and the resultant novel [Frankenstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankenstein)—and its reanimated construct. In Frankenstein, Victor studies the principles of galvanism but it is not mentioned in reference to the creation of the Monster.
- Galvani's name also survives in everyday language as the verb 'galvanize' as well as in more specialized terms: [Galvani potential,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galvani_potential) [galvanic anode,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galvanic_anode) [galvanic bath,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galvanic_bath) [galvanic cell,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galvanic_cell) [galvanic corrosion,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galvanic_corrosion) [galvanic couple,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galvanic_corrosion) [galvanic current,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_current) [galvanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galvanic_isolation) [isolation,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galvanic_isolation) [galvanic series,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galvanic_series) [galvanic skin response,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galvanic_skin_response) [galvanism,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galvanism) [galvanization,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galvanization) [hot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot-dip_galvanization)[dip galvanization,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot-dip_galvanization) [galvanometer,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galvanometer) Galvalume, and psycho-galvanic reflex.

Galvani, according to William Fox, was "by nature courageous and religious." [Jean-Louis-Marc Alibert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Louis-Marc_Alibert) said of Galvani that he never ended his lessons "without exhorting his hearers and leading them back to the idea of that eternal Providence, which develops, conserves, and circulates life among so many diverse beings."[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luigi_Galvani#cite_note-15)

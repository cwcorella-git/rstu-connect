# **Canada's Free Birth Control and Insulin Program: Complete Overview**

Canada has officially launched the first phase of its national universal pharmacare program, making birth control and insulin free for millions of Canadians. This landmark healthcare initiative represents a significant step toward universal healthcare coverage in the country.

#### **Legislative Foundation**

Canada's pharmacare bill, known as Bill C-64 or the Pharmacare Act, officially became law in October 2024 after receiving royal assent. The legislation establishes the framework for the first phase of national universal pharmacare, focusing specifically on contraceptive medications and diabetes treatments. The bill was introduced in February 2024 as part of negotiations between the Liberal government and the NDP under their supply-and-confidence agreement.

The federal government has committed \$1.5 billion in investments to launch this first phase of the national pharmacare plan. The program implements a universal first-dollar coverage system, meaning the government pays the costs upfront rather than requiring patients to pay and seek reimbursement later.

#### **Implementation Structure**

The pharmacare program requires bilateral agreements between the federal government and individual provinces and territories before coverage can begin. Each province must negotiate and sign its own agreement with Ottawa, which explains why implementation timelines vary across the country. The federal government provides funding while provinces handle the actual delivery of the program through their existing healthcare systems.

This decentralized approach means that while the framework is now law, actual access to free medications depends on where you live in Canada and whether your province has signed an agreement with the federal government.

# **Diabetes Coverage Details**

The diabetes component of the program covers essential medications that millions of Canadians rely on daily. Insulin, which is used by patients with both Type 1 and Type 2 diabetes, is included in the coverage. The program also covers metformin, a first-line treatment for Type 2 diabetes, along with medications often used in combination with insulin and metformin, including sulfonylureas and SGLT-2 inhibitors.

Beyond medications, the federal government has announced plans to establish a fund to support access to diabetes devices and supplies, including items like insulin pumps, syringes, glucose monitors, and test strips. This additional support recognizes that managing diabetes effectively requires more than just medication access.

The program is expected to benefit approximately 3.7 million Canadians living with diabetes, with potential savings of up to \$1,700 per year for individuals who previously had to pay out of pocket for their medications. The average diabetes patient currently pays about \$25 per day for insulin, continuous glucose monitors, and insulin pumps according to healthcare foundations.

## **Birth Control Coverage Details**

The contraceptive coverage component aims to provide reproductive freedom for approximately 9 million Canadian women and gender-diverse people of reproductive age. The program covers a comprehensive range of birth control options, including oral contraceptive pills, intrauterine devices (IUDs), contraceptive implants, and emergency contraception such as the morning-after pill.

This variety ensures that individuals can choose the contraceptive method that best suits their medical needs and personal circumstances, regardless of their ability to pay. Cost has been identified by Canadian contraceptive care providers as the single most important barrier to accessing these medications, with oral contraceptives typically costing around \$300 per year and IUDs costing up to \$500 upfront.

The program specifically aims to improve equality by reducing the risk of unintended pregnancies and improving reproductive rights. Research from the University of British Columbia estimated that nocost contraception has the potential to save the BC healthcare system approximately \$27 million per year, demonstrating that the program could generate long-term cost savings for the healthcare system.

## **Provincial Implementation Progress**

Several provinces have already begun implementing the pharmacare program, though timelines and specific coverage details vary by jurisdiction.

Manitoba became the first province to sign a bilateral agreement with the federal government on February 27, 2025. The Manitoba Enhanced Pharmacare Program (MEPP) began providing coverage on April 15, 2025, offering no-cost access to diabetes medications and birth control for eligible Manitoba residents with active health cards. The program absorbed previous provincial programs like the Manitoba Prescription Birth Control Program and Manitoba HIV Medications Program into the broader MEPP framework.

Prince Edward Island followed with its own agreement, beginning coverage on May 1, 2025. The federal government is providing \$30 million over four years to support the program in PEI, with residents automatically covered if they have a valid provincial health card. However, some medications and supplies are only partially funded, meaning residents may still need to pay price differences for brand-name drugs when generic versions are available.

British Columbia signed a four-year, \$670-million pharmacare agreement in March 2025, though universal access to the free medications won't begin until March 2026. Notably, BC had already been providing free contraceptives to residents for nearly two years before the federal program launched. The federal funding in BC will be used to cover hormone replacement therapy for women experiencing menopause symptoms, demonstrating how provinces can adapt the federal framework to address specific local healthcare needs.

# **Coverage Limitations and Considerations**

While the program provides significant coverage, there are some limitations to be aware of. Individuals who already have 100 percent coverage through other federal or provincial programs, such as Indigenous Services Canada's Non-Insured Health Benefit Program or provincial social assistance programs, are generally not eligible for the new pharmacare coverage and should continue accessing medications through their existing programs.

International students, temporary foreign workers, and others without provincial health cards are not eligible for coverage under the program. Additionally, while the medications themselves are covered, patients may still be responsible for other pharmacy fees such as delivery charges or prescribing fees.

The program currently focuses only on contraceptives and diabetes medications as the first phase of what the government describes as an eventual universal pharmacare system. Private and public drug plans will continue to cover other medications outside of these specific categories.

## **Future Expansion and Expert Committee**

The Pharmacare Act requires the establishment of a committee of experts within thirty days of the law coming into force. This committee will make recommendations on the operation and financing of a national, universal, single-payer pharmacare system in Canada that could eventually cover a much broader range of medications.

The Act also mandates that Canada's Drug Agency develop a list of essential drugs and related products to inform the development of a national formulary, create a national bulk purchasing strategy to help reduce drug prices, and establish a pan-Canadian strategy on the appropriate use of prescription medications.

The government has indicated it will take a step-by-step, incremental approach to expanding pharmacare coverage, evaluating how the first phase performs before deciding on broader implementation. Future phases could potentially include coverage for additional medication categories, though specific timelines and details have not yet been announced.

## **Provincial Resistance and Opt-Out Provisions**

Not all provinces have embraced the federal pharmacare program. Both Alberta and Quebec have expressed intentions to opt out of the national program if it conflicts with their existing healthcare priorities. These provinces prefer to receive their share of federal funding and invest it directly into their own healthcare systems rather than participating in the standardized national program.

This resistance highlights ongoing tensions between federal healthcare initiatives and provincial jurisdiction over healthcare delivery in Canada's federal system. However, the federal government continues to encourage all provinces and territories to sign agreements and begin providing coverage to their residents as soon as possible.

# **Long-term Healthcare Impact**

The pharmacare program represents more than just cost savings for individual patients. Healthcare experts view it as a crucial step toward improving health equity, affordability, and outcomes across Canada. By removing financial barriers to essential medications, the program aims to ensure that healthcare decisions are based on medical need rather than ability to pay.

The initiative also supports broader public health goals by potentially reducing emergency room visits and hospitalizations that can result when people cannot afford their medications. For diabetes patients in particular, consistent access to insulin and other essential medications can prevent serious complications and improve quality of life significantly.

The program's focus on contraceptive access also supports women's economic participation by allowing them to make informed choices about family planning, educational pursuits, and workforce participation without financial constraints limiting their reproductive healthcare options.

# **Current Status and Next Steps**

As of September 2025, the pharmacare program is in various stages of implementation across Canada. While the federal legislation is in place and some provinces have begun providing coverage, many Canadians are still waiting for their provincial governments to negotiate and sign agreements with Ottawa.

The federal government continues to urge provinces and territories to come to the negotiating table and sign agreements as quickly as possible. Prime Minister Justin Trudeau has emphasized that this represents real progress but noted that provincial cooperation is essential for Canadians to benefit from the program.

For Canadians interested in accessing the program, the key step is checking whether their province has signed an agreement and when coverage begins in their jurisdiction. Those in provinces with existing coverage can typically access the program through their regular healthcare providers and pharmacies, with coverage automatically applied for eligible medications.

The success of this first phase will likely influence both the timeline and scope of future pharmacare expansions, making it a critical test case for universal healthcare coverage in Canada.

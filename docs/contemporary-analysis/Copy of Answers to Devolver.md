If you were to make your dream game, what would the budget be and what would you want to include in the final game?

The production budget for my dream game is modest. Craftsmanship and DIY are really important to me. I need to create nearly everything, learn, grow and to make the best game I can. I'm definitely a team player though, and I'm more than happy to work with other people where my skills may not suffice or if time is of the essence.

Consequently, the budget of my dream game would be: the cost of my time + the cost of consultants on specific subjects + the inherent cost of marketing, localization, distribution, and porting.

Please, let me know if I misunderstood this part of your question!

There are tons of things I'd like to include in my dream game, so I'll limit it to my top 5.

- An intriguing blend of strategy with another genre (Strategy/Shooter, Strategy/Puzzle, etc...).
- Enabling players to express their creativity through the game mechanics or with modding tools (level editor, textures packs, ...).
- The accessibility features needed so that the more people possible can play the game in some shape or form despite their disabilities.
- A Magic Circle: A compelling world with its own story where players can immerse themselves and escape from the real world.
- Meaningful Choices: Choices and their consequences are always central to my games.

How long do you anticipate the average player to spend in Lueur to completion?

TLDR; From 15 to 30 hours excluding the roguelike mode and optional challenges

Lueur has been designed with short gaming sessions in mind, making it easy to play on the go. Each level will take between 30 to 60 minutes to complete depending on the game mode.

The analytics from the 4500 players who played the first level seem to confirm this. An average time played of 54 minutes, with 60% of them playing more than 30 minutes and 7% of them that played for more than 2h.

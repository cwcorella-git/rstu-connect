**Illinois** ( [IL-in-OY](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key)) is a [state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U. S._state) in the [Midwestern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midwestern_United_States) [United States.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States) It borders on [Lake Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Michigan) to its northeast, the [Mississippi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_River) to its west, and the [Wabash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wabash_River) and [Ohio rivers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_River) to its south[.\[b\]](#page-89-6) Of the fifty U. S. states, Illinois has the [fifth-largest gross domestic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U. S._states_and_territories_by_GDP) [product \(GDP\),](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U. S._states_and_territories_by_GDP) the [sixth-largest population,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U. S._states_and_territories_by_population) and the [25th-most](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U. S._states_and_territories_by_area) [land area.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U. S._states_and_territories_by_area) Its capital city is [Springfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Springfield,_Illinois) in the center of the state, and the state's largest city is [Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago) in the northeast.

Present-day Illinois was inhabited by [Indigenous cultures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas#History) for thousands of years. The French were the first Europeans to arrive, settling near the Mississippi and [Illinois River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_River) in the 17th century [Illinois Country,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_Country) as part of their sprawling colony of [New France.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_France) A century later, the revolutionary war [Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_campaign) [campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_campaign) prefigured American involvement in the region. Following [U. S. independence in 1783,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Paris_(1783)) which made the Mississippi River the national boundary, American settlers began arriving from [Kentucky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kentucky) via the Ohio River. Illinois was soon part of the United States' oldest territory, the [Northwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_Territory) [Territory,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_Territory) and in 1818 it achieved [statehood.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U. S._states_by_date_of_admission_to_the_Union) The [Erie Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erie_Canal) brought increased commercial activity in the Great Lakes, and the invention of the self-scouring [steel plow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plough) by Illinoisan [John](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Deere_(inventor)) [Deere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Deere_(inventor)) turned the state's rich [prairie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prairie) into some of the world's most productive and valuable farmland, attracting [immigrant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration_to_the_United_States) farmers from [Germany,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_American) [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_American) and elsewhere. In the mid-19th century, the [Illinois and Michigan Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_and_Michigan_Canal) and a sprawling railroad network facilitated trade, commerce, and settlement, making the state a transportation hub for the nation[.\[6\]](#page-89-7) By 1900, the growth of industrial jobs in the northern cities and [coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal) mining in the central and southern areas attracted immigrants from [Eastern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Europe) and [Southern Europe.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Europe) Illinois became one of America's most industrialized states and remains a major manufacturing center[.\[7\]](#page-90-0) The [Great Migration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Migration_(African_American)) from the South established a large Black community, particularly in [Chicago,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago) which became a leading cultural, economic, and population center; its [metropolitan area,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_area) informally referred to as [Chicagoland,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_metropolitan_area) holds about 65% of the state's 12.8 million residents.

Two [World Heritage Sites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Heritage_Site) are in Illinois, the ancient [Cahokia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cahokia_Mounds) [Mounds,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cahokia_Mounds) and part of the [Wright architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_20th-Century_Architecture_of_Frank_Lloyd_Wright) site. A wide variety of [protected areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_protected_areas_of_Illinois) seek to conserve Illinois' natural and cultural resources. Major centers of learning include the [University of](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) [Chicago,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) [University of Illinois,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois) and [Northwestern University.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwestern_University) Three [U. S. presidents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_United_States) have been elected while residents of Illinois: [Abraham Lincoln,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Lincoln) [Ulysses S. Grant,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulysses_S._Grant) and [Barack Obama;](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama) additionally, [Ronald Reagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_Reagan) was born and raised in the state. Illinois honors Lincoln with its official state slogan Land of Lincoln. [\[8\]](#page-90-1)[\[9\]](#page-90-2) The state is the site of the [Abraham Lincoln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Lincoln_Presidential_Library_and_Museum) [Presidential Library and Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Lincoln_Presidential_Library_and_Museum) in Springfield and the future home of the [Barack Obama Presidential Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama_Presidential_Center) in Chicago.

Illinois has a highly diverse [economy,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Illinois) with the [global city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_city) of [Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago) in the northeast, major industrial and [agricultural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agricultural_productivity) hubs in the north and center, and [natural resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_resource) such as coal, timber, and petroleum in the south. Owing to its central location and favorable geography, the state is a major [transportation hub:](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transportation_hub) the [Port of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Chicago) has access to the [Atlantic Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Ocean) through the [Great Lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lakes) and [Saint Lawrence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Lawrence_Seaway) [Seaway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Lawrence_Seaway) and to the [Gulf of Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Mexico) from the [Mississippi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_River) via the [Illinois Waterway.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_Waterway) Chicago has been the nation's [railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railroad) hub since the 1860s[,\[10\]](#page-90-3) and its [O'Hare International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O%27Hare_International_Airport) has been among the world's busiest airports for decades. Illinois has long been considered a [microcosm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macrocosm_and_microcosm) of the United

States and a [bellwether](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellwether) in American culture, exemplified by the phrase [Will it play in Peoria?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Will_it_play_in_Peoria%3F). [\[11\]](#page-90-4)

## **Etymology**

"Illinois" is the modern spelling for the early [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language) [Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholicism) [missionaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missionary) and explorers' name for the [Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_Confederation) [Native](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Americans_in_the_United_States) [Americans,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Americans_in_the_United_States) a name that was spelled in many different ways in the early records[.\[12\]](#page-90-5)

American scholars previously thought the name Illinois meant 'man' or 'men' in the [Miami-Illinois language,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miami-Illinois_language) with the original iliniwek transformed via French into Illinois[.\[13\]](#page-90-6)[\[14\]](#page-90-7) This etymology is not supported by the Illinois language,[[citation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed) [needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed)] as the word for "man" is ireniwa, and plural of "man" is ireniwaki. The name [Illiniwek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illiniwek) has also been said to mean 'tribe of superior men'[,\[15\]](#page-91-0) which is a [false etymology.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/False_etymology) The name Illinois derives from the Miami-Illinois verb irenwe·wa 'he speaks the regular way'. This was taken into the [Ojibwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ojibwe_language) [language,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ojibwe_language) perhaps in the [Ottawa dialect,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottawa_dialect) and modified into ilinwe· (pluralized as ilinwe·k). The French borrowed these forms, spelling the /we/ ending as -ois, a [transliteration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transliteration) of that sound in the French of that time. The current spelling form, Illinois, began to appear in the early 1670s, when French colonists had settled in the western area. The Illinois's name for themselves, as attested in all three of the French missionaryperiod dictionaries of Illinois, was [Inoka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inoka), of unknown meaning and unrelated to the other terms[.\[16\]](#page-91-1)[\[17\]](#page-91-2)

## **History**

### **Pre-European**

## ![](_page_9_Picture_3.jpeg)

[Mississippian copper plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippian_copper_plate) found at the Saddle Site in [Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_County,_Illinois) [County, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_County,_Illinois)

[American Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas) of successive cultures lived along the waterways of the Illinois area for thousands of years before the arrival of Europeans. The [Koster Site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koster_Site) has been excavated and demonstrates 7,000 years of continuous habitation. [Cahokia,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cahokia) the largest regional [chiefdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiefdom) and [Urban Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_center) of the [Pre-](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-Columbian_era)[Columbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-Columbian_era) [Mississippian culture,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippian_culture) was located near present-day [Collinsville, Illinois.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collinsville,_Illinois) They built an urban complex of more than 100 [platform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platform_mound) and [burial mounds,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burial_mound) a 50-acre (20 ha) [plaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plaza) larger than 35 football fields[,\[18\]](#page-91-3) and a woodhenge of sacred cedar, all in a planned design expressing the culture's cosmology. [Monks Mound,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monks_Mound) the center of the site, is the largest Pre-Columbian structure north of the [Valley of Mexico.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valley_of_Mexico) It is 100 ft (30 m) high, 951 ft (290 m) long, 836 ft (255 m) wide, and covers 13.8 acres (5.6 ha)[.\[19\]](#page-91-4) It contains about 814,000 cu yd (622,000 m3) of earth[.\[20\]](#page-91-5) It was topped by a structure thought to have measured about 105 ft (32 m) in length and 48 ft (15 m) in width, covered an area 5,000 sq ft (460 m2), and been as much as 50 ft (15 m) high, making its peak 150 ft

(46 m) above the level of the plaza. The finely crafted ornaments and tools recovered by archaeologists at [Cahokia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cahokia) include elaborate ceramics, finely sculptured stonework, carefully embossed and engraved copper and [mica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mica) sheets, and one funeral blanket for an important chief fashioned from 20,000 shell beads. These artifacts indicate that Cahokia was truly an urban center, with clustered housing, markets, and specialists in toolmaking, hide dressing, potting, jewelry making, shell engraving, weaving and salt making.[\[21\]](#page-92-0)

The civilization vanished in the 15th century for unknown reasons, but historians and archeologists have speculated that the people depleted the area of resources. Many indigenous tribes engaged in constant warfare. According to Suzanne Austin Alchon, "At one site in the central [Illinois River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_River) valley, one third of all adults died as a result of violent injuries."[\[22\]](#page-92-1) The next major power in the region was the [Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_Confederation) [Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_Confederation) or Illini, a political alliance[.\[23\]](#page-92-2) Around the time of European contact in 1673, the Illinois confederation had an estimated population of over 10,000 people[.\[24\]](#page-92-3) As the Illini declined during the [Beaver Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beaver_Wars) era, members of the [Algonquian-](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algonquian_languages)speaking [Potawatomi,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potawatomi) [Miami,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miami_people) [Sauk,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sauk_people) and other tribes including the Fox [\(Meskwaki\)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meskwaki), [Iowa,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_people) [Kickapoo,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kickapoo_people) [Mascouten,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mascouten) [Piankeshaw,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piankeshaw) [Shawnee,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shawnee) [Wea,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wea) and Winnebago [\(Ho-](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho-Chunk)[Chunk\)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho-Chunk) came into the area from the east and north around the Great Lakes[.\[25\]](#page-92-4)[\[26\]](#page-92-5)

### **European exploration and settlement prior to 1800**

## ![](_page_10_Picture_5.jpeg)

## ![](_page_11_Picture_2.jpeg)

Illinois in 1718, approximate modern state area highlighted, from Carte de la Louisiane et du cours du Mississipi by [Guillaume de L'Isle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guillaume_de_L%27Isle)[\[27\]](#page-92-6)

French explorers [Jacques Marquette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Marquette) and [Louis Jolliet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Jolliet) explored the [Illinois River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_River) in 1673. Marquette soon after founded a mission at the [Grand Village of the Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Village_of_the_Illinois) in [Illinois Country.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_Country) In 1680, French explorers under [René-Robert Cavelier, Sieur de](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ren%C3%A9-Robert_Cavelier,_Sieur_de_La_Salle) [La Salle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ren%C3%A9-Robert_Cavelier,_Sieur_de_La_Salle) and [Henri de Tonti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_de_Tonti) constructed a fort at the site of present-day [Peoria,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peoria,_Illinois) and in 1682, a fort atop [Starved Rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starved_Rock) in today's Starved Rock State Park. French Empire [Canadiens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadiens) came south to settle particularly along the Mississippi River, and Illinois was part of first [New France,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_France) and then of [La](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Louisiane) [Louisiane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Louisiane) until 1763, when it passed to the British with their defeat of France in the [Seven Years' War.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Years%27_War) The small French settlements continued, although many French migrated west to [Ste. Genevieve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ste._Genevieve,_Missouri) and [St. Louis, Missouri,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis,_Missouri) to evade British rule. [\[28\]](#page-92-7)

A few British soldiers were posted in Illinois, but few British or American settlers moved there, as the Crown made it part of the territory reserved for Indians west of the Appalachians, and then part of the [British Province of Quebec.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Quebec_(1763%E2%80%931791)) In 1778, [George](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Rogers_Clark)

[Rogers Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Rogers_Clark) claimed [Illinois County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_County) for [Virginia.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony_of_Virginia) In a compromise, Virginia (and other states that made various claims) ceded the area to the new United States in the 1780s and it became part of the [Northwest Territory,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_Territory) administered by the federal government and later organized as states.[\[28\]](#page-92-7)

### **19th century**

### **Prior to statehood**

## ![](_page_12_Picture_5.jpeg)

The bell donated by King Louis XV in 1741 to the French mission at Kaskaskia. It was later called the "Liberty Bell of the West", after it was rung to celebrate U. S. victory in the Revolution

The [Illinois-Wabash Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois-Wabash_Company) was an early claimant to much of Illinois. The [Illinois Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_Territory) was created on February 3, 1809, with its capital at [Kaskaskia,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaskaskia,_Illinois) an early French settlement.

During the discussions leading up to Illinois's [admission to the](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admission_to_the_Union) [Union,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admission_to_the_Union) the proposed northern boundary of the state was moved twice[.\[29\]](#page-93-0) The original provisions of the [Northwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_Ordinance) [Ordinance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_Ordinance) had specified a boundary that would have been tangent to the southern tip of Lake Michigan. Such a boundary would have left Illinois with no shoreline on Lake Michigan at all. However, as Indiana had successfully been granted a 10 mi

(16 km) northern extension of its boundary to provide it with a usable lakefront, the original bill for Illinois statehood, submitted to Congress on January 23, 1818, stipulated a northern border at the same latitude as Indiana's, which is defined as 10 miles north of the southernmost extremity of Lake Michigan. However, the Illinois delegate, [Nathaniel Pope,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathaniel_Pope) wanted more, and lobbied to have the boundary moved further north. The final bill passed by Congress included an amendment to shift the border to 42° 30' north, which is approximately 51 mi (82 km) north of the Indiana northern border. This shift added 8,500 sq mi (22,000 km2) to the state, including the [lead mining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lead_mining) region near [Galena.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galena,_Illinois) More importantly, it added nearly 50 miles of Lake Michigan shoreline and the Chicago River. Pope and others envisioned a canal that would connect the Chicago and Illinois rivers and thus connect the Great Lakes to the Mississippi.

## **The State of Illinois prior to the Civil War**

The second [Governor of Illinois,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Illinois) [Edward Coles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Coles) brought his slaves from his home state of [Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia) to give them their [freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manumission) when they arrived in Illinois.

## ![](_page_14_Picture_4.jpeg)

[Old State Capitol:](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_State_Capitol_State_Historic_Site) Abraham Lincoln and other area legislators were instrumental in moving the state capitol to centrally located Springfield in 1839.

In 1818, Illinois became the 21st U. S. state. The capital remained at Kaskaskia, headquartered in a small building rented by the state. In 1819, [Vandalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vandalia,_Illinois) became the capital, and over the next 18 years, three separate buildings were built to serve successively as the capitol building. In 1837, the state legislators representing [Sangamon County,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangamon_County) under the leadership of state representative [Abraham Lincoln,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Lincoln) succeeded in having the capital moved to [Springfield,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Springfield,_Illinois) [\[30\]](#page-93-1) where a [fifth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_State_Capitol_State_Historic_Site_(Illinois)) [capitol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_State_Capitol_State_Historic_Site_(Illinois)) building was constructed. A [sixth capitol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_State_Capitol) building was erected in 1867, which continues to serve as the Illinois capitol today.

Though it was ostensibly a ["free state"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slave_and_free_states), there was nonetheless [slavery in Illinois.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_slavery_in_Illinois) The ethnic French had owned black slaves since the 1720s, and American settlers had already brought slaves into the area from [Kentucky.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kentucky) Slavery was nominally banned by the Northwest Ordinance, but that was not enforced for those already holding slaves. When Illinois became a state in 1818, the Ordinance no longer applied, and about 900 slaves were held in the state. As the southern part of the state, later known as "Egypt" or "Little Egypt"[,\[31\]](#page-93-2)[\[32\]](#page-93-3) was largely settled by migrants from the South, the section was hostile to free blacks. [Edward Coles,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Coles) the second Governor of Illinois who was born in Virginia, participated in a campaign to block extending existing slavery in Illinois after winning the [1822](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1822_Illinois_gubernatorial_election) [Illinois gubernatorial election.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1822_Illinois_gubernatorial_election) In 1824, state residents voted against making slavery legal by a vote of 6640 against to 4972 for[.\[33\]](#page-93-4)

Still, most residents opposed allowing free blacks as permanent residents. Some settlers brought in slaves seasonally or as house servants[.\[34\]](#page-93-5) The [Illinois Constitution of](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Illinois) [1848](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Illinois) was written with a provision for exclusionary laws to be passed. In 1853, [John A. Logan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_A._Logan) helped pass a law to prohibit all African Americans, including [freedmen,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedman) from settling in the state[.\[35\]](#page-93-6)

The [winter of 1830–1831](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Snow_Winter_of_1880%E2%80%931881) is called the "Winter of the Deep Snow"[;\[36\]](#page-93-7) a sudden, deep snowfall blanketed the state, making travel impossible for the rest of the winter, and many travelers perished. Several severe winters followed, including the "Winter of the Sudden Freeze". On December 20, 1836, a fast-moving cold front passed through, freezing puddles in minutes and killing many travelers who could not reach shelter. The adverse weather resulted in crop failures in the northern part of the state. The southern part of the state shipped food north, and this may have contributed to its name, ["Little](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Illinois) [Egypt"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Illinois), after the Biblical story of [Joseph in Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_(Genesis)) supplying grain to his brothers[.\[37\]](#page-93-8)

In 1832, the [Black Hawk War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Hawk_War) was fought in Illinois and presentday [Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin) between the United States and the [Sauk,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sauk_people) [Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meskwaki) [\(Meskwaki\),](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meskwaki) and [Kickapoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kickapoo_people) Indian tribes. It represents the end of Indian resistance to white settlement in the Chicago region. [\[38\]](#page-93-9) The Indians had been forced to leave their homes and move to [Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa) in 1831; when they attempted to return, they were attacked and eventually defeated by [U. S. militia.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Militia_(United_States)) The survivors were forced back to Iowa[.\[39\]](#page-94-0) By 1832, when the last Indian lands in Illinois were ceded to the United States, the indigenous population of the state had been reduced by infectious diseases, warfare, and [forced westward removal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_removal) to only one village with fewer than 300 inhabitants.[\[24\]](#page-92-3)

By 1839, the [Latter Day Saints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latter_Day_Saints) had founded a [utopian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utopian) city called [Nauvoo,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nauvoo,_Illinois) formerly called Commerce. Located in [Hancock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hancock_County,_Illinois) [County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hancock_County,_Illinois) along the [Mississippi River,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_River) Nauvoo flourished and, by 1844, briefly surpassed Chicago for the position of the state's largest city[.\[40\]](#page-94-1)[\[41\]](#page-94-2) But in that same year, the [Latter Day Saint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latter_Day_Saint_movement) [movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latter_Day_Saint_movement) founder, [Joseph Smith,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Smith) [was killed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_of_Joseph_Smith) in the [Carthage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carthage_Jail) [Jail,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carthage_Jail) about 30 miles away from Nauvoo. Following a [succession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Succession_crisis_(Latter_Day_Saints)) [crisis,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Succession_crisis_(Latter_Day_Saints)) [Brigham Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigham_Young) led most Latter Day Saints out of Illinois in a [mass exodus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mormon_pioneers) to present-day [Utah;](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utah) after close to six years of rapid development, Nauvoo quickly declined afterward.

After it was established in 1833, [Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago) gained prominence as a [Great Lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lakes) port, and then as an [Illinois and Michigan Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_and_Michigan_Canal) port after 1848, and as a rail hub soon afterward. By 1857,

Chicago was Illinois's largest city[.\[28\]](#page-92-7) With the tremendous growth of mines and factories in the state in the 19th century, Illinois was the ground for the formation of [labor unions in the](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labor_unions_in_the_United_States) [United States.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labor_unions_in_the_United_States)

In 1847, after lobbying by [Dorothea L. Dix,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorothea_Dix) Illinois became one of the first states to establish a system of state-supported treatment of mental illness and disabilities, replacing local [almshouses.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almshouse) Dix came into this effort after having met J. O. King, a [Jacksonville, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacksonville,_Illinois) businessman, who invited her to Illinois, where he had been working to build an asylum for the insane. With the lobbying expertise of Dix, plans for the Jacksonville State Hospital (now known as the [Jacksonville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacksonville_Developmental_Center) [Developmental Center\)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacksonville_Developmental_Center) were signed into law on March 1, 1847. [\[42\]](#page-94-3)

## **Civil War and after**

## ![](_page_17_Picture_5.jpeg)

[Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_(American_Civil_War)) troops embarking at [Cairo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cairo,_Illinois) on January 10, 1862

During the [American Civil War,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War) Illinois ranked fourth in soldiers who served (more than 250,000) in the [Union Army,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Army) a figure surpassed by only New York, [Pennsylvania,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania) and [Ohio.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio) Beginning with President [Abraham Lincoln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Lincoln)'s first call for troops and continuing throughout the war, Illinois mustered 150 infantry regiments, which were numbered from the 7th to the

156th regiments. Seventeen cavalry regiments were also gathered, as well as two light artillery regiments.[\[43\]](#page-94-4) The town of [Cairo,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cairo,_Illinois) at the southern tip of the state at the confluence of the Mississippi and Ohio Rivers, served as a strategically important supply base and training center for the [Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_(American_Civil_War)) army. For several months, both General [Grant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulysses_S._Grant) and Admiral [Foote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Hull_Foote) had headquarters in Cairo.

During the Civil War, and more so afterwards, Chicago's population skyrocketed, which increased its prominence. The [Pullman Strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pullman_Strike) and [Haymarket Riot,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haymarket_affair) in particular, greatly influenced the development of the American [labor movement.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_movement) From Sunday, October 8, 1871, until Tuesday, October 10, 1871, the [Great Chicago Fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Chicago_Fire) burned in downtown Chicago, destroying four sq mi (10 km2)[.\[44\]](#page-94-5)

## **20th century**

## ![](_page_18_Picture_5.jpeg)

Police and protesters at the [1968 Democratic National](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1968_Democratic_National_Convention) [Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1968_Democratic_National_Convention) in Chicago.

## ![](_page_18_Picture_7.jpeg)

## ![](_page_19_Picture_2.jpeg)

Rising waters in [Alton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alton,_Illinois) in 1993.

At the turn of the 20th century, Illinois had a population of nearly 5 million. Many people from other parts of the country were attracted to the state by employment caused by the expanding industrial base. Whites were 98% of the state's population[.\[45\]](#page-94-6) Bolstered by continued [immigration from](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_immigration_to_the_United_States) [southern and eastern Europe,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_immigration_to_the_United_States) and by the African-American [Great Migration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Migration_(African_American)) from the South, Illinois grew and emerged as one of the most important states in the union. By the end of the century, the population had reached 12.4 million.

The [Century of Progress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Century_of_Progress) [World's fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World%27s_fair) was held at Chicago in 1933. Oil strikes in [Marion County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marion_County,_Illinois) and [Crawford County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crawford_County,_Illinois) led to a boom in 1937, and by 1939, Illinois ranked fourth in U. S. oil production. Illinois manufactured 6.1 percent of total United States military armaments produced during [World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) II, ranking seventh among the 48 states[.\[46\]](#page-94-7) Chicago became an ocean port with the opening of the [Saint Lawrence Seaway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Lawrence_Seaway) in 1959. The seaway and the [Illinois Waterway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_Waterway) connected Chicago to both the Mississippi River and the [Atlantic Ocean.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Ocean) In 1960, [Ray Kroc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_Kroc) opened the first [Mc Donald's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mc Donald%27s) franchise in [Des](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Des_Plaines,_Illinois) [Plaines,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Des_Plaines,_Illinois) which was demolished in 1984[.\[47\]](#page-94-8) In 1985 a replica was built on the same site to recreate how the original one looked[.\[47\]](#page-94-8) Though this replica was demolished in 2017, due to repeated flooding of the building[.\[48\]](#page-95-0)[\[49\]](#page-95-1)

Illinois had a prominent role in the emergence of the [nuclear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_age) [age.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_age) In 1942, as part of the [Manhattan Project,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan_Project) the [University of](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Pile-1)

[Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Pile-1) conducted the first sustained [nuclear chain reaction.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_chain_reaction) In 1957, [Argonne National Laboratory,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argonne_National_Laboratory) near [Chicago,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago) activated the first experimental nuclear power generating system in the United States. By 1960, the first privately financed nuclear plant in the United States, [Dresden 1,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dresden_Nuclear_Power_Plant) was dedicated near [Morris.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morris,_Illinois) In 1967, [Fermilab,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermilab) a national nuclear research facility near [Batavia,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batavia,_Illinois) opened a [particle accelerator,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particle_accelerator) which was the world's largest for over 40 years. With eleven plants currently operating, Illinois leads all states in the amount of electricity generated from nuclear power[.\[50\]](#page-95-2)[\[51\]](#page-95-3)

In 1961, Illinois became the first state in the nation to adopt the recommendation of the [American Law Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Law_Institute) and pass a comprehensive criminal code revision that repealed the law against [sodomy.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodomy_laws_in_the_United_States) The code also abrogated [common law crimes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_law_crime) and established an [age of consent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_consent) of 18[.\[52\]](#page-95-4) The state's [fourth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_Constitution) [constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_Constitution) was adopted in 1970, replacing the 1870 document[.\[53\]](#page-95-5)

The first [Farm Aid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farm_Aid) concert was held in [Champaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Champaign,_Illinois) to benefit American farmers, in 1985. The worst [upper Mississippi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Mississippi_River) flood of the century, the [Great Flood of 1993,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Flood_of_1993) inundated many towns and thousands of acres of farmland[.\[28\]](#page-92-7)

## **Geology**

During the early part of the [Paleozoic Era,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleozoic_Era) the area that would one day become Illinois was submerged beneath a shallow sea and located near the Equator. Diverse marine life lived at this time, including [trilobites,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trilobite) [brachiopods,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brachiopod) and [crinoids.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crinoid) Changing environmental conditions led to the formation of large [coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal_forest) [swamps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal_forest) in the [Carboniferous.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carboniferous)

Illinois was above sea level for at least part of the [Mesozoic,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesozoic) but by its end was again submerged by the [Western Interior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Interior_Seaway) [Seaway.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Interior_Seaway) This receded by the [Eocene Epoch.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eocene)

During the [Pleistocene Epoch,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pleistocene_Epoch) vast ice sheets covered much of Illinois, with only the [Driftless Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Driftless_Area) remaining exposed. These [glaciers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glacier) carved the basin of [Lake Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Michigan) and left behind traces of ancient glacial lakes and [moraines.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moraine) [\[64\]](#page-97-1)

# **Geography**

Illinois is located in the [Midwest region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midwestern_United_States) of the United States and is one of the eight states in the [Great Lakes region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lakes_region_(North_America)) of North America (which also includes [Ontario,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontario) Canada).

## **Boundaries**

Illinois's eastern border with [Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana) consists of a north–south line at 87°31′ 30″ west longitude in [Lake Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Michigan) at the north, to the [Wabash River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wabash_River) in the south above [Post Vincennes.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vincennes,_Indiana) The Wabash River continues as the eastern/southeastern border with Indiana until the Wabash enters the [Ohio River.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_River) This marks the beginning of Illinois's southern border with [Kentucky,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kentucky) which runs along the northern shoreline of the Ohio River.[\[65\]](#page-97-2) Most of the western border with [Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri) and [Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa) is the [Mississippi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_River) [River;](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_River) Kaskaskia is an [exclave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclave) of Illinois, lying west of the Mississippi and reachable only from Missouri. The state's northern border with [Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin) is fixed at 42°30′ north latitude. The northeastern border of Illinois lies in [Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Michigan) [Michigan,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Michigan) within which Illinois shares a water boundary with the state of [Michigan,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan) as well as Wisconsin and Indiana[.\[25\]](#page-92-4)

## **Topography**

## ![](_page_23_Picture_4.jpeg)

[Charles Mound,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Mound) the highest natural point in Illinois at 1,235 feet (376 m) above sea level, is located in the [Driftless Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Driftless_Area) in the northwestern part of the state.

Though Illinois lies entirely in the [Interior Plains,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interior_Plains) it does have some minor variation in its elevation. In extreme northwestern Illinois, the [Driftless Area,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Driftless_Area) a region of unglaciated and therefore higher and more rugged topography, occupies a small part of the state. [Southern Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Illinois) includes the hilly areas around the [Shawnee National Forest.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shawnee_National_Forest)

[Charles Mound,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Mound) located in the Driftless region, has the state's highest natural elevation above sea level at 1,235 ft (376 m). Other highlands include the [Shawnee Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shawnee_Hills) in the south, and there is varying topography along its rivers; the [Illinois River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_River) bisects the state northeast to southwest. The floodplain on the Mississippi River from [Alton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alton,_Illinois) to the [Kaskaskia River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaskaskia_River) is known as the [American Bottom.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Bottom)

### **Divisions**

## ![](_page_24_Picture_4.jpeg)

At 279 feet (85 m) above sea level[,\[66\]](#page-97-3) the lowest elevation point in the state is located near [Cairo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cairo,_Illinois) and the confluence of the Ohio and Mississippi Rivers.

Illinois has three major geographical divisions. [Northern Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Illinois) is dominated by [Chicago metropolitan area,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_metropolitan_area) or Chicagoland, which is the city of Chicago and its suburbs, and the adjoining exurban area into which the metropolis is expanding. As defined by the federal government, the Chicago metro area includes several counties in Illinois, [Indiana,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana) and [Wisconsin,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin) and has a population of over 9.8 million. Chicago itself is a cosmopolitan city, densely populated, industrialized, the transportation hub of the nation, and settled by a wide variety of ethnic groups. The city of [Rockford,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockford,_Illinois) Illinois's third-largest city and center of the state's fourth largest metropolitan area, sits along Interstates [39](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_39) and [90](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_90) some 75 mi (121 km) northwest of

Chicago. The [Quad Cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quad_Cities) region, located along the [Mississippi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_River) [River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_River) in northern Illinois, had a population of 381,342 in 2011.

The midsection of Illinois is the second major division, called [Central Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Illinois). Historically [prairie,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prairie) it is now mainly agricultural and known as the Heart of Illinois. It is characterized by small towns and medium–small cities. The western section (west of the Illinois River) was originally part of the [Military Tract of 1812](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Tract_of_1812) and forms the conspicuous western bulge of the state. Agriculture, particularly [corn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize) and [soybeans,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soybean) as well as educational institutions and manufacturing centers, figure prominently in Central Illinois. Cities include [Peoria;](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peoria,_Illinois) [Springfield,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Springfield,_Illinois) the state capital; [Quincy;](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quincy,_Illinois) [Decatur;](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decatur,_Illinois) [Bloomington-Normal;](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomington-Normal,_Illinois) and [Champaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Champaign,_Illinois)[-Urbana.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urbana,_Illinois) [\[25\]](#page-92-4)

The third division is [Southern Illinois,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Illinois) comprising the area south of [U. S. Route 50,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U. S._Route_50_in_Illinois) including [Little Egypt,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Illinois) near the juncture of the [Mississippi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_River) and [Ohio River.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_River) Southern Illinois is the site of the ancient city of [Cahokia,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cahokia) as well as the site of the first state capital at [Kaskaskia,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaskaskia,_Illinois) which today is separated from the rest of the state by the Mississippi River[.\[25\]](#page-92-4)[\[67\]](#page-97-4) This region has a somewhat warmer winter climate, different variety of crops (including some cotton farming in the past), more rugged topography (due to the area remaining unglaciated during the [Illinoian Stage,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinoian_(stage)) unlike most of the rest of the state), as well as small-scale oil deposits and [coal mining.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal_mining) The Illinois suburbs of [St. Louis,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis,_Missouri) such as [East St. Louis,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_St._Louis) are located in this region, and collectively, they are known as the [Metro-East.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro-East) The other somewhat significant concentration of population in Southern Illinois is the Carbondale-Marion-Herrin, Illinois Combined

Statistical Area centered on [Carbondale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbondale,_Illinois) and [Marion,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marion,_Illinois) a twocounty area that is home to 123,272 residents[.\[25\]](#page-92-4) A portion of southeastern Illinois is part of the extended [Evansville, Indiana,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evansville,_Indiana) Metro Area, locally referred to as the Tri-State with Indiana and Kentucky. Seven Illinois counties are in the area.

In addition to these three, largely latitudinally defined divisions, all of the region outside the Chicago metropolitan area is often called ["downstate"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downstate_Illinois) Illinois. This term is flexible, but is generally meant to mean everything outside the influence of the Chicago area. Thus, some cities in Northern Illinois, such as [De Kalb,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De Kalb,_Illinois) which is west of Chicago, and [Rockford—](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockford,_Illinois)which is actually north of Chicago—are sometimes incorrectly considered to be 'downstate'.

### **Climate**

## ![](_page_26_Figure_5.jpeg)

[Köppen climate types](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) of Illinois

Illinois has a climate that varies widely throughout the year. Because of its nearly 400-mile distance between its northernmost and southernmost extremes, as well as its midcontinental situation, most of Illinois has a [humid continental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental_climate) [climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental_climate) [\(Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) Dfa), with hot, humid summers and cold winters. The southern part of the state, from about [Carbondale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbondale,_Illinois) southward, has a [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) (Koppen Cfa), with more moderate winters. Average yearly precipitation for Illinois varies from just over 48 in (1,219 mm) at the southern tip to around 35 in (889 mm) in the northern portion of the state. Normal annual snowfall exceeds 38 in (965 mm) in the Chicago area, while the southern portion of the state normally receives less than 14 in (356 mm).[\[68\]](#page-97-5) The all-time high temperature was 117 °F (47 °C), recorded on July 14, 1954, at [East St. Louis,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_St._Louis,_Illinois) and the all-time low temperature was −38 °F (−39 °C), recorded on January 31, 2019, during the [January 2019 North American cold wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/January_2019_North_American_cold_wave) at a weather station near [Mount Carroll,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Carroll,_Illinois) [\[69\]](#page-97-6)[\[70\]](#page-97-7) and confirmed on March 5, 2019[.\[71\]](#page-98-0) This followed the previous record of −36 °F (−38 °C) recorded on January 5, 1999, near [Congerville.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congerville,_Illinois) [\[71\]](#page-98-0) Prior to the Mount Carroll record, a temperature of −37 °F (−38 °C) was recorded on January 15, 2009, at [Rochelle,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rochelle,_Illinois) but at a weather station not subjected to the same quality control as official records.[\[72\]](#page-98-1)[\[73\]](#page-98-2) Illinois averages approximately 51 days of [thunderstorm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thunderstorm) activity a year, which ranks somewhat above average in the number of thunderstorm days for the United States. Illinois is vulnerable to tornadoes, with an average of 54 occurring annually, which puts much of the state at around five tornadoes per 10,000 sq mi (30,000 km2) annually[.\[74\]](#page-98-3) Some of [Tornado Alley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tornado_Alley)'s deadliest tornadoes on record have occurred in the state. The [Tri-State Tornado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tri-State_Tornado) of 1925 killed 695 people in three states; 613 of the victims died in Illinois.[\[75\]](#page-98-4)

## **Demographics**

The [United States Census Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Census_Bureau) found that the population of Illinois was 12,812,508 in the [2020 United States census,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census) moving from the fifth-largest state to the sixth-largest state (losing out to Pennsylvania). Illinois' population slightly declined in 2020 from the [2010 United States census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_United_States_census) by just over 18,000 residents and the overall population was quite higher than recent census estimates[.\[84\]](#page-99-4)

## Illinois 2020 Population Density Map

Illinois is the most populous state in the [Midwest region.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midwestern_United_States) Chicago, the [third-most populous city in the United States,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_cities_by_population) is the center of the [Chicago metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_metropolitan_area) or Chicagoland, as this area is nicknamed. Although the Chicago metropolitan area comprises only 9% of the land area of the state, it contains 65% of the state's residents, with 21.4% of Illinois' population living in the city of Chicago itself as of 2020.[\[85\]](#page-99-5) The losses of population anticipated from the 2020 census results do not arise from the Chicago metro area; rather the declines are from the Downstate counties[.\[86\]](#page-99-6) As of the 2020 census, the state's geographic mean [center of population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_of_population) is located at 41° 18′ 43″N 88° 22 23″W in [Grundy County,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grundy_County,_Illinois) about six miles northwest of [Coal City.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal_City,_Illinois) [\[87\]](#page-99-7)

Illinois is the most racially and ethnically diverse state in the Midwest. By several metrics, including racial and ethnic background, religious affiliation, and percentage of rural and urban divide, Illinois is the most representative of the larger [demography of the United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demography_of_the_United_States) States. Over half of this demographic also identified as being Hispanic or Latino.[\[106\]](#page-102-0)

### **Religion**

#### **Christianity**

Roman Catholics constitute the single largest religious denomination in Illinois; they are heavily concentrated in and around Chicago and account for nearly 30% of the state's population[.\[145\]](#page-106-1) However, taken together as a group, the various Protestant denominations comprise a greater percentage of the state's population than do Catholics. In 2010, Catholics in Illinois numbered 3,648,907. The largest Protestant denominations were the [United Methodist Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Methodist_Church) with 314,461 members and the [Southern Baptist Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Baptist_Convention) with 283,519. Illinois has one of the largest concentrations of [Missouri Synod Lutherans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_Synod_Lutherans) in the United States.

Illinois played an important role in the early [Latter Day Saint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latter_Day_Saint_movement) [movement,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latter_Day_Saint_movement) with Nauvoo becoming a gathering place for Mormons in the early 1840s. Nauvoo was the location of the [succession crisis,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Succession_crisis_(Latter_Day_Saints)) which led to the separation of the Mormon movement into [several Latter Day Saint sects.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_denominations_in_the_Latter_Day_Saint_movement) [The Church of](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Church_of_Jesus_Christ_of_Latter-day_Saints) [Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Church_of_Jesus_Christ_of_Latter-day_Saints) the largest of the sects to emerge from the Mormon schism, has more than 55,000

adherents in Illinois today[.\[146\]](#page-106-2)

## **Other Abrahamic religious communities**

The Bahá'[í House of Worship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bah%C3%A1%CA%BC%C3%AD_House_of_Worship_(Wilmette,_Illinois)) in [Wilmette, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilmette,_Illinois)

The largest and oldest surviving Bahá'[í House of Worship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bah%C3%A1%CA%BC%C3%AD_House_of_Worship_(Wilmette,_Illinois)) in the world is located on the shores of [Lake Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Michigan) in [Wilmette,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilmette,_Illinois) [Illinois,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilmette,_Illinois) one of eight continental Bahá'[í House of Worship.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bah%C3%A1%CA%BC%C3%AD_House_of_Worship) [\[150\]](#page-106-6) It serves as a space for people of all backgrounds and religions to gather, meditate, reflect, and pray, expressing the [Bahá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bah%C3%A1%CA%BC%C3%AD_Faith)'í principle of the [oneness of religions.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bah%C3%A1%CA%BC%C3%AD_Faith_and_the_unity_of_religion) [\[151\]](#page-107-0) The Chicago area has a very large Jewish community, particularly in the suburbs of [Skokie,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skokie,_Illinois) [Buffalo Grove,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffalo_Grove,_Illinois) [Highland Park,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highland_Park,_Illinois) and surrounding suburbs.

Former Chicago Mayor [Rahm Emanuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rahm_Emanuel) was the Windy City's first Jewish mayor.

### **Agriculture**

## ![](_page_52_Picture_12.jpeg)

A [John Deere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Deere) [combine harvester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combine_harvester) on an Illinois farm; the company is headquartered in [Moline, Illinois.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moline,_Illinois)

Illinois's major agricultural outputs are [corn,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize) [soybeans,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soybean) [hogs,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pig) [cattle,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cattle) [dairy products,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dairy_product) and wheat. In most years, Illinois is either the first or second state for the highest production of soybeans, with a harvest of 427.7 million bushels (11.64 million [metric tons\)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonne) in 2008, after Iowa's production of 444.82 million bushels (12.11 million metric tons).[\[155\]](#page-107-4) Illinois ranks second in U. S. corn production with more than 1.5 billion bushels produced annually[.\[156\]](#page-107-5) With a production capacity of 1.5 billion gallons per year, Illinois is a top producer of ethanol, ranking third in the United States in 2011[.\[157\]](#page-107-6) Illinois is a leader in food manufacturing and meat processing.[\[158\]](#page-108-0) Although Chicago may no longer be ["Hog Butcher for the](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_(poem)) [World"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_(poem)), the Chicago area remains a global center for [food](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_manufacturers_of_Chicago) [manufacture and meat processing,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_manufacturers_of_Chicago) [\[158\]](#page-108-0) with many plants, processing houses, and distribution facilities concentrated in the area of the former [Union Stock Yards.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Stock_Yards) [\[159\]](#page-108-1) Illinois also produces [wine,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_wine) and the state is home to two [American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_viticultural_area) [viticultural areas.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_viticultural_area) In the area of The Meeting of the Great Rivers Scenic Byway, peaches and apples are grown. The German immigrants from agricultural backgrounds who settled in Illinois in the mid- to late 19th century are in part responsible for the profusion of fruit orchards in that area of Illinois.[\[160\]](#page-108-2) Illinois's universities are actively researching alternative agricultural products as alternative crops.

### **Manufacturing**

Illinois is one of the nation's manufacturing leaders, boasting annual value added productivity by manufacturing of over \$107 billion in 2006. As of 2011, Illinois is ranked as the 4thmost productive manufacturing state in the country, behind California, Texas, and Ohio[.\[161\]](#page-108-3) About three-quarters of the state's manufacturers are located in the Northeastern Opportunity Return Region, with 38 percent of Illinois's approximately 18,900 manufacturing plants located in Cook County. As of 2006, the leading manufacturing industries in Illinois, based upon value-added, were chemical manufacturing (\$18.3 billion), machinery manufacturing (\$13.4 billion), food manufacturing (\$12.9 billion), fabricated metal products (\$11.5 billion), transportation equipment (\$7.4 billion), plastics and rubber products (\$7.0 billion), and computer and electronic products (\$6.1 billion)[.\[162\]](#page-108-4)

## **Coal**

## ![](_page_55_Figure_8.jpeg)

### Location of the [Illinois Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_Basin)

The coal industry of Illinois has its origins in the middle 19th century, when entrepreneurs such as [Jacob Loose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Loose) discovered coal in locations such as [Sangamon County.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangamon_County) [Jacob Bunn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Bunn) contributed to the development of the Illinois coal industry and was a founder and owner of the [Western Coal & Mining](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Western_Coal_%26_Mining_Company&action=edit&redlink=1) [Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Western_Coal_%26_Mining_Company&action=edit&redlink=1) of Illinois. About 68% of Illinois has coal-bearing strata of the [Pennsylvanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvanian_(geology)) geologic period. According to the Illinois State Geological Survey, 211 billion tons of [bituminous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bituminous_coal) [coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bituminous_coal) are estimated to lie under the surface, having a total heating value greater than the estimated oil deposits in the [Arabian Peninsula.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabian_Peninsula) [\[164\]](#page-108-6) However, this coal has a high [sulfur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulfur) content, which causes [acid rain,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acid_rain) unless special equipment is used to reduce [sulfur dioxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulfur_dioxide) [emissions.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_pollution) [\[25\]](#page-92-4)[\[28\]](#page-92-7)[\[67\]](#page-97-4) Many Illinois [power plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_station) are not equipped to burn high-sulfur coal. In 1999, Illinois produced 40.4 million tons of coal, but only 17 million tons (42%) of Illinois coal was consumed in Illinois. Most of the coal produced in Illinois is exported to other states and countries. In 2008, Illinois exported three million tons of coal and was projected to export nine million in 2011, as demand for energy grows in places such as China, India, and elsewhere in Asia and Europe[.\[165\]](#page-108-7) As of 2010, Illinois was ranked third in recoverable coal reserves at producing mines in the nation[.\[157\]](#page-107-6) Most of the coal produced in Illinois is exported to other states, while much of the coal burned for power in Illinois (21 million tons in 1998) is mined in the [Powder River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Powder_River_Basin) [Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Powder_River_Basin) of [Wyoming.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wyoming) [\[163\]](#page-108-5)

[Mattoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mattoon,_Illinois) was chosen as the site for the [Department of Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Energy)'s [Future Gen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Future Gen) project, a 275-megawatt experimental [zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero_emission) [emission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero_emission) coal-burning power plant that the DOE just gave a second round of funding. In 2010, after a number of setbacks, the city of Mattoon backed out of the project[.\[166\]](#page-108-8)

### **Petroleum**

Illinois is a leading refiner of petroleum in the American [Midwest,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midwestern_United_States) with a combined crude oil distillation capacity of nearly 900,000 bbl/d (140,000 m3/d). However, Illinois has very limited crude oil proved reserves that account for less than 1% of the U. S. total reserves. Residential heating is 81% natural gas compared to less than 1% [heating oil.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heating_oil) Illinois is ranked 14th in [oil production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_oil-producing_states#North_America) among states, with a daily output of approximately 28,000 bbl (4,500 m3) in 2005[.\[167\]](#page-109-0)[\[168\]](#page-109-1)

## **Nuclear power**

## ![](_page_57_Picture_6.jpeg)

[Byron Nuclear Generating Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byron_Nuclear_Generating_Station) in [Ogle County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ogle_County,_Illinois)

[Nuclear power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_power) arguably began in Illinois with the [Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Pile-1) [Pile-1,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Pile-1) the world's first artificial self-sustaining [nuclear chain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_chain_reaction) [reaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_chain_reaction) in the world's first [nuclear reactor,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_reactor_technology) built on the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) campus. There are six operating [nuclear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_power_plant) [power plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_power_plant) in Illinois: [Braidwood,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braidwood_Nuclear_Generating_Station) [Byron,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byron_Nuclear_Generating_Station) [Clinton,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinton_Nuclear_Generating_Station) [Dresden,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dresden_Nuclear_Power_Plant)

[La Salle,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La Salle_County_Generating_Station) and [Quad Cities.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quad_Cities_Nuclear_Generating_Station) [\[169\]](#page-109-2) With the exception of the singleunit Clinton plant, each of these facilities has two reactors. Three reactors have been permanently shut down and are in various stages of decommissioning: [Dresden-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dresden-1) and [Zion-1 and](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zion_Nuclear_Power_Station) [2.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zion_Nuclear_Power_Station) Illinois ranked first in the nation in 2010 in both nuclear capacity and nuclear generation. Generation from its nuclear power plants accounted for 12 percent of the nation's total. [\[157\]](#page-107-6) In 2007, 48% of Illinois's electricity was generated using nuclear power[.\[170\]](#page-109-3) The [Morris Operation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morris_Operation) is the only de facto high-level [radioactive waste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radioactive_waste) storage site in the United States.

#### **Wind power**

## ![](_page_58_Figure_4.jpeg)

Average annual wind power distribution for Illinois, 50 m (160 ft) height above ground (2009)

Illinois has seen growing interest in the use of [wind power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_power) for electrical generation[.\[171\]](#page-109-4) Most of Illinois was rated in 2009 as "marginal or fair" for wind energy production by the [U. S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U. S._Department_of_Energy) [Department of Energy,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U. S._Department_of_Energy) with some western sections rated "good" and parts of the south rated "poor"[.\[172\]](#page-109-5) These ratings are for wind turbines with 50 m (160 ft) hub heights; newer wind turbines are taller, enabling them to reach [stronger winds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_profile_power_law) [farther from the ground.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_profile_power_law) As a result, more areas of Illinois have become prospective wind farm sites. ## **Biofuels**

Illinois is ranked second in [corn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize) production among U. S. states, and Illinois corn is used to produce 40% of the [ethanol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethanol) consumed in the United States[.\[156\]](#page-107-5) The [Archer Daniels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archer_Daniels_Midland) [Midland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archer_Daniels_Midland) corporation in [Decatur, Illinois,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decatur,_Illinois) is the world's leading producer of ethanol from corn.

The National Corn-to-Ethanol Research Center (NCERC), the world's only facility dedicated to researching the ways and means of converting corn (maize) to ethanol is located on the campus of [Southern Illinois University Edwardsville.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Illinois_University_Edwardsville) [\[177\]](#page-110-1)[\[178\]](#page-110-2)

[University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign) is one of the partners in the [Energy Biosciences Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_Biosciences_Institute) (EBI), a \$500 million biofuels research project funded by petroleum giant [BP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BP). #### **Taxes**

Tax is collected by the [Illinois Department of Revenue.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_Department_of_Revenue) State [income tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Income_tax) is calculated by multiplying [net income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Net_income) by a [flat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flat_fee) [rate.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flat_fee) In 1990, that rate was set at 3%, but in 2010, the General Assembly voted for a temporary increase in the rate to 5%; the new rate went into effect on January 1, 2011; the personal income rate partially sunset on January 1, 2015, to 3.75%, while the corporate income tax fell to 5.25%[.\[181\]](#page-110-5)[\[182\]](#page-110-6) Illinois failed to pass a budget from 2015 to 2017, after the 736-day [budget impasse,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_Budget_Impasse) a budget was passed in Illinois after lawmakers overturned Governor Bruce Rauner's veto; this budget raised the personal income rate to 4.95% and the corporate rate to 7%[.\[183\]](#page-110-7) There are two rates for state [sales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sales_tax) [tax:](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sales_tax) 6.25% for general merchandise and 1% for qualifying food, drugs, and medical appliances[.\[184\]](#page-111-0) The [property tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Property_tax) is a major source of tax revenue for local government taxing districts. The property tax is a local—not state—tax imposed by local government taxing districts, which include counties, [townships,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_township) municipalities, [school districts,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_district) and special taxation districts. The property tax in Illinois is imposed only on [real](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_property) [property.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_property) [\[25\]](#page-92-4)[\[28\]](#page-92-7)[\[67\]](#page-97-4)

On May 1, 2019, the Illinois Senate voted to approve a [constitutional amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_Fair_Tax) that would have stricken language from the [Illinois Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Illinois) requiring a flat state income tax, in a 73–44 vote. If approved, the amendment would have allowed the state legislature to impose a graduated income tax based on annual income. The governor, [J. B. Pritzker,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._B._Pritzker) approved the bill on May 27, 2019. It was scheduled for a 2020 general election ballot vot[e\[185\]](#page-111-1)[\[186\]](#page-111-2) and required 60 percent voter approval to effectively amend the state constitution.[\[187\]](#page-111-3) The amendment was not approved by Illinoisans, with 55.1% of voters voting "No" on approval and 44.9% voting "Yes".[\[188\]](#page-111-4) As of 2017 Chicago had the highest state and local sales tax rate for a U. S. city with a populations above 200,000, at 10.250%[.\[189\]](#page-111-5) The state of Illinois has the second highest rate of real estate tax: 2.31%, which is second only to New Jersey at 2.44%[.\[190\]](#page-111-6)

[Toll roads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toll_road) are a de facto user tax on the citizens and visitors to the state of Illinois. Illinois ranks seventh out of the 11 states with the most miles of toll roads, at 282.1 miles. Chicago ranks fourth in most expensive toll roads in America by the mile, with the [Chicago Skyway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Skyway) charging 51.2 cents per mile[.\[191\]](#page-111-7) Illinois also has the 11th highest gasoline tax by state, at 37.5 cents per gallon[.\[192\]](#page-112-0)

## **Museums**

Illinois has numerous museums; the greatest concentration of these are in Chicago. Several museums in Chicago are ranked as some of the best in the world. These include the [John G.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shedd_Aquarium) [Shedd Aquarium,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shedd_Aquarium) the [Field Museum of Natural History,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_Museum_of_Natural_History) the [Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Institute_of_Chicago) [Institute of Chicago,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Institute_of_Chicago) the [Adler Planetarium,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adler_Planetarium) and the [Museum of](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Science_and_Industry_(Chicago)) [Science and Industry.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Science_and_Industry_(Chicago))

The modern [Abraham Lincoln Presidential Library and Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Lincoln_Presidential_Library_and_Museum) in Springfield is the largest and most attended [presidential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_library_system) [library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_library_system) in the country. The Illinois State Museum boasts a collection of 13.5 million objects that tell the story of Illinois life, land, people, and art. The ISM is among only 5% of the nation's museums that are accredited by the American Alliance of Museums. Other historical museums in the state include the [Polish Museum of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Museum_of_America) in [Chicago;](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago) [Magnolia Manor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnolia_Manor_(Cairo,_Illinois)) in [Cairo;](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cairo,_Illinois) [Easley Pioneer Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Easley_Pioneer_Museum) in [Ipava;](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ipava,_Illinois) the [Elihu Benjamin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elihu_Benjamin_Washburne_House) [Washburne;](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elihu_Benjamin_Washburne_House) [Ulysses S. Grant Homes,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulysses_S._Grant_Home) both in [Galena;](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galena,_Illinois) and the Chanute Air Museum, located on the former Chanute Air Force Base in Rantoul.

The Chicago metropolitan area also hosts two zoos: The [Brookfield Zoo,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brookfield_Zoo) located about ten miles west of the city center in suburban [Brookfield,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brookfield,_Illinois) contains more than 2,300 animals and covers 216 acres (87 ha). The [Lincoln Park Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln_Park_Zoo) is located in [Lincoln Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln_Park) on Chicago's North Side, approximately 3 miles (4.8 km) north of the [Loop.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Chicago) The zoo accounts for more than 35 acres (14 ha) of the park.

## **Music**

Illinois is a leader in music education, having hosted the [Midwest Clinic International Band and Orchestra Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midwest_Clinic) since 1946, as well being home to the Illinois Music Educators Association (ILMEA, formerly IMEA), one of the largest professional music educator's organizations in the country. Each summer since 2004, [Southern Illinois University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Illinois_University_Carbondale) [Carbondale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Illinois_University_Carbondale) has played host to the Southern Illinois Music Festival, which presents dozens of performances throughout the region. Past featured artists include the [Eroica Trio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eroica_Trio) and violinist [David Kim.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Kim_(violinist))

[Chicago,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago) in the northeast corner of the state, is a [major center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Chicago) [for music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Chicago)[\[193\]](#page-112-1) in the [midwestern United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midwestern_United_States) where distinctive forms of blues (greatly responsible for the future creation of [rock and roll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_and_roll)), and [house music,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_music) a genre of electronic dance music, were developed.

The [Great Migration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Migration_(African_American)) of poor black workers from the South into the industrial cities brought traditional [jazz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz) and [blues music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blues_music) to the city, resulting in [Chicago blues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_blues) and "Chicago-style" [Dixieland jazz.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dixieland_jazz) Notable blues artists included [Muddy Waters,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muddy_Waters) [Junior Wells,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junior_Wells) [Howlin' Wolf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howlin%27_Wolf) and both [Sonny Boy Williamsons;](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonny_Boy_Williamson_II) jazz greats included [Nat King Cole,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nat_King_Cole) [Gene Ammons,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene_Ammons) [Benny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benny_Goodman) [Goodman,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benny_Goodman) and [Bud Freeman.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bud_Freeman) Chicago is also well known for its [soul music.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soul_music)

In the early 1930s, [Gospel music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gospel_music) began to gain popularity in Chicago due to [Thomas A. Dorsey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_A._Dorsey)'s contributions at [Pilgrim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilgrim_Baptist_Church) [Baptist Church.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilgrim_Baptist_Church)

In the 1980s and 1990s, [heavy rock,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_rock) [punk,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punk_rock) and [hip hop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hip_hop_music) also became popular in Chicago. [Orchestras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestra) in Chicago include the [Chicago Symphony Orchestra,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Symphony_Orchestra) the [Lyric Opera of Chicago,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyric_Opera_of_Chicago) and the [Chicago Sinfonietta.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Sinfonietta) [\[194\]](#page-112-2)

## **State government**

## ![](_page_70_Picture_6.jpeg)

The [Illinois State Capitol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_State_Capitol) in [Springfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Springfield,_Illinois)

The [government of Illinois,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Illinois) under the [Constitution of Illinois,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Illinois) has three branches of government: executive, legislative, and judicial. The executive branch is split into several statewide elected offices, with the governor as chief executive. Legislative functions are granted to the Illinois General Assembly. The judiciary is composed of the Supreme Court and lower courts.

The executive branch is composed of six elected officers and their offices as well as numerous other departments.[\[200\]](#page-112-8) The six elected officers are[:\[200\]](#page-112-8) [Governor,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Illinois) [Lieutenant Governor,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant_Governor_of_Illinois) [Attorney General,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_Attorney_General) [Secretary of State,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_Secretary_of_State) [Comptroller,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_Comptroller) and [Treasurer.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_Treasurer) The government of Illinois has numerous departments, agencies, boards and commissions, but the socalled code departments provide most of the state's services. [\[200\]](#page-112-8)[\[201\]](#page-113-0)

![](_page_71_Picture_3.jpeg)

[Illinois House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_House_of_Representatives)

The [Illinois General Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_General_Assembly) is the state legislature, composed of the 118-member [Illinois House of](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_House_of_Representatives) [Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_House_of_Representatives) and the 59-member [Illinois Senate.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_Senate) The members of the General Assembly are elected at the beginning of each even-numbered year. The [Illinois Compiled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_Compiled_Statutes) [Statutes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_Compiled_Statutes) (ILCS) are the [codified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Codification_(law)) statutes of a general and permanent nature[.\[202\]](#page-113-1)[\[203\]](#page-113-2)

The [Judiciary of Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary_of_Illinois) is the unified court system of Illinois. It consists of the [Supreme Court,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Illinois) [Appellate Court,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_Appellate_Court) and [Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_Circuit_Courts) [Courts.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_Circuit_Courts) The Supreme Court oversees the administration of the court system.

The [administrative divisions of Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_Illinois) are counties, townships, precincts, cities, towns, villages, and special-purpose districts. [\[204\]](#page-113-3) The basic subdivision of Illinois are the 102 counties[.\[205\]](#page-113-4) Eighty-five of the 102 counties are in turn divided into townships and precincts[.\[205\]](#page-113-4)[\[206\]](#page-113-5) Municipal governments are the cities, villages, and incorporated towns.[\[205\]](#page-113-4) Some localities possess [home rule,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_rule) which allows them to govern themselves to a certain extent[.\[207\]](#page-113-6)

### **Party balance**

## ![](_page_72_Picture_4.jpeg)

Governor [J. B. Pritzker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._B._Pritzker) (D)

In modern national and state politics, Illinois is a [Democratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_states_and_blue_states) [stronghold.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_states_and_blue_states) [\[208\]](#page-113-7) Historically, Illinois was a political [swing state,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swing_state) with near-parity existing between the [Republican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Party_(United_States)) and the [Democratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_(United_States)) parties. However, in recent elections, the Democratic Party has gained ground, and Illinois has come to be seen as a solid ["blue" state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_states_and_blue_states) in both presidential and congressional campaigns[.\[209\]](#page-114-0)[\[210\]](#page-114-1) Illinois's Democratic tendencies are mostly attributable to [Cook County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cook_County,_Illinois) and [Chicago,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago) by far the state's largest county and city, respectively, which have long been strongly Democratic. The [collar counties,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collar_counties) affluent suburban counties that surround Cook County, were ancestrally Republican and helped keep the state competitive; however, they have swung toward the left in recent elections as the national Republican Party has become increasingly conservative, which has cemented Democratic dominance in state politics[.\[211\]](#page-114-2) [Outside of the Chicago metropolitan area,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downstate_Illinois) the state's rural areas are heavily Republican. The dominance of the Chicago area in state elections is so overwhelming that it has influenced a [secessionist movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separation_referendums_in_Illinois) in the downstate region[.\[212\]](#page-114-3)

Illinois was long seen as a national bellwether,[\[213\]](#page-114-4) supporting the winner in every election in the 20th century, except for [1916](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1916_United_States_presidential_election_in_Illinois) and [1976.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976_United_States_presidential_election_in_Illinois) Since the [1992 election,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_United_States_presidential_election) however, Illinois has trended more toward the Democratic Party and is part of the ["blue wall"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_wall_(U. S._politics)) of states that have consistently voted Democratic in the last six presidential elections. In [2000,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_United_States_presidential_election_in_Illinois) [George W. Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_W._Bush) became the first Republican to win the presidency without carrying either Illinois or [Vermont,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vermont) with [Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump) repeating the feat in 2016. Illinois has not elected a Republican to the [Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate) since [Mark Kirk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Kirk) won in [2010;](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_United_States_Senate_elections_in_Illinois) the last Republicans to hold statewide office were Governor [Bruce Rauner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Rauner) and Lieutenant Governor [Evelyn Sanguinetti,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evelyn_Sanguinetti) who both left office in 2019.

#### **History of corruption**

Politics in the state have been infamous for highly visible corruption cases, as well as for crusading reformers, such as governors [Adlai Stevenson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adlai_Stevenson_II) and [James R. Thompson.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_R._Thompson) In 2006, former governor [George Ryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Ryan) was convicted of racketeering and bribery, leading to a six-and-a-half-year prison sentence. On December 7, 2011, former governor [Rod Blagojevich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rod_Blagojevich) was sentenced to 14 years in prison for allegations that he conspired to sell the vacated Senate seat left by President [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama) to the highest bidder. Blagojevich had earlier been impeached and convicted by the legislature, resulting in his removal from office. In the late 20th century, Congressman [Dan Rostenkowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Rostenkowski) was imprisoned for mail fraud; former governor and federal judge [Otto Kerner, Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Kerner,_Jr.) was imprisoned for bribery; Secretary of State [Paul Powell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Powell_(politician)) was investigated and found to have gained great wealth through bribes, and State Auditor of Public Accounts (Comptroller) [Orville Hodge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orville_Hodge) was imprisoned for embezzlement. In 1912, William Lorimer, the GOP boss of Chicago, was expelled from the U. S. Senate for bribery, and in 1921, Governor [Len Small](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Len_Small) was found to have defrauded the state of a million dollars[.\[28\]](#page-92-7)[\[67\]](#page-97-4)[\[214\]](#page-114-5)

### **U. S. presidential elections**

Illinois has shown a strong presence in presidential elections. Three presidents have claimed Illinois as their political base when running for president: [Abraham Lincoln,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Lincoln) [Ulysses S. Grant,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulysses_S._Grant) and most recently [Barack Obama.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama) Lincoln was born in [Kentucky,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kentucky) but he moved to Illinois at age 21. He served in the [General Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_General_Assembly) and represented the [7th congressional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois%27s_7th_congressional_district) [district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois%27s_7th_congressional_district) in the [U. S. House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_House_of_Representatives) before his election to the presidency in 1860. [Ulysses S. Grant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulysses_S._Grant) was born in [Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio) and had a military career that precluded settling down, but on the eve of the [Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War) and approaching middle age, he moved to Illinois and thus utilized the state as his home and political base when running for president. [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama) was born in [Hawaii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawaii) and made Illinois his home after graduating from [law school,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_school) and later represented Illinois in the [U. S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate)

[Senate.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate) He then became president in 2008, running as a candidate from his Illinois base.

[Ronald Reagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_Reagan) was born in Illinois, in the city of [Tampico,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tampico,_Illinois) raised in [Dixon, Illinois,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dixon,_Illinois) and educated at [Eureka College,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eureka_College) outside [Peoria.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peoria,_Illinois) Reagan later moved to [California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California) during his young adulthood. He then became an actor, and later became [California's Governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_California) before being elected president.

[Hillary Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillary_Clinton) was born and raised in the suburbs of Chicago and became the first woman to represent a major political party in the general election of the U. S. presidency. Clinton ran from a platform based in [New York State.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_(state))

#### **Illinois State Board of education**

The Illinois State Board of Education (ISBE) is autonomous of the governor and the state legislature, and administers [public](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_education) [education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_education) in the state. Local municipalities and their respective [school districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_district) operate individual public schools, but the ISBE audits performance of public schools with the [Illinois School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_School_Report_Card) [Report Card.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_School_Report_Card) The ISBE also makes recommendations to state leaders concerning education spending and policies.

### **Primary and secondary schools**

Education is compulsory for ages 7–17 in Illinois. Schools are commonly, but not exclusively, divided into three tiers of primary and secondary education: elementary school, [middle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_school) [school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_school) or [junior high school,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_school) and high school. District territories are often complex in structure. Many areas in the state are actually located in two school districts—one for high school and the other for elementary and middle schools. And such districts do not necessarily share boundaries. A given high school may have several elementary districts that feed into it, yet some of those feeder districts may themselves feed into multiple high school districts.

### **Colleges and universities**

Using the criterion established by [the Carnegie Foundation for](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Carnegie_Foundation_for_the_Advancement_of_Teaching) [the Advancement of Teaching,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Carnegie_Foundation_for_the_Advancement_of_Teaching) there are eleven "National Universities" in the state.

As of 19 August 2010, six of these rank in the "first tier" among the top 500 National Universities in the nation, as determined by the [U. S. News & World Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U. The University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign is often ranked among the best engineering schools in the world and United States.

Illinois also has more than twenty additional [accredited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Educational_accreditation) fouryear universities, both public and private, and dozens of small [liberal arts colleges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_arts_college) across the state. Additionally, Illinois supports 49 public [community colleges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_college) in the [Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_Community_College_System) [Community College System.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_Community_College_System)

### **School financing**

Schools in Illinois are funded primarily by property taxes, based on state assessment of property values, rather than direct state contributions. Scholar Tracy Steffes has described Illinois public education as historically "inequitable", a system where one of "the wealthiest of states" is "the stingiest in its support for education". There have been several attempts to reform school funding in Illinois. The most notable attempt came in 1973 with the adoption of the Illinois Resource Equalizer Formula, a measure through which it was hoped funding could be collected and distributed to Illinois schools more equitably. However, opposition from affluent Illinois communities who objected to having to pay for the less well-off school districts (many of them Black majority communities, produced by redlining, white flight, and other "soft" segregation methods) resulted in the formula's abolition in the late 1980s.[\[221\]](#page-115-1)

Because of its central location and its proximity to the [Rust Belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rust_Belt)

and [Grain Belt,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grain_Belt) Illinois is a national crossroads for air, auto, rail, and truck traffic.

### **Airports**

## ![](_page_81_Picture_4.jpeg)

Concourse B inside [O'Hare International Airport,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O%27Hare_International_Airport) one of the [world's busiest airports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_busiest_airports_by_passenger_traffic)

From 1962 until 1998, Chicago's [O'Hare International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O%27Hare_International_Airport) (ORD) was the busiest airport in the world, measured both in terms of total flights and passengers. While it was surpassed by [Atlanta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlanta)'s [Hartsfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hartsfield%E2%80%93Jackson_Atlanta_International_Airport) in 1998 (as Chicago splits its air traffic between O'Hare and Midway airports, while Atlanta uses only one airport), with 59.3 million domestic passengers annually, along with 11.4 million international passengers in 2008,[\[222\]](#page-115-2) O'Hare consistently remains one of the two or three busiest airports globally, and in some years still ranks number one in total flights. It is a major [hub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airline_hub) for both [United Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Airlines) and [American Airlines,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Airlines) and a major airport expansion project is currently underway. [Midway Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Midway_International_Airport) (MDW), which had been the busiest airport in the world at one point until it was supplanted by O'Hare as the busiest airport in 1962, is now the secondary airport in the Chicago metropolitan area and still ranks as one of the nation's busiest airports. Midway is a major hub for [Southwest Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwest_Airlines) and services many other carriers as well. Midway served 17.3 million domestic and international passengers in 2008[.\[223\]](#page-115-3)

## **Railroads**

## ![](_page_87_Figure_13.jpeg)

## Illinois major rail network

Illinois has an extensive passenger and freight rail transportation network. Chicago is a national [Amtrak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amtrak) hub and in-state passengers are served by Amtrak's [Illinois Service,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_Service)

featuring the Chicago to Carbondale [Illini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illini_(Amtrak)) and [Saluki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saluki_(Amtrak)), the Chicago to Quincy [Carl Sandburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_Zephyr_and_Carl_Sandburg) and [Illinois Zephyr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_Zephyr_and_Carl_Sandburg), and the Chicago to St. Louis [Lincoln Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln_Service). Currently there is trackwork on the Chicago–St. Louis line to bring the maximum speed up to 110 mph (180 km/h), which would reduce the trip time by an hour and a half. Nearly every North American railway meets at Chicago (including all six [Class I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railroad_classes) railroads), making it the largest and most active rail hub in the country. Extensive heavy rail service is provided in the city proper and some immediate suburbs by the [Chicago Transit Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Transit_Authority)'s ['L'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_%22L%22) system. One of the largest suburban commuter rail system in the United States, operated by [Metra,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metra) uses existing rail lines to provide direct commuter rail access for hundreds of suburbs to the city and beyond.

### **Waterways**

In addition to the state's rail lines, the [Mississippi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_River) and [Illinois River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_River) provide major transportation routes for the state's agricultural interests. [Lake Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Michigan) gives Illinois access to the Atlantic Ocean by way of the [Saint Lawrence Seaway.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Lawrence_Seaway)

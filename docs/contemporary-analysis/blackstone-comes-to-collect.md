# Blackstone Comes to Collect

*How America's Largest Landlord and Wall Street's Highest Paid CEO Are Jacking Up Rents and Ramping Up Evictions*

This report from the Private Equity Stakeholder Project (PESP) and Alliance of Californians for Community Empowerment (ACCE) documents Blackstone's aggressive rental practices following the pandemic, focusing on San Diego County.

## Portfolio Scale

Blackstone operates as **"the largest landlord in the U.S."**, managing:

- Over **300,000 rental units** nationwide
- Expanded by more than **200,000 units** in recent years
- Continues aggressive acquisition strategy

## Rent Increases Far Exceeding Legal Limits

At San Diego's Fashion Hills complex, Blackstone raised rents dramatically after acquiring units in 2021:

| Unit Type | Increase |
|-----------|----------|
| One-bedroom | **43%** above prior averages |
| Two-bedroom | **64%** above prior averages |

These increases **substantially exceeded California's 10% annual rent increase cap** under AB 1482.

California's rent control law only protects existing tenants. By displacing residents and bringing in new tenants, Blackstone can reset rents to any level—creating a financial incentive for eviction.

## Post-Moratorium Eviction Surge

Following the August 2022 end of its voluntary eviction moratorium, Blackstone immediately resumed aggressive eviction practices:

- Filed **over 350 evictions in Florida** between August and November 2022 alone
- Extrapolating this pattern suggests **thousands of potential filings nationally** during that period
- Filed evictions against tenants owing just **one month's rent**

## Future Rent Projections

Blackstone's Americas real estate leadership estimated capacity to increase rents an additional **20% beyond existing rates** as of December 2022.

This projection reveals the company's strategy: maximize rental income extraction regardless of tenant affordability.

## Political Opposition to Tenant Protections

Blackstone has actively fought against rent control and tenant protections:

- Invested **over $7 million in 2018** opposing California rent control ballot initiative
- Invested **over $7 million in 2020** opposing similar measure
- Used investor capital from public pension funds and UC rather than corporate accounts

The company profits from housing scarcity while actively preventing policies that would address it.

## The Affordability Impact

The report documents how Blackstone's aggressive pricing strategy:

1. **Reduces naturally occurring affordable housing stock**
2. **Intensifies housing affordability crises** in affected regions
3. **Displaces long-term residents** from their communities
4. **Transfers wealth from tenants to investors**

## Recommendations

The report urges Blackstone to:

1. **Limit annual rent increases to 3%**
2. **Provide relocation assistance** to displaced tenants
3. **Cease opposing rent-control efforts**
4. **Recognize tenant organizations** and negotiate collectively
5. **Work with behind-on-rent tenants** through affordable payment plans

## Implications for Tenant Organizing

This report demonstrates:

- **Corporate landlords prioritize extraction over stability.** Profit motives drive decisions, not tenant welfare.

- **Legal limits are insufficient.** Rent control caps only protect existing tenants; turnover allows unlimited increases.

- **Eviction is a business strategy.** Quick evictions maximize rent resets and maintain investment returns.

- **Political power matters.** Blackstone's millions in political spending defeat tenant protections at the ballot.

- **Tenant unions are essential.** Only organized collective power can counter corporate landlord practices.

## From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

## ![](_page_0_Picture_7.jpeg)

"**The Bonnie Blue Flag**", also known as "**We Are a Band of Brothers**", is an 1861 [marching song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marching_song) associated with the

[Confederate States of America.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederate_States_of_America) The words were written by the entertainer [Harry Mc Carthy,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Mc Carthy) with the melody taken from the song ["The Irish Jaunting Car"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Irish_Jaunting_Car). The song's title refers to the unofficial first [flag of the Confederacy,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flags_of_the_Confederate_States_of_America) the [Bonnie Blue Flag.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonnie_Blue_Flag) The left flag on the sheet-music is the Bonnie Blue Flag.

The song was premiered by lyricist Harry Mc Carthy during a concert in [Jackson, Mississippi,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackson,_Mississippi) in the spring of 1861 and performed again in September of that same year at the New Orleans Academy of Music for the First Texas Volunteer Infantry regiment mustering in celebration.[[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed)]

The [New Orleans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Orleans) music publishing house of A. E. Blackmar issued six editions of "The Bonnie Blue Flag" between 1861 and 1864 along with three additional arrangements.

The "band of brothers" mentioned in the first line of the song recalls the well known [St. Crispin's Day Speech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Crispin%27s_Day_Speech) in [William](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Shakespeare) [Shakespeare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Shakespeare)'s play [Henry V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_V_(play)) (Act IV, scene ii).

The first verse of the song goes:

We are a band of brothers, and native to the soil, Fighting for our liberty with treasure, blood, and toil; And when our rights were threatened, the cry rose near and far,

Hurrah! for the Bonnie Blue Flag, that bears a single star.

These lyrics appear in a version held by the [Library of](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Library_of_Congress) [Congress.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Library_of_Congress) [\[1\]](#page-14-0) It was published by [A. E. Blackmar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armand_Blackmar) and Brother in [New Orleans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Orleans) in 1861. The second line is sometimes given as "fighting for the property we gained by honest toil." [University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_San_Diego) [of San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_San_Diego) professor Steve Schoenher[r\[2\]](#page-14-1) and the library of [Duke University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_University)[\[3\]](#page-14-2) record the "property" version which also has a publication date of 1861. When Major General [Benjamin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Butler_(politician)) [Butler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Butler_(politician)) [captured New Orleans,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capture_of_New_Orleans) he allegedly arrested Blackmar, [\[4\]](#page-14-3) fined him \$500, [destroyed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_burning) all copies of the music, and ordered that anyone caught whistling or singing "The Bonnie Blue Flag" would be fined \$25 (roughly \$500 in the 2010s[\[5\]\)](#page-14-4). Eleven other editions of the song were published with different lyrics.[[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed)]

[Annie Chambers Ketchum,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annie_Chambers_Ketchum) a Confederate widow who risked her liberty to publish new verses to be sung, published a new version of the song under the title "The Gathering Song."[[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed)] The following verses were published in a

#### eulogy by Gilberta S. Whittle in the 1904 Richmond Times Dispatch: [\[6\]](#page-14-5)

## **Historical inaccuracies**

## [\[edit\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=The_Bonnie_Blue_Flag&action=edit§ion=3)

The song is a useful mnemonic for the list of states that seceded, although for reasons of meter the third verse rearranges the order of secession. The actual dates on which the states seceded are as follows:

- 1. [South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Carolina) (December 20, 1860)
- 2. [Mississippi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi) (January 9, 1861)
- 3. [Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida) (January 10, 1861)
- 4. [Alabama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alabama) (January 11, 1861)
- 5. [Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_(U. S._state)) (January 19, 1861)
- 6. [Louisiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana) (January 26, 1861)

- 7. [Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas) (February 1, 1861)
- 8. [Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia) (April 17, 1861)
- 9. [Arkansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas) (May 6, 1861)
- 10. [North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina) (May 20, 1861)
- 11. [Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennessee) (June 8, 1861[\)\[10\]](#page-15-2)

Thus, [Alabama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alabama) took [South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Carolina) by the hand only figuratively but actually delayed her [secession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secession) until the departure of [Mississippi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi) and [Florida.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida)

As with many songs from the time of the American Civil War, this song had multiple versions for both the Union and Confederate sides. One Union version, written by [J. L. Geddes,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Lorraine_Geddes) in 1863[,\[11\]](#page-15-3)[\[12\]](#page-15-4) a [British-](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_of_Great_Britain_and_Ireland)born colonel who immigrated to the U. S., was called "The Bonnie Flag With the Stripes and Stars". Singing of [Unionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_(American_Civil_War)) and equality, it went:

We're fighting for our [Union,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_(American_Civil_War)) We're fighting for our trust, We're fighting for that happy land Where sleeps our father dust. It cannot be dissevered, Though it cost us bloody wars, We never can give up the land Where floats the stripes and stars.

# J[oule heating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Main_Page)

Joule heating (also known as resistive, resistance, or Ohmic heating) is the process by which the passage of an [electric current](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_current) through a [conductor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conductor_(material)) produces [heat.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heat)

Joule's first law (also just Joule's law), also known in countries of the former [USSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) as the Joule–Lenz law, [\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-BSE-1) states that the [power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_(physics)) of heating generated by an [electrical conductor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_conductor) equals the product of its [resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_resistance_and_conductance) and the square of the current. Joule heating affects the whole electric conductor, unlike the [Peltier e](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peltier_effect)ffect which transfers heat from one electrical junction to another.

## ![](_page_0_Figure_6.jpeg)

A coiled [heating element](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heating_element) from an electric toaster, showing red to yellow [incandescence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incandescence)

Joule-heating or resistive-heating is used in multiple devices and industrial process. The part that converts electricity into heat is called a [heating element.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heating_element)

Among the many practical uses are:

- An [incandescent light bulb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incandescent_light_bulb) glows when the filament is heated by Joule heating, due to [thermal radiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal_radiation) (also called [blackbody radiation\)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackbody_radiation).
- [Electric fuses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuse_(electrical)) are used as a safety, breaking the circuit by melting if enough current flows to melt them.
- [Electronic cigarettes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_cigarette) vaporize propylene glycol and vegetable glycerine by Joule heating.
- Multiple heating devices use Joule heating, such as [electric stoves,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_stove) [electric heaters,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_heating) [soldering irons,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soldering_iron) [cartridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartridge_heater) [heaters.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartridge_heater)
- Some [food processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_processing) equipment may make use of Joule heating: running current through food material (which behave as an electrical resistor) causes heat release inside the food.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-2) The alternating electrical current coupled with the resistance of the food causes the generation of heat.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:0-3) A higher resistance increases the heat generated. Ohmic heating allows for fast and uniform heating of food products, which maintains quality. Products with particulates heat up faster (compared to conventional heat processing) due to higher resistance.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:1-4)

## History

[James Prescott Joule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Prescott_Joule) first published in December 1840, an abstract in the [Proceedings of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proceedings_of_the_Royal_Society), suggesting that heat could be generated by an electrical current. Joule immersed a length of wire in a fixed [mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass) of [water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water) and measured the [temperature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temperature) rise due to a known current flowing through the wire for a 30 [minute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minute) period. By varying the current and the length of the wire he deduced that the heat produced was [proportional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proportionality_(mathematics)) to the [square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_(algebra)) of the current multiplied by the [electrical resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_resistance) of the immersed wire[.\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-APS-5)

In 1841 and 1842, subsequent experiments showed that the amount of heat generated was proportional to the [chemical energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_energy) used in the [voltaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voltaic_pile) [pile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voltaic_pile) that generated the template. This led Joule to reject the [caloric theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caloric_theory) (at that time the dominant theory) in favor of the [mechanical theory of](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanical_theory_of_heat) [heat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanical_theory_of_heat) (according to which heat is another form of [energy\)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy)[.\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-APS-5)

Resistive heating was independently studied by [Heinrich Lenz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_Lenz) in 1842[.\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-BSE-1)

The [SI unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SI_unit) of [energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy) was subsequently named the [joule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule) and given the symbol J. The commonly known unit of power, the [watt,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watt) is equivalent to one joule per second.

### Microscopic description

Joule heating is caused by interactions between [charge carriers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charge_carrier) (usually [electrons\)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrons) and the body of the conductor.

A [potential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_potential) difference [\(voltage\)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voltage) between two points of a conductor creates an [electric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_field) field that accelerates charge carriers in the direction of the electric field, giving them [kinetic energy.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinetic_energy) When the charged particles collide with the quasi-particles in the conductor (i.e. the canonically quantized, ionic lattice oscillations in the harmonic approximation of a crystal), energy is being transferred from the electrons to the lattice (by the creation of further lattice oscillations). The oscillations of the ions are the origin of the radiation (["thermal energy"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal_energy)) that one measures in a typical experiment.

### Power loss and noise

Joule heating is referred to as ohmic heating or resistive heating because of its relationship to [Ohm's Law.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohm%27s_law) It forms the basis for the large number of practical applications involving [electric heating.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_heating) However, in applications where heating is an unwanted [by-product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/By-product) of current use (e.g., [load losses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Load_loss) in [electrical transformers\)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_transformer) the diversion of energy is often referred to as resistive loss. The use of [high voltages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_voltage) in [electric power transmission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_power_transmission) systems is specifically designed to reduce such losses in cabling by operating with commensurately lower currents. The [ring circuits,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_circuit) or ring mains, used in UK homes are another example, where power is delivered to outlets at lower currents (per wire, by using two paths in parallel), thus reducing Joule heating in the wires. Joule heating does not occur in [superconducting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconductivity) materials, as these materials have zero electrical resistance in the superconducting state.

Resistors create electrical noise, called [Johnson–Nyquist noise.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnson%E2%80%93Nyquist_noise) There is an intimate relationship between Johnson–Nyquist noise and Joule heating, explained by the fl[uctuation-dissipation theorem.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluctuation-dissipation_theorem)

#### Direct current

The most fundamental formula for Joule heating is the generalized power equation:

## $$P = I(V_A - V_B)$$

where

- is the [power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_power) (energy per unit time) converted from electrical energy to thermal energy,
- is the current travelling through the resistor or other element,
- is the [voltage drop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voltage_drop) across the element.

The explanation of this formula ( ) is:[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-meier-6)

(Energy dissipated per unit time) = (Charge passing through resistor per unit time) × (Energy dissipated per charge passing through resistor)

Assuming the element behaves as a perfect resistor and that the power is completely converted into heat, the formula can be re-written by substituting [Ohm's law,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohm%27s_law) , into the generalized power equation:

## $$P = IV = I^2R = V^2/R$$

where R is the [resistance.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_resistance_and_conductance)

Voltage can be increased in DC circuits by connecting batteries or solar panels in series.

#### Electricity transmission

[Overhead power lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overhead_power_line) transfer electrical energy from electricity producers to consumers. Those power lines have a nonzero resistance and therefore are subject to Joule heating, which causes transmission losses.

The split of power between transmission losses (Joule heating in transmission lines) and load (useful energy delivered to the consumer) can be approximated by a [voltage divider.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voltage_divider) In order to minimize transmission losses, the resistance of the lines has to be as small as possible compared to the load (resistance of consumer appliances). Line resistance is minimized by the use of [copper conductors,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper_conductor) but the resistance and [power supply](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_supply) specifications of consumer appliances are fixed.

Usually, a [transformer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transformer) is placed between the lines and consumption. When a high-voltage, low-intensity current in the primary circuit (before the transformer) is converted into a low-voltage, high-intensity current in the secondary circuit (after the transformer), the equivalent resistance of the secondary circuit becomes higher[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-7) and transmission losses are reduced in proportion.

During the [war of currents,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_currents) [AC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternating_current) installations could use transformers to reduce line losses by Joule heating, at the cost of higher voltage in the transmission lines, compared to [DC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_current) installations.

### Applications

#### Food processing

Joule heating is a fl[ash pasteurization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flash_pasteurization) (also called "high-temperature short-time" (HTST)) aseptic process that runs an alternating current of 50– 60 Hz through food.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:2-8) Heat is generated through the food's electrical resistance[.\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:2-8)[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:48-9)[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:010-10)[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:113-11) As the product heats, electrical conductivity increases linearly.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:0-3) A higher electrical current frequency is best as it reduces oxidation and metallic contamination[.\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:2-8) This heating method is best for foods that contain particulates suspended in a weak salt-containing medium due to their high resistance properties.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:1-4)[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:2-8)

Heat is generated rapidly and uniformly in the liquid matrix as well as in [particulates,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particulates) producing a higher quality sterile product that is suitable for [aseptic processing.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aseptic_processing)[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:113-11)[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:24-12)

Electrical energy is linearly translated to thermal energy as [electrical conductivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_conductivity) increases, and this is the key process parameter that affects heating uniformity and heating rate[.\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:113-11) This heating method is best for foods that contain particulates suspended in a weak salt containing medium due to their high [resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_resistance_and_conductance) properties.[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:010-10) Ohmic heating is beneficial due to its ability to inactivate [microorganisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microorganism) through thermal and non-thermal cellular damage.[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:113-11)[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:36-13)[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:52-14)

## General process for joule heating in food

This method can also inactivate [antinutritional factors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antinutritional_factor) thereby maintaining nutritional and [sensory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organoleptic) [properties.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organoleptic)[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:36-13) However, ohmic heating is limited by [viscosity,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viscosity) electrical conductivity, and [fouling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fouling) deposits.

[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:48-9)[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:010-10)[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:113-11) Although ohmic heating has not yet been approved by the Food and Drug Administration [\(FDA\)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_and_Drug_Administration) for commercial use, this method has many potential applications, ranging from [cooking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooking) to [fermentation.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermentation_in_food_processing)[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:113-11)

There are different configurations for continuous ohmic heating systems, but in the most basic process[,\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:113-11) a power supply or generator is needed to produce electrical current.[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:010-10) [Electrodes,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrode) in direct contact with food, pass electric current through the matrix[.\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:010-10) The distance between the electrodes can be adjusted to achieve the optimum electrical field strength[.\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:010-10)

The generator creates the electrical current which flows to the first electrode and passes through the food product placed in the electrode gap[.\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:010-10) The food product resists the flow of current causing internal heating[.\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:113-11) The current continues to flow to the second electrode and back to the power source to close the circuit.[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:010-10) The [insulator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insulator_(electricity)) caps around the electrodes controls the environment within the system[.\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:010-10)

The [electrical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_field) field strength and the [residence time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Residence_time) are the key process parameters which affect heat generation[.\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:113-11)

The ideal foods for ohmic heating are viscous with particulates[.\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:113-11)

- Thick soups
- Sauces
- Stews
- Salsa
- Fruit in a syrup medium
- Milk
- Ice cream mix
- Egg

- Whey
- Heat sensitive liquids
- Soymilk

The efficiency by which electricity is converted to heat depends upon on salt, water, and fat content due to their [thermal conductivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal_conductivity) and resistance factors[.\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:36-13) In particulate foods, the particles heat up faster than the liquid matrix due to higher resistance to electricity and matching conductivity can contribute to uniform heating.[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:113-11) This prevents overheating of the liquid matrix while particles receive sufficient heat processing[.\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:48-9) Table 1 shows the electrical conductivity values of certain foods to display the effect of composition and salt concentration.[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:113-11) The high electrical conductivity values represent a larger number of [ionic compounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ionic_compound) suspended in the product, which is directly proportional to the rate of heating. [\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:010-10) This value is increased in the presence of [polar compounds,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_polarity) like acids and salts, but decreased with [nonpolar compounds,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonpolar) like fats[.\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:010-10) Electrical conductivity of food materials generally increases with temperature, and can change if there are structural changes caused during heating such as [gelatinization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starch_gelatinization) of starch.[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:113-11) Density, pH, and specific heat of various components in a food matrix can also influence heating rate[.\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:36-13)

Benefits of Ohmic heating include: uniform and rapid heating (>1°Cs<sup>−</sup>1), less cooking time, better [energy e](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Efficient_energy_use)fficiency, lower capital cost, and heating simulataneously throughout food's volume as compared to [aseptic processing,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aseptic_processing) [canning,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canning) and [PEF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulsed_electric_field_processing)[.\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:24-12) Volumetric heating allows internal heating instead of transferring heat from a secondary medium[.\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:48-9) This results in the production of safe, high quality food with minimal changes to structural, nutritional, and [organoleptic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organoleptic) properties of food[.\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:48-9) Heat transfer is uniform to reach areas of food that are harder to heat[.\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:113-11) Less fouling accumulates on the electrodes as compared to other heating methods.[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:010-10) Ohmic heating also requires less cleaning and maintenance, resulting in an environmentally cautious heating method[.\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:48-9)[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:113-11)[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:24-12)

Microbial inactivation in ohmic heating is achieved by both thermal and non-thermal cellular damage from the electrical field.[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:52-14) This method destroys [microorganisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microorganism) due to [electroporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electroporation) of [cell membranes,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_membrane) physical membrane rupture, and [cell lysis.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_lysis)[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:113-11)[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:36-13) In electroporation, excessive leakage of [ions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ion) and [intramolecular components](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organelle) results in cell death[.\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:36-13) In membrane rupture, cells swell due to an increase in moisture diffusion across the cell membrane[.\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:24-12) Pronounced disruption and decomposition of cell walls and cytoplasmic membranes causes cells to lyse[.\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:113-11)[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:36-13)[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:52-14)

Decreased processing times in ohmic heating maintains nutritional and sensory properties of foods.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:48-9) Ohmic heating inactivates [antinutritional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antinutrient) factors like [lipoxigenase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lipoxygenase) (LOX), [polyphenoloxidase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyphenol_oxidase) (PPO), and [pectinase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pectinase) due to the removal of active metallic groups in enzymes by the electrical field[.\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:36-13) Similar to other heating methods, ohmic heating causes [gelatinization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starch_gelatinization) of starches, melting of fats, and [protein agglutination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agglutination_(biology))[.\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:113-11) Watersoluble nutrients are maintained in the suspension liquid allowing for no loss of nutritional value if the liquid is consumed[.\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-15)

Ohmic heating is limited by viscosity, electrical conductivity, and fouling deposits[.\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:48-9)[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:010-10)[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:113-11) The density of particles within the suspension liquid can limit the degree of processing. A higher viscosity fluid will provide more resistance to heating, allowing the mixture to heat up quicker than low viscosity products[.\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:113-11) A food product's electrical conductivity is a function of temperature, frequency, and product composition[.\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:48-9)[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:010-10)[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:113-11) This may be increased by adding ionic compounds, or decreased by adding non-polar constituents.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:48-9) Changes in electrical conductivity limit ohmic heating as it is difficult to model the thermal process when temperature increases in multi-component foods[.\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:48-9)[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:010-10)

The potential applications of ohmic heating range from cooking, thawing, [blanching,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blanching_(cooking)) peeling, evaporation, extraction, [dehydration,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dehydration_(food)) and fermentation.[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:113-11) These allow for ohmic heating to pasteurize particulate foods for hot filling, pre-heat products prior to canning, and aseptically process ready-to-eat meals and refrigerated foods.[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:010-10) Prospective examples are outlined in Table 2 as this food processing method has not been commercially approved by the FDA[.\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:010-10) Since there is currently insufficient data on electrical conductivities for solid foods, it is difficult to prove the high quality and safe process design for ohmic heating.[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:6-16) Additionally, a successful [12D reduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D-value_(microbiology)) for [C. botulinum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clostridium_botulinum) prevention has yet to be validated[.\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-:6-16)

#### Materials synthesis, recovery and processing

Flash joule heating (transient high-temperature electrothermal heating) has been used to synthesize [allotropes of carbon,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allotropes_of_carbon) including graphene and diamond. Heating various solid carbon feedstocks (carbon black, coal, coffee grounds, etc.) to temperatures of ~3000 K for 10-150 milliseconds produces [turbostratic graphene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graphene#Turbostratic) flakes.[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-17) FJH has also been used to [recover rare-earth elements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rare-earth_element#Recycling_and_reusing_REEs) used in modern electronics [from industrial wastes.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circular_economy) [\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-18)[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-19) Beginning from a fluorinated carbon source, fluorinated activated carbon, fluorinated [nanodiamond,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanodiamond) concentric carbon (carbon shell around a nanodiamond core), and fluorinated flash graphene can be synthesized[.\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-20)[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating#cite_note-21)

## Heating efficiency

Heat is not to be confused with [internal energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_energy) or synonymously [thermal energy.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal_energy) While intimately connected to [heat,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heat) they are distinct physical quantities.

As a heating technology, Joule heating has a coeffi[cient of performance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coefficient_of_performance) of 1.0, meaning that every joule of electrical energy supplied produces one joule of heat. In contrast, a [heat pump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heat_pump) can have a coefficient of more than 1.0 since it moves additional thermal energy from the environment to the heated item.

The definition of the efficiency of a heating process requires defining the boundaries of the system to be considered. When heating a building, the overall efficiency is different when considering heating effect per unit of electric energy delivered on the customer's side of the meter, compared to the overall efficiency when also considering the losses in the power plant and transmission of power.

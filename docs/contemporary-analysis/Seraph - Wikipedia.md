# **Seraph**

A **seraph** [\(Hebrew:](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language) שָׂרָף , [romanized:](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Hebrew) *sārāf* [/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English)[ˈ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English)[s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English)[ɛ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English)r[ə](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English)[f/;](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English) pl.: [Hebrew:](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language) שְׂרָפִםי , [romanized:](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Hebrew) *sərāfīm* [/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English)[ˈ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English)[s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English)[ɛ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English)r[ə](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English)[f](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English)[ɪ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English)[m/\)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English)[\[a\]](#page-8-0) is a [celestial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angelic_being) or [heavenly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavenly_being) [being](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavenly_being) originating in [Ancient Judaism.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Judaism) The term plays a role in subsequent [Judaism,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judaism) [Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam) and [Christianity.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity)

Tradition places seraphim in the highest rank in [Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_angelology) [angelology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_angelology) and in the fifth rank of ten in the [Jewish angelic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angels_in_Judaism) [hierarchy.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angels_in_Judaism) A seminal passage in the [Book of Isaiah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Isaiah) [\(Isaiah 6:1–8\)](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Bible_(King_James)/Isaiah#6:1) used the term to describe six-winged beings that fly around the [Throne of God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Throne_of_God) crying ["holy, holy, holy"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trisagion). This throne scene, with its triple invocation of holiness, profoundly influenced subsequent [theology,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theology) literature and art. Its influence is frequently seen in works depicting angels, heaven and [apotheosis.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apotheosis) Seraphim are mentioned as celestial beings in the semi-canonical [Book of](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Enoch) [Enoch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Enoch) and the [canonical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bible_canon) [Book of Revelation.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Revelation)

### **Origins and development**

In Hebrew, the word *saraph* means "burning", and is used seven times throughout the text of the Hebrew Bible as a noun, usually to denote ["serpent"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serpents_in_the_Bible)[,\[4\]](#page-9-0) twice in the [Book of Numbers,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Numbers)[\[5\]](#page-9-1)[\[6\]](#page-9-2) once in the [Book of Deuteronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Deuteronomy)[,\[7\]](#page-9-3) and four times in the [Book of](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Isaiah) [Isaiah.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Isaiah)[\[8\]](#page-9-4)[\[9\]](#page-9-5)[\[10\]](#page-9-6)[\[11\]](#page-9-7) The reason why the word for "burning" was also used to denote a serpent is not universally agreed upon; it may be due to a certain snake species' fiery colors, or perhaps the burning sensation left by its venomous bite. Regardless, its plural form, *seraphim*, occurs in both Numbers and Isaiah, but only in Isaiah is it used to denote an angelic being; likewise, these angels are referred to *only* as the plural *seraphim* – Isaiah later uses the singular *saraph* to describe a "fiery fl[ying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiery_flying_serpent) *[serpent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiery_flying_serpent)*", in line with the other uses of the term throughout the [Tanakh.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_Bible)

### **In Judaism**

The 12th-century scholar [Maimonides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maimonides) placed the seraphim in the fifth of ten ranks of angels in his exposition of the [Jewish angelic hierarchy.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_angelic_hierarchy) In [Kabbalah,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabbalah) the seraphim are the higher angels of the World of [Beriah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beri%27ah) ("Creation", first created realm, divine understanding),[\[29\]](#page-10-8) whose understanding of their distance from the absolute divinity of [Atziluth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atziluth) causes their continual "burning up" in [self-nulli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayin_and_Yesh)fication. Through this they ascend to God, and return to their place. Below them in the World of [Yetzirah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yetzirah) ("Formation", archetypal creation, divine emotions) are the [Hayot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hayot) angels of [Ezekiel's vision,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ezekiel_1#The_Vision_of_the_Throne-Chariot_(1:4-28)) who serve God with self-aware instinctive emotions ("face of a lion, ox, eagle"). Seraphim are part of the angelarchy of modern [Orthodox Judaism.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthodox_Judaism) Isaiah's vision is repeated several times in daily [Jewish services,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_services) including at [Kedushah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kedushah_(prayer)) prayer as part of the repetition of the [Amidah,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amidah) and in several other prayers as well. [Conservative Judaism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Judaism) retains the traditional doctrines regarding angels and includes references to them in the liturgy, although a literal belief in angels is by no means universal among adherents. Adherents of [Reform Judaism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reform_Judaism) and [Reconstructionist Judaism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reconstructionist_Judaism) generally take images of angels as symbolic.

A Judean seal from the 8th century BCE depicts them as flying [asp \(snake\),](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asp_(snake)) yet having human characteristics, as encountered by Isaiah in his commissioning as a prophet.[\[30\]](#page-10-9)

### **In Christianity**

[Medieval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval) Christian [theology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theology) places seraphim in the highest choir of the angelic hierarchy. They are the caretakers of [God'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/God)s throne, continuously singing "holy, holy, holy". [Pseudo-Dionysius the Areopagite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudo-Dionysius_the_Areopagite) in his *[Celestial Hierarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Coelesti_Hierarchia)* (vii), drew upon the [Book of Isaiah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Isaiah) in fixing the fiery nature of seraphim in the medieval imagination. Seraphim in his view helped God maintain perfect order and are not limited to chanting the *[trisagion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trisagion)*. Taking his cue as well from writings in the Rabbinic tradition, the author gave an etymology for the *Seraphim* as "those who kindle or make hot"

The name seraphim clearly indicates their ceaseless and eternal revolution about Divine Principles, their heat and keenness, the exuberance of their intense, perpetual, tireless activity, and their elevative and energetic assimilation of those below, kindling them and firing them to their own heat, and wholly purifying them by a burning and all-consuming flame; and by the unhidden, unquenchable, changeless, radiant and enlightening power, dispelling and destroying the shadows of darknes[s\[31\]](#page-10-10)

[Origen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Origen) wrote in *[On First Principles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On_First_Principles)* that the Seraphim, in the [Book of Isaiah,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Isaiah) are the physical representation of the [Christ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christ) and the [Holy Spirit.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Spirit_in_Christianity) His rationale comes from the idea that nothing "can wholly know the beginnings of all things and the ends of the universe" aside from [God.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/God) Origen concludes this section in writing about the Seraphim as beings that have the knowledge of God revealed to them which elevates the role of the Seraphim to divine levels:

Nevertheless whatever it is that these powers may have learned through the revelation of the Son of God and of the Holy Spirit-and they will certainly be able to acquire a great deal of knowledge, and the higher ones much more than the lower-still it is impossible for them to comprehend everything; for it is written, 'The more part of God's works are secret.[\[32\]](#page-10-11)

This quote suggests that Origen believed the Seraphim are revealed this knowledge because of their anointed status as Son of God and the Holy Spirit. He was later criticized for making such claims and labeled a heretic by the Christian church. However, his theory about the Seraphim, as referred to in [Isaiah,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Isaiah) would be reflected in other early Christian literature, as well as early Christian belief through the second century.

The seraphim took on a mystic role in [Giovanni Pico della Mirandola'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Pico_della_Mirandola)s *[Oration on the Dignity of Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oration_on_the_Dignity_of_Man)* (1487), the epitome of [Renaissance humanism.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance_humanism) Pico took the fiery Seraphim—"they burn with the fire of charity"—as the highest models of human aspiration: "impatient of any second place, let us emulate dignity and glory. And, if we will it, we shall be inferior to them in nothing", the young Pico announced, in the first flush of optimistic confidence in the human capacity that is the coinage of the Renaissance. "In the light of intelligence, meditating upon the Creator in His work, and the work in its Creator, we shall be resplendent with the light of the Cherubim. If we burn with love for the Creator only, his consuming fire will quickly transform us into the flaming likeness of the Seraphim."

Christian theology developed an idea of seraphim as beings of pure light who enjoy direct communication with God[.\[33\]](#page-10-12)[\[34\]](#page-10-13)

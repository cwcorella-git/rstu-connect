*Eviction Lab — 2024*

The Eviction Lab's Eviction Tracking System (ETS) documented over one million eviction cases filed across monitored jurisdictions in 2024. This report analyzes patterns, trends, and disparities in eviction filings nationally.

## National Overview

### Aggregate Trends

- **Just over one million eviction cases** filed in tracked jurisdictions in 2024
- Filings have rebounded to near pre-pandemic levels
- Represents **approximately 7% fewer cases** than typical pre-2020 years
- However, this aggregate decline masks significant regional variation

### The Uneven Recovery

While national numbers suggest stabilization, the reality varies dramatically by location:

**Some cities saw dramatic increases:**
- Phoenix, Houston, and Las Vegas experienced substantial rises
- Several cities set post-pandemic records

**Other cities saw decreases:**
- New York City accounted for most national improvement
- Philadelphia achieved notably low filing rates

## Geographic Analysis

### Cities With Increased Filings

| City | 2024 Rate | Prior Rate | Change |
|------|-----------|------------|--------|
| **Phoenix** | 14.3% | 10.6% | +3.7 pts |
| **Houston** | 9.2% | 6.9% | +2.3 pts |
| **Las Vegas** | 13.2% | 9.8% | +3.4 pts |

#### Phoenix: A Record-Breaking Crisis

Phoenix saw the most alarming numbers:

- **86,946 eviction filings** in 2024
- That's **one eviction filing every six minutes**
- Record-breaking for the city
- Filing rate jumped from 10.6% to 14.3%

#### Houston

- Added approximately **20,000 filings** compared to prior periods
- Rate increased from 6.9% to 9.2%

#### Las Vegas

- Added roughly **12,000 cases**
- Rate increased from 9.8% to 13.2%

### Cities With Decreased Filings

| City | 2024 Rate | Notes |
|------|-----------|-------|
| **New York City** | 52% of pre-pandemic | Accounted for most national improvement |
| **Philadelphia** | 4.0% | Second-lowest filing rate tracked |

#### New York City

New York's continued suppression of filings relative to pre-pandemic levels is responsible for much of the apparent national improvement. Without NYC, the national picture would look considerably worse.

#### Philadelphia

At 4.0%, Philadelphia achieved the second-lowest eviction filing rate among tracked cities, demonstrating that high eviction rates are not inevitable.

### Highest Overall Rate

**Richmond, Virginia** maintained the highest overall eviction filing rate:

- 2024: 24.1%
- Prior: 27.6%
- Despite the drop, nearly **1 in 4 renter households** faced eviction filing

## Demographic Disparities

### Gender

**Women are disproportionately targeted for eviction:**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Women as % of eviction defendants | **58%** |
| Women as % of renters | 53% |
| **Disparity** | +5 percentage points |

### Race

**Black renters face dramatically disproportionate eviction filings:**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Black individuals as % of eviction filings | **36%** |
| Black individuals as % of renter population | 28% |
| **Disparity** | +8 percentage points |

These disparities persist across jurisdictions and cannot be explained by income differences alone.

## Concentration of Filings

### The "Super-Evictors"

A small number of properties account for an outsized share of eviction filings:

> "The top 100 buildings accounted for **32.6% of filings** across fifteen tracked cities."

This concentration suggests that addressing evictions at a relatively small number of properties could significantly reduce overall filing rates.

### Implications

The concentration of evictions in specific buildings indicates:

1. **Certain landlords use eviction as routine practice**
2. **Targeting high-filing properties could be efficient intervention**
3. **Some landlords' business models depend on constant tenant turnover**

## Key Findings

### 1. National Numbers Mask Local Crises

The 7% decline nationally obscures the reality that some cities face record eviction rates while others have dramatically reduced filings.

### 2. Policy Matters

The variation between cities demonstrates that eviction rates are **policy-responsive**. Cities with stronger tenant protections see lower rates.

### 3. Disparities Are Systemic

The consistent targeting of women and Black renters across jurisdictions indicates systemic bias, not local anomalies.

### 4. Concentration Creates Opportunity

The fact that a small number of properties generate a large share of filings means targeted intervention could be highly effective.

## Implications for Organizing

### Target the Super-Evictors

The concentration of filings in specific properties suggests organizing should:

- Identify and target high-filing landlords
- Use public data to hold them accountable
- Campaign for policies targeting repeat filers

### Address Demographic Targeting

The racial and gender disparities require:

- Explicit attention to how eviction perpetuates inequality
- Coalition building across affected communities
- Policy advocacy for fair housing enforcement

### Learn From Success Stories

Philadelphia's low rate and New York's continued suppression demonstrate that high eviction rates can be addressed through policy and organizing.

### Local Conditions Vary

National data cannot substitute for local analysis. Each city's eviction crisis has specific characteristics requiring tailored responses.

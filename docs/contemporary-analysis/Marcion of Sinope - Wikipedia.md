# **Marcion of Sinope**

**Marcion of Sinope** [\(/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English)[ˈ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English)[m](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English)[ɑː](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English)[rki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English)[ə](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English)[n, -si](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English)[ə](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English)[n/;](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English) [Ancient Greek:](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_language) Μαρκίων[\[2\]](#page-3-1)[\[note 1\]](#page-3-2) Σινώπης; c. 85 – c. 160[\[3\]\)](#page-3-3) was a [theologian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diversity_in_early_Christian_theology)[\[4\]](#page-3-4) in [early Christianity.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Christianity)[\[4\]](#page-3-4)[\[5\]](#page-3-5) Marcion preached that [God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/God_in_Christianity) had sent [Jesus Christ,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus_Christ) who was distinct from the "vengeful" God [\(Demiurge\)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demiurge#Marcion) who had created the world[.\[4\]](#page-3-4) [\[5\]](#page-3-5)[\[6\]](#page-3-6) He considered himself a follower of [Paul the Apostle,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_the_Apostle) whom he believed to have been the only true [apostle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apostles_in_the_New_Testament) of Jesus Christ; his doctrine is called [Marcionism.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcionism)[\[4\]](#page-3-4)[\[5\]](#page-3-5)[\[7\]](#page-3-7) Marcion published the earliest record of a canon of New Testament books.[\[4\]](#page-3-4)[\[8\]](#page-3-8)

[Early Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Church_Fathers) writers such as [Justin Martyr,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Martyr) [Irenaeus,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irenaeus) and [Tertullian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tertullian) denounced Marcion as a [heretic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heresy_in_Christianity) or antichrist,[\[9\]](#page-3-9) and he was excommunicated by the church of [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese_of_Rome) around 144.[\[10\]](#page-3-10) He published his own [canon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biblical_canon) of [Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity) [sacred scriptures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_text#Christianity)[,\[4\]](#page-3-4)[\[11\]](#page-3-11)[\[12\]](#page-3-12) which contained ten [Pauline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pauline_epistles) [epistles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pauline_epistles) (including the [Epistle to the Laodiceans,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epistle_to_the_Laodiceans) while excluding the [Pastoral epistles\)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pastoral_epistles) and the [Gospel of Marcion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gospel_of_Marcion) which historically is claimed to be an edited version of the [Gospel of Luke.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gospel_of_Luke)[\[4\]](#page-3-4)[\[13\]](#page-3-13) Some modern scholars, such as [Matthias Klinghardt,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthias_Klinghardt)[\[14\]](#page-4-0) have theorized that [Marcion's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Priority_of_the_Gospel_of_Marcion) [Gospel was the oldest,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Priority_of_the_Gospel_of_Marcion) although this has been [contested.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Priority_of_the_Gospel_of_Marcion#Criticism)

This made Marcionism a catalyst in the process of the [development of the New Testament canon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Development_of_the_New_Testament_canon#Marcion_of_Sinope) by forcing the [proto-orthodox Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-orthodox_Christianity) to respond to his canon[.\[4\]](#page-3-4)[\[15\]](#page-4-1)

#### **Marcionite Church**

The Marcionite church expanded greatly within Marcion's lifetime, becoming a major rival to the [other](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_early_Christianity) [emerging church.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_early_Christianity) After his death, it retained its following and survived Christian controversy and imperial disapproval for several centuries[.\[24\]](#page-4-10) Several theologians have viewed him as a proto-protestant[.\[25\]](#page-4-11)

#### **Teachings**

Study of the [Hebrew Bible,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_Bible) along with received writings circulating in the nascent Church, led Marcion to conclude that many of the [teachings of Jesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Jesus) were incompatible with the actions of [Yahweh,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yahweh) characterized as the belligerent god of the [Hebrew Bible.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_Bible) Marcion responded by developing a [ditheistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dualistic_cosmology) system of belief around the year 144.[\[note 2\]](#page-3-14) This notion of two gods—a higher transcendent one and a lower world-creator and ruler—allowed Marcion to reconcile his perceived contradictions between Christian [Covenant theology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Covenant_theology) and [the gospel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_gospel) proclaimed by the [New Testament.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Testament)

In contrast to other leaders of the nascent Christian Church, however, Marcion declared that Christianity was in complete discontinuity with [Judaism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judaism) and entirely opposed to the scriptures of Judaism. Marcion did not claim that these were false. Instead, he asserted that they were entirely true, but were to be read in an absolutely literalistic manner, one which led him to develop an understanding that Yahweh was not the same God spoken of by Jesus. For example, Marcion argued that the [Genesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Genesis) account of Yahweh walking through the [Garden of Eden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garden_of_Eden) asking where Adam was, proved that Yahweh inhabited a physical body and was without [universal knowledge,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omniscience) attributes wholly incompatible with the Heavenly Father professed by Jesus.

According to Marcion, the God of the [Old Testament,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Testament) whom he called the [Demiurge,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demiurge) the [creator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creator_deity) of the material [universe,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universe) is a jealous [tribal deity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_god) of the [Jews,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jews) whose [law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torah) represents [legalistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legalism_(theology)) [reciprocal justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eye_for_an_eye) and who punishes mankind for its sins through suffering and death. In contrast, the God that Jesus professed is an altogether different being, a universal God of compassion and love who looks upon humanity with benevolence and mercy. Marcion also produced a book titled *Antitheses*, which is no longer extant,[\[26\]](#page-4-12)

contrasting the Demiurge of the Old Testament with the Heavenly Father of the New Testament.

Marcion held Jesus to be the son of the Heavenly Father but understood the [incarnation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incarnation_(Christianity)) in a [docetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Docetism) manner, i.e. that Jesus' body was only an imitation of a material body, and consequently denied Jesus' physical and bodily birth, death, and resurrection.

Marcion was the first to codify a Christian [canon.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Development_of_the_New_Testament_canon#Marcion_of_Sinope) His canon consisted of only eleven books, grouped into two sections: the *[Evangelikon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gospel_of_Marcion)*, a shorter version of the Gospel of Luke, and the *[Apostolikon](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Apostolikon&action=edit&redlink=1)*, a selection of ten epistles of Paul the Apostle, which were also slightly shorter than the canonical text. Early Christians such as Irenaeus, Tertullian, and Epiphanius claimed that Marcion's editions of Luke and the Pauline epistles were intentionally edited by Marcion to match his theological views, and many modern scholars agree.[\[27\]](#page-4-13) However, some scholars argue that Marcion's texts were not substantially edited by him, and may in some respects represent an earlier version of these texts than the canonical versions.[\[6\]](#page-3-6)[\[28\]](#page-4-14)[\[29\]](#page-4-15)[\[30\]](#page-4-16) Like the [Gospel of](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gospel_of_Mark) [Mark,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gospel_of_Mark) the gospel used by Marcion did not contain elements relating to Jesus's [birth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nativity_of_Jesus) and childhood. Interestingly, it did contain some Jewish elements, and material that challenged Marcion's ditheism—a fact that was utilized by early Christians in their articulations against Marcion.[\[31\]](#page-4-17)

The centrality of the Pauline epistles in Marcion's canon reflects the fact that Marcion considered Paul to be the correct interpreter and transmitter of Jesus' teachings, in contrast to the Twelve Disciples and the early Jerusalem church[.\[7\]](#page-3-7) In Marcion's view, the other apostles were under the auspices of the Demiurge[.\[26\]](#page-4-12)

#### **Gnosticism**

Marcion is sometimes described as a [Gnostic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gnosticism) philosopher. In some essential respects, Marcion proposed ideas which aligned well with Gnostic thought. Like the Gnostics, he believed that this world was not the creation of the one true God[.\[32\]](#page-4-18)

However, Marcionism conceptualizes God in a way which cannot be reconciled with broader Gnostic thought. For Gnostics, some human beings are born with a small piece of God's soul lodged within their spirit (akin to the notion of a [Divine Spark\)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divine_Spark).[\[33\]](#page-4-19) God is thus intimately connected to and part of his creation. Salvation lies in turning away from the physical world (which Gnostics regard as an illusion) and embracing the godlike qualities within oneself. Marcion, by contrast, held that the Heavenly Father (the father of Jesus Christ) was an utterly alien God; he had no part in making the world, nor any connection with it.[\[33\]](#page-4-19) According to [Bart Ehrman:](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bart_Ehrman) "Marcion himself should not be thought of as a Gnostic; he held that there were only [two gods,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dualism_in_cosmology) not many; he did not think of this world as a cosmic disaster, but as the creation of the Old Testament God; and he did not think divine sparks resided in human bodies that could be set free by understanding the true 'gnosis.' Moreover, his [docetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Docetic) view does not appear to have been the typical view of Gnostics."[\[32\]](#page-4-18)

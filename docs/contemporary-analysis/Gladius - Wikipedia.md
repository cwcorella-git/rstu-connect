# *Gladius*

*Gladius* (Classical Latin: [[ˈɡɫ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Latin)[adi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Latin)ʊs]) is a [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) word properly referring to the type of [sword](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sword) that was used by [ancient Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome) foot soldiers starting from the 3rd century BC and until the 3rd century AD. Linguistically, within Latin, the word also came to mean "sword", regardless of the type used.

Early ancient Roman swords were similar to those of the Greeks, called *[xiphe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiphos)* (pl., sg.: *xiphos*). From the 3rd century BC, however, the [Romans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Republic) adopted a weapon based on the sword of the [Celtiberians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celtiberians) of [Hispania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispania) in service to [Carthage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carthage) during the [Punic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punic_Wars) [Wars,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punic_Wars) known in Latin as the *gladius hispaniensis*, meaning ["Hispanic-](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispania)type sword". The Romans improved the weapon and modified it depending on how their battle units waged war, and created over time new types of "*gladii*" such as the *Mainz gladius* and the *Pompeii gladius*. Finally, in the third century AD the heavy Roman infantry replaced the *gladius* with the *[spatha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spatha)* (already common among Roman cavalrymen),[\[1\]](#page-5-0) relegating the *gladius* as a weapon for light Roman infantry.

A fully equipped Roman [legionary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legionary) after the consulships of [Gaius Marius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaius_Marius) (the [Marian reforms\)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marian_reforms) was armed with a sword (*gladius*), a shield (*[scutum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scutum)*), one or two javelins (*[pila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilum)*), often a dagger (*[pugio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pugio)*), and perhaps, in the later empire period, darts (*[plumbatae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plumbata)*). Conventionally, soldiers threw *pila* to disable the enemy's shields and disrupt enemy formations before engaging in close combat, for which they drew the *gladius*. A soldier generally led with the shield and thrust with the sword[.\[2\]](#page-5-1)

# **Predecessors and origins**

According to [Polybius,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polybius) the sword used by the Roman army during the [Battle of Telamon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Telamon) in 225 BC, though deemed superior to the cumbersome Gallic swords, was mainly useful to thrust[.\[8\]](#page-5-7) These thrusting swords used before the adoption of the Gladius were possibly based on the Greek *[xiphos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiphos)*. [\[9\]](#page-5-8) Later, during the [Battle of Cannae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Cannae) in 216 BC, they found Hannibal's [Celtiberian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celtiberians) [mercenaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercenaries_of_the_ancient_Iberian_peninsula) wielding swords that excelled at both slashing and thrusting[.\[9\]](#page-5-8) A text attributed to Polybius describes the adoption of this design by the Romans even before the end of the [war,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Punic_War) which canonical Polybius reaffirms by calling the later Roman sword *gladius hispaniensis* in [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) and *iberiké machaira* in [Greek.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_greek_language)[\[9\]](#page-5-8) It is believed [Scipio Africanus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scipio_Africanus) was the promoter of the change after the [Battle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Cartagena_(209_BC)) [of Cartagena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Cartagena_(209_BC)) in 209 BC, after which he set the inhabitants to produce weapons for the Roman army.[\[10\]](#page-5-9)[\[11\]](#page-5-10)

In 70 BC, both [Claudius Quadrigarius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quintus_Claudius_Quadrigarius) and Livy relate the story of [Titus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titus_Manlius_Torquatus_(347_BC)) [Manlius Torquatus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titus_Manlius_Torquatus_(347_BC)) using a "Hispanic sword" (*gladius Hispanus*) in a duel with a Gaul in 361 BC.[\[12\]](#page-6-0)[\[13\]](#page-6-1) However, the Gladius was not yet used by the Romans in the 4th century BC, and because of that this has been traditionally considered a terminological [anachronism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anachronism) caused by the long established naming convention.[\[8\]](#page-5-7) It's possible that the Celtiberian sword was first adopted by Romans after encounters with Carthaginian

# **Manufacturing**

### **Technique**

By the time of the [Roman Republic,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Republic) which flourished during the [Iron Age,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Age) [steel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steel) and the steel-making process was known to the classical world. Pure iron is relatively soft, but pure iron is never found in nature. Natural iron ore contains various impurities in [solid solution,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solid_solution) which harden the reduced metal by producing irregular-shaped metallic crystals. The *gladius* was generally made out of steel.

In Roman times, workers [reduced ore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_reduced_iron) in a [bloomery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomery) furnace. The resulting pieces were called *blooms*, [\[16\]](#page-6-4) which they further worked to remove slag inclusions from the porous surface.

A recent metallurgical study of two [Etrurian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etruria) swords, one in the form of a Greek *[kopis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kopis)* from 7th century BC [Vetulonia,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vetulonia) the other in the form of a *gladius Hispaniensis* from 4th century BC [Clusium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clusium) [\(Chiusi\)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiusi), gives insight concerning the manufacture of Roman swords.[\[17\]](#page-6-5) The Chiusi sword comes from Romanized *etruria*; thus, regardless of the names of the forms (which the authors do not identify), the authors believe the process was continuous from the Etruscans to the Romans.

The Vetulonian sword was crafted by the [pattern welding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pattern_welding) process from five blooms reduced at a temperature of 1,163 °C (2,125 °F). Five strips of varying carbon content were created. A central core of the sword contained the highest: 0.15–0.25% carbon. On its edges were placed four strips of low-carbon steel, 0.05– 0.07%, and the whole thing was welded together by forging on the pattern of hammer blows. A blow increased the temperature sufficiently to produce a friction weld at that spot. Forging continued until the steel was cold, producing some central [annealing.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annealing_(metallurgy)) The sword was 58 cm (23 in) long.[\[17\]](#page-6-5)

The Chiusian sword was created from a single bloom by forging from a temperature of 1,237 °C (2,259 °F). The carbon content increased from 0.05–0.08% at the back side of the sword to 0.35–0.4% on the blade, from which the authors deduce that some form of [carburization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carburization) may have been used. The sword was 40 cm (16 in) long and was characterized by a wasp-waist close to the hilt.

Romans continued to forge swords, both as composites and from single pieces. Inclusions of sand and rust weakened the two swords in the study, and no doubt limited the strength of swords during the Roman period.

### **Production**

The craftsmen with the strategic task of making the *gladii* were called *gladiarii*. They were part of the Roman legions as *fabri*, enjoying the status of *[immunes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immunes)*. There were also public workshops, *fabricae*, dedicated to the making of the *gladii*. Epigraphic attestations of the *gladiarii* have been found in Italy, especially in areas of ancient metallurgic tradition such as [Capua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capua) and [Aquileia.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquileia)[\[18\]](#page-6-6)

# **Description**

The word *gladius* acquired a general meaning as any type of sword. This use appears as early as the 1st century AD in the *Biography of Alexander the Great* by [Quintus Curtius Rufus.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quintus_Curtius_Rufus)[\[19\]](#page-6-7) The republican authors, however, appear to mean a specific type of sword, which is now known from archaeology to have had variants.

*Gladii* were two-edged for cutting and had a tapered point for stabbing during thrusting. A solid grip was provided by a knobbed hilt added on, possibly with ridges for the fingers. Blade strength was achieved by welding together strips, in which case the sword had a channel down the centre, or by fashioning a single piece of high-carbon steel, rhomboidal in cross-section. The owner's name was often engraved or punched on the blade.

The [hilt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilt) of a Roman sword was the *capulus*. It was often ornate, especially the sword-hilts of officers and dignitaries.

Stabbing was a very efficient technique, as stabbing wounds, especially in the abdominal area, were almost always deadly[.\[20\]](#page-6-8) However, the *gladius* in some circumstances was used for cutting or slashing, as is indicated by [Livy'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titus_Livius)s account of the [Macedonian Wars,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macedonian_Wars) wherein the Macedonian soldiers were horrified to see dismembered bodies.[\[21\]](#page-6-9)

Though the primary infantry attack was thrusting at stomach height, they were trained to take any advantage, such as slashing at kneecaps beneath the shield wall.

The *gladius* was sheathed in a [scabbard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scabbard) mounted on a belt or shoulder strap. Some say the soldier reached across his body to draw it, and others claim that the position of the shield made this method of drawing impossible. A [centurion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centurion) wore it on the opposite side as a mark of distinction.[\[22\]](#page-6-10)

Towards the end of the 2nd century AD and during the 3rd century the *[spatha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spatha)* gradually took the place of the *gladius* in the Roman legions.

### **Types**

Several different designs were used; among collectors and [historical reenactors,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_reenactment) the three primary kinds are known as the *Mainz gladius*, the *Fulham gladius*, and the *Pompeii gladius* (these names refer to where or how the canonical example was found). More recent archaeological finds have uncovered an earlier version, the *gladius Hispaniensis*.

The differences between these varieties are subtle. The original Hispanic sword, which was used during the republic, had a slight "wasp-waist" or "leaf-blade" curvature. The Mainz variety came into use on the frontier in the early empire. It kept the curvature, but shortened and widened the blade and made the point triangular. At home, the less battle-effective Pompeii version came into use. It eliminated the curvature, lengthened the blade, and diminished the point. The Fulham was a compromise, with straight edges and a long point[.\[23\]](#page-6-11)

### **Gladius Hispaniensis**

*The gladius Hispaniensis* was a Roman sword used from around 216 BC until 20 BC. Its blade had a length of 60–68 cm (24–27 in), and the sword was 75–85 cm (30–33 in) long. The width of the sword was 5 cm (2.0 in). It was the largest and heaviest of the *gladii*, weighing 1 kg (2.2 lb) or 900 g (2.0 lb). This gladius was also the earliest and longest blade. It had a pronounced leaf-shape.

#### **Mainz Gladius**

*The [Mainz Gladius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainz_Gladius)* is made of heavily corroded iron and a sheath made of tinned and gilded bronze. The blade was 50–55 cm (20–22 in) long and 7 cm (2.8 in) in width. The sword was 65–70 cm (26–28 in) long. The sword weighed 800 g (1.8 lb). The point of the sword was more triangular than the Gladius Hispaniensis. The Mainz Gladius still had wasp-waisted curves. The decoration on the scabbard illustrates the ceding of military victory to [Augustus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustus) by Tiberius after a successful Alpine campaign. Augustus is semi-nude, and sits in the pose of Jupiter, flanked by the Roman gods of Victory and Mars Ultor, while [Tiberius,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiberius) in military dress, presents Augustus with a statuette of Victory.

### **Fulham gladius**

The *Fulham gladius* or *Mainz-Fulham gladius* was a Roman sword that was used after [Aulus Plautius'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aulus_Plautius) invasion of Britain in 43 AD[.\[24\]](#page-6-12) The Romans used it until the end of the 1st century. The *Fulham gladius* has a triangular tip. The length of the blade is 50–55 cm (20– 22 in). The length of the sword is 65–70 cm (26–28 in). The width of the blade is 6 cm (2.4 in). The swords weighs 700 g (1.5 lb) (wooden hilt).[\[25\]](#page-6-13)[\[26\]](#page-7-0) A full size replica can be seen at [Fulham Palace,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulham_Palace) [Fulham.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulham)

### **Pompeii gladius**

The *Pompeii gladius* was named by modern historians after the Roman town of [Pompeii.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pompeii) This type of *gladius* was by far the most popular one. Four examples of the sword type were found in Pompeii, with others turning up elsewhere. The sword has parallel cutting edges and a triangular tip. This is the shortest of the *gladii*. It is often confused with the *[spatha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spatha)*, which was a longer, slashing weapon used initially by mounted *auxilians*. Over the years, the Pompeii got longer, and these later versions are called semi-*spathes*. The length of the blade was 45–50 cm (18–20 in). The length of the sword is 60–65 cm (24–26 in). The width of the blade is 5 cm (2.0 in). The sword weighs 700 g (1.5 lb) (wooden hilt).

The **John Reed Clubs** (1929–1935), often referred to as **John Reed Club** (JRC), were an American federation of local organizations targeted towards [Marxist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marxist) writers, artists, and intellectuals, named after the American [journalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journalist) and [activist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Activist) [John Reed.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Reed_(journalist)) Established in the fall of 1929, the John Reed Clubs were a [mass organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_organization) of the [Communist Party USA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_USA) which sought to expand its influence among radical and liberal intellectuals. The organization was terminated in 1935.[\[1\]](#page-20-0)[\[2\]](#page-20-1)[\[3\]](#page-20-2) [\[4\]](#page-20-3)

In October 1929, the John Reed Club was founded by eight staff members of the [New Masses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Masses) magazine to support leftist and Marxist artists and writers. They included: [Mike Gold,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Gold) [Walt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walt_Carmon) [Carmon,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walt_Carmon) [William Gropper,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Gropper) Keene Wallis, [Hugo Gellert,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugo_Gellert) Morris

Pass, and Joseph Pass[.\[1\]](#page-20-0)

According to [Alan M. Wald,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_M._Wald) The John Reed Clubs were not founded by the Communist Party. New Masses managing editor Walt Carmon became frustrated with a group of young writers who were hanging out in the office and getting in his way[.\[5\]](#page-21-0) He told them to "go out and form a club" and "call it the John Reed Club.["\[5\]](#page-21-0) The John Reed Clubs would be a constant source of drama within the New Masses family, and members of the Clubs would eventually found the [Partisan Review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partisan_Review), which became a main competitor to the New Masses.


[New Masses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Masses) cover by [Hugo Gellert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugo_Gellert) (May 1926)

The New Masses announced the new club in its November 1929 issue:

The radical artists and writers of New York have organized the John Reed Club. The group includes all creative workers in art, literature, sculpture, music, theater, and the movies... The purpose of the Club is to bring closer all creative workers; to maintain contact with the American revolutionary labor movement.

In cooperation with workers groups and cultural organizations, discussion, literary evenings, and exhibits will be organized. Hopefully, the organization will be national in scope... For the first time, a group of socially conscious creative workers has been organized in America to compare with existing groups in Europe. Steps have been taken to make immediate contact with writers, artists, and all creative workers in France, Germany, Russia, and Japan.[\[6\]](#page-21-1)


[Mike Gold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Gold) (here, 1930s before crowd in New York City) was a prominent JRC co-founder

In January 1930, [Mike Gold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Gold) described the JRC in the New Masses as a "small group" comprising writers, artists, sculptors, musicians, and dancers "of revolutionary tendencies". They were already building a clubhouse. Harold Hickerson had a music school with 100 pupils. Gropper and Lozowick taught graphic arts to 30. Edith Siegel led a "worker's ballet" for a Lenin memorial. Em Jo Basshe directed a Jewish Workers' theatre. Others taught at the [New York Workers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Workers_School) [School.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Workers_School) They cooperated with [Workers International Relief.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Workers_International_Relief) Gold recommended that every writer-member work in industry.

He cited as example Ed Falkowski (miner), Martin Russak (textile worker), H. H. Lewis (farmer), and Joe Kalar (lumberman)[.\[7\]](#page-21-2)

In July 1930, Harry Alan Potemkin, JRC secretary, reported in the New Masses that the JRC had supported [May Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May_Day) as well as signed a petition for the [International Labor Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Labor_Defense) for prisoners of war. The club also collaborated with ["Proletpen"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proletpen), a Jewish proletarian writing group. It also supported the "United Front Conference Against Lynching", created by the New York district of the [Communist Party USA.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_USA) Books published by member writers included: [Charles Yale Harrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Yale_Harrison)'s [Generals Die](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generals_Die_in_Bed)

[in Bed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generals_Die_in_Bed) and [Mike Gold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Gold)'s children's story Charlie Chaplin's Parde. [\[9\]](#page-21-4)

By November 1930, although originally politically independent, the JRC and the [New Masses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Masses) officially affiliated with the Communist Party. This turn coincided with the JRC's participation in the Kharkov Conference of the [International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Union_of_Revolutionary_Writers) [Union of Revolutionary Writers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Union_of_Revolutionary_Writers) (IURW), November 6–15, 1930. The joint JRC-New Masses delegation included: Mike Gold, A. B. Magil, Fred Ellis, William Gropper, Harry Potamkin, Josephine Herbst, and John Herrmann. The conference led to a ten-point "Program of Action" to promote proletarian literature as an important part of promoting Marxism[.\[1\]](#page-20-0)


[John Dos Passos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Dos_Passos) was a prominent writer and JRC member

On January 1, 1932, [Diego Rivera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diego_Rivera) spoke before the John Reed Club's New York chapter. Later, when the JRC heard of Rivera's support for [Leon Trotsky,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Trotsky) they disavowed him and returned a \$100 contribution he made[.\[1\]](#page-20-0)

The JRCs held a national conference on May 29–30, 1932, in Chicago[.\[1\]](#page-20-0) During the conference, the JRCs announced they were "an integral part of the "Workers Cultural Federation".[\[10\]](#page-21-5)

Conference ("presidium") members elected included: Joseph Freeman, Jan Wittenber, Conrad Komorowski, [Kenneth Rexroth,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenneth_Rexroth) Charles Natterstad, Harry Carlisle, George Gay, Carl Carlsen, and Jack Walters. Honorary members included [Maxim Gorki,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxim_Gorki) [Romain Rolland,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romain_Rolland) [John Dos Passos,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Dos_Passos) Seikichi Fujimori, [Lo Hsun,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lu_Xun) [Johannes Becher,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannes_R._Becher) [Vallant-Couturier,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marie-Claude_Vaillant-Couturier) and [Langston Hughes,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langston_Hughes) with [Maurice Sugar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurice_Sugar) as chairman and [Oakley C. Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oakley_C._Johnson) as secretary[.\[1\]](#page-20-0)

Chapter reports consistently criticized the original New York City chapter of ignoring the others. Harry Carlisle of JRC Hollywood opposed Mike Gold of JRC NYC for falling down on principle when opening the JRC to non-Marxist writers and artists. Instead, Carlisle urged, the JRC should focus on "artists and writers of distinctly working class origin."[\[1\]](#page-20-0)[\[10\]](#page-21-5)

The July 1932 issue of the New Masses included the "John Reed Club Resolution Against War", stating its stance against "imminent imperial war", noting that the Soviet Union "stands for peace", and calling on all writers, artists, and professionals to unite "in defense of the first workers' republic, the Union of Soviet Socialist Republics.["\[11\]](#page-21-6)

In November 1932, JRC members who publicly endorsed the Communist Party's [US presidential slate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1932_United_States_presidential_election) [\(William Z. Foster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Z._Foster) and [James W. Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_W._Ford)) included: Em Jo Basshe, [Robert Cantwell,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Cantwell) [Orrick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orrick_Glenday_Johns) [Johns,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orrick_Glenday_Johns) [Grace Lumpkin,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grace_Lumpkin) [Langston Hughes,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langston_Hughes) [Mike Gold,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Gold) and [Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Lozowick) [Lozowick.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Lozowick) [\[1\]](#page-20-0)

In early 1933, the JRCs took a strong stance against [Hitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitler) and the rising tide of [Fascism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascism) in Europe[.\[1\]](#page-20-0)

In mid-1933, the JRCs held a second national conference. Attendees include: [Jack Conroy,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Conroy) Meridel Le Sueur, Alan Calmer, Orrick Johns, Joe Jones, [Nelsen Algren,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nelson_Algren) [William Phillips,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Phillips_(editor)) [Philip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Rahv) [Rahv,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Rahv) Alfred Hayes, Gilbert Rocke, Jan Wittenber, Mike Gold, Richard Wright, [Alexander Trachtenberg,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Trachtenberg) A. B. Magil, Jack S. Balch, Joseph North[.\[1\]](#page-20-0)

In 1936, the John Reed Clubs dissolved into the [American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Artists%27_Congress) [Artists' Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Artists%27_Congress) by order of the Communist Party USA[.\[12\]](#page-21-7)

The John Reed Club's slogan was "Art is a weapon in the class struggle.["\[1\]](#page-20-0)

In May 1930, the headquarters for the John Reed Club was 102 West [Fourteenth Street,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/14th_Street_(Manhattan)) New York City[.\[8\]](#page-21-3) In 1932, its location was 63 West [Fifteenth Street,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/15th_Street_(Manhattan)) New York City[.\[1\]](#page-20-0)[\[10\]](#page-21-5)

New York City and Los Angeles were the two centers of writermembers[.\[1\]](#page-20-0)

In 1931, there were 13 JRC chapters[.\[1\]](#page-20-0) Chapters peaked at thirty. From New York, it spread to Chicago, Detroit, San Francisco, Boston, and other cities. The Boston chapter was cofounded by writer [Eugene Gordon.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugene_Gordon_(writer)) [\[13\]](#page-21-8)

## **John Reed Club School of Art**

## [\[edit\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=John_Reed_Clubs&action=edit§ion=10)

During the 1932 national convention, the JRCs announced the opening of a "John Reed Club School of Art" in New York City at 450 [Sixth Avenue.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sixth_Avenue) Classes were to start on November 14, 1932, for Monday evenings and Saturday afternoons. Instruction was open beyond JRC members. Instructors included [Hugo Gellert,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugo_Gellert) [William Gropper,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Gropper) [Louis Lozowick,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Lozowick) and William Siegel[.\[1\]](#page-20-0) One of the students was [Norman Lewis,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_Lewis_(artist)) studying from 1933 to 1935[.\[14\]](#page-22-0)

By 1933, the New York chapter had 380 members, of whom some 200 were artists and the rest writers. The only paid job was secretary-treasurer at \$15 per week[.\[1\]](#page-20-0)

In her 1977 work The John Reed Clubs, Laurie Ann Alexandre stated:

It would be inaccurate to call the John Reed Club a [Marxist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marxist) organization. Its charter simply stated that any member who recognized class struggle and wished to give it support would be welcomed. It cannot be said that the JRC was committed beyond that general point. Many of its members were not Marxists, and the Clubs spent little time educating its members in the theoretical underpinnings of [Engels,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Engels) [Marx,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Marx) or [Lenin.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lenin) [\[1\]](#page-20-0)

## **Books by JRC members**

### [\[edit\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=John_Reed_Clubs&action=edit§ion=17)

[Eitaro Ishigaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eitaro_Ishigaki) (~1940 from [Archives of American Art\)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archives_of_American_Art) was a JRC co-founder

Artistic members of the John Reed Club of New York began holding art exhibitions in late 1929, shortly after the club's formation:

- **1929:** The first art exhibition occurred at the United Workers' Cooperative Apartments (aka [United Workers Cooperative](https://www.pbs.org/independentlens/athomeinutopia/) [Colony,](https://www.pbs.org/independentlens/athomeinutopia/) aka "Commie Coops") on [Bronx Park East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx_Park) in December 1929. Artists included: [Jacob Burck,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Burck) [Fred Ellis,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_Ellis_(cartoonist)) [William Gropper,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Gropper) [Eitaro Ishigaki,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eitaro_Ishigaki) [\[25\]](#page-23-1) [Gan Kolski,](https://web.archive.org/web/20100929091725/http://www.comradesinart.net/comrades_flv/Gan_Kolski/index.html) [Louis Lozowick,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Lozowick) [Jan Matulka,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Matulka) Morris Pass, [Anton Refregier,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anton_Refregier) [Louis Leon Ribak,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Leon_Ribak) [Esther Shemitz,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esther_Shemitz) Otto Soglow, and [Art Young.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Young) [\[7\]](#page-21-2)[\[26\]](#page-23-2)
- • **1930:** The second exhibition occurred in January 1930: 42 drawings, paintings, and lithographs that traveled from the Borough Park Workers' Club (43rd Street, Brooklyn) to other clubs in [Brownsville,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brownsville,_Brooklyn) [Williamsburg,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Williamsburg,_Brooklyn) the [Bronx,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx) and [Manhattan.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan) [\[26\]](#page-23-2)
- • **1931:** The third exhibition occurred in April 1931 with the

["Proletpen"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proletpen), a [Yiddish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yiddish) cultural group of the [Communist Party:](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_USA) it comprised some 100 paintings, drawings, and cartoons by some 30 artists[.\[26\]](#page-23-2)

- **1932:** "Twenty John Reed Club Artists on Proletarian and Revolutionary Themes" occurred at the American Contemporary Art (ACA) Gallery in November 1932: 36 paintings, drawings, and lithographs by 21 artists – Albert Abramowitz, Bard, Mark Baum, [Joseph Biel,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Biel) [Jacob Burck,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Burck) Dehn, [Hugo Gellert,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugo_Gellert) [William Gropper,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Gropper) [William Hernandez], [Eitaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eitaro_Ishigaki) [Ishigaki,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eitaro_Ishigaki) Limbach, [Louis Lozowick,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Lozowick) Moses Oley, Quirt, [Anton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anton_Refregier) [Refregier,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anton_Refregier) Philip Resman, [Louis Leon Ribak,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Leon_Ribak) [William Siegel,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Segal) Soglow, [Raphael Soyer,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raphael_Soyer) and Max Spivach. Four known works comprised Gellert's "Karl Max' Capital in Lithographs" (from a set of 60 lithographs)[.\[26\]](#page-23-2)
- • **1935:** Another exhibition occurred again at the ACA Gallery[\[27\]](#page-23-3) in 1935: its theme was the [Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan) invasion of [Manchuria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchuria) and included "Roustabouts" by [Joe Jones.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Jones_(artist))

The last known exhibition occurred at the ACA Gallery: its theme was "The Capitalist Crisis" and gained little notice outside of Communist press organs.

The site of the John Reed Club in New York held exhibitions of member work from the summer of 1930; it established a gallery there in 1932. Records are scarce for 1932–1935[.\[26\]](#page-23-2)

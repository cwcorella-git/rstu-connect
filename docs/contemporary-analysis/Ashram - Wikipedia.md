## ![](_page_0_Picture_8.jpeg)

Ashram of sage [Kanva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanva) depicted on terracotta plaque, 2nd century BCE

An **ashram**[\[1\]](#page-4-0) [\(Sanskrit:](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit_language) आशरम् , āśrama) is a spiritual [hermitage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermitage_(religious_retreat)) or a [monastery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monastery) in [Hinduism.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism) [\[2\]](#page-4-1)[\[3\]](#page-4-2)

The Sanskrit noun āśrama- is a thematic nominal derivative from the root śrami 'toil' (< [PIE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Indo-European) \*ḱremh2, cf. ś[rama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%9Arama%E1%B9%87a)ṇa) with the prefix ā 'towards.'[\[4\]](#page-4-3) An ashram is a place where one strives towards a goal in a disciplined manner. Such a goal could be [ascetic,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ascetic) [spiritual,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spirituality) [yogic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yogic) or any other[.\[5\]](#page-4-4)

## ![](_page_1_Picture_2.jpeg)

## Sevagram Ashram in Sabramati, 1936

An ashram would traditionally, but not necessarily in contemporary times, be located far from human habitation, in [forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest) or [mountainous regions,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain) amidst refreshing natural surroundings conducive to spiritual instruction and [meditation.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meditation) The residents of an ashram regularly performed spiritual and physical exercises, such as the various forms of [yoga.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoga) Other sacrifices and penances, such as [yajnas,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yajna) were also performed. [\[6\]](#page-4-5) Many ashrams also served as [gurukulas,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurukula) residential schools for children under the [guru-shishya tradition.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guru-shishya_tradition)

Sometimes, the goal of a pilgrimage to the ashram was not tranquility, but instruction in some art, especially warfare. In the [Ramayana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramayana), the princes of ancient [Ayodhya,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayodhya_(Ramayana)) [Rama,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rama) and [Lakshmana,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakshmana) go to [Vishvamitra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vishvamitra)'s ashram to protect his yajnas from being defiled by emissary-demons of [Ravana.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ravana) After they prove their mettle, the princes receive martial instruction from the sage, especially in the use of divine weapons. In the [Mahabharata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahabharata), [Krishna,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krishna) in his youth, goes to the ashram of [Sandipani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandipani) to gain knowledge of both intellectual and spiritual matters.[[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed)]

## **Schools in Maharashtra**

[\[edit\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Ashram&action=edit§ion=3)

[Boarding schools,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boarding_school) especially in the tribal areas of [Maharashtra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maharashtra) and elsewhere in [India,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India) are called ashram shala or ashram schools. One such school is the [Lok Biradari Prakalp Ashram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lok_Biradari_Prakalp#Ashram_Shala) [Shala.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lok_Biradari_Prakalp#Ashram_Shala) [\[7\]](#page-4-6)[\[8\]](#page-5-0)

A number of ashrams have been established outside India. Typically, these ashrams are connected to Indian lineages,[\[9\]](#page-5-1) focus on imparting [Yoga-](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoga)related teachings, often in [residential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoga_tourism) [retreats,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoga_tourism) and are headed by spiritual teachers (Indians or Western).

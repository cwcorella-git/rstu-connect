# Information wants to be free

## Contributors to Wikimedia projects

8–10 minutes

"Information wants to be free" is an expression that means either that all people should be able to access information freely, or that information (formulated as an actor) naturally strives to become as freely available among people as possible. It is often used by technology activists to criticize laws that limit transparency and general access to information. People who criticize [intellectual](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intellectual_property#Criticisms) [property](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intellectual_property#Criticisms) law say the system of such government-granted monopolies conflicts with the development of a public domain of information. The expression is often credited to [Stewart Brand,](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stewart_Brand) who was recorded saying it at a Hackers Conference in 1984.[\[1\]](about:reader?url=https%3A%2F%2Fen.m.wikipedia.org%2Fwiki%2FInformation_wants_to_be_free#cite_note-R._Polk_Wagner-1)

History [edit](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Information_wants_to_be_free&action=edit§ion=1)

The phrase is attributed to [Stewart Brand,](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stewart_Brand) [\[1\]](about:reader?url=https%3A%2F%2Fen.m.wikipedia.org%2Fwiki%2FInformation_wants_to_be_free#cite_note-R._Polk_Wagner-1) who, in the late 1960s, founded the [Whole Earth Catalog](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whole_Earth_Catalog) and argued that technology could be liberating rather than oppressing.[\[2\]](about:reader?url=https%3A%2F%2Fen.m.wikipedia.org%2Fwiki%2FInformation_wants_to_be_free#cite_note-Ronald_J._Baker-2) What is considered the earliest recorded occurrence of the expression was at the first [Hackers Conference](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hackers_Conference) in 1984, although the video recording of the conversation shows that what Brand actually said is slightly different. Brand told [Steve Wozniak:](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Wozniak)

On the one hand you have—the point you're making Woz—is that information sort of wants to be expensive because it is so valuable —the right information in the right place just changes your life. On the other hand, information almost wants to be free because the costs of getting it out is getting lower and lower all of the time. So you have these two things fighting against each other.[\[3\]](about:reader?url=https%3A%2F%2Fen.m.wikipedia.org%2Fwiki%2FInformation_wants_to_be_free#cite_note-3)

Brand's conference remarks are transcribed accurately by Joshua Gan[s\[4\]](about:reader?url=https%3A%2F%2Fen.m.wikipedia.org%2Fwiki%2FInformation_wants_to_be_free#cite_note-4) in his research on the quote as used by Steve Levy in his own history of the phrase.[\[5\]](about:reader?url=https%3A%2F%2Fen.m.wikipedia.org%2Fwiki%2FInformation_wants_to_be_free#cite_note-5)

A later form appears in his The Media Lab: Inventing the Future at MIT: [\[6\]](about:reader?url=https%3A%2F%2Fen.m.wikipedia.org%2Fwiki%2FInformation_wants_to_be_free#cite_note-6)

Information Wants To Be Free. Information also wants to be expensive. ... That tension will not go away.[\[7\]](about:reader?url=https%3A%2F%2Fen.m.wikipedia.org%2Fwiki%2FInformation_wants_to_be_free#cite_note-7)

According to historian Adrian Johns, the slogan expresses a view that had already been articulated in the mid-20th century by [Norbert](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norbert_Wiener) [Wiener,](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norbert_Wiener) [Michael Polanyi](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Polanyi) and [Arnold Plant,](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnold_Plant) who advocated for the free communication of scientific knowledge, and specifically criticized the [patent](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patent) system[.\[8\]](about:reader?url=https%3A%2F%2Fen.m.wikipedia.org%2Fwiki%2FInformation_wants_to_be_free#cite_note-8)

## Gratis versus libre [edit](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Information_wants_to_be_free&action=edit§ion=2)

The various forms of the original statement are ambiguous: the slogan can be used to argue the benefits of propertied information, of liberated, free, and open information, or of both. It can be taken amorally as an expression of a fact of information-science: once information has passed to a new location outside of the source's control there is no way of ensuring it is not propagated further, and therefore will naturally tend towards a state where that information is widely distributed. Much of its force is due to the [anthropomorphic](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pathetic_fallacy) [metaphor](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pathetic_fallacy) that imputes desire to information. In 1990 [Richard](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Stallman) [Stallman](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Stallman) restated the concept normatively, without the [anthropomorphization:](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropomorphism)

I believe that all generally useful information should be free. By "free" I am not referring to price, but rather to the freedom to copy the information and to adapt it to one's own uses ... When information is generally useful, redistributing it makes humanity wealthier no matter who is distributing and no matter who is receiving.[\[9\]](about:reader?url=https%3A%2F%2Fen.m.wikipedia.org%2Fwiki%2FInformation_wants_to_be_free#cite_note-denning-9)

Stallman's reformulation incorporates a political stance into Brand's [value-neutral](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Value_judgment) observation of social trends.

#### Cypherpunk [edit](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Information_wants_to_be_free&action=edit§ion=3)

Brand's attribution of will to an abstract human construct (information) has been adopted within a branch of the [cypherpunk](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cypherpunk) movement, whose members espouse a particular political viewpoint ([anarchism\)](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarchism). The construction of the statement takes its meaning beyond the simple judgmental observation, "Information should be free", by acknowledging that the internal force or [entelechy](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entelechy) of information and knowledge makes it essentially incompatible with notions of [proprietary software,](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proprietary_software) copyrights, patents, [subscription](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subscription_service) [services,](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subscription_service) etc. They believe that information is dynamic, evergrowing and evolving and cannot be contained within (any) [ideological](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ideological) structure.[[citation needed](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed)]

According to this philosophy, [hackers,](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hacker_(computer_security)) [crackers,](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_cracking) and [phreakers](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phreaker) are liberators of information which is being held hostage by agents demanding money for its release. Other participants in this network include [cypherpunks](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cypherpunk) who educate people to use [public-key](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public-key_cryptography) [cryptography](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public-key_cryptography) to protect the privacy of their messages from corporate or governmental snooping and [programmers](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Programmer) who write free software and [open source](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_source) [code.](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Code) Still others create [Free-Nets](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free-Net) allowing users to gain access to computer resources for which they would otherwise need an account. They might also break copyright law by swapping music, movies, or other copyrighted materials over the Internet.[[citation needed](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed)]

[Chelsea Manning](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelsea_Manning) is alleged to have said "Information should be free["\[10\]](about:reader?url=https%3A%2F%2Fen.m.wikipedia.org%2Fwiki%2FInformation_wants_to_be_free#cite_note-10) to [Adrian Lamo](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Lamo) when explaining a rationale for US government documents to be released to [Wiki Leaks.](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiki Leaks) The narrative goes on with Manning wondering if she is a "'hacker', 'cracker', 'hacktivist', 'leaker' or what".[\[11\]](about:reader?url=https%3A%2F%2Fen.m.wikipedia.org%2Fwiki%2FInformation_wants_to_be_free#cite_note-11)

## Literary usage [edit](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Information_wants_to_be_free&action=edit§ion=4)

In the ["Fall Revolution"](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Star_Fraction) series of [science-](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_fiction)fiction books, author [Ken](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Macleod) [Macleod](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Macleod) riffs and puns on the expression by writing about entities composed of information actually "wanting", as in desiring, freedom and the machinations of several human characters with differing political and ideological agendas, to facilitate or disrupt these entities' quest for freedom.

In the [Warcross](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warcross) duology by Marie Lu, the virtual space "The Pirate's Den" sports the slogan.

In the cyberpunk world of [post-singularity](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technological_singularity) transhuman culture described by [Charles Stross](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Stross) in his books like [Accelerando](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accelerando_(novel)) and [Singularity Sky](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singularity_Sky), the wish of information to be free is a law of nature. [\[12\]](about:reader?url=https%3A%2F%2Fen.m.wikipedia.org%2Fwiki%2FInformation_wants_to_be_free#cite_note-accelerando-12)[\[13\]](about:reader?url=https%3A%2F%2Fen.m.wikipedia.org%2Fwiki%2FInformation_wants_to_be_free#cite_note-Singularity_Sky-13)

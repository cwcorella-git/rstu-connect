**James Cleveland** "**Jesse**" **Owens** (September 12, 1913 – March 31, 1980) was an American [track and field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Track_and_field) athlete who made history at the [1936 Olympic Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletics_at_the_1936_Summer_Olympics) by winning four gold medals[.\[4\]](#page-48-2)[\[5\]](#page-48-3) He is widely regarded as one of the greatest athletes in track and field history[.\[6\]](#page-48-4)[\[7\]](#page-49-0)[\[8\]](#page-49-1)

Owens excelled in events like short [sprints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sprint_(running)) and the [long jump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_jump) and was recognized in his lifetime as "perhaps the greatest and most famous athlete in track and field history"[.\[9\]](#page-49-2) He won four events and set five world records and tied another, all in less than an hour, at the 1935 [Big Ten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Ten_Conference) Championships in [Ann Arbor,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ann_Arbor,_Michigan) [Michigan,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ann_Arbor,_Michigan) a feat that has never been equaled and has been called "the greatest 45 minutes ever in sport"[.\[10\]](#page-49-3) He won four [NCAA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Collegiate_Athletic_Association) titles in both 1935 and 1936, bringing his total to eight an unparalleled achievement that remains unmatched to this day[.\[11\]](#page-49-4)

He achieved international fame at the 1936 Summer Olympics in [Berlin,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin) Germany, by winning four gold medals: 100 meters, long jump, 200 meters, and 4 × 100-meter relay. He was the most successful athlete at the Games and, as a black American man, was credited by [ESPN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN) with "single-handedly crushing [Hitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Hitler)'s myth of [Aryan supremacy"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aryan_supremacy)[.\[12\]](#page-49-5)

The [Jesse Owens Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesse_Owens_Award) is [USA Track & Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USA_Track_%26_Field)'s highest accolade for the year's best track and field athlete[.\[13\]](#page-49-6) In a 1950 [Associated Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associated_Press) poll, Owens was voted the greatest track and field athlete for the first half of the century[.\[14\]](#page-49-7) In 1999, he was on the six-man short-list for the [BBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC)'s [Sports Personality of the](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Sports_Personality_of_the_Year#Sports_Personality_of_the_Century_Award) [Century.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Sports_Personality_of_the_Year#Sports_Personality_of_the_Century_Award) [\[15\]](#page-49-8) That same year, he was ranked the sixth greatest [North American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_America) athlete of the twentieth century and the highest-ranked in his sport by ESPN[.\[16\]](#page-49-9)

### **Early life and education**

Jesse Owens, originally known as J. C., was the youngest of ten children (three girls and seven boys) born to Henry Cleveland Owens [1881–1942] (a [sharecropper\)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharecropping) and Mary Emma Fitzgerald in [Oakville, Alabama,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oakville,_Alabama) on September 12, 1913. He was the grandson of a slave[.\[4\]](#page-48-2) At the age of nine, he and his family moved to [Cleveland,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland) [Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio) for better opportunities as part of the [Great Migration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Migration_(African_American)) (1910–70) when millions of African Americans left the [segregated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racial_segregation_in_the_United_States) and rural South for the urban and industrial North. When his new teacher asked his name to enter in her roll book, he said "J. C.", but because of his strong Southern accent, she thought he said "Jesse". The name stuck, and he was known as Jesse Owens for the rest of his life.[\[17\]](#page-50-0) In his younger years, Owens took different menial jobs in his spare time: he delivered groceries, loaded freight cars, and worked in a shoe repair shop while his father and older brother worked at a steel mill[.\[18\]](#page-50-1) During this period, Owens realized that he had a passion for running. Throughout his life, Owens attributed the success of his athletic career to the encouragement of Charles Riley, his [junior high school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_school) track coach at Fairmount Junior High School. Since Owens worked after school, Riley allowed him to practice before school instead.

Owens and Minnie Ruth Solomon (1915–2001) met at Fairmont Junior High School in Cleveland when he was 15 and she was 13. They dated steadily through high school. Ruth gave birth to their first daughter Gloria in 1932. They married on July 5, 1935, and had two more daughters together: Marlene, born in 1937, and Beverly, born in 1940. They remained married until his death in 1980[.\[19\]](#page-50-2)[\[20\]](#page-50-3)

Owens first came to national attention when he was a student of [East Technical High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Technical_High_School) in Cleveland; he equaled the world record of 9.4 seconds in the 100 yards (91 m) dash, broke the national high school record with 20.7 seconds in the 220 yards (201 m) dash, and long-jumped 24 feet 91⁄2 inches (7.56 m) at the 1933 National High School Championship in [Chicago.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago) [\[21\]](#page-50-4) His 100-yard dash remained the national high school record until 1967, while his 200-yard dash held the national record for 20 years[.\[22\]](#page-50-5)

### **Career**

#### **Ohio State University**

Owens attended [Ohio State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_State_University) after his father found employment, which ensured that the family could be supported[.\[23\]](#page-50-6) Affectionately known as the "Buckeye Bullet" and under the coaching of [Larry Snyder,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Snyder_(athlete)) Owens won a record eight individual [NCAA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NCAA) championships, four each in 1935 and 1936[.\[10\]](#page-49-3) His career total of eight individual NCAA titles remains the most, despite only two years of Varsity competition—which included an undefeated junior year in 1936 where he won all 42 events he entered[.\[24\]](#page-50-7) Though Owens enjoyed athletic success, he had to live off campus with other African-American athletes. When he traveled with the team, Owens was restricted to ordering carry-out or eating at "blacks-only" restaurants. Similarly, he had to stay at "blacksonly" hotels. Owens did not receive a scholarship for his efforts, so he continued to work part-time jobs to pay for school.[\[25\]](#page-50-8)

#### **Day of days**

May 25, 1935, is remembered as the day when Jesse Owens won four events and established six [world records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_world_records_in_athletics) in [athletics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport_of_athletics)

at the [Big Ten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Ten_Conference) Championships[.\[26\]](#page-50-9) On that day, Owens battled through a lower back injury and set five [world records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_records) and tied a sixth in a span of 45 minutes from 3:15–4 p.m. during the [Big](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Ten_Conference) [Ten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Ten_Conference) meet at [Ferry Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferry_Field) in [Ann Arbor,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ann_Arbor,_Michigan) Michigan[.\[27\]](#page-51-0) He equaled the world record for the [100-yard dash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/100-yard_dash) (9.4 seconds) (not to be confused with the 100-meter dash), and set world records in the [long jump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_jump) (26 feet 81⁄4 inches or 8.13 metres, a world record that would last for 25 years); 220 yards (201.2 m) sprint (20.3 seconds); and 220-yard [low hurdles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low_hurdles) (22.6 seconds, becoming the first to break 23 seconds)[.\[28\]](#page-51-1) Both 220-yard records had also beaten the metric records for [200 meters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/200_meters) (flat and hurdles), which counted as two additional world records from the same performances[.\[29\]](#page-51-2)[\[12\]](#page-49-5) In 2005, [University of](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Central_Florida) [Central Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Central_Florida) professor of sports history Richard C. Crepeau chose these wins on one day as the most impressive athletic achievement since 1850[.\[30\]](#page-51-3)

#### **1936 Big Ten Championships**

At the 1936 Big Ten Championships, Owens dominated the competition, winning the long jump, 100-yard dash, 220-yard dash, and [100-yard low hurdles.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/100_metres_hurdles) With these victories, he concluded his Big Ten Championship career undefeated—nine titles in nine events[.\[b\]](#page-45-1)[\[31\]](#page-51-4)

#### **USA Track and Field Championships**

At the [1934 USA Indoor Track and Field Championships,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1934_USA_Indoor_Track_and_Field_Championships) Owens captured the long jump gold with a world-record leap of 25 ft 3 1⁄8 in (7.699 m)[.\[32\]](#page-51-5) Two years later, at his final appearance at the [Outdoor Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USA_Outdoor_Track_and_Field_Championships) in [1936,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1936_USA_Outdoor_Track_and_Field_Championships) he shattered the long jump world record once again with a remarkable jump of 26 feet, 8¼ inches. That same meet, he also set a new championship record in the 100 meters, clocking in at 10.4 seconds[.\[33\]](#page-51-6) Over the course of his career at these championships, Owens amassed a total of six gold medals five in the long jump and one in the 100 meters[.\[34\]](#page-51-7)

#### **1936 Berlin Summer Olympics**

## ![](_page_8_Picture_4.jpeg)

Owens competing in the long jump at the [1936 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1936_Summer_Olympics) in [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin)

On December 4, 1935, [NAACP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NAACP) Secretary [Walter Francis White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Francis_White) wrote a letter to Owens, but never sent it[.\[35\]](#page-51-8) He was trying to dissuade Owens from taking part in the [1936 Summer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1936_Summer_Olympics) [Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1936_Summer_Olympics) in [Nazi Germany,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Germany) arguing that an African American should not promote a racist regime after what his race had suffered at the hands of racists in his own country. In the months prior to the Games, a movement gained momentum in favor of a boycott. Owens was convinced by the NAACP to declare: "If there are minorities in Germany who are being discriminated against, the United States should withdraw from the 1936 Olympics". Yet he and others eventually took part after [Avery Brundage,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avery_Brundage) president of the [American Olympic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Olympic_Committee) [Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Olympic_Committee) branded them "un-American agitators"[.\[36\]](#page-52-0)

## ![](_page_9_Picture_5.jpeg)

2015 photograph of Jesse Owens's room in the 1936 Olympic Village in Berlin

In 1936, Owens and his United States teammates sailed on the [SS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SS_Manhattan_(1931)) [Manhattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SS_Manhattan_(1931)) and arrived in Germany to compete at the Summer Olympics in Berlin. Just before the competitions, founder of [Adidas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adidas) athletic shoe company [Adi Dassler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adi_Dassler) visited Owens in the Olympic village and persuaded Owens to wear Gebrüder Dassler Schuhfabrik shoes; this was the first sponsorship for a male African American athlete[.\[37\]](#page-52-1)

On August 3, Owens won the 100 m das[h\[38\]](#page-52-2) with a time of 10.3 seconds, defeating a teammate and a college friend[\[2\]](#page-48-1) [Ralph Metcalfe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ralph_Metcalfe) by a tenth of a second and defeating [Tinus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tinus_Osendarp)

[Osendarp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tinus_Osendarp) of the Netherlands by two-tenths of a second.

On August 4, he won the long jump with a leap of 8.06 metres (26 ft 5 in) (3¼ inches short of his own world record). He initially credited this achievement to the technical advice that he received from [Luz Long,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luz_Long) the German competitor whom he defeated[,\[12\]](#page-49-5) but later admitted that this was not true, as he and Long did not meet until after the competition was over.[\[39\]](#page-52-3) On August 5, he won the 200 meter sprint with a time of 20.7 seconds, defeating fellow American teammate [Mack Robinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mack_Robinson_(athlete)) (the older brother of [Jackie Robinson\)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackie_Robinson).

On August 9, Owens won his fourth gold medal in the 4 × 100 m sprint relay when head coach [Lawson Robertson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawson_Robertson) replaced Jewish-American sprinters [Marty Glickman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marty_Glickman) and [Sam Stoller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Stoller) with Owens and Ralph Metcalfe[,\[40\]](#page-52-4) who teamed with [Frank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Wykoff) [Wykoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Wykoff) and [Foy Draper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foy_Draper) to set a world record of 39.8 seconds in the event[.\[41\]](#page-52-5) Owens had initially protested the last-minute switch, but assistant coach [Dean Cromwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dean_Cromwell) said to him, "You'll do as you are told."[[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed)] Owens's record-breaking performance of four gold medals was not equaled until [Carl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Lewis) [Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Lewis) won gold medals in the same events at the [1984](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_Summer_Olympics) [Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_Summer_Olympics) in [Los Angeles.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles) Owens had set the world record in the long jump with a leap of 8.13 m (26 ft 8 in) in 1935, the year before the Berlin Olympics, and this record stood for 25 years until it was broken in 1960 by countryman [Ralph Boston.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ralph_Boston) Coincidentally, Owens was a spectator at the [1960 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1960_Summer_Olympics) in Rome when Boston took the gold medal in the long jump.

The long-jump victory is documented, along with many other 1936 events, in the 1938 film [Olympia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympia_(1938_film)) by [Leni Riefenstahl.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leni_Riefenstahl) On August 1, 1936, Nazi Germany's leader, [Adolf Hitler,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Hitler) shook hands with the German victors only and then left the stadium. International Olympic Committee president [Henri de Baillet-](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_de_Baillet-Latour)[Latour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_de_Baillet-Latour) insisted that Hitler greet every medalist or none at all. Hitler opted for the latter and skipped all further medal presentations[.\[42\]](#page-52-6)[\[43\]](#page-52-7)

Owens ran his first race on Day 2 of the Olympics (August 2). That day, He ran in the first (10:30 a.m.) and second (3:00 p.m.) qualifying rounds for the 100-meter final. He tied the Olympic and world record in the first race and broke them in the second race, but the new time was not recognized, because it was wind-assisted[.\[44\]](#page-52-8) Later the same day, Owens's African-American team-mate [Cornelius Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornelius_Johnson_(athlete)) won gold in the high jump final (which began at 5:00 p.m.) with a new Olympic record of 2.03 meters[.\[45\]](#page-52-9) Hitler did not publicly congratulate any of the medal winners this time; even so, the communist New York City newspaper the [Daily Worker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daily_Worker) claimed Hitler received all the track winners except Johnson and left the stadium as a "deliberate snub" after watching Johnson's winning jump[.\[46\]](#page-53-0) Hitler was subsequently accused of failing to acknowledge Owens (who won gold medals on August 3, 4 (two), and 9) or shake his hand. Owens responded to these claims at the time:

Hitler had a certain time to come to the stadium and a certain time to leave. It happened he had to leave before the victory ceremony after the 100 meters [race began at 5:45 p.m[.\[47\]\]](#page-53-1).

But before he left I was on my way to a broadcast and passed near his box. He waved at me and I waved back. I think it was bad taste to criticize the "man of the hour" in another country. [\[48\]](#page-53-2)[\[49\]](#page-53-3)

In an article dated August 4, 1936, the African-American newspaper editor [Robert L. Vann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_L._Vann) describes witnessing Hitler "salute" Owens for having won gold in the 100 m sprint (August 3):

And then ... wonder of wonders ... I saw Herr Adolph [sic] Hitler, salute this lad. I looked on with a heart which beat proudly as the lad who was crowned king of the 100 meters event, get an ovation the like of which I have never heard before. I saw Jesse Owens greeted by the Grand Chancellor of this country as a brilliant sun peeped out through the clouds. I saw a vast crowd of some 85,000 or 90,000 people stand up and cheer him to the echo[.\[50\]](#page-53-4)

Owens salutes the American flag after winning the long jump at the [1936 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1936_Summer_Olympics). (L–R) [Naoto Tajima,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naoto_Tajima) Owens, [Luz Long.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luz_Long)

In 2014, [Eric Brown,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Brown_(pilot)) British fighter pilot and test pilot, aged 17 in 1936 and later becoming the [Fleet Air Arm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fleet_Air_Arm)'s most decorated pilot[,\[51\]](#page-54-0) stated in a [BBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC) documentary: "I actually witnessed Hitler shaking hands with Jesse Owens and congratulating him on what he had achieved"[.\[52\]](#page-54-1) Additionally, an article in [The](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Baltimore_Sun) [Baltimore Sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Baltimore_Sun) in August 1936 reported that Hitler sent Owens a commemorative inscribed cabinet photograph of himself.[\[53\]](#page-54-2) Later, on October 15, 1936, Owens repeated this claim when he addressed an audience of African Americans at a Republican rally in [Kansas City,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_City) remarking: "Hitler didn't snub me—it was our president who snubbed me. The president didn't even send me a telegram.["\[54\]](#page-55-0)[\[55\]](#page-55-1)[\[56\]](#page-55-2)

Owens's success at the games caused consternation for Hitler, who was using them to show the world a resurgent Nazi Germany[.\[57\]](#page-55-3) He and other government officials had hoped that German athletes would dominate the games[.\[57\]](#page-55-3)[\[58\]](#page-55-4) Nazi minister [Albert Speer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Speer) wrote that Hitler "was highly annoyed by the series of triumphs by the marvelous colored American runner, Jesse Owens. People whose antecedents came from the jungle were primitive, Hitler said with a shrug; their physiques were stronger than those of civilized whites and hence should be excluded from future games.["\[59\]](#page-55-5)

In Germany, Owens had been allowed to travel with and stay in the same hotels as whites, at a time when [African Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Americans) in many parts of the United States had to stay in [segregated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racial_segregation_in_the_United_States) hotels that accommodated only blacks[.\[60\]](#page-55-6) When Owens returned to the United States, he was greeted in New York City by Mayor [Fiorello La Guardia.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiorello_La_Guardia) [\[61\]](#page-55-7) During a [Manhattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan) [ticker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ticker-tape_parade)[tape parade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ticker-tape_parade)[\[62\]](#page-55-8) in his honor along Broadway's [Canyon of](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canyon_of_Heroes) [Heroes,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canyon_of_Heroes) someone handed Owens a paper bag. Owens paid it little mind until the parade concluded. When he opened it up, he found that the bag contained \$10,000 in cash (equivalent to \$227,000 in 2024). Owens's wife Ruth later said: "And he [Owens] didn't know who was good enough to do a thing like that. And with all the excitement around, he didn't pick it up right away. He didn't pick it up until he got ready to get out of the car"[.\[63\]](#page-56-0)

After the parade, Owens was not permitted to enter through the main doors of the [Waldorf Astoria New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waldorf_Astoria_New_York) and instead forced to travel up to the reception honoring him in a freight elevator[.\[60\]](#page-55-6)[\[64\]](#page-56-1) President [Franklin D. Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_D._Roosevelt) (FDR) never invited Jesse Owens to the White House following his triumphs at the Olympic Games[.\[65\]](#page-56-2) When the Democrats bid for his support, Owens rejected those overtures: as a staunch Republican, he endorsed [Alf Landon,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alf_Landon) Roosevelt's Republican opponent in the [1936 presidential race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1936_United_States_presidential_election). [\[66\]](#page-56-3)[\[67\]](#page-56-4) Owens was employed to do campaign outreach for African American votes for Landon in the [1936 presidential election.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1936_United_States_presidential_election) [\[68\]](#page-56-5)[\[69\]](#page-56-6)

# **Life after the Olympics**

## ![](_page_14_Picture_5.jpeg)

## ![](_page_15_Picture_2.jpeg)

Owens was quoted saying the secret behind his success was, "I let my feet spend as little time on the ground as possible. From the air, fast down, and from the ground, fast up."[\[70\]](#page-56-7)[\[71\]](#page-56-8)

After the 1936 Olympics, [Avery Brundage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avery_Brundage) organized a grueling European exhibition tour to profit the [AAU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amateur_Athletic_Union) and [USOC,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Olympic_%26_Paralympic_Committee) both of which he led. Owens, exhausted but pressured to compete, ran multiple races across Europe with little rest, food, or support. Despite such treatment, Brundage continued booking events across [Scandinavia.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scandinavia) Owens, drained and frustrated, eventually refused to continue. Brundage retaliated by having Owens permanently suspended from amateur competition which immediately ended his career. Owens was angry and stated that "A fellow desires something for himself." As Ruth Owens later recalled, "That Avery Brundage feller tore a big hole inside Jesse.["\[72\]](#page-57-0)[\[73\]](#page-57-1) Owens argued that the [racial discrimination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racism_in_the_United_States) he had faced throughout his athletic career, such as not being eligible for scholarships in college and therefore being unable to take classes between training and working to pay his way, meant he had to give up on amateur athletics in pursuit of financial gain elsewhere[.\[74\]](#page-57-2)

After returning to the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States) following his Olympic success, racism back home led to difficulty earning a living despite his international acclaim. Owens struggled to find work and took on menial jobs as a gas station attendant, playground janitor[,\[75\]](#page-57-3) and manager of a dry cleaning firm and at times resorted to racing against motorbikes, cars, trucks and horses for a cash prize[.\[76\]](#page-57-4)[\[77\]](#page-57-5)

People say it was degrading for an Olympic champion to run against a horse, but what was I supposed to do? I had four gold medals, but you can't eat four gold medals[.\[76\]](#page-57-4)

Jesse Owens had broken racial barriers and done things that no other man had done before him. Yet after he returned home from the [Olympic Games,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_Games) he was not greeted with the glory and praise that other White Olympians had received. Owens stated, "No one had offered me a job" and "I had jumped farther and run faster than any man ever had before, and it left me with next to nothing.["\[78\]](#page-57-6) Jesse wasn't being treated like an Olympic gold medalist but instead, just like any other African American at that time. Another quote said "So I sold myself into a new kind of slavery. I was no longer a proud man who had won four Olympic gold medals. I was a spectacle, a freak who made his living by competing—dishonestly—against dumb animals.["\[78\]](#page-57-6) Despite his athletic triumphs, he was not spared from poverty and was forced to take on degrading work just to afford basic necessities.

Owens bridged the gap between racial disenfranchisement and opportunity. His Olympic medals showed the Jim Crow South and the world what was possible when African Americans were given a fair chance. Though many resisted racial integration, Owens served as a key figure for the beginning building blocks of the civil rights movement.[\[79\]](#page-57-7)

Owens was banned from attending amateur events to enhance his visibility, and soon discovered that commercial opportunities had almost completely dried up. In 1937, he briefly toured with a twelve-piece jazz band under contract with Consolidated Artists but found it unfulfilling. He also made appearances at baseball games and other events.[\[80\]](#page-57-8)

Owens was involved politically and lent his support to the Republican Party and [Alf Landon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Landon) in the [1936 United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1936_United_States_Presidential_Election) [Presidential Election,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1936_United_States_Presidential_Election) saying that [Adolf Hitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Hitler) congratulated him but that he was snubbed by President [Franklin Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_Roosevelt) after winning a gold medal[.\[81\]](#page-57-9)[\[82\]](#page-58-0) In 1942, [Willis Ward—](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willis_Ward)a friend and former competitor from the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan)[\[83\]—](#page-58-1)who was then working at [Ford Motor Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Motor_Company) as Assistant Personnel Director, invited Owens to [Detroit.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detroit) Ward worked for the [Ford Motor Company's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Motor_Company) "ad hoc civil rights division, serving as the liaison between black and white workers"[\[84\]](#page-58-2)[\[85\]](#page-58-3) and was an advocate for African American employees in the personnel department. Owens wound up replacing him, and remained with Ford until 1946[.\[86\]](#page-58-4) In the late 1940s, Owens moved his family to Chicago and opened his own [public](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_relations) [relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_relations) agency.

In 1946, Owens collaborated with [Abe Saperstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abe_Saperstein) to establish the [West Coast Negro Baseball League,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Coast_Negro_Baseball_League) where he served as Vice-President and owned the [Portland \(Oregon\) Rosebuds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_Rosebuds_(baseball)) franchise in Oregon[.\[87\]](#page-58-5) He toured with the Rosebuds, sometimes entertaining the audience in between [doubleheader games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doubleheader_(baseball)) by competing in races against horses[.\[88\]](#page-58-6) The WCBA disbanded after only two months[.\[89\]](#page-58-7)[\[88\]](#page-58-6) Owens helped promote the [exploitation film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exploitation_film) [Mom and Dad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mom_and_Dad_(1945_film)) in

African American neighborhoods[.\[90\]](#page-59-0) He tried to make a living as a sports promoter, essentially an entertainer. He would give local sprinters a ten- or twenty-yard start and beat them in the 100-yd (91-m) dash. He also challenged and defeated racehorses; as he revealed later, the trick was to race a highstrung [Thoroughbred](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thoroughbred) that would be frightened by the starter's shotgun and give him a bad jump. On the lack of opportunities, Owens added, "There was no television, no big advertising, no endorsements then. Not for a black man, anyway."[\[74\]](#page-57-2)

He traveled to [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome) for the [1960 Summer Olympics,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1960_Summer_Olympics) where he met the 1960 100 meters champion [Armin Hary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armin_Hary) of Germany, who had defeated American [Dave Sime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_Sime) in a [photo finish.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photo_finish) [\[91\]](#page-59-1) During spring training in 1965, Owens was hired by the [New](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Mets) [York Mets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Mets) as a running instructor[.\[92\]](#page-59-2)

Owens ran a [dry cleaning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dry_cleaning) business and worked as a gas station attendant to earn a living, but he eventually filed for [bankruptcy.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bankruptcy) In 1966, he was successfully prosecuted for [tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax_evasion) [evasion.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax_evasion) [\[93\]](#page-59-3) At rock bottom, he was aided in beginning his rehabilitation. Republican President Dwight D. Eisenhower enlisted Owens as a [goodwill ambassador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodwill_ambassador) in 1955 and sent the world-renowned track star to India, the Philippines, and Malaya to promote physical exercise as well as tout the cause of American freedom and economic opportunity in the developing world. He would continue his goodwill tours in the 1960s and 1970s. Although he lost his patronage job with the Illinois Youth Commission in 1960, Owens continued his product endorsement work for such corporations as Quaker Oats, Sears and Roebuck, and Johnson & Johnson. Owens traveled the world and spoke to companies such as the [Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Motor_Company) [Motor Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Motor_Company) and stakeholders such as the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Olympic_Committee) [Olympic Committee.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Olympic_Committee) [\[94\]](#page-59-4) In 1972, he and his wife retired to Arizona[.\[95\]](#page-59-5)

Owens initially refused to support the [black power salute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1968_Olympics_Black_Power_salute) by [African-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African-American) sprinters [Tommie Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tommie_Smith) and [John Carlos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Carlos) at the [1968 Summer Olympics.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1968_Summer_Olympics) He told them[:\[96\]](#page-59-6)

The black fist is a meaningless symbol. When you open it, you have nothing but fingers—weak, empty fingers. The only time the black fist has significance is when there's money inside. There's where the power lies.

Four years later in his 1972 book I Have Changed, he revised his opinion:

I realized now that militancy in the best sense of the word was the only answer where the black man was concerned, that any black man who wasn't a militant in 1970 was either blind or a coward.

Owens traveled to [Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich) for the [1972 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1972_Summer_Olympics) as a special guest of the West German government[,\[97\]](#page-59-7) meeting West German Chancellor [Willy Brandt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willy_Brandt) and former boxer [Max](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Schmeling) [Schmeling.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Schmeling) [\[98\]](#page-59-8)

From 1974 to 1977, Owens served on the [Boys Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boys_Town_(organization)) Board of Directors, frequently meeting with students to share his life experiences and the challenges he overcame[.\[99\]](#page-59-9)

A few months before his death, Owens had unsuccessfully tried to convince [President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_United_States) [Jimmy Carter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Carter) to withdraw his demand that the United States [boycott the 1980 Moscow Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_Summer_Olympics_boycott) in protest of the [Soviet invasion of Afghanistan.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet%E2%80%93Afghan_War) He argued that the Olympic ideal was supposed to be observed as a time-out from war and that it was above politics[.\[100\]](#page-60-0)

#### **Death**

## ![](_page_20_Picture_4.jpeg)

## Owens's grave at Oak Woods Cemetery

Owens was a pack-a-day [cigarette smoker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobacco_smoking) for 35 years, starting at age 32[.\[101\]](#page-60-1) Beginning in December 1979, he was hospitalized on and off with an extremely aggressive and [drug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drug-resistant)[resistant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drug-resistant) type of [lung cancer.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lung_cancer) He died of the disease at age 66 in [Tucson, Arizona,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tucson,_Arizona) on March 31, 1980, with his wife and other family members at his bedside[.\[102\]](#page-60-2) He was buried next to the Lake of Memories at [Oak Woods Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oak_Woods_Cemetery) in Chicago, near where his children and extended family still lived. The grave is inscribed:

Jesse Owens. Olympic Champion. 1936. Athlete and humanitarian. A master of the spirit as well as the mechanics of sports. A winner who knew that winning was not everything. He showed extraordinary love for his family and friends. His achievements have shown us all the promise of America. His faith in America inspired countless others to do their best for themselves and their country. September 12, 1913 – March 31, 1980.

President Jimmy Carter issued a tribute to Owens, stating: "Perhaps no athlete better symbolized the human struggle against tyranny, poverty and racial bigotry."[\[103\]](#page-60-3)

### **Legacy**

## ![](_page_21_Picture_5.jpeg)

Waxwork of Owens at [Madame Tussauds,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madame_Tussauds) London

Owens is widely considered one of the greatest [athletes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletes) in the history of [track and field.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Track_and_field) [\[104\]](#page-60-4)[\[105\]](#page-60-5) Over the course of his career, he earned nine [Big Ten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Ten_Conference) title[s\[b\],](#page-45-1) eight [NCAA titles,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NCAA_Division_I_Men%27s_Outdoor_Track_and_Field_Championships) and six [USA Track & Field titles.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USA_Track_%26_Field_Championships) [\[106\]](#page-60-6)[\[31\]](#page-51-4) His international legacy was cemented at the [Olympics,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1936_Summer_Olympics) where he won gold in all four events he entered—each in [Olympic record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_record) time–delivering a powerful rebuttal to [Adolf Hitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Hitler)'s [ideology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ideology) of [Aryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aryan_race) supremacy and dealt a symbolic blow to the [Nazi regime's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Germany) [racist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propaganda_in_Nazi_Germany) [propaganda.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propaganda_in_Nazi_Germany) [\[107\]](#page-60-7)[\[108\]](#page-60-8) Several of his world records endured for decades, including his long jump record, which lasted 25 years, and his 100-meter dash record, which stood for 20.[\[109\]](#page-60-9)[\[110\]](#page-61-0)

Following his athletic career, Owens experienced difficulties securing financial stability, a circumstance attributed in part to limited opportunities available to [African American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_American) athletes during that period[.\[111\]](#page-61-1) Although he was celebrated for his Olympic accomplishments, he was not invited to the [White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_House) [House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_House) or formally recognized by the [U. S. government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_government_of_the_United_States) at the time[.\[112\]](#page-61-2) Later in life, his contributions to sport and society were acknowledged through various honors, including the [Presidential Medal of Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Medal_of_Freedom) in [1976](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Presidential_Medal_of_Freedom_recipients#Awarded_by_Gerald_Ford) and the [Congressional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congressional_Gold_Medal) [Gold Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congressional_Gold_Medal) posthumously in [1990.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Congressional_Gold_Medal_recipients#Recipients_of_Congressional_Gold_Medals) [\[113\]](#page-61-3)

"Giants like Jesse Owens show us why politics will never defeat the Olympic spirit. His character, his achievements have continued to inspire Americans as they did the whole world in 1936.["\[114\]](#page-61-4) — [Gerald Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerald_Ford)

Owens has been honored with schools, streets, and athletic facilities named after him—including [Jesse Owens Memorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesse_Owens_Memorial_Stadium) [Stadium—](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesse_Owens_Memorial_Stadium)and his life has inspired documentaries, books, and the biopic [Race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_(2016_film)). [\[115\]](#page-61-5)[\[116\]](#page-61-6)[\[117\]](#page-61-7) Notably, the documentary [Olympic Pride, American Prejudice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_Pride,_American_Prejudice) also highlights his story as part of a broader examination of the 18 Black American athletes who competed in the 1936 Berlin Olympics[.\[118\]](#page-61-8)[\[119\]](#page-61-9) He is also a member of several [halls of fame,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_halls_and_walks_of_fame) including the [U. S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Olympic_%26_Paralympic_Hall_of_Fame) [Olympic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Olympic_%26_Paralympic_Hall_of_Fame) and [National Track and Field Hall of Fame.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Track_and_Field_Hall_of_Fame) [\[120\]](#page-61-10)[\[121\]](#page-62-0)

The [dormitory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dormitory) that Owens occupied during the Berlin Olympics has been fully restored into a living museum, with pictures of his accomplishments at the games, and a letter (intercepted by the [Gestapo\)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gestapo) from a fan urging him not to shake hands with

Hitler[.\[122\]](#page-62-1)[\[123\]](#page-62-2)

### **The Jesse Owens Rising Star Award**

Beginning in 2024, a collaboration among the Owens family, the Jesse Owens Foundation, and the [Wanda Diamond League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wanda_Diamond_League) will recognize two exceptional emerging top-performing male and female athletes, aged 23 or under[.\[278\]](#page-78-0)

Each winner will receive a bronze statuette of Owens designed by Belgian sculptor Jan Desmarets. Two [oak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oak) trees will also be planted in the host city in honor of the two winners.[\[279\]](#page-78-1)

The inaugural awards ceremony was held in [Brussels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brussels) in September 2024, honoring [2023 World Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_World_Athletics_Championships) silver medalist [Diribe Welteji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diribe_Welteji) as the top female performer and [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletics_at_the_2024_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_100_metres) [Olympic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletics_at_the_2024_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_100_metres) gold medalist [Letsile Tebogo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letsile_Tebogo) as the top male performer[.\[280\]](#page-78-2)

## From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

"**Globalize the intifada**" is an [anti-Zionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Zionist) slogan that has been used to advocate for international support of [Palestinian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_right_to_resist) [resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_right_to_resist) against [Israeli occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_occupation) as well as various other causes users of the phrase see as tied to the Palestinian cause. The Arabic word [intifada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intifada) [\(Arabic:](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language) ة َ ضا فَتِْاِن intifāḍa), derived from the [root](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semitic_root)ض ف ن) n f ḍ), means 'a shaking off' and can refer to a popular uprising or [rebellion.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebellion)

In the context of [Palestine,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine) it refers to Palestinian uprisings or resistance against [Israel;](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel) the call to "globalize" it suggests extending the spirit and actions of these uprisings beyond the regional context to a worldwide movement[.\[1\]](#page-6-0)[\[2\]](#page-6-1)[\[3\]](#page-6-2)

The slogan and related chants have been a subject of controversy and discussion regarding their impact and implications. The slogan has been criticized by some Jewish groups as inciting [political violence,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_violence) terrorism, and [antisemitism.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antisemitism) [\[4\]](#page-6-3)[\[5\]](#page-6-4)[\[6\]](#page-6-5)

### **Morphology**

Intifāḍa ( ةضافتنا ( is an [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_Standard_Arabic) maṣ[dar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_verbs#Verbal_noun_(ma%E1%B9%A3dar)) ( ردصم( , or [verbal noun,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_verbs#Verbal_noun_(ma%E1%B9%A3dar)) of [instance](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Noun_of_instance_(Arabic)&action=edit&redlink=1) [\[ar\]](https://ar.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D8%A7%D8%B3%D9%85_%D9%85%D8%B1%D8%A9) ( ةرم مسا [ism marra](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Noun_of_instance_(Arabic)&action=edit&redlink=1) [\[ar\]](https://ar.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D8%A7%D8%B3%D9%85_%D9%85%D8%B1%D8%A9)) of the verb intafaḍa ( ضفتنا( , derived from the [triconsonantal Semitic root](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semitic_root) n-f-ḍ (-ن ض-ف (related to shaking (off), dusting (off), and making something shiver[.\[7\]:](#page-7-0)1157 The verb ضفتنا intafaḍa is in the [verb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_verbs) [form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_verbs) لَ عَتَ ْ اِف iftaʿala, referred to in Western sources as 'form VIII,' denoting [reflexivity.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reflexive_verb)["\[7\]:](#page-7-0)1157

#### **Meaning**

The [Dictionary of Modern Written Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Dictionary_of_Modern_Written_Arabic) gives the meaning of the verb ضفتنا intafaḍa as: "to be shaken off, be dusted off; to shake; to shudder, shiver, tremble (jo with); to sheke of from oneself (i s.th.); to wake up, come to consciousness," and of its verbal noun ةضافتنا intifāḍa as a "shiver, shudder, tremor; awakening (pol.); popular uprising.["\[7\]:](#page-7-0)1157

### **Usage**

The slogan "globalize the intifada" has been used as a chant in various anti-Zionist protests, along with variations like "there is only one solution, intifada revolution" and "intifada until victory"[.\[12\]](#page-7-1)[\[2\]](#page-6-1)[\[13\]](#page-8-0)[\[6\]](#page-6-5) It has been used as a denunciation of Israel's actions during the [Gaza war.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_war) [\[6\]](#page-6-5)[\[14\]](#page-8-1) According to users of the slogan, it was chosen as a rallying cry for resistance against what they see as ["colonial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zionism_as_settler_colonialism) violence and oppression". [\[15\]](#page-8-2)

In April 2002, antiwar protesters in the United States held signs with slogans reflecting their concerns about [racial justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racial_justice) and US involvement in the Middle East, including the [Second](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Intifada) [Intifada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Intifada) and the [Iraqi conflict.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_conflict) [\[16\]](#page-8-3)

Soon after the [October 7 attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_7_attacks) in 2023, [pro-Palestinian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_war_protests_in_the_United_States) [protesters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_war_protests_in_the_United_States) chanted "globalize the intifada" near a [Cooper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooper_Union) [Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooper_Union) library where some [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jews) students were gathering, sparking accusations of [antisemitism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antisemitism) from New York City and national leaders. New York City mayor [Eric Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Adams) responded to the incident, affirming that hate—in this case, anti-Jewish hate—has no place in New York City, emphasizing the right to peaceful protest but not the [normalization of antisemitism.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normalization_of_antisemitism) [\[17\]](#page-8-4) The [NYPD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NYPD) later released a statement that students were not barricaded within the library and that the protest had been preplanned[.\[18\]](#page-8-5) Pro-Palestinian protesters, who said they had planned a march throughout the college ahead of time, argued that the library contained students of diverse backgrounds at the time of the march and that the march itself had included some Jewish students[.\[18\]](#page-8-5)

In November 2023, the pro-Palestinian group [Within Our](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Within_Our_Lifetime) [Lifetime,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Within_Our_Lifetime) which supported the 2023 Hamas attack on Israel,[\[19\]](#page-8-6) posted to [Instagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instagram) a map of [Midtown Manhattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midtown_Manhattan) with the headline "Globalize the Intifada". The map contained annotations for locations of Israeli and American companies, along with other points of interest, calling them "location[s] of an office of an enemy of both the Palestinian people and colonized people all over the world", implying they are legitimate targets. The post was condemned by elected officials in New York City and by Jewish groups as "a dangerous and abhorrent incitement to violence and poses a direct threat to [New York's Jewish communities.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_New_York_City)["\[20\]](#page-9-0)

In December 2023, the use of banners utilizing the slogan led to the arrest of nine individuals in [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London) under the [Public](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Order_Act_1986) [Order Act 1986.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Order_Act_1986) [\[21\]](#page-9-1) In May 2024, a similar slogan—"Intifada, Revolution!"—was used by pro-Palestinian protesters in central London, prompting an investigation by the [Met Police.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Met_Police) [\[22\]](#page-9-2)

#### **2025 New York City mayoral election**

In June 2025, during the [2025 New York City Democratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_New_York_City_Democratic_mayoral_primary) [mayoral primary,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_New_York_City_Democratic_mayoral_primary) [Zohran Mamdani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zohran_Mamdani) was asked about the phrase "globalize the intifada" in an interview for [The Bulwark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bulwark_(website)#Podcasts). He described it as a symbolic call for [Palestinian human rights,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights_violations_against_Palestinians_by_Israel) not for violence or antisemitism, adding, "These words have different meanings for many different people ... I've been clear that any incitement to violence is something that I'm in opposition to.["\[23\]](#page-9-3) He said the word "intifada" had been used by the [United States Holocaust Memorial Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Holocaust_Memorial_Museum) (USHMM) in Arabic translations referring to [Jewish resistance in German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_resistance_in_German-occupied_Europe)[occupied Europe.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_resistance_in_German-occupied_Europe) [\[23\]](#page-9-3)[\[24\]](#page-9-4)[\[25\]](#page-9-5) When asked about the phrase again on [The Brian Lehrer Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Lehrer#The_Brian_Lehrer_Show) later that month, he added, "That is not language that I use. The language that I use is that of clarity, and I do not believe it is the mayor's position to be policing language.["\[26\]](#page-9-6)

Mamdani's statements in the interview with The Bulwark were condemned by the USHMM itself and by Jewish public figures, including US representatives [Ted Deutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Deutch) and [Dan Goldman,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Goldman) [Jonathan Greenblatt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Greenblatt) of the [Anti-Defamation League,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Defamation_League) former [World Jewish Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Jewish_Congress) vice president [Marc Schneier,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marc_Schneier) Rabbi [Ammiel Hirsch,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ammiel_Hirsch) and [The Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Atlantic) columnist [Jonathan Chait.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Chait) [\[27\]](#page-9-7) [\[25\]](#page-9-5)[\[24\]](#page-9-4)[\[28\]](#page-9-8) In a statement that did not name Mamdani, the USHMM said, "Exploiting the museum and the [Warsaw Ghetto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_Ghetto_Uprising) [Uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_Ghetto_Uprising) to sanitize 'Globalize the Intifada' is outrageous and especially offensive to survivors. Since 1987, Jews have been attacked and murdered under its banner. All leaders must condemn its use and the abuse of history.["\[24\]](#page-9-4) [Brad Lander,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brad_Lander) Jewish [New York City Comptroller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_Comptroller) and fellow mayoral candidate, defended Mamdani, saying, "I don't like the phrase ... but I do believe he will protect [Jewish New Yorkers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_New_Yorkers) and our rights.["\[29\]](#page-10-0)[\[27\]](#page-9-7)[\[30\]](#page-10-1)

## **Criticism**

Both the [Anti-Defamation League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Defamation_League) and [American Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Jewish_Committee) [Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Jewish_Committee) interpret the slogan as endorsing acts of terrorism and indiscriminate violence against Israelis and Jews worldwide[.\[5\]](#page-6-4)[\[31\]](#page-10-2) According to [The Sunday Telegraph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sunday_Telegraph), the phrase has been associated with incitement of violence against Jewish communities[.\[12\]](#page-7-1) Some Jewish writers including [David Hazony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Hazony) have interpreted the use of the slogan not just as a challenge to Israel but as being a broader declaration of war against Jews, promoting [antisemitism,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antisemitism) and calling for violence instead of peace[.\[32\]](#page-10-3)[\[33\]](#page-10-4)[\[34\]](#page-10-5)[\[35\]](#page-10-6) Following the 2023

[death of Jewish-American Paul Kessler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_of_Paul_Kessler) after a fatal altercation during dual pro-Palestine and pro-Israel protests, Prime Minister of Israel [Yair Lapid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yair_Lapid) criticized the slogan and argued the protests were inherently hateful of Jewish peoples.[\[36\]](#page-10-7)

During a [December 2023 United States congressional hearing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_United_States_Congress_hearing_on_antisemitism) with presidents of major universities, representative [Elise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elise_Stefanik) [Stefanik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elise_Stefanik) argued the use of the term was a call for genocide of Jewish people[.\[37\]](#page-10-8)

The call to "globalize" the Intifada through protests and social media posts is seen by critics as an endorsement of past uprisings and a call for their expansion on a global scale.[\[2\]](#page-6-1) During the [Gaza war,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_war) some opinion writers argued protesters using the slogan were supporters of the [2023 Hamas attack on](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Hamas_attack_on_Israel) [Israel.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Hamas_attack_on_Israel) [\[38\]](#page-11-0)

### **Defense**

Others argue that calls for intifada are not inherently violent, arguing that the term "Intifada" does not translate into [genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genocide), being more correctly translated into "uprising".[\[39\]](#page-11-1) [\[40\]](#page-11-2)[\[41\]](#page-11-3)[\[42\]](#page-11-4) Daniel Lefkowitz of the University of Virginia hypothesizes that, though Israelis and many Jews consider the term to be violent, Palestinians associate "Intifada" with the First Intifada, which was largely non-violent; however, Palestinians also attacked Israelis[.\[43\]](#page-11-5) Some argue the target of "intifada" is the [Israeli occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_occupation) with the goal of [Palestinian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_independence) [independence.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_independence) [\[41\]](#page-11-3)

**Judea** or **Judaea** ([;\[1\]](#page-18-0) [Hebrew:](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language) הדוהי , [Modern:](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_Hebrew) Yəhūda,

[Tiberian:](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiberian_vocalization) Yehūḏā; [Arabic:](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language) ادوهي , Yahūdā; [Greek:](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language) Ἰουδαία, Ioudaía; [Latin:](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_language) Iudaea) is a mountainous region of the [Levant.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levant) Traditionally dominated by the city of [Jerusalem,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerusalem) it is now part of [Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel) and the [West Bank.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Bank) The name's usage is historic, having been used in antiquity and still into the present day; it originates from [Yehudah,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judah_(son_of_Jacob)) the [Hebrew name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_name) of the tribe, called Juda(h) in English. Yehudah was a son of [Jacob,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob) later known as '[Israel,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_(name))' whose sons collectively headed the [Twelve Tribes of](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve_Tribes_of_Israel) [Israel.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve_Tribes_of_Israel) Yehudah's progeny among the [Israelites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israelites) formed the [Tribe of Judah,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribe_of_Judah) with whom the [Kingdom of Judah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Judah) is associated. Related nomenclature continued to be used under the rule of the [Babylonians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babylonia) (the [Yehud province\)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yehud_(Babylonian_province)), the [Persians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_Empire) (the [Yehud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yehud_Medinata) [province\)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yehud_Medinata), during the Hellenistic period [\(Hasmonean Judea\)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hasmonean_Judea), and under the [Romans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) (the [Herodian Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herodian_kingdom) and the Provincia Iudaea, or [Province of Judaea\)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judaea_(Roman_province))[.\[2\]](#page-18-1) Under the Hasmoneans, the Herodians, and the Romans, the term was applied to an area larger than Judea of earlier periods. In the aftermath of the [Bar Kokhba revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar_Kokhba_revolt) (c. 132–136 CE), the Roman province of Judaea was renamed [Syria Palaestina.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria_Palaestina) [\[3\]](#page-19-0)[\[4\]](#page-19-1)[\[5\]](#page-19-2)

The term *Judea* was used by English speakers for the hilly internal part of Mandatory Palestine. [6][7] Judea roughly corresponds to the West Bank (Arabic: الضِفَّة الغَرْبِيَّة, romanized: aḍ-ḍiffa al-gharbiya), [8] a territory Israel has occupied since 1967 and administered as the "Judea and Samaria Area" (מחוז יהודה, Makhoz Yehuda Ve Shomron). [9][10] Usage of the term "Judea and Samaria" is associated with the right wing in Israeli politics. [11]

# **Etymology**

The name *Judea* is a Greek and Roman adaptation of the Hebrew name Yehudah (Hebrew יהודה, 'Judah'), which originally encompassed the territory of the Israelite tribe of that name and later of the ancient Kingdom of Judah. Nimrud Tablet K.3751, dated c. 733 BCE, is the earliest known extra-biblical record of the name Judah (written in Assyrian cuneiform as Yaudaya or KUR.ia-ú-da-a-a).

Judea was sometimes used as the name for the entire region, including parts beyond the river Jordan. [12] In 200 CE Sextus Julius Africanus, cited by Eusebius (Church History 1.7.14), described "Nazara" (Nazareth) as a village in Judea. [13] The King James Version of the Bible refers to the region as "Jewry". [14]

'Judea' was a name used by English speakers for the hilly internal part of Mandatory Palestine until the Jordanian rule of the area in 1948. For example, the borders of the two states to be established according to the UN's 1947 partition scheme [6]

were officially described using the terms 'Judea' and 'Samaria' and in its reports to the League of Nations Mandatory Committee, as in 1937, the geographical terms employed were 'Samaria and Judea.'[7] Jordan called the area aq-qiffa algharbiya (الضِفَّة الغَرْبيَّة translated into English as 'the West Bank').[8] 'Yehuda' (יהודה) is the Hebrew term used for the area in modern Israel since the region was captured and occupied by Israel in the 1967 Six Day War. [9] According to Britannica, referring to this region as 'Judea and Samaria' (יהודה ושומרון, Yehuda Ve Shomron) has been associated with the right wing in Israeli politics, which does not support a two state solution to the Israeli-Palestinian conflict.[11] The term 'West Bank' is what appears on international treaties such as the Oslo Accords established between the Palestine Liberation Organization and the Israeli government.[11] The names "West Bank" (הַגַּדַה ָהַמֵּעַרַבִּית, *Ha Gadah Ha Ma ʿaravit*) or, alternatively, "the Territories" (השטחים, *Ha Shtahim*) are also current in Israeli usage. Generally, preference for one term over the other indicates the speaker's position on the Israeli political spectrum.

### **Roman-era definition**

The first century Roman-Jewish historian [Josephus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josephus) wrote ([The](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Jewish_War) [Jewish War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Jewish_War) 3.3.5):

In the limits of Samaria and Judea lies the village [Anuath, which](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/As-Sawiya#Khan_as-Sawiya_(Khirbet_Berkit)) [is also named Borceos.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/As-Sawiya#Khan_as-Sawiya_(Khirbet_Berkit)) [\[15\]](#page-20-6) This is the northern boundary of Judea. The southern parts of Judea, if they be measured lengthways, are bounded by a village adjoining to the confines of [Arabia;](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabia) the [Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jews) that dwell there call it Jordan. However, its breadth is extended from the [river Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_River) to [Joppa.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaffa) The city Jerusalem is situated in the very middle; on which account some have, with sagacity enough, called that city the Navel of the country. Nor indeed is Judea destitute of such delights as come from the sea, since its maritime places extend as far as [Ptolemais:](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemais_in_Phoenicia) it was parted into eleven portions, of which the royal city [Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerusalem) was the supreme, and presided over all the neighboring country, as the head does over the body. As to the other cities that were inferior to it, they presided over their several [toparchies;](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toparchies) [Gophna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jifna) was the second of those cities, and next to that [Acrabatta,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aqraba,_Nablus) after them [Thamna,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khirbet_Tibnah) and [Lydda,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lod) and [Emmaus,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmaus) and Pella, and [Idumea,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idumea) and [Engaddi,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ein_Gedi) and [Herodium,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herodium) and [Jericho;](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jericho) and after them came [Jamnia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yavne) and [Joppa,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaffa) as presiding over the neighboring people; and besides these there was the region of [Gamala,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamla) and [Gaulonitis,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golan_Heights) and [Batanea,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batanaea) and [Trachonitis,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trachonitis) which are also parts of the kingdom of [Agrippa.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herod_Agrippa_II) This [last] country begins at [Mount Libanus,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Lebanon) and the fountains of Jordan, and reaches breadthways to [Lake Tiberias;](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_of_Galilee) and in length is extended from a village called Arpha, as far as Julias. Its inhabitants are a mixture of Jews and Syrians. And thus have I, with all possible brevity, described the country of Judea, and those that lie round about it[.\[16\]](#page-21-0)

Elsewhere, Josephus wrote that "Arabia is a country that borders on Judea.["\[17\]](#page-21-1)

The first century Roman historian [Tacitus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tacitus) defined Judaea as bordered by Arabia to the east, Egypt to the south, Phoenicia and the Mediterranean Sea to the west, and Syria to the north. [\[18\]](#page-21-2) His conception, presented in [Histories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Histories_(Tacitus)) 5.6, mirrors a conventional understanding of Judaea as the territory where Jews predominated from the Hasmonaean era onward, standing apart from the provincial borders of the province of Judaea in his own period[.\[18\]](#page-21-2)

## southwestern Judea

Judea is a mountainous region, part of which is considered a [desert.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desert) It varies greatly in height, rising to an altitude of 1,020 metres (3,350 ft) in the south at the [Hebron Hills,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebron_Hills) 30 km (19 mi) southwest of [Jerusalem,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerusalem) and descending to as much as 400 metres (1,300 ft) below sea level in the east of the region. It also varies in rainfall, starting with about 400–500 millimetres (16–20 in) in the western hills, rising to 600 millimetres (24 in) around western Jerusalem (in central Judea), falling back to 400 millimetres (16 in) in eastern Jerusalem and dropping to around 100 millimetres (3.9 in) in the eastern parts, due to a [rain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain_shadow) [shadow:](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain_shadow) this is the [Judaean Desert.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judaean_Desert) The climate, accordingly, moves between [Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_climate) in the west and [desert climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desert_climate) in the east, with a strip of [semi-arid climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-arid_climate) in the middle. Major urban areas in the region include Jerusalem, [Bethlehem,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bethlehem) [Gush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gush_Etzion) [Etzion,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gush_Etzion) Jericho and [Hebron.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebron) [\[19\]](#page-21-3)

Geographers divide Judea into several regions: the Hebron hills, the Jerusalem saddle, the [Bethel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bethel) hills and the Judaean Desert east of Jerusalem, which descends in a series of steps to the [Dead Sea.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dead_Sea) The hills are distinct for their [anticline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anticline) structure. In ancient times the hills were forested, and the [Bible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bible) records agriculture and sheep farming being practiced in the area. Animals are still grazed today, with shepherds moving them between the low ground to the hilltops as summer approaches, while the slopes are still layered with centuries-old stone [terracing.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrace_(agriculture)) The Jewish Revolt against the Romans ended in the devastation of vast areas of the Judean countryside.[\[20\]](#page-21-4)

# **History**

# **Biblical Era**

## ![](_page_7_Figure_5.jpeg)

Map of the southern [Levant,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levant) c. 830s BCE Kingdom of Judah

According to the biblical story of the Patriarchs,[\[21\]](#page-21-5) Abraham came to the Land of [Canaan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canaan) as commanded by God and moved around in the hill country (Judaea and Samaria) and the Negev. The country is described as populated by [Canaanites,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canaanites) [Hittites,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hittites) [Jebusites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jebusites) and other population groups. This pattern continued with his son [Isaac,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaac) his son [Jacob](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob) and his 12 sons and daughter, [Dina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dina) and their families[.\[22\]](#page-21-6) The [Patriarchs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriarchs) [Sarai,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah) [Abraham,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham) [Isaac,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaac) [Rebecca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebecca_(biblical_figure)) and [Jacob](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob) were buried at [Hebron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebron) in the [Tomb of the Patriarchs.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomb_of_the_Patriarchs) [\[23\]](#page-21-7) according to [Genesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Genesis) and [Exodus.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Exodus)

After the Conquest of [Joshua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joshua) the Israelite tribes conquered and lived in most of the land west of the river [Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan) and in the northern part east of that river for close to 400 years.

The biblical account in the Books of Kings describes how King [Saul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saul) and later King [David](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David) and his son [Solomon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solomon) [\(Shlomo\)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shlomo) succeeded in fighting the last remnants of non-Israelite populations and unified the tribes into one united monarchy. According to our[[who?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Words_to_watch#Unsupported_attributions)] understanding of the text as well as recent archeological findings, this was to a large degree possible through the Israelite adaption of [Iron Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Age) technologies. Scholarship has been divided as to the historical veracity of the existence and extension of a kingdom that unified Judea and Samaria, but archeological excavations of the last 30 years[[when?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Dates_and_numbers#Chronological_items)] have time and again found solid evidence that confirms the bibilcal descriptions.[\[24\]](#page-22-0)[\[25\]](#page-22-1)[\[26\]](#page-22-2)[\[27\]](#page-22-3)

Regardless, the Northern Kingdom was conquered by the [Neo-](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Assyrian_Empire)[Assyrian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Assyrian_Empire) in 720 BCE and parts of the population of the 10 northern tribes exiled. The northern Kingdom of Judah remained nominally independent, but paid tribute to the Assyrian Empire from 715 and throughout the first half of the 7th century BCE, regaining its independence as the Assyrian Empire declined after 640 BCE, but after 609 again fell under the sway of imperial rule, this time paying tribute at first to the Egyptians and after 601 BCE to the [Neo-Babylonian Empire,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Babylonian_Empire) until 586 BCE, when it was finally conquered by Babylonia, the temple in Jerusalem destroyed and many of the inhabitants of Judea exiled to Babylonia.

The Babylonian Empire fell to the conquests of [Cyrus the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyrus_the_Great) in 539 BCE[.\[28\]](#page-22-4) Judea remained under Persian rule until the conquest of [Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great) in 332 BCE, eventually falling under the rule of the [Hellenistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenistic_period) [Seleucid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seleucid_Empire) until the revolt of [Judas Maccabeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judas_Maccabeus) resulted in the [Hasmonean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hasmonean) dynasty of kings who ruled in Judea for over a century[.\[29\]](#page-22-5)

### **Early Roman period**

Judea lost its independence to the Romans in the 1st century BCE, becoming first a [tributary kingdom,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Client_kingdoms_in_ancient_Rome) then a [province,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_province) of the Roman Empire. The Romans had allied themselves to the [Maccabees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maccabees) and interfered in 63 BCE, at the end of the [Third](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Mithridatic_War) [Mithridatic War,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Mithridatic_War) when the [proconsul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proconsul) [Pompey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pompey) ("Pompey the Great") stayed behind to make the area secure for Rome, including his [siege of Jerusalem in 63 BCE.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Jerusalem_(63_BC)) Queen [Salome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salome_Alexandra) [Alexandra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salome_Alexandra) had recently died, and a civil war broke out between her sons, [Hyrcanus II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyrcanus_II) and [Aristobulus II.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristobulus_II) Pompeius restored Hyrcanus, but political rule soon passed to the [Herodian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herodian_dynasty) [dynasty,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herodian_dynasty) who ruled as [client kings.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judaea_(Roman_province)#Background)

In 6 CE, Judea came under direct Roman rule as the southern part of the province of [Judaea,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judaea_(Roman_province)) although Jews living there still maintained some form of independence and could judge offenders by their own laws, including capital offences, until c. 28 CE[.\[30\]](#page-22-6) The Hashmonean kingdom, after Pompey's conquest, was divided in 57 BCE by Gabinius, the governor of Syria, into five administrative districts [\(synedria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synedrion) or [toparchies\)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toparches), as mentioned by Josephus, later on the region of historical Judaea proper being further divided; the exact number of Judaean districts (in the end ten or eleven according to Josephus and [Pliny\)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pliny_the_Elder) and their location is disputed, [Schürer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emil_Sch%C3%BCrer) amending the ancient authors' list as follows: Jerusalem in the centre, later becoming the district of Orine ("Orine Judaea", 'mountainous [region of] Judaea'); [Gophna,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jifna) [Akrabatta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aqraba,_Nablus) north of it; [Thamna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khirbet_Tibnah) and [Lydda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydda) to the northwest; [Emmaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmaus) (possibly future [Nicopolis/Imwas,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imwas#History) although other towns in the region also bore that name) to the west; [Bethleptepha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayt_Nattif) (rather than Josephus' [Pella\)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pella,_Jordan) to the southwest; [Idumaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edom#Classical_Idumaea) to the south; [Engaddi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engaddi) and [Herodeion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herodeion) to the southeast; and [Jericho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jericho) to the east. Schürer dismisses Pliny's listing of "Jopica" [\(Joppa\)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaffa) and Josephus' of Pella, as these were, in his opinion, independent cities not included in Judaea proper[.\[31\]](#page-22-7)[\[32\]](#page-22-8)

Other regions outside Judaea proper, which had belonged to the Hasmonean and Herodian kingdoms and came under Roman dominance and then direct rule, remained or became also split into districts with regional capitals, these being [Galilee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galilee) (with the capital at [Sepphoris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sepphoris) and later [Tiberias\)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiberias), and [Perea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perea) in [Transjordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transjordan_(region)) (with [Amathus\)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amathus,_Transjordan); however, a district administered from a certain [Gadara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gadara_(disambiguation)) is also mentioned, which can be in three different locations - either in Perea (at or near [Al-Salt\)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Salt), in the [Decapolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decapolis) at [Umm Qais,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umm_Qais) [\[33\]](#page-22-9)[\[34\]](#page-23-0)[\[35\]](#page-23-1)[\[36\]](#page-23-2)[\[37\]](#page-23-3) or which is relevant for Judaea - at biblical [Gezer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gezer) in the foothills of the Judaean Mountains, mentioned by Josephus under a Hellenised form of its Semitic name, Gadara, edited to

"Gazara" in the [Loeb edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loeb_Classical_Library)[\[38\]\)](#page-23-4).

# **Jewish–Roman wars and Late Roman period**

### **First Jewish–Roman War**

In 66 CE, the Jewish population [rose against Roman rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Jewish%E2%80%93Roman_War) in a revolt that was unsuccessful. Jerusalem was [besieged in 70](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Jerusalem_(AD_70)) CE. The city was razed, the [Second Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Temple) was destroyed, and much of the population was killed or enslaved.[\[39\]](#page-24-0)

### **Bar Kokhba revolt**

In 132 CE, the [Bar Kokhba revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar_Kokhba_revolt) (132–136 CE) broke out. After an initial string of victories, rebel leader [Simeon Bar Kokhba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simeon_Bar_Kokhba) was able to form an independent Jewish state that lasted several years and included most of the district of Judea, including the Judean Mountains, the Judean Desert, and northern Negev desert, but probably not other sections of the country.

### **Aftermath**

When the Romans finally put an end to the uprising, most of the Jews in Judea were killed or displaced, and a sizable number of captives were sold into slavery, leaving the district mostly depopulated. Jews were expelled from the area surrounding Jerusalem[.\[40\]](#page-24-1)[\[41\]](#page-24-2)[\[42\]](#page-24-3) No village in the district of Judea whose remains have been excavated so far has not been destroyed during the revolt[.\[43\]](#page-25-0) Roman emperor [Hadrian,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadrian)

determined to root out Jewish nationalism, changed the name of the province from [Judaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judaea_(Roman_province)) to [Syria Palaestina.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria_Palaestina) [\[44\]](#page-25-1) The province's Jewish population was now mainly concentrated in Galilee, the [coastal plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_coastal_plain) (especially in [Lydda,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydda) [Joppa,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaffa) and [Caesarea\)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caesarea_Maritima), and smaller Jewish communities continued to live in the [Beit She'an Valley,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beit_She%27an_Valley) the [Carmel,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Carmel) and Judea's northern and southern frontiers, including the southern [Hebron Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebron_Hills) and along the shores of the Dead Sea[.\[45\]](#page-25-2)[\[46\]](#page-26-0)

The suppression of the Bar Kokhba revolt led to widespread destruction and displacement throughout Judea, and the district saw a decline in population. The Roman colony of [Aelia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aelia_Capitolina) [Capitolina,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aelia_Capitolina) which was built on the ruins of Jerusalem, remained a backwater for the duration of its existence[.\[41\]](#page-24-2) The villages around the city were depopulated, and arable lands in the region were confiscated by the Romans. Having no alternative population to fill the empty villages led the authorities to establish imperial or legionary estates and monasteries on confiscated village lands to benefit the elites and, later, the church[.\[47\]](#page-26-1) This also initiated a process of romanization that took place during the Late Roman period, with pagan populations penetrating the region and settling alongside Roman veterans[.\[48\]](#page-26-2)[\[40\]](#page-24-1) There was only a revival of village settlement on the eastern edges of Jerusalem's hinterland, on the transition between the arable highlands and the [Judaean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judaean_Desert) [Desert.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judaean_Desert) Those settlements grew on marginal lands with vague ownership and unenforced state land dominion[.\[47\]](#page-26-1)

# **Byzantine period**

## ![](_page_13_Picture_2.jpeg)

5th century CE: Byzantine provinces of Palaestina I (Philistia, Judea and Samaria) and Palaestina II (Galilee and Perea)

Judea's decline only came to an end in the fifth century CE, when it developed into a monastic center, and Jerusalem became a major Christian pilgrimage and ecclesiastical hub. [\[41\]](#page-24-2) Under [Byzantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Palestine) rule, the regional population, composed of pagan populations who had migrated there after Jews were driven out following the Bar Kokhba revolt, gradually converted to [Christianity.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity) [\[40\]](#page-24-1)

The Byzantines redrew the borders of the land of Palestine. The various Roman provinces [\(Syria Palaestina,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria_Palaestina) [Samaria,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samaria) [Galilee,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galilee) and [Peraea\)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peraea) were reorganized into three dioceses of Palaestina, reverting to the name first used by Greek historian [Herodotus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herodotus) in the mid-5th century BCE: [Palaestina Prima,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palaestina_Prima) [Secunda,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palaestina_Secunda) and Tertia or [Salutaris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palaestina_Salutaris) (First, Second, and Third Palestine), part of the [Diocese of the East.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese_of_the_East) [\[49\]](#page-27-0)[\[50\]](#page-27-1) Palaestina Prima consisted of Judea, Samaria, the [Paralia,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paralia_(Palestine)) and [Peraea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perea_(Holy_Land)) with the governor residing in [Caesarea.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caesarea_Palaestina) Palaestina Secunda consisted of Galilee, the lower [Jezreel Valley,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jezreel_Valley) the regions east of Galilee, and the western part of the former [Decapolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decapolis) with the seat of government at [Scythopolis.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beit_She%27an) Palaestina Tertia included the [Negev,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negev) southern Jordan—once part of Arabia—and most of [Sinai,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinai_Peninsula) with [Petra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petra) as the usual residence of the governor. Palestina Tertia was also known as Palaestina Salutaris.[\[49\]](#page-27-0)[\[51\]](#page-27-2) According to historian H. H. Ben-Sasson[,\[52\]](#page-27-3) this reorganisation took place under [Diocletian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocletian) (284–305), although other scholars suggest this change occurred later, in 390.

### **Crusader period**

The mostly French army of the First [Crusade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crusade) conquered Jerusalem from the [Seljuks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seljuks) in 1099 and expanded the territory they held in the following years. According to Ellenblum, the Franks tended to settle in the southern half of the region between Jerusalem and Nablus since there was a sizable Christian population there[.\[53\]](#page-27-4)[\[54\]](#page-27-5)

# **Mamluk period**

Most of the people living in the northern portion of Judea in the late 16th century were Muslims; some of them resided in towns that today have significant Christian populations. According to the 1596–1597 Ottoman census, [Birzeit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birzeit) and [Jifna,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jifna) for instance, were wholly Muslim villages, while [Taybeh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taybeh,_Ramallah) had 63 Muslim families and 23 Christian families. There were 71 Christian families and 9 Muslim families in [Ramallah,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramallah) although the Christians there were recent arrivals who had moved from the [Kerak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Karak) area only a few years previously. According to Ehrlich, the region's Christian population decreased as a result of a combination of factors including impoverishment, oppression, marginalization, and persecution. [Sufi activity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sufism) took place in Jerusalem and the surrounding area, which most likely pushed Christian villagers in the region to convert to Islam.[\[54\]](#page-27-5)

- 41–44: The Romans temporarily restored Jewish royalty under [Herod Agrippa.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herod_Agrippa) After his death, Judaea reverted to direct Roman rule, which now encompassed Judea, Samaria, Idumaea, Galilee, and Perea.
- 66–73: [First Jewish–Roman War,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Jewish%E2%80%93Roman_War) includes [Destruction of the](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Destruction_of_the_Second_Temple) [Second Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Destruction_of_the_Second_Temple) in 70
- 115–117: [Kitos War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitos_War)
- 132–136: [Bar Kokhba revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar_Kokhba_revolt)
- c. 136: Judaea is renamed [Syria Palaestina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria_Palaestina)

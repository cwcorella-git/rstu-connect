### A C K N O W L E D G E M E N T S

It was a cold December back in 2014 when we first unveiled our plans for Worlds Adrift, asking the players what they thought about it and if we should even be creating such a game at all. It seems like ages ago, but that timid step has set us firmly in the path of working side by side with our community.- listening to their ideas, getting shouted at when we got something wrong (rightly so!) and becoming deeply emotional when we first saw their first islands pouring onto the Steam Workshop.

We wouldn't get anywhere without the fans, founders, players, you. Every post to the forums, every word of encouragement, tweet or email - it all converged to form a new way of crafting our games: Open Development. Turns out our first step was also the boldest and best, because it led to a cherished relationship which made our game much better all while establishing new friendships along the way.

As a founder, you made Worlds Adrift. We owe you a huge debt, you helped us achieve a dream. We hope this small token you're now reading starts us on the road to acknowledging you for being awesome, and for believing in us even when we ourselves did not.

## Thank you. The Worlds Adrift Team

## F O R E W O R D

I find myself wondering what drives an Artist to write a story, paint a canvas, develop a game. Surely there are easier ways to go about life (and certainly more financially rewarding alternatives), and yet here we are burning the candle on both ends, day after day, trying our best to surprise, amaze, and perhaps move those willing to put up with our shortcomings in the pursuit of the fabled perfection.

Whatever is the engine that keeps us going, be it passion, pride, egotism or simply the animalistic fear of going hungry due the lack of a paycheck, for some twisted and quite ironic reason, we seem unable to appreciate our own work at the time of its craft. It takes hindsight, distance and, perhaps, an art book, to enable us to take a break and finally say 'actually, this doesn't look half bad, does it?'

Within the pages of this compendium you'll find some of our best efforts at drawing a soul for Worlds Adrift, a game whose universe is as complex and rich as its game mechanics' features list, which at many occasions had us scratching our heads for a starting point at any art task: 'How does a Sky Whale look like?' and 'how would a humanoid who spends most of its waking time hanging from a rope run while on the ground?' do spring to mind as examples that stumped us for quite a few reworks, sometimes at the expense of a looming deadline.

At the time of the writing of these words, the game is a couple of months away from its debut. It is rather fitting that we're putting this book together at such a period, in the absence of the knowledge of its fate after facing the golden test of the players. We're missing the hindsight at this point. Whatever happens, I'm sure in a few years, when opening up these pages while enjoying a beer, we'll be at last able to say:

## 'Actually, this doesn't look half bad, does it?'

## Henrique Olifiers Gamer-In-Chief at Bossa Studios

#### THE BEGINNING

For the art team the beginning of a project can be the most exciting and yet daunting stage. You are in a strange limbo where anything is possible and yet the choices you make regarding art style and world design will have such a huge impact on the rest of the game it can sometimes feel overwhelming. After discussing the mood/atmosphere of Worlds Adrift we jumped in and began designing the world.

Worlds Adrift started its life as a game-jam called Travelers, and while this already had a vision in terms of flying ships, athletic characters and floating islands it still didn't have a visual identity. This would come later, and there were key moments along the journey that would help to bring this visual identity

#### VISUAL STORYTELLING

Writing and visuals can sometimes form a bit of a chicken and egg type scenario. Concepts are much easier to create when you really understand the subject you are concepting, and writing is much easier when you have some idea of the final visuals. With the Saborians we started with some idea of their principles/personality and a few sketches of their building styles and then our writer used this as a jumping off point to start creating them in ernest. All of the Saborian concepts shown here were done after they'd been fully fleshed out in writing, and many clues to their beliefs, principles and ways of life can be discovered visually without reading a thing.

Far Left - An early design for a Saborian Apotheon. The Apotheon is treated as a form of competition between Saborians in the lead up to Apotheosis. Overcoming it is one of the five tenets of Apotheosis.

#### KIOKI UNITY

One of the two great Superpowers. The Kioki were perhaps the biggest rivals of Sabor during the Stone War. In the years before the cataclysm many of their cities were reduced to rubble whilst under the rule of Empress Yatai. Kioki is a fusion of three different nations, which only came together in 2750 after Ishgiru declared their sisterhood. The three nations were Pin, Karem, and Ishgiru. Their unification was in part due to the peace and prosperity that the Golden Age had brought.

#### EARLY DESIGNS

Early on we knew that we wanted our game to have creatures as opposed to enemies. Our creatures have their own sets of needs including food, rest and even a need to reproduce. This is not to say they won't attack players but they will usually have a biological reason for doing so be it fear, hunger or anger.

Here are some of the first designs for the beetles (thuntomites).

They started out being much more influenced by insect anatomy but later developed more crustacean-like features.

## ![](_page_48_Picture_0.jpeg)

#### THUNTOMITES

Like most of the fauna that appeared after the cataclysm thuntomites are very tough. They survive on a diet of vegetation and fruit and have developed a tough outer carapace to protect themselves from predators. Their tough and often horned heads are mostly used to scare off other thuntomites during mating but if provoked they use their heads to ram their enemies causing huge damage.

## ![](_page_49_Picture_0.jpeg)

#### SKY WHALE

The Sky Whale is the largest known creature in Foundation and mainly lives beneath the clouds. Because of their elusive nature little is known of their behaviour. They are sometimes referred to as the children of Ten-Rui.

Its design takes elements from many real-world animals including axolotl, turtles, shrimp and various species of whales.

### ENGINES AND MODULARITY

We have three ship parts that are constructed modularly and those are engines, wings and cannons. This means that when you discover a new schematic for one of these it will have procedurally generated stats as well as procedurally generated artwork. To enable this to work we had to work out rules and systems for each item to allow them to be made from different pieces and slotted together.

The first of these systems we made was for the engines. They are split into three components: Propellers, Bodies and Heads. With a few different pieces for each component we can generate hundreds of different engines.

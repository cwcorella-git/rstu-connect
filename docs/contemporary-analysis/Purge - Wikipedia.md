# **Purge**

## Contributors to Wikimedia projects

17–21 minutes

#### From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

## ![](_page_0_Picture_7.jpeg)

Russian Count [Nikolay Yevdokimov,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolay_Yevdokimov) who organized the extermination campaigns of ["Tsitsekun"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsitsekun), designated Russian military operations targeting Circassian natives by the term "ochishchenie" (cleansing).

In [history,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History) [religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion) and [political science,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_science) a **purge** is a position removal or [execution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Execution) of people who are considered undesirable by those in power from a government, another, their team leaders, or society as a whole. A group undertaking such an effort is labeled as purging itself.

Purges can be either nonviolent or violent, with the former often resolved by the simple removal of those who have been purged from office, and the latter often resolved by the [imprisonment,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imprisonment) [exile,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exile) or [murder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murder) of those who have been purged[.\[1\]](#page-8-0)[\[2\]](#page-8-1) Governments who enact purges but want to obscure that they are doing so, may often justify their actions as dealing with security threats (such as terrorists) or corruption[.\[3\]](#page-8-2)[\[4\]](#page-8-3)

The [Shanghai massacre of 1927](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanghai_massacre_of_1927) in China and the [Night of the](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Night_of_the_Long_Knives) [Long Knives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Night_of_the_Long_Knives) of 1934 in Nazi Germany, in which the leader of a political party turns against a particular section or group within the party and kills its members, are commonly called "purges". Mass expulsions of populations on the grounds of [racism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racism) and [xenophobia,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xenophobia) such as the [deportation of the Crimean Tatars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deportation_of_the_Crimean_Tatars) in the Soviet Union, are not.[[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed)]

Though sudden and violent purges are notable, most purges do not involve immediate execution or imprisonment, for example the periodic massive purges of the [Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_Czechoslovakia#Membership) [of Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_Czechoslovakia#Membership) on grounds of apathy or dereliction, or the [purge of Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_for_the_Restoration_of_the_Professional_Civil_Service) and [political dissenters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_resistance_to_Nazism) from the German Civil Service in 1933–1934.

Beginning in 1966, [Chairman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chairman_of_the_Chinese_Communist_Party) [Mao Zedong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mao_Zedong) and his associates purged much of the [Chinese Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Communist_Party)'s [leadership,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generations_of_Chinese_leadership) including the [head of state,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state) [President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_People%27s_Republic_of_China) [Liu Shaoqi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liu_Shaoqi) and the then-[Secretary-General,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary-general_of_the_Chinese_Communist_Party) [Deng Xiaoping,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deng_Xiaoping) as part of what the leaders termed the [Cultural Revolution.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_Revolution) In [Maoist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maoism) states, sentences usually involved hard labor in [laogai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laogai) camps and executions. Deng Xiaoping acquired a reputation for returning to power after he had been purged several times.[[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed)]

Purges are particularly likely when the power of competing elites is temporarily low, such as when a new dictator has taken office[,\[5\]](#page-8-4) or when a leader has just survived a coup[.\[6\]](#page-9-0) New dictators often target the military for a purge[.\[7\]](#page-9-1)

# **Historical use of the term**

[\[edit\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Purge&action=edit§ion=2)

#### **English Civil War purge, 1648–1650**

### [\[edit\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Purge&action=edit§ion=3)

The earliest use of the term dates back to the [English Civil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Civil_War) [War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Civil_War)'s [Pride's Purge.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pride%27s_Purge) In 1648–1650, the moderate members of the English [Long Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Parliament) were purged by the [New Model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Model_Army) [Army.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Model_Army) The [Parliament of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_England) would suffer subsequent purges under [Oliver Cromwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Cromwell)'s [Commonwealth of England,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_England) including the purge of the entire [House of Lords.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Lords) Counterrevolutionaries such as royalists and more radical revolutionaries such as the [Levellers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levellers) were purged. After the [Stuart Restoration,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuart_Restoration) obstinate [republicans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republicanism_in_England) were purged while some[[which?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Avoid_weasel_words)] fled to the [New England Colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_England_Colonies) in [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_America) [America.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_America)

Purges were frequent in the Soviet Union[.\[8\]](#page-9-2) In the Soviet Union, military and internal security elites were more likely to be detained than civilian elites[.\[8\]](#page-9-2)[\[9\]](#page-9-3)

The term "purge" is often associated with [Stalinism.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stalinism) While leading the USSR, [Joseph Stalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Stalin) carried out repeated purges which resulted in tens of thousands of people sentenced to

[Gulag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulag) labor camps and the outright executions of rival communists, military officers, [ethnic minorities,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_minorities) [wreckers,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrecking_(Soviet_Union)) and citizens accused of plotting against [communism.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communism) [\[10\]](#page-9-4) Stalin together with [Nikolai Yezhov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolai_Yezhov) initiated the most notorious of the [CPSU purges,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purges_of_the_Communist_Party_of_the_Soviet_Union) the [Great Purge,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Purge) during the mid to late 1930s[.\[11\]](#page-9-5) In 1934, Chancellor [Adolf Hitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Hitler) ordered the execution of [Ernst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_R%C3%B6hm) [Röhm,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_R%C3%B6hm) other leaders of the [Sturmabteilung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sturmabteilung) militia, and political opponents.

After [France's liberation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberation_of_France) by the [Allies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II) in 1944, the [Provisional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provisional_Government_of_the_French_Republic) [Government of the French Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provisional_Government_of_the_French_Republic) and particularly the [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Resistance) [Resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Resistance) carried out purges of former [collaborationists,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collaboration_with_Nazi_Germany_and_Fascist_Italy) the so-called ["vichystes"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vichy_France). The process became known in legal terms as [épuration légale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89puration_l%C3%A9gale) ("legal purging"). Similar processes in other countries and on other occasions included [denazification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denazification) in [Allied-occupied Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allied-occupied_Germany) and [decommunization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decommunization) in [post-communist states.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-communism)

The [Red Purge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Purge) was an [anticommunist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anticommunist) movement in [occupied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_Japan) [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_Japan) from the late 1940s to the early 1950s[.\[12\]](#page-9-6)[\[13\]](#page-10-0)[\[14\]](#page-10-1) Carried out by the [Japanese government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_government) and private corporations with the aid and encouragement of the [Supreme Commander for](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Commander_for_the_Allied_Powers) [the Allied Powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Commander_for_the_Allied_Powers) (SCAP), the Red Purge resulted in tens of thousands of alleged members, supporters, or sympathizers of left-wing groups, especially those said to be affiliated with the [Japanese Communist Party,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_Communist_Party) removed from their jobs in government, the private sector, universities, and schools.[\[15\]](#page-10-2) The Red Purge emerged from rising [Cold War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_War) tensions and the [Red Scare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Scare) after World War II[,\[16\]](#page-10-3)[\[17\]](#page-10-4) and was a significant element within a broader ["Reverse Course"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reverse_Course) in Occupation policies[.\[18\]](#page-10-5) The Red Purge reached a peak following the outbreak of the [Korean War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_War) in 1950[,\[18\]](#page-10-5) when communist China supported North Korea. It began to ease after General [Douglas Mac Arthur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_Mac Arthur) was replaced as commander of the Occupation by General [Matthew Ridgway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_Ridgway) in 1951, and came to a conclusion with the end of the Occupation in 1952.

After the [Cuban Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuban_Revolution) in 1959, [Fidel Castro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fidel_Castro) of [Cuba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuba) often purged those who had previously been involved with the [Batista regime.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulgencio_Batista) Purges usually involved the execution of the condemned. Castro periodically carried out purges in the [Communist Party of Cuba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_Cuba) thereafter. One prominent purge was carried out in 1989 when a high-ranking [Cuban Revolutionary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuban_Revolutionary_Armed_Forces) [Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuban_Revolutionary_Armed_Forces) general named [Arnaldo Ochoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnaldo_Ochoa) was sentenced to death and executed by [firing squad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firing_squad) on charges of [drug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illegal_drug_trade) [trafficking.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illegal_drug_trade) Purges became less common in Cuba during the 1990s and 2000s.

#### **United States: Red Scares, HUAC and Mc Carthyism**

## [\[edit\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Purge&action=edit§ion=9)

In the period 1938–1975, the [House Un-American Activities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_Un-American_Activities_Committee) [Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_Un-American_Activities_Committee) (HUAC), an investigative committee of the [United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_House_of_Representatives) [States House of Representatives,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_House_of_Representatives) carried out a campaign of purging alleged "communist sympathizers" from positions in public life. While non-violent, HUAC's campaign destroyed the careers of many individuals, particularly in the entertainment industry, where HUAC attempted to purge left-wing voices entirely from the industry through the [Hollywood blacklist.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_blacklist)

While not part of HUAC, U. S. Senator [Joseph Mc Carthy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Mc Carthy) was a major driver of efforts to purge real and perceived communist sympathizers through the 1940s and 1950s, which ended in his condemnation and censure in 1954.

# **In the 21st century**

#### [\[edit\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Purge&action=edit§ion=10)

Xi Jinping's [anti-corruption campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-corruption_campaign_under_Xi_Jinping) is widely considered to be a purge[.\[19\]](#page-10-6)[\[20\]](#page-10-7) A far-reaching campaign against corruption began in [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China) following the conclusion of the [18th National](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/18th_National_Congress_of_the_Chinese_Communist_Party) [Congress of the Chinese Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/18th_National_Congress_of_the_Chinese_Communist_Party) in 2012. The campaign, carried out under the aegis of [Xi Jinping,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xi_Jinping) [General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Secretary_of_the_Chinese_Communist_Party) [Secretary of the Chinese Communist Party,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Secretary_of_the_Chinese_Communist_Party) was the largest organized purported anti-graft effort in the [history of](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_People%27s_Republic_of_China) [Communist rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_People%27s_Republic_of_China) in China.

The Supreme Council of the Cultural Revolution of the Iranian government purged universities.

In August 2023, the government reportedly had a program to hire 15,000 replacements for people in universities and to place clerics in schools[.\[21\]](#page-10-8)[\[22\]](#page-11-0) It also removed Tehran [University of Art'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Art)s major curricula for sculpting, music and cinematography/filmmaking[.\[23\]](#page-11-1) The government added Islamic studies even more so[.\[24\]](#page-11-2) Many academics were terminated/fired[.\[25\]](#page-11-3) On 14 December 2023 the Ministry of Education announced that it would hire 7000 clerics instead of teachers[.\[26\]](#page-11-4)

[De-Ba'athification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De-Ba%27athification) was undertaken by the [Coalition Provisional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coalition_Provisional_Authority) [Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coalition_Provisional_Authority) (CPA) and subsequent Iraqi governments to remove the [Ba'ath Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Socialist_Ba%27ath_Party_%E2%80%93_Iraq_Region)'s influence in the new [Iraqi political system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Iraq) after the [U. S.-led invasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_invasion_of_Iraq) in 2003[.\[27\]](#page-11-5) It was first outlined in CPA Order 1 which entered into force on 16 May 2003[.\[27\]](#page-11-5) The order declared that all [public sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_sector) employees affiliated with the Ba'ath Party were to be removed from their positions and to be banned from any future employment in the public sector. [\[27\]](#page-11-5)

It is estimated that, before 2007, 50,000 civil government employees, as well as employees of other organizations [listed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coalition_Provisional_Authority_Order_2#Dissolved_entities) [in Annex A of Order No. 2,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coalition_Provisional_Authority_Order_2#Dissolved_entities) were removed from their positions as a result of de-Ba'athification[.\[28\]](#page-11-6) Another estimate places the number, also before 2007, at "100,000 civil servants, doctors, and teachers", who were forcibly removed from the public sector due to low-level affiliation[.\[29\]](#page-11-7)

Members of the [Kim family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_family_(North_Korea)) have each periodically purged their political rivals or perceived threats since consolidating their control over [North Korea,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Korea) beginning in the 1950s. The most senior Kim purged those who opposed his son's succession to the supreme leadership of North Korea. [Kim Il Sung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Il_Sung)'s most prominent purge occurred during the ["August Incident"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/August_Incident) in 1956, when the pro-Soviet and pro-Chinese [Yanan factions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yanan_faction) of the [Workers' Party of Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Workers%27_Party_of_Korea) (WPK) attempted to depose Kim. Most of those involved in the plot were executed while some others fled to the USSR and China. While some purges were carried out under [Kim Jong Il,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Jong_Il) they were not as common as they were under his father/son. [Kim Jong Un](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Jong_Un) purged several highranking officials and generals installed by his father Kim Jong Il in the former's first years in power, including, most prominently, his uncle [Jang Song-thaek.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jang_Song-thaek)

After the failed [2016 Turkish coup d'état attempt,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Turkish_coup_d%27%C3%A9tat_attempt) the [Government of Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Turkey) under [Recep Tayyip Erdo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recep_Tayyip_Erdo%C4%9Fan)ğan began a purge against members of its civil service and the [Turkish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_Armed_Forces) [Armed Forces.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_Armed_Forces) The purge ostensibly focused mainly on public servants and soldiers alleged to be part of the [Gülen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%BClen_movement) [movement,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%BClen_movement) the group the government blamed for the coup. As part of the purge, about 200,000 public officials, including thousands of judges, were dismissed and detained. Politicized [Kurds in Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurds_in_Turkey) have also been a major target of the [Justice and](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justice_and_Development_Party_(Turkey)) [Development Party-](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justice_and_Development_Party_(Turkey))led purge.

The 2017–2019 Saudi Arabian purge was the mass arrest of a number of prominent Saudi Arabian princes, government ministers, and business people in [Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabia) on 4 November 2017[.\[30\]](#page-11-8) It took place weeks after the creation of an [anti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corruption)[corruption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corruption) committee led by [Crown Prince](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_Prince_of_Saudi_Arabia) [Mohammed bin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammed_bin_Salman) [Salman.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammed_bin_Salman) [\[31\]](#page-12-0)

The purge helped centralize political powers in the hands of bin Salman and undermine the pre-existing structure of [consensus-based governance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consensus_decision-making) among Saudi elites[.\[32\]](#page-12-1)[\[33\]](#page-12-2) The arrests resulted in the final sidelining of the faction of King [Abdullah,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdullah_of_Saudi_Arabia) and Mohammed bin Salman's complete consolidation of control of all three branches of the security forces[.\[34\]](#page-12-3)[\[35\]](#page-12-4) It also cemented bin Salman's supremacy over business elites in Saudi Arabia and resulted in a mass seizure of assets by the bin Salman regime[.\[33\]](#page-12-2)

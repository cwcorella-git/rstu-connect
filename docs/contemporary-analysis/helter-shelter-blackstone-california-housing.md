*Private Equity Stakeholder Project Report — August 2024*

This comprehensive report documents how Blackstone, the nation's largest landlord with nearly 350,000 rental units, systematically exploits California's affordable housing crisis for profit while actively working to prevent solutions.

## Portfolio Expansion

Since 2021, Blackstone has aggressively expanded its rental holdings:

- Added over **256,000 units** through acquisitions
- Purchased **66 San Diego properties** with 5,800 units for **$1 billion** in May 2021 alone
- Controls nearly **350,000 rental units** nationwide

## Rent Increases Double Market Averages

At San Diego properties, Blackstone's rent increases far exceeded market norms:

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Average rent increase (Q3 2021 - Q1 2024) | **38%** |
| Average rent (before) | $1,696 |
| Average rent (after) | $2,339 |
| San Diego market average increase | 20% |
| **Blackstone vs. market** | **Nearly 2x** |

Some buildings saw even more extreme increases:

- **Mar at Cottonwood**: 79% rent increase
- Multiple properties: Over 70% increases

## Eviction Strategy

After the pandemic moratorium, Blackstone resumed aggressive eviction practices:

- California's rent-control cap (10% annually) **doesn't apply to new tenants**
- This creates financial incentive to **displace existing residents**
- Company filed evictions against tenants owing **just one month's rent**
- Pattern suggests systematic use of eviction to enable rent resets

## Profiting From Shortage

Blackstone explicitly tells investors it benefits from housing scarcity. Company president Jon Gray stated:

> "The good news is multifamily construction is now down about a third."

The company celebrates reduced housing supply because scarcity boosts rental asset values. This creates a perverse incentive: Blackstone profits more when housing is harder to find.

## Political Opposition to Rent Control

Blackstone has spent heavily to defeat tenant protections:

| Year | Amount | Purpose |
|------|--------|---------|
| 2018 | **$7+ million** | Defeat California rent control ballot measure |
| 2020 | **$7+ million** | Defeat similar measure |

Notably, Blackstone used **investor capital from public pension funds and UC** rather than corporate accounts—meaning public employees and students unwittingly funded opposition to tenant protections.

## Price-Fixing Allegations

Evidence indicates Blackstone uses **RealPage's YieldStar software** for revenue management. This technology is now the subject of major legal action:

- **Arizona Attorney General**: Filed lawsuit alleging illegal price-fixing
- **DC Attorney General**: Filed similar lawsuit
- Allegations: Software enables landlord cartels to coordinate rent increases
- Multiple class-action suits pending nationally

If proven, these allegations would demonstrate that Blackstone's rent increases result not from market forces but from **coordinated anti-competitive behavior**.

## Recommendations

The report calls on Blackstone to:

1. **Limit annual rent increases to 3%**
2. **Provide relocation assistance** to displaced tenants
3. **Cease opposing rent-control legislation**
4. **Recognize tenant organizations**
5. **Negotiate in good faith** with behind-on-rent tenants
6. **Discontinue use of algorithmic pricing** tools facing legal challenge

## Key Takeaways for Tenant Organizers

### The System Is Working as Designed

Blackstone's practices are not aberrations—they are the logical outcome of treating housing as a financial asset. When the goal is maximizing returns to investors, tenant welfare becomes an obstacle.

### Scarcity Is Profitable

Corporate landlords benefit from housing shortages. They have no incentive to support increased housing supply and every incentive to oppose it.

### Legal Limits Have Loopholes

Rent control protects existing tenants, but corporate landlords use eviction to create turnover and reset rents. Comprehensive tenant protections must address this dynamic.

### Political Power Is Essential

Blackstone's millions in political spending have defeated tenant protections at the ballot. Tenant movements must build countervailing political power.

### Algorithmic Collusion Is Real

The RealPage allegations reveal how technology can facilitate price-fixing among nominally competing landlords. This is an emerging frontier for tenant advocacy.

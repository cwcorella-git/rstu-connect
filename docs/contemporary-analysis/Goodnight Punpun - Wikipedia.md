**Goodnight Punpun** [\(Japanese:](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_language) おやすみプンプン, [Hepburn:](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hepburn_romanization) Oyasumi Punpun) is a Japanese [manga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manga) series written and illustrated by [Inio Asano.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inio_Asano) It was originally serialized in [Shogakukan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shogakukan)'s [seinen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seinen_manga) [manga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seinen_manga) magazine [Weekly Young Sunday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weekly_Young_Sunday) between 2007 and 2008, and was transferred to [Weekly Big](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weekly_Big_Comic_Spirits) [Comic Spirits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weekly_Big_Comic_Spirits), where it ran from 2008 to 2013. Its chapters were collected in thirteen [tank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tank%C5%8Dbon)ōbon volumes. In North America, it was licensed for English release by [Viz Media.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viz_Media)

A [coming-of-age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coming-of-age_story) [drama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drama) story, it follows the life of a child named Onodera Punpun, from his elementary school years to his early 20s, as he copes with his dysfunctional family, love life, friends, life goals and hyperactive mind, while occasionally focusing on the lives and struggles of his schoolmates and family. Punpun and the members of his family are normal humans, but are depicted to the reader in the form of crudely drawn birds. The manga explores themes such as depression, love, trauma, social isolation, death, and family.

Goodnight Punpun follows the life experiences of Punpun Onodera, a young boy living in Japan, as well as a few of his friends. The story follows Punpun as he grows up, splitting it into around four stages of his life: elementary school, middle school, high school, and his early 20s.

Punpun Onodera (⼩野寺 プンプン, Onodera Punpun) / Punpun Punyama (プン⼭ プンプン, Punyama Punpun)

A young boy who is most often depicted as a bird, although he is also shown in other forms as his character evolves. When he is feeling confused about life or depressed he consults "God" using a chant his uncle taught him.

## Aiko Tanaka (田中 愛⼦, Tanaka Aiko)

Punpun's primary love interest. Early on, she suggests that she and Punpun run away to [Kagoshima.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kagoshima) She occasionally takes on the last name Orihara (織原).

## God (神, Kami, literally God)

A being, displayed as a photographic afro head, that often appears in front of Punpun in his times of need (Punpun "summons" him by saying, "Dear God, Dear God, Tinkle hoy" (神様神様チンクルホイ, Kamisama Kamisama Chinkuru hoi).

## Mama Punpun

Punpun's mother, depressed and suffering from mood swings and anger issues. She has a very conflicted relationship with Punpun, to whom she gives her last name, Onodera, after she and Punpun's father get divorced.

## Yūichi Onodera (⼩野寺 雄⼀, Onodera Yūichi)

Punpun's uncle, a 30-something [freeter.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freeter) Yūichi takes care of Punpun while his mother is in the hospital.

## Midori Ōkuma (⼤隈 翠, Ōkuma Midori)

Yūichi's girlfriend who runs a cafe. She briefly joins Punpun's family during middle school and helps take care of him and his family.

## Sachi Nanjō (南条 幸, Nanjō Sachi)

A young woman Punpun meets in his young adult life. She is an aspiring manga artist who grows to be one of Punpun's close friends.

## Masumi Seki (関 真澄, Seki Masumi)

One of Punpun's childhood friends, who is a close companion of Shimizu. He is cynical and aloof, but cares deeply for Shimizu.

## Kō Shimizu (清⽔ コー, Shimizu Kō)

One of Punpun's childhood friends. Shimizu has a wild imagination and depends on Seki. He later joins Pegasus' cult.

Toshiki Hoshikawa (星川 敏樹, Hoshikawa Toshiki) / Pegasus The leader of a cult revolving around [Akashic records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akashic_records) in

Punpun's city and a recurring side character.

## Mitsuko Tanaka (田中 光⼦, Tanaka Mitsuko)

Aiko's mother. A cult member, she is cruel and abusive to her daughter and shows signs of mental instability. Though she ends up crippled, later chapters reveal this was an act.

## Heiroku Shishido (宍⼾平六, Shishido Heiroku)

Punpun's landlord, who becomes his friend.

Shuntarō Harumi (晴⾒俊太郎, Harumi Shuntaro) One of Punpun's childhood friends. He becomes an elementary school teacher.

Asano announced the manga a year after finishing [Solanin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solanin). Encouraged by its success, Asano said he was done with "feelgood stories"[.\[4\]](#page-16-3) Despite initial opposition from his editor and publisher, he went through with the manga. Tokie Komuro, the editor-in-chief of [Monthly Sunday Gene-X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monthly_Sunday_Gene-X), who is a supporter of Asano said that the only reason Asano was able to serialize the manga was because of his good track record and reputation from his earlier works[.\[5\]](#page-16-4)

I wanted [to] take the readers coming to the book because they thought Punpun was cute, and upset them. (Laugh) I wanted to say to the reader, "Here's a different kind of manga. Look at what kind of depths of reality manga can plumb."

When he initially planned the story, Asano wanted to chronicle Punpun's growth spanning ten years over seven volumes.[\[5\]](#page-16-4) The first half was supposed to be a romance, and the second half when Punpun and Aiko go on the run similar to a [road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_movie) [movie.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_movie) The manga grew to thirteen volumes because Asano wanted to focus on the art and because many characters developed their own side stories. Asano purposely emphasized elements of the first half like its silliness to increase the shock of the second half. With every dark turn in the manga, sales dropped, which Asano regretted because his readers were being alienated. He also saw his readers as an enemy when he received criticism, which led him to react harshly and cause more backlash. The manga also served as an outlet for Asano's doubts and fears, such as the fear that he might be a victim or perpetrator of murder[.\[4\]](#page-16-3)

When designing Punpun, Asano wanted to find a balance between making his male protagonist too handsome or too ugly and decided to let readers imagine his face.[\[5\]](#page-16-4) Asano originally planned to depict all the characters like Punpun's family, but his editor did not like the idea[.\[6\]](#page-16-5) Asano utilized photography and computer graphics for the backgrounds of the manga. Outdoor backgrounds were created by taking photographs, converting them to black-and-white, and printing them so his assistants could draw outlines and objects on them. Interiors were created in [3D modeling software,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3D_modeling_software) which had the benefit of capturing angles impossible with cameras. When asked why he placed so much emphasis on the backgrounds, Asano said that it allows the drawings to have more impact, especially since characters like Punpun are lacking in dynamism[.\[5\]](#page-16-4) Asano later came to regret digitally processing his images because he felt he was ruining his pen art[.\[4\]](#page-16-3)

Punpun's depiction as a faceless [caricature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caricature) was meant to help readers identify with Punpun and encourage them to keep reading, both when he was depicted as a bird, and in his later forms[.\[4\]](#page-16-3) Asano also utilized Punpun's simple look for symbolism[,\[5\]](#page-16-4) like giving him bull horns to represent [Altair,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altair) the cowherd star, to symbolize his love triangle as the [Summer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summer_Triangle) [Triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summer_Triangle) with Aiko as [Vega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vega) and Sachi as [Deneb.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deneb) [\[4\]](#page-16-3)

Asano described the young Punpun as a [fundamentalist,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundamentalist) which leads to his regrets and dislike of gray areas later. Asano also ascribed these characteristics to the other characters: "The main characters in Punpun always remain children in the way their purity leads them to fail and become social misfits." At end of the manga, Asano was originally going to make Punpun die while saving a child of a friend, but he felt that it was too "clean" of an ending. He continued the theme of nothing going right for Punpun by making him live and by denying Punpun solitude after Aiko's death by pairing him up with Sachi. In the final chapter, Punpun's experiences are contrasted with those of his childhood friend Harumi to show Punpun from the perspective of a normal person. Harumi sees Punpun surrounded by friends, but in reality nothing went right for him, further emphasizing the theme of failure[.\[4\]](#page-16-3)

In terms of genres, Asano disliked the labeling of the manga as an utsumanga (depressing manga) or [surreal,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surrealism) which he felt pigeonholed the manga. Since the manga was serialized in a [seinen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seinen) magazine, Asano created the manga for readers who could accept immorality rather than see the protagonist as a role model[.\[4\]](#page-16-3)

Written and illustrated by [Inio Asano,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inio_Asano) Goodnight Punpun was first serialized in [Shogakukan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shogakukan)'s [Weekly Young Sunday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weekly_Young_Sunday) from March 15, 2007[,\[7\]](#page-17-0)[\[8\]](#page-17-1) until July 31, 2008, when the magazine ceased its publication[.\[9\]](#page-17-2) It was then transferred to [Weekly Big](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weekly_Big_Comic_Spirits) [Comic Spirits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weekly_Big_Comic_Spirits), being published from October 20, 2008[,\[10\]](#page-17-3)[\[11\]](#page-17-4) to November 2, 2013[.\[12\]](#page-17-5)[\[13\]](#page-17-6) Shogakukan compiled the 147 chapters into thirteen volumes between August 3, 200[7\[14\]](#page-18-0) and December 27, 2013[.\[15\]](#page-18-1) Some of these volumes have been sold as limited [special editions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_edition) with extras like: a phone strap, T-shirt, colored pencil set with figures, and lensless glasses[.\[16\]](#page-18-2)

In July 2015, [Viz Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viz_Media) announced at [Otakon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otakon) that they licensed the mang[a\[17\]](#page-18-3) and would be releasing the manga in seven omnibus volumes[,\[18\]](#page-18-4) with the first published on March 15, 2016[,\[19\]](#page-18-5) and the last one on September 19, 2017[.\[20\]](#page-18-6)

Punpun and Aiko continue their journey, as their depressions and relationship are at their worst. Aiko insists on how happy she is that the two fell in love, but when Punpun wakes up from his nap, he discovers that she committed suicide by hanging herself. Refusing to accept the truth, Punpun carries her body with him, but it is eventually discovered and taken away by locals. He comes back to Tokyo and attempts to kill himself near an old building he, Aiko and their friends visited back in elementary school, but is found by Sachi who saves him and brings him to a hospital. Meanwhile, Pegasus and his cultists commit mass suicide, but Seki rescues an unconscious Shimizu from the burning building. Although Shimizu survives, he becomes amnesiac, and doesn't remember who Seki is. A year later, Punpun, whose [p](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probation)robation for the death of Aiko's mother just finished, has a normal job, having found relative peace in an unexciting normal life. Harumi, an old friend of Punpun from elementary school, meets him by chance in a street, and after a short, unremarkable discussion, the two part ways. As Punpun, surrounded by the friends he made through those last years, tearfully waves him goodbye, Harumi waves back, knowing that they will likely never meet again. At the school where Harumi teaches, a girl transfers into his class, and a young student falls in love with her at first sight, just like Punpun did with Aiko eleven years before.

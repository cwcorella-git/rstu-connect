# **Ethnic nationalism**

**Ethnic nationalism**, also known as **ethnonationalism**, [\[1\]](#page-4-0) is a form of [nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalism) wherein the [nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nation) and [nationality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationality) are defined in terms of [ethnicity,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnicity)[\[2\]](#page-4-1)[\[3\]](#page-4-2) with emphasis on an [ethnocentric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnocentric) (and in some cases an [ethnostate/ethnocratic\)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnocracy) approach to various [political](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political) issues related to national affirmation of a particular ethnic group.[\[4\]](#page-4-3)[\[5\]](#page-5-0)

The central tenet of ethnic nationalists is that "nations are defined by a shared heritage, which usually includes a [common language,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_language) a common faith, and a [common ethnic ancestry"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Y-DNA_haplogroups_by_ethnic_group).[\[6\]](#page-5-1) Those of other ethnicities may be classified as [second-class citizens.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second-class_citizens)[\[7\]](#page-5-2)[\[8\]](#page-5-3)

Scholars of [diaspora studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diaspora_studies) broaden the concept of "nation" to [diasporic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diasporic) communities. The terms "ethnonation" and "ethnonationalism" are sometimes used to describe a conceptual collective of dispersed ethnics[.\[9\]](#page-5-4) Defining an [ethnos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_group) widely can lead to ethnic nationalism becoming a form of [pan-nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-nationalism) or macronationalism, as in cases such as [pan-Germanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-Germanism) or [pan-Slavism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-Slavism)[.\[10\]](#page-5-5)

In scholarly literature, ethnic nationalism is usually contrasted with [civic nationalism,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civic_nationalism) although this distinction has also been criticized.[\[11\]](#page-5-6)[\[12\]](#page-5-7)[\[13\]](#page-5-8)

### **Conceptual development**

#### **History**

The study of ethnonationalism emerged in the early 20th century in the interwar period between [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I) and [World War II,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) with the "redrawing of the political map of Europe in part along ethnic and national lines according to a proclaimed "right of peoples" to [self-determination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-determination) and the rise of [fascist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascism) ethnocentric ideologies (including [Nazism\)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazism).[\[14\]](#page-5-9) Philosopher [Hans Kohn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Kohn) was one of the first to differentiate ethnic nationalism from [civic nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civic_nationalism) in his 1944 publication *[The Idea of Nationalism: A Study in Its Origins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Idea_of_Nationalism:_A_Study_in_Its_Origins_and_Background) [and Background](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Idea_of_Nationalism:_A_Study_in_Its_Origins_and_Background)*. [\[15\]](#page-5-10)

During the [Cold War,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_War) the independence movement initiated in former European colonies in Asia and Africa reinvigorated research into ethnic, tribal and national identities and the "political difficulties" stemming from their interactions with territorial statehood[,\[14\]](#page-5-9) while the [collapse of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union) in the 1980s and 1990s and the "resurgence of ethnic and national claims and conflicts in its aftermath" only further spurred ethnonationalism scholarship in the late 20th century.[\[14\]](#page-5-9)

Increased international migration as a function of contemporary globalization has also given rise to "ethnonational" movements, including reactionary ["nativist"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nativism_(politics)) groups focused on exclusionary [identity politics.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identity_politics) In the developed world, such trends have in some cases taken on an explicitly xenophobic and racist character, as seen in the example of ["white nationalism"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_nationalism) in the United States.[\[14\]](#page-5-9)

# **Characteristics**

The central political tenet of ethnic nationalism is that [ethnic groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_group) are entitled to [self-determination.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-determination) The outcome of this right to self-determination may vary, from calls for self-regulated administrative bodies within an already established society, to an [autonomous entity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_entity) separate from that society, to the institution of [ethnic federalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_federalism) within a multi-ethnic society, to establishing an independent [sovereign state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sovereign_state) removed from that society. In international relations, it also leads to policies and movements for [irredentism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irredentism) to claim a common nation based upon ethnicity, or for the establishment of an [ethnocratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnocracy) (mono-ethnocratic or polyethnocratic) political structure in which the [state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_(polity)) apparatus is controlled by a politically and militarily dominant ethnic nationalist group or a group of several ethnic nationalist groups from select ethnicities to further its interests, power and resources.[\[16\]](#page-5-11)

In scholarly literature, ethnic nationalism is usually contrasted with [civic nationalism.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civic_nationalism) Ethnic nationalism bases membership of the nation on descent or heredity, often articulated in terms of common blood or kinship, rather than on political membership. Hence, [nation states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nation_state) with strong traditions of ethnic nationalism tend to define nationality or citizenship by *[jus sanguinis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jus_sanguinis)* (the law of blood, descent from a person of that nationality), and countries with strong traditions of civic nationalism tend to define nationality or citizenship by *[jus soli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jus_soli)* (the law of soil, birth within the nation state). Ethnic nationalism is, therefore, seen as exclusive, while civic nationalism tends to be inclusive. Rather than allegiance to common civic ideals and cultural traditions, then, ethnic nationalism tends to emphasise narratives of common descent.

Some types of ethnic nationalism are firmly rooted in the idea of ethnicity as an inherited characteristic, for example [black nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_nationalism) or [white nationalism.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_nationalism) Often ethnic nationalism also manifests in the [assimilation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forced_assimilation) of minority ethnic groups into the dominant group, for example as with [Italianisation.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italianisation) This assimilation may or may not be predicated on a belief in some common ancestry with assimilated groups (for example with [Germanisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germanisation#Under_the_Third_Reich) in the Second World war). An extreme version is [racial nationalism.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racial_nationalism)

Recent theories and empirical data suggest that people maintain dual lay beliefs about nationality, such that it can be both inherited biologically at birth and acquired culturally in life[.\[17\]](#page-5-12)

#### **Role in discrimination**

In 2018, [Tendayi Achiume,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E._Tendayi_Achiume) a UN Special Rapporteur on [racism,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racism) released a UN Human Rights Council report which states that "more than 75% of the world's known stateless populations belong to minority groups" and highlights the role of ethnonationalism in the international deprivation of citizenship rights.[\[18\]](#page-5-13) In the report, Achiume re-stated that international human rights law prohibits citizens from discriminating against noncitizens on the basis of their race, descent, national or ethnic origin and she also stated that citizenship, nationality, and immigration laws which discriminate against non-citizens are violations of international law. [\[18\]](#page-5-13) She also noted the role of laws restricting marriage rights with respect to certain national, religious, ethnic or racial groups, which she said were "often deployed by states to preserve notions of national, ethnic and racial "purity"."[\[18\]](#page-5-13) Achiume called ethnonationalist politics the "most obvious driver of racial discrimination in citizenship and immigration laws" and driven by populist leaders defining nations "in terms of assumed blood ties and ethnicity"[.\[18\]](#page-5-13)

In the 19th and 20th centuries, European colonial powers used ethnonationalism to justify barring colonial subjects from citizenship, and in Europe, [Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jews) and [Roma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romani_people) were excluded from citizenship on the same grounds[.\[18\]](#page-5-13) Today, migrants are a frequent target of ethnonationalist rhetoric related to "ethnic purity and religious, cultural or linguistic preservation".[\[18\]](#page-5-13) Even countries with proud histories of immigration have fallen prey to the vilification of "certain racial, religious and national groups" on prejudicial grounds. Achiume called the case of the [Rohingya Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rohingya_Muslims) a "chilling example", with the Burma Citizenship Act of 1982 discriminating based on ethnicity and rendering many Rohingya stateless.[\[18\]](#page-5-13) The violation of the rights of [Afro-Caribbean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_African-Caribbean_people) [British citizens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_citizens) from the ["Windrush generation"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windrush_generation) is a pertinent example of similar prejudice in the developed world but states all over the world use misinformation to portray "certain racial, national and religious groups as inherent threats to national security" and justify stripping or denying rights. [\[18\]](#page-5-13)

Extreme forms of ethnic nationalism, as in the case of [Myanmar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myanmar) and its government's [persecution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rohingya_genocide) of the [Rohingya,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rohingya_people) have been identified as causes of various [genocides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genocides) and episodes of [ethnic cleansing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_cleansing)[.\[19\]](#page-5-14)[\[20\]](#page-5-15)[\[21\]](#page-5-16) In his 2005 book *[The Great Game of Genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Great_Game_of_Genocide)*, historian [Donald Bloxham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Bloxham) argued that the [Armenian genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_genocide) "represents a clear logic of ethnic nationalism when it is carried to its absolute extreme in multinational societies."[\[22\]](#page-5-17)

### **Left-wing nationalist variant**

#### **Social Ethno-nationalism**

Social Ethno-nationalism is a doctrine aimed at promoting social progress while defending the ethnic interests of different peoples, both emancipation and supremacy. For example, the [Australian Labor Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Labor_Party) had a strong [white nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_nationalism) component and has openly supported the [White Australia policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Australia_policy) in the past.[\[23\]](#page-5-18)

#### **Ethno-regionalism**

The [People's Republic of Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People%27s_Republic_of_Congo) regime was designed as a Soviet-style socialist one-party state, it was essentially a military regime with a strongly ethno-regional character. Members of the southern ethnic groups, who were far more numerous than northerners, were included in the power structure, but the top leaders were consistently northerners. In Guinea-Bissau under [Luís Cabral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lu%C3%ADs_Cabral) was by some sections of the ruling party accused that he and the other members with Cape Verdean origins of dominating the party."

# **Contemporary examples**

Ethnic nationalism is present in many states' immigration policies in the form of [repatriation laws.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Repatriation_laws) [Armenia,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Repatriation_of_Armenians) [Bosnia and Herzegovina,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnia_and_Herzegovina) [Bulgaria,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgaria) [Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatia), [Finland,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finland) [Germany,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany) [Hungary,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungary) [Ireland,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Ireland) [Israel,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel) [Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbia) and [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey) provide automatic or rapid citizenship to members of diasporas of their predominant ethnic group, if desired. #### **Azerbaijan**

Since 1918, political elites with [pan-Turkist-](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-Turkist)oriented sentiments in Azerbaijan have depended on the concept of ethnic nationalism to create an anti-Iranian sense of ethnicity among [Iranian Azerbaijanis.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_Azerbaijanis) While Azerbaijani President [Ilham Aliyev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilham_Aliyev) has attempted to dismiss claims that his government is taking any anti-Iranian ethnic nationalist positions, the Iranian government and groups like Iranian nationalist circles, especially Iranian Azerbaijanis, are dubious about the intentions of the Azerbaijani government due to the ongoing actions of irredentist elements like [Congress of World Azerbaijanis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_World_Azerbaijanis) or [Southern Azerbaijan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Azerbaijan_National_Awakening_Movement) [National Awakening Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Azerbaijan_National_Awakening_Movement) and the support that they receive from Azerbaijani authorities, both state and [non-state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-state_actor)[.\[25\]](#page-5-20) According to [Akbar Ahmed,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akbar_Ahmed) after the country gained independence in 1991, the Lezgin and Avar peoples of Azerbaijan "became subjected to [Ataturk-style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustafa_Kemal_Atat%C3%BCrk#Unification_and_nationalisation_efforts) ethnic nationalism by the Azeri-dominated center."[\[26\]](#page-5-21)

#### **Israel**

The [State of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel) originated from the conceptions and principles of [Theodore Herzl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodor_Herzl)'s work [Der Judenstaat,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Der_Judenstaat) which envisioned the creation of a [state for the Jewish people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_state) in [Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_(region)) or [Argentina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentina) [\[27\]](#page-5-22) as a solution to the [Jewish question](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_question) in [Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europe) posed by [Romantic nationalists.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romantic_nationalism) In his later work, *[The Old New Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Old_New_Land)*, Herzl envisioned a [bi-national state,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multinational_state) where Jews only made up a majority in a few cities and everyone spoke [German.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language)[\[28\]](#page-6-0)[\[29\]](#page-6-1)[\[30\]](#page-6-2)

Concerns about ethnonationalism in Israel were raised since the [Knesset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knesset)'s adoption of the *[Israel as the](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basic_Law:_Israel_as_the_Nation-State_of_the_Jewish_People) [Nation-State of the Jewish People](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basic_Law:_Israel_as_the_Nation-State_of_the_Jewish_People)* as part of its [basic laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basic_Laws_of_Israel) under the [Fourth Netanyahu government,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty-fourth_government_of_Israel) an act which was [highly controversial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basic_Law:_Israel_as_the_Nation-State_of_the_Jewish_People#In_Israel) within Israel itself[\[31\]](#page-6-3)[\[32\]](#page-6-4) and among various Jewish groups in the [diaspora,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_diaspora) [\[33\]](#page-6-5) as it potentially threatened the formula of Israel as a [Jewish and democratic state.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_and_democratic_state) This law was controversial especially with many [Left Zionists,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labor_Zionism) who saw the bill as undermining Israeli democracy[.\[34\]](#page-6-6)[\[35\]](#page-6-7) [\[36\]](#page-6-8)[\[37\]](#page-6-9)

#### **Malaysia**

In [Malaysia,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysia) the [Bumiputera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bumiputera_(Malaysia)) principle recognises the "special position" of the Malays provided in the [Constitution of Malaysia,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Malaysia) in particular [Article 153.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Article_153_of_the_Constitution_of_Malaysia) However, the constitution does not use the term bumiputra; it defines only "Malay" and ["indigenous peoples"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orang_Asli) (Article 160(2)),[\[38\]](#page-6-10) "natives" of Sarawak (161A(6)(a)),[\[39\]](#page-6-11) and "natives" of Sabah (Article 161A(6) (b))[.\[39\]](#page-6-11) Some pro-Bumiputra policies exist as affi[rmative action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affirmative_action) for bumiputras since the [Malaysian New Economic Policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_New_Economic_Policy) is based on race, not deprivation. For instance, all Bumiputra, regardless of their financial standing, are entitled to a 7 percent discount on houses or property, including luxurious units, but low-income non-Bumiputra receives no such financial assistance.

Other preferential policies include quotas for admission to government educational institutions, qualifications for public scholarships, marking of universities exam papers, special classes prior to university's end of term exams, positions in government and ownership of businesses. Most of the policies were established in the 1970s. Many policies focus on trying to achieve a Bumiputra share of corporate equity of at least 30% of the total. [Ismail Abdul Rahman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ismail_Abdul_Rahman) proposed that target after the government was unable to agree on a suitable policy goal.

#### **United States**

Although American nationalism is typically described as a paradigmatic example of civic nationalism,[\[40\]](#page-7-0) political scientist Jack Thompson has argued that since the [2016 US presidential election,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_US_presidential_election) ethnonationalism has been pushed to the fore of the American political consciousness by the identity politics of [Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump) surrounding what it means to be a "true" American, which has resulted in ethnocentric ideals becoming "a robust predictor of vote choice for Trump" among [white Americans.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Americans)[\[41\]](#page-7-1) Thompson notes that data from the 2016 [American National Election Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_National_Election_Studies) (ANES) has revealed a positive association between ethnonationalism and anti-immigrant attitudes among white Americans, whose opposition to immigration is "often grounded in fears of the threat that immigration poses to the robustness of America's national identity" that is shaped by the belief set concerning the traits of "true" Americans[.\[42\]](#page-7-2)

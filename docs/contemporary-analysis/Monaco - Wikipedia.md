Monaco, [a] officially the Principality of Monaco, [b] is a sovereign city-state and microstate in Western Europe.

Situated on the French Riviera, it is a semi-enclave bordered by France to the north, east, and west, with the Mediterranean Sea to the south; the Italian region of Liguria is about 15 km (9.3 mi) east. [10] With a population of 38,423 living in an area of 2.08 km² (0.80 sq mi), Monaco is the second smallest sovereign state in the world, after Vatican City, as well as the most densely populated. It also has the world's shortest national coastline of any non-landlocked nation, at 3.83 km (2.38 mi). [11] Fewer than 10,000 of its residents are Monégasque nationals. [12] While the official language of Monaco is French, Italian and Monégasque are also widely spoken and understood. [C]

Monaco is governed under a form of semi-constitutional monarchy, with Prince Albert II as head of state, who holds substantial political powers. The prime minister, who is the

[head of government,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_government) can be either a Monégasque or French citizen; the monarch consults with the [Government of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_France) before an appointment. Key members of the judiciary are detached French magistrates[.\[13\]](#page-62-3) The [House of Grimaldi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Grimaldi) has ruled Monaco, with brief interruptions, since 1297.[\[14\]](#page-62-4) The state's sovereignty was officially recognised by the [Franco-](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco-Mon%C3%A9gasque_Treaties)[Monégasque Treaty of 1861](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco-Mon%C3%A9gasque_Treaties), with Monaco becoming a full [United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations) voting member in 1993. Despite Monaco's independence and separate foreign policy, its defence is the responsibility of France, notwithstanding two [small military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_of_Monaco) [units.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_of_Monaco)

Monaco is recognised as one of the wealthiest and most expensive places in the world[.\[15\]](#page-62-5)[\[16\]](#page-62-6) Its economic development was spurred in the late 19th century with the opening of the state's first casino, the [Monte Carlo Casino,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte_Carlo_Casino) and a [rail connection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marseille%E2%80%93Ventimiglia_railway) to Paris[.\[17\]](#page-63-0) The country's mild climate, scenery, and gambling facilities contributed to its status as a tourist destination and recreation centre for the wealthy. Monaco has become a major banking centre and sought to diversify into the services sector and small, [high-value-added,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_value_products) non-polluting industries. Monaco is a [tax haven;](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax_haven) it has no personal [income tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Income_tax) (except for French citizens) and [low](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax_rates_around_the_world) [business taxes.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax_rates_around_the_world) Over 30% of residents are millionaires[,\[18\]](#page-63-1) with real estate prices reaching €100,000 (\$116,374) per square metre in 2018. Monaco is a global hub of money laundering, and in June 2024 the [Financial Action Task Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_Action_Task_Force) placed Monaco under increased monitoring to combat money laundering and terrorist financing[.\[19\]](#page-63-2)[\[20\]](#page-63-3)

Monaco is among the 46 Member States which constitute the [Council of Europe.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Europe) It is not part of the [European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union) (EU), but [participates in certain EU policies,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monaco%E2%80%93European_Union_relations) including customs and border controls. Through its relationship with France, Monaco uses the [euro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euro) as its sole currency. Monaco joined the [Council of](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Europe) [Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Europe) in 2004 and is a member of the [Organisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organisation_internationale_de_la_Francophonie) [internationale de la Francophonie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organisation_internationale_de_la_Francophonie) (OIF). It hosts the annual motor race, the [Monaco Grand Prix,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monaco_Grand_Prix) one of the original Grands Prix of [Formula One.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formula_One) The local motorsports association gives its name to the [Monte Carlo Rally,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte_Carlo_Rally) hosted in January in the [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Alps) [Alps.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Alps) The principality has a club football team, [AS Monaco,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AS_Monaco) which competes in French [Ligue 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligue_1) and has been [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_French_football_champions) [champions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_French_football_champions) on multiple occasions, as well as a basketball team, which plays in the [Euro League.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euro League) Monaco is a centre of [marine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_conservation) [conservation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_conservation) and research, being home to one of the world's first protected marine habitats[,\[21\]](#page-63-4) an [Oceanographic Museum,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanographic_Museum_of_Monaco) and the [International Atomic Energy Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Atomic_Energy_Agency) Marine Environment Laboratories, the only [marine laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_laboratory) in the [UN system.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_System) [\[22\]](#page-63-5)[\[23\]](#page-63-6)

### **History**

Monaco was first mentioned by the Greek historian [Hecataeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hecataeus_of_Miletus) [of Miletus,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hecataeus_of_Miletus) who referred to it as "Monoikos, a [Ligurian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligures) town"[.\[24\]](#page-63-7) [\[25\]](#page-63-8) [Strabo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strabo) referred to it as Monoikon [limen](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%CE%BB%CE%B9%CE%BC%CE%AE%CE%BD), explaining that Monoikos (Greek for "the solitary one") was another name for [Heracles,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heracles) who had a temple here[;\[26\]](#page-64-0) it is likely, however, that it is in fact an alteration of an older Ligurian name.[\[24\]](#page-63-7) In 1191, the [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire) granted Monaco to the

[Genoese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Genoa) in return for cracking down on pirates. By the early 1200s, the Genoese had built a castle on the rock and were utilizing the port. An ousted branch of a Genoese family, the [Grimaldis,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Grimaldi) captured it in 1297 by posing as monks, but then had to contest it for a century before gaining official control. Though the [Republic of Genoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Genoa) would last until the 19th century, it allowed the Grimaldi family to keep Monaco. Likewise, both France and Spain left it alone for hundreds of years due to agreements with either of them, especially for defense. France annexed it in the [French Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolution) at the end of the 18th century, but after the defeat of Napoleon it was put under the care of the [Kingdom of Sardinia.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Sardinia_(1720%E2%80%931861))

In the 19th century, when [Sardinia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sardinia) became a part of Italy, the region came under French influence but France allowed it to remain independent and it escaped being incorporated into Italy. However, it shrank considerably when it traded two nearby towns in exchange for sovereignty from France. Monaco relied on tourism from the late 19th century to remain financially solvent, and it was at this time the famous casino and hotels were established. Monaco was overrun by the [Axis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axis_powers) [powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axis_powers) in the 1940s during the Second World War and for a short time was administered by Italy, then [Nazi Germany,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Germany) before being liberated. Although the occupation lasted for just a short time, it resulted in the deportation of the [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jews) population and execution of several [French Resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Resistance) members from Monaco. Since then Monaco has been independent. It has taken some steps towards [integration with](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microstates_and_the_European_Union) [the European Union.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microstates_and_the_European_Union)

#### **Arrival of the Grimaldi family**

## ![](_page_8_Picture_2.jpeg)

[Rainier I,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainier_I_of_Monaco,_Lord_of_Cagnes) victor of the naval battle at [Zierikzee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Zierikzee) and first sovereign Grimaldi ruler of Monaco

Following a grant of land from Emperor [Henry VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_VI,_Holy_Roman_Emperor) in 1191, Monaco was refounded in 1215 as a colony of Genoa.[\[27\]](#page-64-1)[\[28\]](#page-64-2) Monaco was first ruled by a member of the House of Grimaldi in 1297, when [Francesco Grimaldi,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fran%C3%A7ois_Grimaldi) known as "Malizia" (translated from Italian either as "The Malicious One" or "The Cunning One"), and his men captured the fortress protecting the [Rock of Monaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_of_Monaco) while dressed as [Franciscan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franciscans) [friars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friar) - a monaco in Italian - although this is a coincidence as the area was already known by this name[.\[29\]](#page-64-3)

Francesco was evicted a few years later by the Genoese forces, and the struggle over "the Rock" continued for another century[.\[30\]](#page-64-4) The Grimaldi family was Genoese and the struggle was something of a family feud. The Genoese engaged in other conflicts, and in the late 1300s Genoa lost Monaco after fighting the [Crown of Aragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_Aragon) over [Corsica.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corsica) [\[31\]](#page-64-5) [Aragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aragon) eventually became part of a united Spain, and other parts of the land grant came to be integrated piecemeal into other states. Between 1346 and 1355, Monaco annexed the towns of [Menton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menton) and [Roquebrune,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roquebrune-Cap-Martin) increasing its territory by almost ten times[.\[31\]](#page-64-5)

#### **1400-1800**

## ![](_page_9_Picture_4.jpeg)

## Monaco (as part of the Republic of Genoa) in 1494

In 1419, the Grimaldi family purchased Monaco from the Crown of Aragon and became the official and undisputed rulers of "the Rock of Monaco". In 1612, [Honoré II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honor%C3%A9_II) began to style himself "Prince" of Monaco[.\[32\]](#page-64-6) In the 1630s, he sought French protection against the Spanish forces and, in 1642, was received at the court of [Louis XIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_XIII) as a "duc et pair étranger". [\[33\]](#page-64-7)

The princes of Monaco became vassals of the French kings while at the same time remaining sovereign princes. Though successive princes and their families spent most of their lives in [Paris,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris) and intermarried with French and Italian nobilities, the House of Grimaldi is of Genoese origin. The principality continued its existence as a protectorate of France until the [French Revolution.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolution) [\[34\]](#page-64-8)

#### **19th century**

## ![](_page_10_Figure_3.jpeg)

A map of the [County of Nice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_of_Nice) showing the area of the [Italian kingdom of](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Sardinia_(1720%E2%80%931861)) [Sardinia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Sardinia_(1720%E2%80%931861)) annexed in 1860 to France (light brown). The area in red had already become part of France before 1860.

In 1793, Revolutionary forces captured Monaco and until 1814 it was occupied by the French (in this period much of Europe had been overrun by the French armies under the command of Napoleon Bonaparte)[.\[33\]](#page-64-7)[\[35\]](#page-64-9) The principality was reestablished in 1814 under the Grimaldis. It was designated a protectorate of the [Kingdom of Sardinia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Sardinia_(1720%E2%80%931861)) by the [Congress of](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_Vienna) [Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_Vienna) in 1815[.\[35\]](#page-64-9) Monaco remained in this position until 1860 when, by the [Treaty of Turin,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Turin_(1860)) the Sardinian forces pulled out of the principality; the surrounding [County of Nice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_of_Nice) (as well as [Savoy\)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savoy) was ceded to France[.\[36\]](#page-65-0) Monaco became a French protectorate once again. Italian was the official language in Monaco until 1860, when it was replaced by French.[\[37\]](#page-65-1)

Before this time there was unrest in [Menton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menton) and [Roquebrune,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roquebrune-Cap-Martin) where the townspeople had become weary of heavy taxation by the Grimaldi family. They declared their independence as the [Free Cities of Menton and Roquebrune,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Cities_of_Menton_and_Roquebrune) hoping for annexation by Sardinia. France protested. The unrest continued until [Charles III of Monaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_III,_Prince_of_Monaco) gave up his claim to the two mainland towns (some 95% of the principality at the time) that had been ruled by the Grimaldi family for over 500 years. [\[38\]](#page-65-2)

These were ceded to France in return for 4,100,000 francs.[\[39\]](#page-65-3) The transfer and Monaco's sovereignty were recognised by the [Franco-Monégasque Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco-Mon%C3%A9gasque_Treaties) of 1861. In 1869, the principality stopped collecting income tax from its residents -- an indulgence the Grimaldi family could afford to entertain thanks solely to the extraordinary success of the casino.[\[40\]](#page-65-4) This made Monaco a playground for the rich and a favoured place for them to live[.\[41\]](#page-65-5)

#### **20th century**

## ![](_page_11_Picture_5.jpeg)

[Photochrom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photochrom) of Monaco circa 1900

Until the [Monégasque Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mon%C3%A9gasque_Revolution) of 1910 forced the adoption of the 1911 [Constitution of Monaco,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Monaco) the [princes of Monaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_rulers_of_Monaco) were [absolute rulers.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absolute_monarchy) [\[42\]](#page-65-6) The new constitution slightly reduced the autocratic rule of the Grimaldi family and [Prince Albert I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_I,_Prince_of_Monaco) suspended it during the First World War.

In July 1918, a new [Franco-Monégasque Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco-Mon%C3%A9gasque_Treaties) was signed, providing for limited French protection over Monaco. The treaty, endorsed in 1919 by the [Treaty of Versailles,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Versailles) established that Monégasque international policy would be aligned with French political, military and economic interests. It also resolved the [Monaco succession crisis.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monaco_succession_crisis_of_1918)

## ![](_page_12_Picture_3.jpeg)

The [marriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wedding_of_Rainier_III,_Prince_of_Monaco,_and_Grace_Kelly) of Hollywood actress [Grace Kelly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grace_Kelly) to [Prince Rainier III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainier_III,_Prince_of_Monaco) brought media attention to the principality.

In 1943, the Italian Army [invaded and occupied Monaco,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_invasion_and_Occupation_of_Monaco_during_World_War_II) forming a [fascist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascism) administration[.\[43\]](#page-65-7) In September 1943, after Mussolini's fall from power, the German [Wehrmacht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wehrmacht) occupied Italy and Monaco, and the Nazi deportation of the Jewish population began. [René Blum,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ren%C3%A9_Blum_(ballet)) the prominent French Jew who founded the Ballet de l'Opéra in Monte Carlo, was arrested in his [Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris) home and held in the [Drancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drancy_internment_camp) deportation camp outside the French capital before being transported to [Auschwitz,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auschwitz_concentration_camp) where he was later murdered[.\[44\]](#page-65-8) Blum's colleague [Raoul Gunsbourg,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raoul_Gunsbourg) the director of the [Opéra de Monte-Carlo,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Op%C3%A9ra_de_Monte-Carlo) helped by the [French Resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Resistance), escaped arrest and fled to [Switzerland.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland) [\[45\]](#page-65-9) In August 1944, the Germans executed René Borghini, Joseph-Henri Lajoux and Esther Poggio, who were Resistance leaders.

[Rainier III,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainier_III,_Prince_of_Monaco) succeeded to the throne on the death of his grandfather, Prince [Louis II,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_II,_Prince_of_Monaco) in 1949, and ruled until 2005. On 19 April 1956, Prince Rainier married the American actress [Grace Kelly,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grace_Kelly) an event that was widely televised and covered in the popular press, focusing the world's attention on the tiny principality[.\[46\]](#page-66-0)

A 1962 amendment to the constitution abolished capital punishment, provided for [women's suffrage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women%27s_suffrage) and established a [Supreme Court of Monaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Monaco) to guarantee fundamental liberties. In 1963, a crisis developed when [Charles de Gaulle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_de_Gaulle) blockaded Monaco, angered by its status as a tax haven for wealthy French citizens[.\[47\]](#page-66-1)

In 1993, the Principality of Monaco became a member of the [United Nations,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations) with full voting rights[.\[36\]](#page-65-0)[\[48\]](#page-66-2)

#### **21st century**

In 2002, a new treaty between France and Monaco specified that, should there be no heirs to carry on the Grimaldi dynasty, the principality would still remain an independent nation rather than revert to France. Monaco's military defense is still the responsibility of France[.\[49\]](#page-66-3)[\[50\]](#page-66-4)

## Prince's Palace of Monaco

On 31 March 2005, [Rainier III,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainier_III,_Prince_of_Monaco) who was too ill to exercise his duties, relinquished them to his only son and heir, Albert.[\[51\]](#page-66-5) He died six days later, after a reign of 56 years, with his son succeeding him as [Albert II,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_II,_Prince_of_Monaco) [Sovereign Prince of Monaco.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sovereign_Prince_of_Monaco) Following a period of official mourning, Prince Albert II formally assumed the princely crown on 12 July 2005[,\[52\]](#page-66-6) in a celebration that began with a solemn Mass at [Saint Nicholas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Nicholas_Cathedral,_Monaco) [Cathedral,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Nicholas_Cathedral,_Monaco) where his father had been buried three months earlier. His accession to the Monégasque throne was a twostep event with a further ceremony, drawing heads of state for an elaborate reception, held on 18 November 2005, at the historic [Prince's Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince%27s_Palace_of_Monaco) in [Monaco-Ville.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monaco-Ville) [\[53\]](#page-66-7) On 27 August 2015, Albert II apologised for Monaco's role during World War II in facilitating the deportation of a total of 90 Jews and resistance fighters, of whom only nine survived. "We committed the irreparable in handing over to the neighbouring authorities women, men and a child who had taken refuge with us to escape the persecutions they had suffered in France," Albert said at a ceremony in which a monument to the victims was unveiled at the Monaco cemetery. "In distress, they came specifically to take shelter with us, thinking they would find neutrality.["\[54\]](#page-66-8)

In 2015, Monaco unanimously approved a modest [land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_reclamation_in_Monaco) [reclamation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_reclamation_in_Monaco) expansion intended primarily to accommodate desperately needed housing and a small green/park area.[\[55\]](#page-67-0) Monaco had previously considered an expansion in 2008, but had called it off[.\[55\]](#page-67-0) The plan is for about six [hectares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hectare) (15 acres) of apartment buildings, parks, shops and offices to a land value of about 1 billion [euros.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euro) [\[56\]](#page-67-1) The development will be adjacent to the [Larvotto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larvotto) district and also will include a small marina.[\[56\]](#page-67-1) [\[57\]](#page-67-2) There were four main proposals, and the final mix of use will be finalised as the development progresses.[\[58\]](#page-67-3) The name for the new district is [Anse du Portier.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Portier) [\[57\]](#page-67-2)

On 29 February 2020, Monaco announced its first case of [COVID-19,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19) a man who was admitted to the [Princess Grace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princess_Grace_Hospital_Centre) [Hospital Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princess_Grace_Hospital_Centre) then transferred to [Nice University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nice_University) Hospital in France[.\[59\]](#page-67-4)[\[60\]](#page-67-5)

On 3 September 2020, the first Monégasque satellite, OSM-1 CICERO, was launched into space from [French Guiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Guiana) aboard a [Vega rocket.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vega_(rocket)) [\[61\]](#page-67-6) The satellite was built in Monaco by Orbital Solutions Monaco.

Monaco has been governed under a [constitutional monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_monarchy) since 1911, with the [Sovereign Prince of Monaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_of_Monaco) as [head of](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state) [state.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state) [\[62\]](#page-68-0) The [executive branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_(government)) consists of a [Minister of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_State_(Monaco)) as the [head of government,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_government) who presides over the other five members of the [Council of Government.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Government) [\[63\]](#page-68-1) Until 2002, the Minister of State was a French citizen appointed by the prince from among candidates proposed by the Government of France; since a constitutional amendment in 2002, the Minister of State can be French or Monégasque[.\[27\]](#page-64-1) Since July 2025 the office is held by [Christophe Mirmand.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christophe_Mirmand)

Under the 1962 [Constitution of Monaco,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Monaco) the prince shares his [veto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veto) power with the [unicameral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unicameralism) [National Council.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Council_(Monaco)) [\[64\]](#page-68-2) The 24 members of the National Council are elected for five-year terms; 16 are chosen through a majority electoral system and 8 by proportional representation[.\[65\]](#page-68-3) All legislation requires the approval of the National Council. Following the [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Monegasque_general_election) [Monegasque general election,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Monegasque_general_election) all 24 seats are held by the promonarchist [Monegasque National Union.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monegasque_National_Union) [\[66\]](#page-68-4) The election system has often resulted in lopsided victories in terms of seats won by parties, well out of proportion with the received votes: In the [2013 Monegasque general election,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Monegasque_general_election) the [Horizon Monaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horizon_Monaco) and [Union Monegasque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Monegasque) parties got 50.34% and 38.99% of the votes respectively, but got 20 and 3 seats each. [Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_House) [House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_House) ranked Monaco 60th of 207 countries in 2024, deducting many points for having both an unelected political head of state and an unelected head of government.[\[67\]](#page-68-5)

The principality's city affairs are managed by the [Municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipality_of_Monaco) [of Monaco.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipality_of_Monaco) The municipality is directed by the [Communal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communal_Council_of_Monaco) [Council,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communal_Council_of_Monaco) [\[68\]](#page-68-6) which consists of 14 elected members and is presided over by a [mayor.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mayors_of_Monaco) [\[69\]](#page-68-7) [Georges Marsan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georges_Marsan) has been mayor since 2003. Unlike the National Council, communal councillors are elected for four-year term[s\[70\]](#page-68-8) and are strictly [non-partisan;](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-partisan) [oppositions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opposition_(politics)) inside the council frequently form[.\[68\]](#page-68-6)[\[71\]](#page-68-9)

Members of the judiciary of Monaco are appointed by the Sovereign Prince. Key positions within the judiciary are held by French magistrates, proposed by the Government of France. Monaco currently has three [examining magistrates.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Examining_magistrate) [\[72\]](#page-69-0)

#### **Security**

## ![](_page_17_Picture_5.jpeg)

## Palace guards in Monaco

The wider defence of the nation is provided by France. Monaco has no navy or air force, but on both a per-capita and per-area basis, Monaco has one of the largest police forces (515 police officers for about 38,000 people) and police presences in the world[.\[73\]](#page-69-1) Its police includes a special unit which operates patrol and surveillance boats jointly with the military. Police forces in Monaco are commanded by a French officer.[\[74\]](#page-69-2)

There is also a small [military force.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_of_Monaco) This consists of a bodyguard unit for the prince and his [palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince%27s_Palace_of_Monaco) in [Monaco-Ville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monaco-Ville) called the [Compagnie des Carabiniers du Prince](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compagnie_des_Carabiniers_du_Prince) (Prince's Company of Carabiniers)[;\[75\]](#page-69-3) together with the militarised, armed fire and civil defence corps [\(Sapeurs-Pompiers\)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corps_des_Sapeurs-Pompiers_de_Monaco) it forms Monaco's total forces[.\[76\]](#page-69-4) The Compagnie des Carabiniers du Prince was created by [Prince Honoré IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honor%C3%A9_IV,_Prince_of_Monaco) in 1817 for the protection of the principality and the princely family. The company numbers exactly 116 officers and men; while the [non-commissioned officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-commissioned_officer) and [soldiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soldier) are local, the officers have generally served in the [French Army.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Army) In addition to their guard duties as described, the carabiniers patrol the principality's beaches and coastal waters[.\[77\]](#page-69-5)

#### **Foreign policy framework**

Monaco's foreign policy is conducted by the Department of External Relations and Cooperation, headed by the Minister for Foreign Affairs and Cooperation[.\[78\]](#page-69-6) Under a treaty dating from 1918 (noted in the Treaty of Versailles), France is responsible for defending the sovereignty of Monaco, and Monaco in turn aligns certain sovereign functions with French interests.[\[79\]](#page-69-7) Over the decades, the bilateral arrangements have been revised via treaties in 1945, 1963, and a new agreement ratified in 2005[.\[80\]](#page-69-8) Although Monaco is not a member of the European Union, it participates in certain EU policies through France, particularly customs, VAT, and border controls; it also uses the euro as its currency under agreement with the EU and France[.\[80\]](#page-69-8)

Monaco acceded to the [Council of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Europe) on 4 October 2004. [\[81\]](#page-69-9)[\[82\]](#page-69-10) Through this membership, Monaco participates in the Council's mission to protect human rights, strengthen the rule of law, and promote democracy[.\[82\]](#page-69-10)

Monaco engages in multiple Council of Europe bodies and mechanisms:

- In the \*\*Committee of Ministers\*\*, Monaco is represented by its Foreign Minister and Permanent Representative to the Council of Europe (Gabriel Revel)[.\[83\]](#page-70-0)
- • In the \*\*Parliamentary Assembly\*\*, Monaco sends 2 members and 2 substitutes[.\[83\]](#page-70-0)
- • In the \*\*Congress of Local and Regional Authorities\*\*, Monaco similarly has a delegation[.\[83\]](#page-70-0)
- • Monaco has a judge at the \*\*European Court of Human Rights\*\* (currently Sébastien Biancheri), and is bound by the Court's decisions[.\[83\]](#page-70-0) As of 2024, it had 15 applications before ECHR decision bodies and one finding of violation.[\[83\]](#page-70-0)
- • Monaco is party to various Council of Europe conventions, including the anti-money laundering convention (CETS No. 198), and is subject to monitoring by bodies such as GRETA and the Venice Commission[.\[83\]](#page-70-0)
- • Monaco's contribution to the Council budget in 2025 was € 506,540[.\[83\]](#page-70-0)

Monaco's participation in the Council of Europe helps align its legal framework with European human rights norms and gives it a voice in pan-European governance despite its small size.

### **Geography**

## ![](_page_20_Picture_4.jpeg)

Satellite view of Monaco, with the France-Monaco border shown in yellow

Monaco is a sovereign [city-state,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City-state) with five quarters and ten wards[,\[84\]](#page-70-1) located on the [French Riviera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Riviera) in [Western Europe.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Europe) It is bordered by France's [Alpes-Maritimes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpes-Maritimes) department on three sides, with one side bordering the [Mediterranean Sea.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_Sea) Its centre is about 16 km (9.9 mi) from [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy) and only 13 km (8.1 mi) northeast of [Nice.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nice) [\[48\]](#page-66-2)

It has an area of 2.1 km2 (0.81 sq mi), or 208 ha (510 acres), and a population of 38,400[,\[85\]](#page-70-2) making Monaco the [second](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_and_dependencies_by_area)[smallest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_and_dependencies_by_area) and the most [densely populated country in the world.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_population_density) [\[48\]](#page-66-2) The country has a [land border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_border) of only 5.47 km (3.40 mi), [\[85\]](#page-70-2) a [coastline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastline) of 3.83 km (2.38 mi), a [maritime claim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_boundary) that extends 22.2 km (13.8 mi), and a width that varies between 1,700 and 349 m (5,577 and 1,145 ft)[.\[86\]](#page-70-3)[\[87\]](#page-70-4)

#### **Architecture**

## ![](_page_27_Picture_5.jpeg)

## Ville ruelle in Monaco

Monaco exhibits a wide range of architecture, but the principality's signature style, particularly in Monte Carlo, is that of the [Belle Époque.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belle_%C3%89poque) It finds its most florid expression in the 1878-9 [Casino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte_Carlo_Casino) and the [Salle Garnier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Op%C3%A9ra_de_Monte-Carlo) created by [Charles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Garnier_(architect)) [Garnier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Garnier_(architect)) and Jules Dutrou. Decorative elements include turrets, balconies, pinnacles, multi-coloured ceramics, and caryatids. These were blended to create a picturesque fantasy of pleasure and luxury, and an alluring expression of how Monaco sought and still seeks, to portray itself[.\[115\]](#page-73-5) This [capriccio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capriccio_(art)) of French, Italian, and Spanish elements were incorporated into hacienda villas and apartments. Following major development in the 1970s, [Prince Rainier III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainier_III,_Prince_of_Monaco) banned high-rise development in the principality. His successor, [Prince Albert II,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_II,_Prince_of_Monaco) overturned this Sovereign Order[.\[116\]](#page-73-6) In recent years[[when?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Dates_and_numbers#Chronological_items)] the accelerating demolition of Monaco's [architectural heritage,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architectural_Heritage) including its single-family villas, has created dismay[.\[117\]](#page-73-7) The principality has no heritage protection legislation[.\[118\]](#page-73-8)

### **Climate**

Monaco has a [hot-summer Mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot-summer_Mediterranean_climate) [\(Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) [climate classification:](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) Csa), with strong maritime influences, with some resemblances to the [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) (Cfa). As a result, it has balmy warm, dry summers and mild, rainy winters. The winters are very mild considering the city's latitude, being as mild as locations located much further south in the [Mediterranean Basin.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_Basin) [\[119\]](#page-74-0) Cool and rainy interludes can interrupt the dry summer season, the average length of which is also shorter. Summer afternoons are infrequently hot (indeed, temperatures greater than 30 °C or 86 °F are rare) as the atmosphere is temperate because of constant sea breezes. On the other hand, the nights are very mild, due to the fairly high temperature of the sea in summer. Generally, temperatures do not drop below 20 °C (68 °F) in this season. In the winter, frosts and snowfalls are extremely rare and generally occur once or twice every ten years[.\[120\]](#page-74-1)[\[121\]](#page-74-2) On 27 February 2018, both Monaco and [Monte Carlo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte_Carlo) experienced

#### **Gambling industry**

## ![](_page_33_Picture_5.jpeg)

[Monte Carlo Casino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte_Carlo_Casino)

The plan for casino gambling was drafted during the reign of [Florestan I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florestan_I,_Prince_of_Monaco) in 1846. Under Louis-Philippe's [petite-bourgeois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petite_bourgeoisie) regime a dignitary such as the [Prince of Monaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarchy_of_Monaco) was not allowed to operate a gambling house[.\[27\]](#page-64-1) All this changed in the dissolute [Second French Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_French_Empire) under [Napoleon III.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon_III) The [House of Grimaldi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Grimaldi) was in dire need of money.

The towns of [Menton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menton,_France) and [Roquebrune,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roquebrune-Cap-Martin) which had been the main sources of income for the Grimaldi family for centuries, were now accustomed to a much-improved [standard of living](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_of_living) and lenient taxation thanks to the Sardinian intervention and clamoured for financial and political concession, even for separation. The Grimaldi family hoped the newly legal industry would help alleviate the difficulties they faced, above all the crushing debt the family had incurred, but Monaco's first casino would not be ready to operate until after [Charles III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_III,_Prince_of_Monaco) assumed the throne in 1856.

The grantee of the princely concession (licence) was unable to attract enough business to sustain the operation and, after relocating the casino several times, sold the concession to French casino magnates [François](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fran%C3%A7ois_Blanc) and Louis Blanc for 1.7 million francs.

The Blancs had already set up a highly successful casino (in fact the largest in Europe) in [Bad-Homburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bad_Homburg) in the Grand Duchy of [Hesse-Homburg,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hesse-Homburg) a small German principality comparable to Monaco, and quickly petitioned Charles III to rename a depressed seaside area known as "Les Spelugues (Den of Thieves)" to "Monte Carlo (Mount Charles).["\[145\]](#page-76-7) They then constructed their casino in the newly dubbed "Monte Carlo" and cleared out the area's less-than-savoury elements to make the neighbourhood surrounding the establishment more conducive to tourism.

## ![](_page_34_Picture_5.jpeg)

## ![](_page_35_Picture_2.jpeg)

The Blancs opened [Le Grand Casino de Monte Carlo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Grand_Casino_de_Monte_Carlo) in 1858 and the casino benefited from the tourist traffic the newly built French railway system created[.\[146\]](#page-76-8) Due to the combination of the casino and the railroads, Monaco finally recovered from the previous half-century of economic slump and the principality's success attracted other businesses[.\[147\]](#page-77-0) In the years following the casino's opening, Monaco founded its [Oceanographic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanographic_Museum) [Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanographic_Museum) and the [Monte Carlo Opera House,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte_Carlo_Opera_House) 46 hotels were built and the number of jewellers operating in Monaco increased by nearly five-fold. In an apparent effort not to overtax citizens, it was decreed that the Monégasque citizens were prohibited from entering the casino unless they were employees[.\[148\]](#page-77-1) By 1869, the casino was making such a vast sum of money that the principality could afford to end tax collection from the Monegasques--a masterstroke that was to attract affluent residents from all over Europe in a policy that still exists today.

![](_page_35_Picture_4.jpeg)

Monte-Carlo Bay Hotel & Resort overlooks the Mediterranean Sea

Today, [Société des bains de mer de Monaco,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soci%C3%A9t%C3%A9_des_bains_de_mer_de_Monaco) which owns Le Grand Casino, still operates in the original building that the Blancs constructed and has since been joined by several other casinos, including the Le Casino Café de Paris, the Monte Carlo Sporting Club & Casino and the Sun Casino. The most recent[[when?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Dates_and_numbers#Chronological_items)] addition in Monte Carlo is the [Monte Carlo Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte-Carlo_Bay_Hotel_%26_Resort) [Casino,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte-Carlo_Bay_Hotel_%26_Resort) which sits on 4 hectares of the Mediterranean Sea; among other things, it offers 145 slot machines, all equipped with ["ticket-in, ticket-out"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ticket-in,_ticket-out) (TITO). It is the first Mediterranean casino to use this technology[.\[149\]](#page-77-2)

#### **Low taxes**

## ![](_page_36_Picture_4.jpeg)

## Boulevard des Moulins meets Avenue Saint-Michel

## ![](_page_36_Picture_6.jpeg)

## Pedestrian crossing on Avenue de la Costa

Monaco has a 20% VAT plus high social-insurance taxes, payable by both employers and employees. The employers' contributions are between 28% and 40% (averaging 35%) of gross salary, including benefits, and employees pay a further 10% to 14% (averaging 13%)[.\[150\]](#page-77-3)

Monaco has never levied [income tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Income_tax) on [individuals,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Individual) [\[93\]](#page-71-0) and foreigners are thus able to use it as a ["tax haven"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax_haven) from their own country's high taxes, because as an independent country, Monaco is not obliged to pay taxes to other countries.[\[151\]](#page-77-4)[\[152\]](#page-77-5)

The absence of a personal income tax has attracted many wealthy "tax refugee" residents from European countries, who derive the majority of their income from activity outside Monaco. [Celebrities,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celebrity) such as [Formula One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formula_One) drivers, attract most of the attention but the vast majority are lesser-known business people[.\[153\]](#page-77-6)

Per a bilateral treaty with France, French citizens who reside in Monaco must still pay income and wealth taxes to France.[\[154\]](#page-77-7) The principality also actively discourages the registration of foreign corporations, charging a 33 per cent [corporation tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporate_tax) on profits unless they can show that at least three-quarters of turnover is generated within Monaco. Unlike classic tax havens, Monaco does not offer offshore financial services.[\[93\]](#page-71-0) In 1998, the [Centre for Tax Policy and Administration,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_for_Tax_Policy_and_Administration) part of the [Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organisation_for_Economic_Co-operation_and_Development) (OECD), issued a first report on the consequences of the financial systems of known [tax havens.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax_haven) [\[155\]](#page-77-8) Monaco did not appear in the list of these territories until 2004, when the OECD became indignant regarding the Monegasque situation and denounced it in a report, along with [Andorra,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andorra) [Liechtenstein,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liechtenstein) [Liberia,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberia) and the [Marshall Islands.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshall_Islands) The report underlined Monaco's lack of co-operation regarding financial information disclosure and availability[.\[156\]](#page-78-0)[\[157\]](#page-78-1) Later, Monaco overcame the OECD's objections and was removed from the "grey list" of uncooperative jurisdictions. In 2009, Monaco went a step further and secured a place on the "white list" after signing twelve information exchange treaties with other jurisdictions. [\[93\]](#page-71-0)

In 2000, the [Financial Action Task Force on Money Laundering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_Action_Task_Force_on_Money_Laundering) (FATF) stated: "The anti-money laundering system in Monaco is comprehensive. Difficulties have been encountered with Monaco by countries in international investigations on serious crimes that appear to be linked also with tax matters. In addition, the FIU of Monaco (SICCFIN) suffers a great lack of adequate resources. The authorities of Monaco have stated that they will provide additional resources to SICCFIN."[\[158\]](#page-78-2)

![](_page_38_Picture_4.jpeg)

## Tall buildings in Monaco

Also in 2000, a report by French politicians [Arnaud Montebourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnaud_Montebourg) and [Vincent Peillon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vincent_Peillon) stated that Monaco had relaxed policies with respect to money laundering including within its casino and that the Government of Monaco had been placing political pressure on the judiciary so that alleged crimes were not being properly investigated[.\[159\]](#page-78-3) In its Progress Report of 2005, the [International Monetary Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Monetary_Fund) (IMF) identified Monaco, along with 36 other territories, as a [tax haven,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax_haven) [\[160\]](#page-78-4) but in its [FATF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_Action_Task_Force_on_Money_Laundering) report of the same year it took a positive view of Monaco's measures against money-laundering[.\[161\]](#page-78-5)[\[162\]](#page-78-6)

The [Council of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Europe) also decided to issue reports naming tax havens. Twenty-two territories, including Monaco, were thus evaluated between 1998 and 2000 on a first round. Monaco was the only territory that refused to perform the second round, between 2001 and 2003, whereas the 21 other territories had planned to implement the third and final round, planned between 2005 and 2007[.\[163\]](#page-78-7)

In June 2024, the FATF added Monaco to its "grey list", which includes countries needing "increased monitoring" due to statewide issues of money laundering and terrorist financing. [\[20\]](#page-63-3)

Monaco issued its own coins in various devaluations connected to the [écu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89cu) already in the seventeenth century, but its first decimal coins of the Monégasque franc were issued in 1837 continued until 2001.

Although Monaco is not a [European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union) member, it is allowed to use the [euro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euro) as its currency by arrangement with the [Council of the European Union;](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_the_European_Union) it is also granted the right to use its own designs on the national side of the [euro coins,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euro_coins) which was introduced in 2002[.\[164\]](#page-78-8) In preparation for this date, the minting of the new euro coins started as early as 2001. Like Belgium, Finland, France, the Netherlands, and Spain, Monaco decided to put the minting date on its coins. This is why the first euro coins from Monaco have the year 2001 on them, instead of 2002, like the other countries of the [Eurozone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurozone) that decided to put the year of first circulation (2002) on their coins. [\[165\]](#page-79-0) Three different designs were selected for the Monégasque coins[.\[166\]](#page-79-1) The design was changed in 2006 after Prince Rainier's death to feature the effigy of Prince Albert[.\[166\]](#page-79-1)

### **Demographics**

Monaco's total population was 38,400 in 2015, and estimated by the [United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations) to be 36,297 as of 1 July 2023[.\[167\]](#page-79-2)[\[168\]](#page-79-3) As of 2024, Monégasques make up 24.1% of residents, followed by French (21.8%), Italian (19.5%), British (7.6%), Swiss (3.1%), Russian (3.1%), Belgian (2.7%), and German nationals (2.5%)[.\[169\]](#page-79-4) According to 2019 studies, 31% of Monaco's population is reported to be [millionaires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millionaire) equalling up to 12,248 individuals

#### **Language**

The main and official language of Monaco is [French,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language) while [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_language) is spoken by the principality's sizeable community from Italy. French and Italian are in fact more spoken in the principality today than [Monégasque,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mon%C3%A9gasque_dialect) its historic vernacular language. A variety of [Ligurian,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligurian_(Romance_language)) Monégasque is not recognised as an official language; nevertheless, some signage appears in both French and Monégasque, and the language is taught in schools. English is also used.

Italian was the official language in Monaco until 1860, when it was replaced by French[.\[37\]](#page-65-1) This was due to the annexation of the surrounding [County of Nice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_of_Nice) to France following the [Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Turin_(1860)) [of Turin \(1860\).](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Turin_(1860)) [\[37\]](#page-65-1)

The [Grimaldi,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grimaldi_Family) [princes of Monaco,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princes_of_Monaco) are of Ligurian origin; thus, the traditional national language is [Monégasque,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mon%C3%A9gasque_language) a variety of [Ligurian,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligurian_(Romance_language)) now spoken by only a minority of residents and as a common second language by many native residents. In [Monaco-Ville,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monaco-Ville) street signs are printed in both French and Monégasque[.\[172\]](#page-79-7)[\[173\]](#page-79-8)

#### **Motor racing**

The highest-profile annual event in the country is the [Monaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monaco_Grand_Prix) [Grand Prix,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monaco_Grand_Prix) which is held on the temporary [Circuit de Monaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_de_Monaco) in the southern part of the country. The race was founded in [1929](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1929_Grand_Prix_season)[\[182\]](#page-80-7) and has been on the [Formula One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formula_One) calendar since the first season in [1950](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1950_Formula_One_season) with only 4 exceptions. Other major races (mostly open-wheel) are also held on the circuit, including [Formula 2,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIA_Formula_2_Championship) [Formula 3,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIA_Formula_3_Championship) the [Monaco e Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monaco_e Prix) [\(Formula E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formula_E)[\[183\]\)](#page-80-8), [Formula Regional Europe,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formula_Regional_European_Championship) [\[184\]](#page-81-0) [Porsche Supercup,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_Supercup) and the [Historic Grand Prix of Monaco.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historic_Grand_Prix_of_Monaco) In the past it also hosted [Formula Renault.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formula_Renault) Setting up the circuit takes six weeks to complete and the removal after the race takes another three weeks[.\[182\]](#page-80-7)

## ![](_page_46_Picture_2.jpeg)

Formation lap for the [1996 Monaco Grand Prix.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_Monaco_Grand_Prix)

Since 1911 the ending portions of the [Monte Carlo Rally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte_Carlo_Rally) has been held in the principality, originally held at the behest of [Prince Albert I.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_I,_Prince_of_Monaco) For most of the 20th century, it was a "concentration rally", a format where drivers started in different cities far away from the country and then competed to get to Monte Carlo first. From 1991 onwards it has been a strictly stage-based rally, with the stages held in the mountainsides in eastern Provence-Alpes-Côte d'Azur and southeastern [Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auvergne-Rh%C3%B4ne-Alpes) in France. As of October 2025, the last edition to have had a competitive stage inside Monaco that wasn't the final ceremonies was in [2008.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Monte_Carlo_Rally) The rally has been on the [World Rally Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Rally_Championship) calendar for most years after its founding in 1973[,\[185\]](#page-81-1) and was on the [International Rally Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Rally_Championship) calendar while that series operated.

Among motor racing teams, [Maserati MSG Racing,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maserati_MSG_Racing) formerly named [ROKiT Venturi Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venturi_Racing) from 2014 to 2022, is the only team based in the principality, headquartered in Fontvieille. [\[186\]](#page-81-2) The marque competes in Formula E and was one of its founding teams. The team is led by Co-Managing Partners Scott Swid and José M Aznar Botella, and Team Principal Cyril Blais[.\[187\]](#page-81-3) The outfit holds 21 podiums under the ROKiT Venturi Racing title, including 8 race wins, and to date has 6 podiums, including 2 race wins, in the series under the Maserati MSG Racing title. [1997 Formula One World Champion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_Formula_One_World_Championship) [Jacques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Villeneuve) [Villeneuve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Villeneuve) and eleven-time Formula One race winner [Felipe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felipe_Massa) [Massa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felipe_Massa) have raced for the team previously[.\[188\]](#page-81-4)[\[189\]](#page-81-5) [Jake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jake_Hughes) [Hughes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jake_Hughes) and [Season 8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021%E2%80%9322_Formula_E_Championship) Formula E champion [Stoffel Vandoorne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stoffel_Vandoorne) currently race for the team.

From 10 to 12 July 2014 Monaco inaugurated the Solar1 Monte Carlo Cup, a series of ocean races exclusively for solar-powered boats[.\[190\]](#page-81-6)[\[191\]](#page-81-7)

### **Football**

## ![](_page_47_Picture_7.jpeg)

## Stade Louis II, home of AS Monaco FC

Monaco has two major football teams, both of which play in the [French football league system:](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_football_league_system) [AS Monaco FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AS_Monaco_FC) (Men), and OS Monaco [Women.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women) AS Monaco plays at the [Stade Louis II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_Louis_II) and competes in [Ligue 1.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligue_1) The club is historically one of the most successful clubs in the French league, having won Ligue 1 eight times (most recently in [2016-17\)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016%E2%80%9317_Ligue_1) and competed at the top level for all but six seasons since 1953. The club reached the [2004 UEFA Champions League Final,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_UEFA_Champions_League_Final) but lost 3-0 to Portuguese team [FC Porto.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Porto) French World Cup-winners [Thierry Henry,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thierry_Henry) [Fabien Barthez,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabien_Barthez) [David Trezeguet,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Trezeguet) and [Kylian Mbappe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kylian_Mbappe) have played for the club. The Stade Louis II also played host to the annual [UEFA Super Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Super_Cup) from 1998 to 2012 between the winners of the [UEFA Champions League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Champions_League) and the [UEFA Europa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Europa_League) [League.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Europa_League)

OS Monaco plays in the French local regional leagues, deep down in the league system. It once played in the [Division 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Division_1_F%C3%A9minine) [Féminine,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Division_1_F%C3%A9minine) in the 1994-95 season, but was quickly relegated. [[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed)]

The [Monaco national football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monaco_national_football_team) represented the nation in minor [association football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_football) events and was controlled by the [Monégasque Football Federation,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mon%C3%A9gasque_Football_Federation) the governing body for [football in Monaco.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_in_Monaco) As of October 2025, they last played a known match in 2017. The federation continues to manage the national amateur tournament [Challenge Prince Rainier III.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Challenge_Prince_Rainier_III) Monaco is one of two sovereign states in Europe (along with the [Vatican City\)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vatican_City) that is not a member of [UEFA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA) and so does not take part in any UEFA or [FIFA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA) competitions.

### **Transport**

[Monaco Monte Carlo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monaco-Monte-Carlo_station) station

One of the most important modes of local transportation is walking, and the city is expanding its pedestrian network in the 2020s to be more pedestrian friendly. This includes plans for a new pedestrian bridge in Fontvieille that connects to the new

#### Wurtemberg [footbridge.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Footbridge) [\[221\]](#page-84-5)[\[222\]](#page-84-6)

The [Monaco-Monte Carlo station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monaco-Monte-Carlo_station) on the [Marseille-Ventimiglia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marseille%E2%80%93Ventimiglia_railway) [railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marseille%E2%80%93Ventimiglia_railway) is served by the [SNCF,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SNCF) the French national rail system. ## Embassy of Monaco, Paris, France

France and Italy have embassies within Monaco, while most other nations represented via [operations in Paris.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_relations_of_Monaco) [\[227\]](#page-85-0)[\[228\]](#page-85-1)

There are about another 30 or so [consulates.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consulates) [\[227\]](#page-85-0) By the 21st century Monaco maintained embassies in Belgium (Brussels), France (Paris), Germany (Berlin), the Vatican, Italy (Rome), Portugal (Lisbon)[,\[229\]](#page-85-2) Spain (Madrid), Switzerland (Bern), United Kingdom (London) and the United States (Washington). [\[227\]](#page-85-0)

As of 2000, nearly two-thirds of the residents of Monaco were foreigners[.\[230\]](#page-85-3) In 2015, the immigrant population was estimated at 60[%\[226\]](#page-84-10) It is reported to be difficult to gain citizenship in Monaco, or at least in relative number there are not many people who do so. In 2015, an immigration rate of about 4 people per 1,000 was noted, or about 100-150 people a year[.\[231\]](#page-85-4) The population of Monaco went from 35,000 in 2008 to 36,000 in 2013, and of that about 20 per cent were native Monegasqu[e\[232\]](#page-85-5) (see also [Nationality law of Monaco\)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationality_law_of_Monaco).

A recurring issue Monaco encounters with other countries is the attempt by foreign nationals to use Monaco to avoid paying taxes in their own country[.\[226\]](#page-84-10) Monaco actually collects a number of taxes including a 20% VAT and 33% on companies unless they make over 75% of their income inside Monaco.[\[226\]](#page-84-10) Monaco does not allow dual citizenship but does have multiple paths to citizenship including by declaration and naturalisation. [\[233\]](#page-85-6) In many cases the key issue for obtaining citizenship, rather than attaining residency in Monaco, is the person's ties to their departure country[.\[233\]](#page-85-6) For example, French citizens must still pay taxes to France even if they live full-time in Monaco unless they resided in the country before 1962 for at least 5 years[.\[233\]](#page-85-6) In the early 1960s there was some tension between France and Monaco over taxation[.\[234\]](#page-85-7)

There are no border formalities entering or leaving France. For visitors, a souvenir [passport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passport) stamp is available on request at Monaco's tourist office. This is located on the far side of the gardens that face the Casino.

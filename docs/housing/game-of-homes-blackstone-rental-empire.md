*In These Times Investigation — March 31, 2014*

This investigative report examines how Blackstone, the world's largest private equity firm, transformed from Wall Street investor to America's biggest single-family home landlord—and the devastating consequences for tenants.

## The Scale of the Land Grab

Blackstone, with $266 billion in assets, created its subsidiary Invitation Homes in 2012 to capitalize on the foreclosure crisis. The numbers are staggering:

- **Over $7 billion** spent acquiring single-family homes
- **41,000+ properties** purchased in just two years
- Investors overall purchased approximately **200,000 single-family homes** in two years
- In April 2013, Blackstone bought **1,400 Atlanta homes in a single day**

The company became the **largest owner of single-family rental homes in the United States.**

## From Owners to Renters

The acquisitions accelerated America's shift from ownership to renting:

- U.S. homeownership fell to **65.2%** by late 2013—an 18-year low
- This represented a drop from **69.4%** before the crisis
- Many foreclosure victims found themselves **renting back their own homes**

As one organizer noted:

> "If Blackstone buys a house at an auction, they may show up pretty much the next day... They'll knock on the door and say, 'You can either rent the home you used to own, or we'll evict you.'"

## "Slumlord, Not Landlord"

Once houses were occupied, Blackstone operated more as slumlord than landlord:

### Maintenance Failures

- Homes left in disrepair
- Maintenance responsibility shifted to tenants
- Constant requests to fix electrical and plumbing problems ignored
- Antonio Hernandez reported a basement railing failure that went unrepaired

### Management Chaos

- One tenant experienced **six different property managers in a single year**
- Atlanta tenants had no one to relay complaints to directly
- Forced to navigate a "straight-to-voicemail" system

### Lease Violations

According to housing advocate Mark Swartz, Invitation Homes leases allegedly violate Chicago's Residential Landlord Tenant Ordinance through:

- Illegal indemnification clauses
- Improper attorney fee provisions
- Attempts to "shift all the risk" to tenants

## Aggressive Rent Increases

- Antonio Hernandez's rent increased **$200 (11%)**—double Chicago's average increase
- Company charged tenants as much as **180% of market rent**
- Lease renewals saw increases of **10% or more**

## Eviction Machine

The investigation revealed a pattern of aggressive evictions:

- **Charlotte**: Invitation Homes filed evictions against **10% of renters**—compared to just 2.5% for competing landlord Camden Property Trust
- **Quick evictions** tied to maintaining securitized bond payments
- One resident received a **five-day eviction notice** after the company's own online payment system failed

Financial analysts suggested quick evictions may be **part of the business model**, necessary to keep securitized rental payments flowing.

## Vacancy and Financial Concerns

A survey of 50 Invitation Homes properties in Chicago found:

- Only **17 occupied**
- **24 properties** acquired 8+ months prior remained unoccupied
- Rents collected on Blackstone's securitized bonds declined **7.6%** (October 2013-January 2014)

## The Securitization Risk

In October 2013, Blackstone partnered with Deutsche Bank to create the first **"rental-backed security"**—a $480 million offering using monthly rent instead of mortgage payments.

This structure mirrors the mortgage-backed securities that caused the 2008 housing collapse. Industry projections suggested securitized rental bonds could become a **nearly $1 trillion industry** within six years.

Rep. Mark Takano requested House Financial Services Committee hearings, warning of unchecked financial innovation risks. Seventy-five advocacy groups warned federal regulators about an emerging housing bubble.

## Tax Base Erosion

The consequences extend beyond individual tenants. Magnetar Capital, an Ohio hedge fund, obtained the **"largest reduction in residential property taxes in the county's history"**—threatening local school budgets and public services.

## Gentrification Engine

Rob Call observed that Invitation Homes properties concentrate in rising-rent areas, describing the acquisitions as:

> "basically a massive land grab."

The strategy positions institutional investors to profit from neighborhood appreciation while displacing long-term residents.

## The Core Problem

Blackstone claims its model removes blighted inventory and provides housing "30 percent below comparable multifamily rents."

However, researchers note that displaced homebuyers typically pay **higher monthly rent than equivalent mortgage payments would cost.** The shift from ownership to renting represents a massive transfer of wealth from working families to Wall Street.

## Lessons for Tenant Organizers

This investigation reveals several key dynamics:

1. **Scale enables abuse.** Corporate landlords operating thousands of properties prioritize financial metrics over tenant welfare.

2. **Securitization creates perverse incentives.** When rents are packaged into bonds, quick evictions maintain payment flows.

3. **Property management chaos is a feature, not a bug.** Difficulty reaching management discourages tenants from asserting rights.

4. **Collective action is essential.** Individual tenants cannot negotiate with billion-dollar corporations. Only organized tenant power can create accountability.

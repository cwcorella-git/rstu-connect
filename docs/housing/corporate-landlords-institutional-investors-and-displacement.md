# Corporate Landlords, Institutional Investors, and Displacement: Eviction Rates in Single-Family Rentals

*Federal Reserve Bank of Atlanta Community and Economic Development Discussion Paper No. 2016-04*

This foundational research from the Federal Reserve Bank of Atlanta documents the relationship between corporate ownership of single-family rentals and eviction rates, providing empirical evidence that institutional landlords evict tenants at significantly higher rates than small landlords.

## Key Findings

### Overall Eviction Crisis in Atlanta

- **22% of Fulton County renters** experienced eviction proceedings in 2015
- Multifamily properties: 25% eviction filing rate
- Single-family homes: 15% eviction filing rate
- In some zip codes, over **40% of all rental households** received eviction notices
- Up to **12.2% of households were forcibly displaced**

### Corporate Ownership Impact

Large corporate owners—defined as firms controlling 15 or more single-family rental properties in Fulton County—demonstrated significantly higher eviction rates:

- **8% more likely** to file eviction notices than small landlords
- This pattern holds even after controlling for property and neighborhood characteristics

### Institutional Investor Eviction Rates

The most alarming finding concerns properties owned by large private equity-backed institutional investors:

- **18-19% more likely** to file eviction notices than smaller landlords
- Some institutional investor firms filed evictions on **20% of their properties annually**—representing two eviction filings for every ten homes
- Some of the largest firms filed eviction notices on **a third of their properties** in a single year

### Geographic and Demographic Patterns

Evictions are spatially concentrated and disproportionately impact communities of color:

- Neighborhoods with the highest eviction filing rates tend to be **predominantly Black neighborhoods**
- Areas where residents lack bachelor's degrees show elevated filing rates
- Institutional investors primarily purchase in **moderate-income, homeowning communities of color**

### Property Characteristics

- Older single-family homes show slightly elevated eviction risks
- Properties with lower appraised values demonstrate increased filings
- These relationships are "relatively small but statistically significant"

## Market Context

The research places these findings within the broader context of post-foreclosure housing market transformation:

- Following the 2008 financial crisis, institutional investors purchased hundreds of thousands of foreclosed single-family homes
- These purchases concentrated in communities hit hardest by the foreclosure crisis
- The shift from owner-occupancy to corporate rental fundamentally changed neighborhood dynamics

## Implications

The authors conclude that institutional investor purchases:

1. **Crowd out homeownership** in affected communities
2. **Increase housing instability** through elevated eviction rates
3. **Drive gentrification and displacement**
4. **Reduce affordability** as corporate owners maximize rental income

## Methodology

The study utilized comprehensive data on eviction filings in Fulton County, Georgia (Atlanta metropolitan area) to examine the relationship between ownership type and eviction patterns. The researchers controlled for property characteristics, neighborhood demographics, and economic factors to isolate the effect of corporate ownership.

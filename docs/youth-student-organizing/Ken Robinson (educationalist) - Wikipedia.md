**Sir Kenneth Robinson** (4 March 1950 – 21 August 2020[\)\[2\]](#page-8-1) was a British author, speaker and international advisor on education in the arts to government, non-profits, education and arts bodies. He was director of the Arts in Schools Project (1985–1989) and Professor of Arts Education at the [University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warwick) [of Warwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warwick) (1989–2001), and professor emeritus after leaving the university[.\[3\]](#page-9-0) In 2003, he was knighted for services to the arts[.\[4\]](#page-9-1)

Originally from a working-class Liverpool family,[\[5\]](#page-9-2) around September 200[1\[6\]](#page-9-3)[\[7\]](#page-9-4) Robinson moved to Los Angeles with his wife and childre[n\[8\]](#page-9-5) to serve as Senior Advisor to the president of the J. Paul Getty Trust[.\[7\]](#page-9-4)

# **Early life and education**

### [\[edit\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Ken_Robinson_(educationalist)&action=edit§ion=1)

Born in [Liverpool,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool) to James and Ethel Robinson, he was one of seven children from a working-class background. One of his brothers, [Neil,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Robinson_(footballer,_born_1957)) became a professional footballer for [Everton,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Everton_F. C.) [Swansea City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swansea_City_A. F. C.) and [Grimsby Town.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grimsby_Town_F. C.) [\[9\]](#page-9-6) After an industrial accident, his father became [quadriplegic.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadriplegic) Robinson contracted [polio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polio) at age four and spent 8 months in hospital. He attended Margaret Beavan Special School due to the physical effects of polio, then [Liverpool Collegiate School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool_Collegiate_School) (1961–1963) and [Wade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wade_Deacon_High_School#History) [Deacon Grammar School,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wade_Deacon_High_School#History) Cheshire (1963–1968). He then studied English and drama (Bachelor of Education – [BEd\)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BEd) at [Bretton Hall College of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bretton_Hall_College_of_Education) (1968–1972) and completed a PhD in 1981 at the [University of London,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_London) researching drama and theatre in education[.\[1\]](#page-8-0)

## **Career and research**

### [\[edit\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Ken_Robinson_(educationalist)&action=edit§ion=2)

From 1985 to 1988, Robinson was director of the Arts in

Schools Project, an initiative to develop the arts education throughout England and Wales[.\[10\]](#page-9-7) The project worked with over 2,000 teachers, artists and administrators in a network of over 300 initiatives and influenced the formulation of the [National Curriculum for England.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Curriculum_for_England) During this period, Robinson chaired Artswork, the UK's national youth arts development agency, and worked as advisor to the [Hong Kong Academy for](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong_Academy_for_Performing_Arts) [Performing Arts.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong_Academy_for_Performing_Arts) [[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed)]

For twelve years, he was professor of education at the [University of Warwick,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warwick) and became professor emeritus. He has received honorary degrees from the [Rhode Island School of](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhode_Island_School_of_Design) [Design,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhode_Island_School_of_Design) [Ringling College of Art and Design,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ringling_College_of_Art_and_Design) the [Open University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_University) and the [Central School of Speech and Drama,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_School_of_Speech_and_Drama) [Birmingham City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham_City_University) [University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham_City_University) and the Liverpool Institute for Performing Arts. He received the Athena Award of the Rhode Island School of Design for services to the arts and education, the [Peabody](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Peabody_Medal) [Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Peabody_Medal) for contributions to the arts and culture in the United States, the LEGO Prize for international achievement in education, and the [Benjamin Franklin Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Franklin_Medal_(Royal_Society_of_Arts)) of the [Royal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Arts) [Society of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Arts) for contributions to cultural relations between the United Kingdom and the United States. In 2005, he was named as one of [Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_(magazine))/[Fortune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortune_(magazine))[/CNN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNN)'s "Principal Voices"[.\[11\]](#page-9-8) In 2003, he was made a [Knight Bachelor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight_Bachelor) for his services to the arts[.\[11\]](#page-9-8)

In 1998, he led a UK commission on creativity, education and the economy and his report, All Our Futures: Creativity, Culture and Education, was influential. [The Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Times) said of it: "This report raises some of the most important issues facing business in the

21st century. It should have every CEO and human resources director thumping the table and demanding action". Robinson is credited with creating a strategy for creative and economic development as part of the Peace Process in Northern Ireland, publishing Unlocking Creativity, a plan implemented across the region and mentoring to the Oklahoma Creativity Project. In 1998, he chaired the National Advisory Committee on Creative and Cultural Education[.\[12\]](#page-9-9)

In 2001, Robinson was appointed senior advisor for education and creativity at the [Getty Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Getty_Museum) in Los Angeles, which lasted at least until 2005[.\[13\]](#page-9-10)[\[14\]](#page-10-0)

Robinson gave three [TED](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TED_(conference)) talks on the importance of creativity in education, which together have been viewed over 98 million times (2023) on the TED website[.\[15\]](#page-10-1)[\[16\]](#page-10-2) In April 2013, he gave a talk titled "How to escape education's death valley", in which he outlines three principles crucial for the human mind to flourish – and how current American education culture works against them[.\[17\]](#page-10-3) At the time of his death in August 2020, his "Do schools kill creativity?" presentation was the most watched TED talk of all time, with 66.3 million views on the TED channel and millions more on You Tube. It has been translated into 62 languages[.\[10\]](#page-9-7)[\[18\]](#page-10-4)[\[19\]](#page-10-5) In 2010, the [Royal Society of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Arts) animated one of Robinson's speeches about changing education paradigms, which has been viewed more than 17 million times on You Tube as of August 2023[.\[20\]](#page-10-6)

Robinson suggested that to engage and succeed, education has to develop on three fronts[.\[21\]](#page-10-7) Firstly, that it should foster [diversity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diversity_(politics)) by offering a broad curriculum and encourage [individualisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Individualism) of the learning process. Secondly, it should promote curiosity through creative teaching, which depends on high quality teacher training and development. Finally, it should focus on awakening creativity through alternative didactic processes that put less emphasis on standardised testing, thereby giving the responsibility for defining the course of education to individual schools and teachers. He believed that much of the present education system in the United States encourages conformity, [compliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compliance_(psychology)) and standardisation rather than creative approaches to learning. Robinson emphasised that we can only succeed if we recognise that education is an organic system, not a mechanical one: successful school administration is a matter of engendering a helpful climate rather than "command and control"[.\[17\]](#page-10-3)

Learning Through Drama: Report of the Schools Council Drama Teaching (1977) was the result of a three-year national development project for the UK Schools Council. Robinson was principal author of The Arts in Schools: Principles, Practice, and Provision (1982), now a key text on arts and education internationally. He edited The Arts and Higher Education, (1984) and co-wrote The Arts in Further Education (1986), Arts Education in Europe, and Facing the Future: The Arts and Education in Hong Kong. [[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed)]

Robinson's 2001 book, Out of Our Minds: Learning to be Creative (Wiley-Capstone), was described by Director magazine as "a truly mind-opening analysis of why we don't get the best out of people at a time of punishing change." [John](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Cleese) [Cleese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Cleese) said of it: "Ken Robinson writes brilliantly about the different ways in which creativity is undervalued and ignored in Western culture and especially in our educational systems."[\[22\]](#page-11-0) The Element: How Finding Your Passion Changes Everything, [\[23\]](#page-11-1) was published in January 2009 by Penguin. "The element" refers to the experience of personal talent meeting personal passion. He argues that in this encounter, we feel most ourselves, most inspired, and achieve to our highest level. The book draws on the stories of creative artists such as [Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Mc Cartney) [Mc Cartney,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Mc Cartney) [The Simpsons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Simpsons) creator [Matt Groening,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Groening) [Meg Ryan,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meg_Ryan) and physicist [Richard Feynman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Feynman) to investigate this paradigm of success.[[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed)]

# RENT STRIKE
## MAY 1ST, 2026

---

They call it rent. We call it what it is: **tribute.**

Every month, you hand over a third of your wages—or half, or more—to someone who did nothing to earn it. Not for labor. Not for care. For the privilege of sleeping indoors on land that existed before any of us were born.

This is not a market. It is extraction. And it has been going on so long we forgot to be furious.

**We're done forgetting.**

---

## The question is not whether you can afford to strike.

**The question is whether you can afford not to.**

Rents in Reno are up 50% since 2019. Wages haven't moved. You're working the same job, doing the same labor, and every year more of it goes to someone else's pocket. This is not inflation. This is transfer—your work becoming their wealth.

Frederick Douglass said it plain: **"Power concedes nothing without a demand. It never did and it never will."**

On May 1st, 2026, we make the demand.

---

## Why May 1st?

In 1886, 300,000 workers walked off their jobs on May Day demanding the eight-hour day. Four were hanged for it. The world chose May 1st to honor them.

In 1907, 10,000 families on the Lower East Side refused a rent hike. Women organized through tenement hallways. Children burned effigies of landlords in the street. Signs read: **LOW RENT OR NO RENT.**

In 1918, tenant strikes created America's first rent control. Rent froze for nine years.

In the 1930s, neighbors carried evicted families' furniture back inside before police could leave. Crowds held rallies on the furniture. They did it again and again until eviction cost more than the landlord could afford. Result: **77,000 families restored to their homes.**

May 1st is not an arbitrary date. It is 140 years of refusal.

---

## The moral case

The Universal Declaration of Human Rights, Article 25: **"Everyone has the right to a standard of living adequate for the health and well-being of himself and of his family, including housing."**

Housing is a human right. Charging for access to a right is not a transaction. It is ransom.

Henry George saw it clearly in the 19th century: land value comes from community, not landlord labor. When a neighborhood improves, rents rise—not because landlords did anything, but because *you* did. Your labor, your presence, your patronage of local businesses. And the landlord collects the increase.

George compared it to a laborer traveling home from work, waylaid by a series of robbers. Each takes a portion of wages. At the end of the road waits the final robber—the one who demands everything that's left.

That robber has a name. It's the landlord.

---

## The system is not broken. It is working exactly as designed.

In 2008, the banks crashed the economy gambling on mortgages. The government gave them $700 billion. 

Then those same banks backed private equity firms to buy foreclosed homes—the same homes families had just lost. Blackstone spent $10 billion. They bought 50,000 homes.

They made money causing the crash. They made money getting bailed out. They made money buying the wreckage. Now they collect rent on the ruins.

Meanwhile: **20 empty homes exist for every homeless person in America.**

This is not failure. This is design. The system produces homelessness because keeping housing scarce keeps rents high. Your desperation is their profit margin.

---

## What we're asking

**Will you withhold rent on May 1st?**

Not alone. Together.

A single tenant can be evicted. A building of tenants can negotiate. A city of tenants can shut the whole thing down.

Every landlord depends on one thing: your compliance. The moment you stop paying, they have nothing. They cannot compel payment. They can only threaten eviction—and eviction takes time, costs money, creates attention, and leaves them with empty units.

When one building strikes, landlords can hold out. When a thousand tenants strike across the city—*on the same day, with the same demand*—they break.

This is not theory. This is what happened in New York, Glasgow, Kansas City, and a hundred other places where tenants decided they were done being robbed.

---

## What we want

**Rent freeze.** No increases while wages stay flat.

**Repairs.** Habitable housing is already the law. We demand enforcement.

**No retaliation.** Nevada law (NRS 118A.510) prohibits retaliation against tenant organizing. We demand landlords obey it.

**Negotiation.** We sit across the table from the people who profit from our homes. We bargain collectively—like workers, like the tenants before us who won every protection we take for granted.

---

## Why this will work

The 1907 strike won rent reductions across the Lower East Side.

The 1918 strike created rent control that lasted a decade.

The 1930s strikes restored 77,000 families to their homes.

KC Tenants in 2024 struck for 248 days. They won **$300,000 in rent forgiveness, a rent freeze, and formal union recognition.**

Every tenant protection in American law exists because tenants refused to submit. None of it was given. All of it was taken.

## Who we are

We are the **Reno-Sparks Tenants Union**—Nevada's first.

We are nurses, teachers, warehouse workers, students, retirees, parents. We pay rent. We're done paying tribute.

Nevada law protects our right to organize. Landlords cannot retaliate against union members (NRS 118A.510). We have the legal shield. Now we need numbers.

## What you can do right now

**1. Pledge to strike.** Commit—publicly, to your neighbors—that you will withhold rent on May 1st.

**2. Find your building.** Talk to the people who share your walls. You have the same landlord, the same problems, the same power.

**3. Come to a meeting.** We meet weekly. Bring your neighbors.

**4. Spread the word.** The strike works when enough of us move together. Every person you bring is one more the landlords can't ignore.

---

## The landlord's only weapon is fear.

Fear of eviction. Fear of bad credit. Fear of being alone.

We answer fear with solidarity. They cannot evict all of us. They cannot survive without our rent. The moment we stop being afraid—the moment we move *together*—their power evaporates.

Audre Lorde: **"Anger expressed and translated into action in the service of our vision and our future is a liberating and strengthening act of clarification."**

Guilt is paralysis. Anger is fuel. And we have plenty to be angry about.

---

## This is not radical. It is overdue.

Martin Luther King Jr. wrote from Birmingham Jail: **"Freedom is never voluntarily given by the oppressor; it must be demanded by the oppressed."**

He also wrote: **"'Wait' has almost always meant 'Never.'"**

We've waited. We've voted. We've hoped the market would correct, the politicians would act, the landlords would show mercy.

None of it came.

On May 1st, we stop waiting.

That was the slogan in the 1930s. It's still the choice.

You can keep handing over your wages—every month, forever, until there's nothing left.

Or you can fight.

**May 1st, 2026.**

We strike.
